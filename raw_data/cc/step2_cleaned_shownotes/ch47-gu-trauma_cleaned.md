# Chapter 47 - Genitourinary Trauma

## Overview

### Rosen’s in Perspective
- People rarely die from renal injuries in the ER (unless their kidney gets pulverized!)
  - There is a reason why we don’t say “ABC...U”
- Usually the signs and symptoms are subtle, but missing them can lead to long term:
  - Kidney Disease
  - HTN, incontinence, sexual dysfunction
- Diagnosis of urinary tract trauma is done in a retrograde way

### A note on female trauma
- Watch for vaginal lacerations in the female with pelvic #’s, but urethral injuries are VERY rare.
  - Protected because of the symphysis pubis and its shorter length
- Retrograde urethrography is NOT recommended in the ER
  - Speak with your urologist if you are concerned

### Digital Rectal Exam (DRE)
- “A palpably abnormal (high riding) prostate is an insensitive indicator of urethral injury”
- A DRE still may be worthwhile in the tertiary survey to look for sphincter tone integrity, bones, gross blood, etc.

## Core Questions

### 1. List 6 general indicators of genitourinary trauma?
1. Flank / abdominal / rib / back / pelvic / scrotal pain
   - Worry in cases of pelvic #’s
   - Anyone with pelvic/suprapubic tenderness
2. Urinary retention
3. Penile/scrotal ecchymosis
4. Gross hematuria = urologic injury until proven otherwise
   - “Microhematuria” is not a reliable indicator of significant pathology
   - Make sure that it's not just rhabdomyolysis - leading to myoglobinuria (no RBCs on urinalysis)
5. Blood at the urethral meatus

Delayed signs:
- Renovascular hypertension
- Incontinence
- Sexual dysfunction

### Lower urinary tract and external genitalia

#### 1. What are the four parts of the male urethra?
1. Pendulous urethra
2. Bulbous urethra
3. Membranous urethra
4. Prostatic urethra

- Anterior urethra (#1-2) and posterior urethra (#3-4) are divided by the urogenital diaphragm

#### 2. What is the mechanism of an anterior urethral injury (at least 5 causes)? What is the mechanism of a posterior urethral injury?
- Anterior urethral injury:
  - Straddle injuries (crushing of the bulbous urethra by falling astride an object)
  - Falls
  - Gunshot wounds
  - Self-instrumentation
  - Amputation

- Posterior urethral injury:
  - Pelvic fractures involving the ischiopubic rami
    - Straddle #
    - Malgaigne #

#### 3. List 4 indications for retrograde urethrogram before foley placement?
- History of urethral trauma (straddle injury)
- Scrotal or penile hematoma
- Blood at the urinary meatus
- High-risk pelvic fracture

#### 4. Describe the technique for a retrograde urethrogram?
- Prepare, position supine
- Shoot a pre-injection KUB film
- Use a 60 mL syringe (+/- Christmas tree adapter) and snugly fit it into the urethral meatus (index and thumb)
- Inject 60 mL of contrast over 30-60 seconds (firm, slow)
- Take second X-Ray during injection of the last 10 mL

#### 5. Classify bladder injuries and describe the mechanism of injury.
- Bladder at highest risk when full
  - ⅔ from blunt trauma, 90% from MVC ejection/seat belt
  - 80% of ruptures are associated with pelvic #s
  - Occasionally injured in penetrating stab or missile trauma

- Three injury types:
  - Contusions
  - Intra-peritoneal ruptures
  - Extra-peritoneal rupture

#### a. Differentiate between extraperitoneal and Intraperitoneal bladder rupture
- Intraperitoneal
  - Blunt lower abdominal trauma with a distended bladder
  - Leads to intraperitoneal urinary contamination

- Extraperitoneal
  - Usually in the setting of pelvic fractures (shear force) where the anterolateral wall gets torn at its fascial attachments

#### 6. Describe the indications and technique for retrograde cystogram?
- Indications:
  - Any suspicion of bladder injury
  - Gross hematuria without pelvic #
  - Microhematuria with pelvic #

- Technique:
  - Conventional retrograde cystogram:
    - Shoot a conventional KUB x-ray, and keep the patient supine
    - Fill the bladder with contrast

#### 7. Outline the management of the different types of bladder injuries.
- Bladder contusions (no extravasation) = foley catheter
- Extraperitoneal bladder ruptures:
  - Uncomplicated injuries: observation with foley drainage
  - Complicated injuries needing operative repair

- Intraperitoneal bladder ruptures:
  - All NEED OR repair (non-urgently)

#### 8. List 3 clinical findings of a penile fracture
1. Slowly progressive penile hematoma
2. “Snapping sound” followed by immediate pain and detumescence
3. “Eggplant” deformity - swelling and ecchymosis localized to the shaft of the penis

#### 9. Describe the management of penile injury
- Constricting devices
- Superficial hematoma
- Superficial lacerations of penis / scrotum
- Degloving injury
- Penile Fracture
- Penile amputation
- Blunt scrotal trauma
- Bites

### Upper urinary tract

#### 1. What is the presentation of a ureteric injury?
- Signs:
  - No reliable acute symptoms/signs
  - Gross/microscopic hematuria - (absent in 25% of cases)

#### 2. What are the indications for renal imaging in an adult trauma patient? In a pediatric trauma?
- Adult:
  - Gross hematuria
  - Microscopic hematuria WITH shock (systolic BP < 90)
  - A history of sudden deceleration injury

- Pediatric:
  - Gross hematuria
  - Microhematuria with > 50 RBCs/hpf
  - “Significant” deceleration injuries

#### 3. Describe the management of renal injuries:
- Grading system (from Trauma.org)
- Blunt:
  - Patients without any indication for renal imaging, even if they have isolated hematuria may be discharged home
- Penetrating:
  - Almost all penetrating renal injuries need OR

## Wisecracks

### What is the most common site of urethral injuries?
- Two most commonly injured sections (much, much more common in men than women):
  - Membranous urethra (posterior urethra)
  - Bulbous urethra (anterior urethra)
