## Core Questions

### 1. Classify headache based on the IHSC (International Headache Society Classification)

- **Primary**
  - Migraine
  - Cluster
  - Tension

- **Secondary**
  - H/A attributed to trauma or injury to the head or neck
  - Cranial or cervical vascular disorder
  - Nonvascular intracranial disorder
  - A substance or its withdrawal
  - Infection
  - Disorder of homeostasis
  - Headache or facial pain attributed to disorder of cranium, neck, eyes, ears, nose, sinuses, teeth, mouth, or other facial or cranial structures

- **Others**
  - Painful cranial neuropathies, other facial pains, and other headaches
  - Other benign primary headaches
  - Headache attributed to psychiatric disorder

### 2. What are the IHSC for migraine without aura (common migraine) and migraine with aura (classic migraine)?

- **Migraine without aura**
  - Recurrent headache disorder manifesting in attacks lasting 4-72 hours. Typical characteristics of the headache are unilateral location, pulsating quality, moderate or severe intensity, aggravation by routine physical activity, and association with nausea and/or photophobia and phonophobia.

- **Migraine with aura**
  - Recurrent attacks, lasting minutes, of unilateral fully-reversible visual, sensory, or other central nervous system symptoms that usually develop gradually and are usually followed by headache and associated migraine symptoms.

### 3. Describe the pathophysiology of migraine

- No longer thought to be vascular in origin.
- Now vascular changes are thought to be an “epiphenomenon” of what is a primary neurologic event triggered by abnormal trigeminal nerve activation.

### 4. Outline a stepwise approach to treating acute migraine attacks. List 8 therapy options for Migraine.

- **Mild to Moderate**
  - Acetaminophen 500–1000 mg PO
  - Ibuprofen 600–800 mg PO
  - Naproxen sodium 275–550 mg PO

- **Moderate to Severe**
  - Dihydroergotamine (DHE) 1 mg IV or IM
  - Triptans (e.g., Sumatriptan 6 mg SC)
  - Prochlorperazine 10 mg IV
  - Metoclopramide 10 mg IV
  - Droperidol 2.5 mg IV
  - Ketorolac 30 mg IV or 30 to 60 mg IM

- **Second Line Agents**
  - Morphine 4 to 8 mg IM or IV
  - Magnesium 2 g IV
  - Valproic acid 1 g IV

### 5. List four migraine variants.

- Retinal migraine
- Hemiplegic migraine
- Migraine with brainstem aura
- Status migrainosus

### 6. What can be done to prevent migraine headache recurrence? List 8 common precipitants.

- **Prevention**
  - Steroids (e.g., dexamethasone 10 mg IV)
  - Naproxen 500 mg
  - Sumatriptan 100 mg
  - Prophylaxis: Beta blockers, TCAs, Antiepileptic drugs

- **Precipitants**
  - Sleep deprivation
  - Stress
  - Hunger
  - Hormonal changes (menstruation)
  - Drugs (e.g., OCPs, Nitroglycerin)
  - Chocolate
  - Caffeine
  - Tyramine-rich foods

### 7. What are the clinical features of a cluster headache? How are they treated? Tension headache?

- **Cluster Headache**
  - Young, middle-aged men who smoke
  - Sudden onset, lasting 15 mins to 3 hrs
  - Unilateral, sharp, stabbing pain in the eye
  - Treatment: High flow O2, Sumatriptan, Octreotide

- **Tension Headache**
  - Common in women in the 4th decade
  - Lasts 30 mins to 7 days
  - Treatment: NSAIDs, Acetaminophen, Metoclopramide, Acupuncture

### 8. List 6 causes and 6 risk factors for SAH.

- **Causes**
  - Ruptured saccular aneurysms
  - Arteriovenous malformations
  - Cavernous angiomas
  - Mycotic aneurysms
  - Neoplasms
  - Blood dyscrasias

- **Risk Factors**
  - Age > 40
  - HTN
  - Smoking
  - Excessive ETOH
  - Sympathomimetic drug use
  - Familial cerebral aneurysm disorders

### 9. Describe a clinical grading scale for cerebral aneurysms and SAH and how it relates to prognosis.

- **Hunt & Hess Clinical Grading Scale**
  - Grade 0 = Unruptured aneurysm
  - Grade 1 = Asymptomatic or minimal headache and slight nuchal rigidity
  - Grade 2 = Moderate or severe headache, nuchal rigidity, no neurologic deficit other than cranial nerve palsy
  - Grade 3 = Drowsiness, confusion, or mild focal deficit
  - Grade 4 = Stupor, moderate to severe hemiparesis
  - Grade 5 = Deep coma, decerebrate posturing, moribund appearance

- **Prognosis**
  - Grades 1-2 = good prognosis
  - Grade 3 = risk for rapid deterioration
  - Grades 4-5 = poor prognosis

### 10. Describe a diagnostic strategy for SAH.

- **Steps**
  1. Think about the diagnosis
  2. Get a non-contrast CT head
  3. Do an LP in CT negative patients if SAH is still suspected
  4. If > 100 RBCs in tube 4, get a CT angiogram to hunt for any aneurysms

### 11. List 6 components of management for SAH.

- Treat acute medical and neurologic complications
- Prevent recurrent hemorrhage
- Stalling vasospasm
- Comprehensive brain resuscitation
- Blood pressure control
- Start Nimodipine ASAP

### 12. What are features of a HA from CNS malignancy? What primary cancers spread to CNS? What are two components of management of a new CNS malignancy?

- **Features**
  - Headaches due to traction on pain-sensitive structures
  - Symptoms of increased ICP

- **Primary Cancers**
  - Lung, breast, melanoma, GI cancer

- **Management**
  - Urgent referral to neurosurgery
  - Treat with corticosteroids for ICP reduction

### 13. Describe clinical features and treatment of carotid and vertebral artery dissection.

- **Carotid Artery Dissection**
  - Triad: Unilateral headache or neck pain, Ipsilateral Horner’s syndrome, Blindness or contralateral motor deficits

- **Vertebral Artery Dissection**
  - Young person with sudden, severe, unilateral, posterior headache

- **Treatment**
  - Antiplatelets and anticoagulation show similar outcomes

### 14. List 8 common clinical features for patients with giant cell arteritis; and its Dx and Rx.

- **Clinical Features**
  - Age > 50, Female > Male, Headache, Visual symptoms, Jaw claudication, Temporal artery tenderness, Systemic symptoms

- **Diagnosis**
  - ESR > 50-100, CRP elevated, Temporal artery biopsy

- **Treatment**
  - Methylprednisolone for visual symptoms, Prednisone for non-visual symptoms

### 15. What are the criteria to diagnose idiopathic intracranial hypertension? How is it managed?

- **Criteria**
  - Headache that remits with normalization of CSF pressure, Papilledema, Nonfocal neurologic examination, Increased CSF opening pressure

- **Management**
  - Lower ICP with acetazolamide, lasix, steroids

## Wisecracks

### 1. How do migraines differ between children and adults?

- In children and adolescents, attacks may last 2-72 hours. Migraine headache in children is more often bilateral than in adults.

### 2. Describe four unique presentations of migraine headache

- Retinal migraine
- Hemiplegic migraine
- Brainstem migraine
- Status migrainosus

### 3. What is the pathophysiology of idiopathic intracranial hypertension? Describe the typical patient.

- Young, obese, women of childbearing age. CSF production/absorption imbalance.

### 4. List 3 LP factors that reduce the incidence of post-LP HA; and describe the management of post-LP HA.

- Small diameter needles, Inserted with the needle bevel up, Atraumatic needle

### 5. Describe post-traumatic headache & hypertensive headache.

- **Post-Traumatic Headache**
  - Common post-concussion or TBI

- **Hypertensive Headache**
  - BP > 180/120 mmHg and headache resolves when BP is normalized

### 6. What is reversible cranial vasoconstriction syndrome?

- A cerebral arteriopathy characterized by segmental areas of vasoconstriction within large- and medium-sized vessels.

### 7. List 10 HA Red Flags

1. Onset > age 50
2. Onset in immunocompromised patients
3. History of malignancy or “B” symptoms
4. First ever headache/symptom
5. Febrile, with no explanation
6. Thunderclap headache
7. Post-traumatic with no imaging
8. Onset with exertion
9. Focal deficits
10. Signs of increased ICP
