### 3. Differentiate between primary and recurrent herpes infection

**Genital Herpes Infection**

- **Primary**: Infection with HSV-1 or HSV-2 in absence of pre-existing antibodies. Symptoms include a cluster of erythematous, painful lesions, dysuria, tender regional lymphadenopathy, fever, headache, and malaise. Duration is generally 2-4 weeks if untreated.
- **Secondary**: Reactivation of latent infection, more common with HSV-2. Symptoms are generally less severe, with prodromal symptoms of itching, burning, and paresthesias. Duration is generally shorter than primary.

### 4. List 4 techniques for the diagnosis of herpes; how is it managed?

- **Clinical**: History of similar lesions in the same distribution supports clinical diagnosis.
- **PCR**: Diagnostic test of choice with highest sensitivity and specificity.
- **Direct fluorescent antibody (DFA)**
- **Serology for HSV**

*Management*: Not curative, but decreases duration and severity. Majority managed with oral antivirals as outpatient. Daily antiviral therapy decreases frequency while being taken.

### 5. List 3 complications of herpes infection

a. Meningoencephalitis  
b. Hepatitis or Pneumonitis  
c. Disseminated infection  

### 6. What are the clinical stages/phases of syphilis? How do you diagnose syphilis? How are the phases managed?

**Syphilis Manifestation**

- **Primary**: Painless papule at the site of inoculation → ulceration forming chancre.
- **Secondary**: Rash, generalized lymphadenopathy, mucous membrane lesions, systemic symptoms.
- **Latent**: Serologic evidence in absence of clinical signs or symptoms.
- **Tertiary**: Cardiovascular and gummatous disease.

*Diagnosis*: Confirmed with dark field microscopy or serologic testing.

*Management*: Benzathine Penicillin G 2.4 million units IM single dose.

### 7. What is the cause of chancroid? How is it managed?

**Chancroid**: Ulcerating infection caused by Haemophilus ducreyi. Managed with Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose or Azithromycin 1000 mg PO single dose.

### 8. List the treatment options for & complications of chlamydia and gonorrhea infections

- **Chlamydia**: Azithromycin 1 g PO single dose or Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID x 7 days.
- **Gonorrhea**: Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose plus Azithromycin 1 g PO single dose.

*Complications*: Disseminated gonorrhea, gonococcal conjunctivitis, epididymitis/orchitis.

### 9. Compare differences in presentation and treatment between Trichomonas, BV, Candidiasis, and Nongonococcal urethritis.

| Presentation | Physical Exam | Treatment |
|--------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Trichomonas** | Females: vaginal discharge, pruritus, dysuria. Males: often asymptomatic. | Metronidazole 2 g PO single dose. |
| **Bacterial vaginosis** | Often asymptomatic, fishy odor. | Metronidazole 500 mg PO BID x 7 days. |
| **Vulvovaginal candidiasis** | Pruritus, abnormal discharge. | Fluconazole 150 mg PO single dose. |
| **Nongonococcal urethritis** | Often asymptomatic. | Azithromycin 1 g PO single dose. |

### 10. List 5 risk factors for PID

a. Multiple sexual partners  
b. Age <25  
c. Partner with an STI  
d. History of STI  
e. History of PID  

### 11. List clinical findings that support a diagnosis of PID as described by the CDC

Diagnosis should be considered in any sexually active woman at risk for STDs who presents with lower abdominal or pelvic pain and one or more of the following findings on pelvic examination:

a. Cervical motion tenderness  
b. Uterine tenderness  
c. Adnexal tenderness  

### 12. Describe inpatient and outpatient treatment regimens for PID

- **Inpatient**: Cefoxitin 2 g IV q6-12h plus Doxycycline 100 mg PO/IV q12h x 10 days.
- **Outpatient**: Ceftriaxone 250 mg IM single dose plus Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID x 14 days.

### 13. List factors which favor admission in PID

a. Surgical emergencies cannot be excluded  
b. Pregnancy  
c. Tubo-ovarian abscess  
d. Severe illness, nausea, vomiting, or high fever  
e. Inability to follow or tolerate outpatient regimens  
f. Failure to respond to oral therapy  

### 14. What are some treatment options for condylomata acuminata (genital warts)?

- **Patient applied**: Imiquimod cream, Podofilox solution or gel, Sinecatechins ointment.
- **Provider administered**: Surgical excision, Cryotherapy, Topical therapy with TCA or BCA.

### 15. What is molluscum contagiosum and how is it managed?

Localized skin infection presenting with papules. Spontaneous resolution typical in 6-12 months. Treatment options include curettage, cryotherapy, or topical agents.

### 16. Describe the diagnosis and management of ectoparasites (pediculosis pubis and scabies).

- **Pediculosis pubis**: Diagnosis by visual inspection. Managed with topical permethrin 1% creams.
- **Scabies**: Diagnosis by visual inspection and confirmed by microscopic examination. Managed with topical permethrin 5% cream.

## Wisecracks

### 1. What is a Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction?

Acute worsening of symptoms after initiation of antimicrobial therapy for syphilis. Typically includes increased malaise, myalgias, and fever within 24 hours of treatment.

### 2. What is the specific pathogenic cause of:

- **Syphilis**: Treponema pallidum  
- **LGV**: Chlamydia trachomatis (L1, L2, and L3 serovars)  
- **Chancroid**: Haemophilus ducreyi  
- **Granuloma inguinale**: Klebsiella granulomatosis
