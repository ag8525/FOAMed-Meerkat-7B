## Overview

### Rosens in Perspective

- AAA is a TRUE aneurysm involving a localized dilation and weakness to all three layers of an artery.
- Remember, AAA is not an acute aortic dissection; these are different diseases!
  - The term dissecting aortic aneurysm is erroneous.
  - Aortic dissection involves blood entering the media of the artery and splitting the aortic wall.
- An aneurysm can develop anywhere, but usually, a AAA develops infra-renally.
  - Normal = 2 cm
  - AAA = > 3 cm

### Pathophysiology

- This is a disease of aging - rare before 50 years of age.
- AAA seems to occur in people with biochemical disease leading to loss of elastin and collagen; not atherosclerotic disease!
- Infectious, traumatic, or connective tissue disease-related AAA are rare.
- Most risk factors are populational, not individual.
  - The presence or absence of risk factors should not strongly influence diagnostic considerations in any individual patient.
  - Up to half of AAA in the USA occur in non-smokers, women, and people younger than 65 years.
  - Women often present with ruptured AAA.
- Key red flags to consider if suspecting a AAA:
  - Age > 65, Men
  - Anyone with CAD or peripheral vascular disease
  - Brothers of patients with AAA (>30%)

### Diagnostics

- U/S is 100% sensitive when a technically adequate study can be obtained.
  - Does have operator and patient-induced limitations.
- CT is the test of choice for people with a suspected ruptured AAA.
  - IV contrast helps determine the lumen, false lumens, and any fistulae.
  - Contrast isn’t mandatory.
  - Helps see retroperitoneal hemorrhage and peri-aortic hematoma.

## Core Questions

### 1. List six presentations of an abdominal aortic aneurysm.

- Incidental: most AAA are discovered incidentally.
- Pain in the abdomen, back, flank, chest, thigh, groin, or scrotum.
  - Often vague, dull.
  - Throbbing, colicky.
  - Acute/severe pain suggests impending rupture.
- Awareness of an abdominal mass/fullness.
- Back pain due to vertebral body erosion.
- Nausea, vomiting, weight loss, bowel obstruction symptoms due to compression of the duodenum.
- Ureteral colic due to ureters being obstructed by the mass.
- Syncope.
- Upper or lower GI bleeding due to aorto-enteric fistula.
- High-output congestive heart failure due to aortovenous/aortocaval fistula.

### 2. Compare Aneurysm and Pseudoaneurysm.

**Aneurysm**
- Occur due to trans-mural weakening: loss of collagen/elastin/arterial muscle integrity.
- A localized dilatation of the aorta, involving all three layers of the artery.

**Pseudoaneurysm**
- Arise from a defect in the arterial wall.
- A collection of flowing blood that communicates with the true lumen but is enclosed with (contained by) just the adventitia or surrounding soft tissue.

### 3. List common misdiagnoses in patients with ruptured AAA.

- Acute abdomen: pancreatitis, diverticulitis, cholecystitis, appendicitis, bowel obstruction.
- Renal colic.
- Intestinal ischemia.
- MSK back pain.
- Perforated viscus.
- Acute MI.

### 4. List three common complications of traditional AAA repair.

- Infection: difficult to diagnose.
  - Graft contamination.
  - Adjacent infectious spread.
  - Hematogenous seeding.
- Aortoenteric fistula (AEF).
- Pseudoaneurysm (anastomotic aneurysm).

### 5. List common delayed complications of Endovascular repair.

- Infection.
- Ischemic complications: spinal cord ischemia, CVA, extremity ischemia, visceral ischemia.
- Aortoenteric fistula.
- Pseudoaneurysm (anastomotic aneurysm).
- Chylous ascites/chylothorax.
- Endo Leak: blood flow outside the graft lumen but contained within the aneurysm sac.

## Wisecracks

### 1. What to do about the intact, asymptomatic aneurysm?

- Clinical trials have compared early surgery vs. close follow-up with serial ultrasounds/symptom onset/size greater than 5.5 cm.
- Found that watchful waiting has similar mortality to early elective repair.
- Depends on features of aneurysm, but these patients generally need close follow-up in the community. Consult Vascular in the department and arrange outpatient management.
- Important to give good discharge teaching regarding flank/back/abdominal pain as a red flag for possible AAA rupture.

## Additional Notes / Commentary

### Management of a ruptured AAA

- Anyone with a confirmed ruptured AAA should be taken to the OR urgently, regardless of hemodynamic stability.
- MOVIE: Ask for at least 6 units of blood to be immediately available, send for cross match.
- Get that bedside US done ASAP.
- Hypotensive patients should go to the OR, not the CT scanner.
- Don’t attempt to fully resuscitate these patients in the ER; they need a surgeon.
  - Target resuscitation to mental status.
  - A good aim is systolic BP 90-100.
- Resuscitate with BLOOD (MTP).
  - 1:1:1 = RBC, FFP, Platelets.
- CAREFUL in reducing BP in the patient with hypertensive AAA rupture because they will crash HARD.
- Mortality for ruptured AAA = 30-40%.
  - Patients in cardiac arrest or very frail are usually not considered for repair.
