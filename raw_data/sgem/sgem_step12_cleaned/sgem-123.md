## Summary
Intranasal fentanyl is highlighted as an effective and safe analgesic for managing acute moderate to severe pain in pediatric patients, as demonstrated in a systematic review of randomized control trials. The review compared intranasal fentanyl to both intramuscular and intravenous morphine, finding no significant differences in pain reduction, except for a lower pain score at 10 minutes with intranasal fentanyl compared to intramuscular morphine. The studies reported minimal adverse events, with only minor side effects such as a bad taste and vomiting. The review supports the use of intranasal fentanyl in emergency settings for children, offering a non-invasive and distress-minimizing option for pain management. In the case of a 13-year-old boy with a dislocated patella, intranasal fentanyl was effectively used to manage pain prior to reduction.

## Case
Thirteen-year-old boy twists his leg at school. He presents to the emergency department with a laterally displaced patella. Prior to reduction of the dislocated patella, you wonder what you can give the child for pain management.

## Background
Oligoanalgesia is the inadequate management of pain, a significant issue in emergency departments, particularly affecting vulnerable groups such as the elderly, women, the mentally ill, certain ethnic groups, and individuals with specific insurance statuses. Pediatric pain management includes both pharmacological methods, such as analgesics, NSAIDs, nerve blocks, sub-dissociative dose ketamine, and opioids, and non-pharmacological approaches, including distraction, sucrose, infant warmers, and splinting. These strategies are supported by various studies, highlighting the importance of tailored pain management practices in pediatric emergency care to address the variability in treatment and improve patient outcomes.

## Point
Can intranasal fentanyl be used in pediatric patients in pain to safely help control pain?

## SGEM Bottom Line
For children with acute moderate to severe pain, using intranasal fentanyl is a safe and effective way to manage the pain.

## Case Resolution
This child is administered intranasal fentanyl and shortly after a reduction of the child’s dislocated patella.

## Clinical Application
In children with acute moderate to severe pain, intranasal fentanyl can be used safely to manage their pain.
