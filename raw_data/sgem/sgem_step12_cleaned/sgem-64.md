## Summary
The document discusses the effectiveness of advanced life support (ACLS) versus basic life support (BLS) in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) scenarios. The OPALS study, referenced in the document, found no significant improvement in survival to hospital discharge when ACLS, which includes intubation and intravenous drug therapy, was added to BLS, which focuses on rapid defibrillation and CPR. The study involved 5,638 patients and showed similar survival rates between the ACLS and BLS groups, with no statistical difference in primary outcomes. Secondary outcomes like return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) and hospital admission were higher in the ACLS group, but without improved long-term survival or neurological outcomes. The findings suggest that while ACLS is standard care, its efficacy in improving survival to discharge is questionable, emphasizing the importance of early CPR and defibrillation in pre-hospital settings.

## Case Scenario
A 43-year-old man experiences a cardiac arrest on the street. You are the first provider on scene with EMS. A cardiac defibrillator is hooked up and the patient is in ventricular fibrillation. He is unsuccessfully shocked and chest compressions are started. The paramedics ask you if you want to intubate and administer medications or load for transport.

## Background
Sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) is a critical medical condition characterized by the abrupt loss of heart function, leading to significant morbidity and mortality, with approximately 500,000 cases annually in the United States. Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) accounts for about half of these cases, with a survival rate of only 7-9.5%. The American Heart Association (AHA) developed a "chain-of-survival" model to improve outcomes, consisting of early access to emergency care, early cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), early defibrillation, and early advanced care, which includes advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS). ACLS involves airway management and drug administration, although its efficacy is debated due to limited supporting evidence. Despite this, ACLS remains a standard of care, with widespread provider training and certification requirements. The introduction of integrated post-cardiac arrest care as a fifth link in the chain aims to enhance recovery, but concerns persist regarding the potential for ACLS to delay transport and its impact on neurological outcomes.

## Bottom Line
Addition of an advanced life support algorithm to BLS management did not increase the survival to hospital discharge for patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

## Case Resolution
You tell the paramedics to support the patient with bag mask ventilation and rapidly transport to the hospital. This concludes the first ever SGEM Classic Paper critical review.
