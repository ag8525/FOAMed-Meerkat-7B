## Summary
The document discusses the management of rib fractures in elderly patients, emphasizing the potential role of spirometry in determining discharge disposition. Rib fractures in older adults can lead to serious complications, and current guidelines often recommend ICU admission for those over 65 with multiple fractures. The study reviewed suggests that spirometry, particularly FEV1, may predict outcomes and facilitate early discharge, though the evidence is not yet robust enough to change current practices. The study highlights the need for further research to validate spirometry as a reliable tool for safely discharging patients from the ED, as current data is limited by retrospective design and potential biases. The case resolution involves admitting the patient for monitoring despite stable spirometry results, reflecting the cautious approach advised until more conclusive evidence is available.

## Case
A 74-year-old woman who suffered a fall earlier today presents to the emergency department (ED) and is found to have five rib fractures to her right thorax, but no other injury. She is otherwise well, and her vitals are stable, but she is in some pain. Recent guidelines recommend admitting the patient to the intensive care unit (ICU), but the patient is eager to return home to her husband who is also well, and whom she claims will be able to help her.

## Background
Rib fractures are prevalent among the elderly and can lead to severe complications such as pneumonia and pneumothorax. Guidelines suggest admitting patients over 65 with two or more rib fractures to an ICU or monitored setting. Typically, patients with three or more fractures are admitted for pain management and monitoring, often discharged without issues. Early spirometry, particularly forced vital capacity (FVC), may predict outcomes and hospital stay length, potentially reducing unnecessary hospitalizations and associated costs. While peak expiratory flow (PEF) lacks outcome correlation, FVC is a reliable prognostic tool. Hand grip strength, a measure of frailty, has shown weak correlation with frailty in older patients, warranting further research to validate its predictive value.

## Clinical Point
Can spirometry testing identify patients 60 years and older with at least three rib fractures who can safely be discharged home from the ED?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Spirometry represents an interesting prognostic tool for older patients with multiple rib fractures, but we do not have enough high-quality evidence for it to guide us in discharging patients home directly from the ED.

## Case Resolution
You ensure adequate pain management after a thorough history is taken and you feel confident that the patient understands the importance of efficiently treating her pain and is reliably reporting her pain levels. You perform a spirometry and the patient’s FEV1 is 65% of predicted. This provides a baseline but does not impact your decision to admit her to hospital on a monitored bed with the hope of her being discharged home within a few days.

## Clinical Application
This was a prospective study performed in order to test signals from retrospective studies regarding the usability of spirometry in the prognosis of elderly patients with multiple rib fractures. The authors manage to verify the results of the retrospective studies and further our belief that spirometry could potentially be of use in the early discharge of this group of patients.
We feel that it is too early to safely discharge this high-risk population directly from the ED based on the results of the spirometry. Further high-quality evidence is needed to support such action.
Hopefully spirometry will prove to be a reliable prognostic tool in elderly patients with multiple rib fractures. Its use could potentially be considered in identifying those patients who do not need to be admitted to the ICU and can likely be discharged home in a few days following adequate pain management.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You have broken five ribs. This is a very painful injury. We would like to admit you to the hospital for a few days and watch you closely. Things can get worse for a few patients. Broken ribs can cause a collapse of the lung and/or bleeding into the lung. This can be a life-threatening complication. However, we can treat this successfully by putting a tube into your chest. Staying for a few days will also allow us to get your pain well controlled.
