## Summary
In the case of a 68-year-old man with a history of hypertension and TIA, the addition of clopidogrel to aspirin (ASA) for stroke prevention is evaluated. The SPS3 Trial, involving 3020 patients with recent lacunar strokes, found no significant benefit in reducing recurrent strokes when clopidogrel was added to ASA, with a hazard ratio of 0.92 for recurrent CVA. However, the combination significantly increased the risk of major hemorrhage (HR 1.97) and all-cause mortality (HR 1.52). The trial was halted early due to these adverse outcomes. The conclusion is that the risks of adding clopidogrel to ASA for secondary stroke prevention outweigh the benefits, as it does not reduce stroke recurrence and increases bleeding and mortality risks.

## Case Scenario
A 68-year-old man arrives at the emergency department (ED) with 15 minutes of tingling in his right arm and leg. He has a history of hypertension and a previous transient ischemic attack (TIA). The examination is completely normal. He is already taking ASA 325mg daily.

## Background
Stroke is a major health concern, being a leading cause of disability and the third most common cause of death in the United States, with lacunar strokes accounting for 25% of ischemic strokes. The standard treatment for stroke prevention has traditionally been aspirin (ASA). However, dual antiplatelet therapy, which has proven beneficial in other cardiovascular conditions such as acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and stenting, as demonstrated in studies like the CURE Trial, is being considered for its potential advantages in stroke management.

## Point
Should you add clopidogrel to prevent a stroke for someone already taking ASA?

## BEEM Bottom Line
The risk of adding clopidogrel to patients already on ASA for secondary CVA prevention exceeds the benefits.
