## Summary
The document discusses the application of the Canadian C-Spine Rule (CCR) by emergency department triage nurses to safely clear cervical spines in adult blunt trauma patients, as demonstrated in a multicenter study. The study involved training nurses to use the CCR, which allowed them to remove cervical spine immobilization in patients without significant risk factors, thereby reducing patient discomfort and ED crowding. The study found that triage nurses could clear 41% of immobilized patients without missing any clinically important cervical spine injuries, and this practice reduced ED length of stay by 26%. The authors concluded that empowering nurses to apply the CCR improves patient management and comfort without compromising safety, although the study was not a randomized controlled trial and faced some compliance challenges.

## Case
A 51-year-old male patient presents to triage in a collar on a back-board via EMS following a rear-end motor vehicle collision (MVC) at a stop light. He was a belted driver with no past medical history and GCS 15. The driver of the car that hit him was texting and did not appear to slow before striking the rear of the patient’s car at about 50 km/hr. The patient complains of left shoulder and neck pain.

## Background
Clearing the cervical spine (c-spine) is a routine procedure in emergency departments, primarily involving the use of the Canadian C-Spine Rule (CCR) or imaging to assess patients, particularly those with blunt trauma. The CCR is a validated clinical decision tool designed to safely clear the c-spine without the need for imaging, thereby reducing unnecessary diagnostic procedures. Despite the low incidence of cervical spine fractures in these patients, the process of c-spine clearance is crucial as it impacts patient comfort and emergency department efficiency. Patients often arrive immobilized on a backboard with a cervical collar and head restraints, which can lead to discomfort and contribute to emergency department crowding while awaiting assessment. Protocols exist to expedite the removal of spine boards, but delays in c-spine clearance can still occur, underscoring the importance of efficient assessment protocols.

## Clinical Point
Can emergency department triage nurses apply the Canadian C-Spine Rule to adult blunt trauma patients and safely clear the c-spine?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Properly educated emergency department triage nurses can apply the Canadian C-Spine Rule to adult blunt trauma patients and safely clear the c-spine.

## Case Resolution
The triage nurse could apply the CCR to the patient because he was involved in a simple rear-end MVC which is one of the low-risk criteria. The triage nurse removed the front of his collar and palpated his c-spines. The patient reported pain all over. The triage nurse then asked the patient to look 45 degrees to the right, then the left. He was able to do the motion, so the triage nurse removed the collar and asked that the patient be offloaded to a regular stretcher.

## Clinical Application
The triage nurses at Victoria Hospital at London Health Sciences Centre were the nerdiest of all nine sites in this study with over 90% of them volunteering to be a part of the implementation. Since the study completion, triage nurses at Victoria Hospital are using the CCR to clear c-spines and you know what, this Canadian C-Spine Rule is now being used in the pre-hospital environment all over Ontario now!

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
I know being in a collar and on a backboard can be very uncomfortable. The paramedics correctly put one on because of the pain you were having in your neck. We have a way to safely remove the collar and so a few little tests without missing any serious neck injuries. You don’t need to be in that collar anymore so we can move you over to a regular stretcher or a chair if you like.
