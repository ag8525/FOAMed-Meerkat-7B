## Summary
The document discusses the current landscape of artificial intelligence-based clinical decision support (AI-CDS) tools in emergency departments (EDs), highlighting their potential to enhance diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic decisions through real-time, evidence-informed recommendations. Despite the promise of AI-CDS tools, the majority remain in early development stages, with few advancing beyond preclinical phases to clinical evaluation or implementation. The scoping review by Kareemi et al. identified 605 studies, predominantly retrospective, with a focus on prognosis, yet lacking robust clinical trials in live ED settings. The findings underscore the need for rigorous testing to ensure safety and efficacy before widespread adoption, emphasizing a cautious approach to integrating AI into clinical practice.

## Background
Clinical decision support (CDS) tools are designed to assist clinicians in emergency departments by providing real-time, evidence-based recommendations to improve diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic decisions. These tools range from traditional paper-based rules to smartphone applications and integrated electronic health record systems, utilizing patient data and expert algorithms to guide care pathways, risk stratification, and resource utilization. Recently, there has been increased interest in applying artificial intelligence (AI), particularly machine learning, to CDS, allowing for pattern recognition and predictions from large datasets. While AI-driven CDS tools offer potential advantages over traditional methods, most remain in early development stages, with limited rigorous testing in emergency settings and insufficient evidence of improved patient outcomes or clinician workflow. Despite some tools receiving FDA clearance, emergency physicians remain cautiously optimistic, emphasizing the need for evidence, safety, and usability in the adoption of AI in clinical practice.
