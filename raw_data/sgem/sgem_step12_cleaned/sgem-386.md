## Summary
In the case of a 71-year-old male with a mid-shaft femoral fracture and hemothorax, the management involved activating a massive transfusion protocol due to ongoing tachycardia and hypotension. The clinical focus is on the optimal ratio of fresh frozen plasma (FFP) to packed red blood cells (pRBC) in massive transfusions for trauma in older adults. An observational cohort study by Hohle et al. found that a 1:1 FFP to pRBC ratio significantly reduces 24-hour and 30-day mortality compared to lower ratios, aligning with findings in younger populations. This study, involving over 3,000 patients aged 65 or older, supports the use of a 1:1 transfusion ratio to improve survival outcomes in older trauma patients, prompting a review of current transfusion protocols.

## Case
A 71-year-old man is brought to your emergency department (ED) by emergency medical service (EMS) having fallen two steps at home. EMS have already splinted an obvious mid-shaft femoral fracture, but he continues to be tachycardic and hypotensive. After a bedside ultrasound shows fluid in the right hemithorax, you insert an intercostal drain which immediately fills with one liter of blood. Noting with some relief that at least he isn’t anticoagulated, you activate the hospital massive transfusion protocol. The transfusion tech calls to remind you that your protocol is currently under review, and asks if you would like the 1:1 or the 1:3 version of fresh-frozen plasma (FFP) to packed red blood cells (pRBC)?

## Background
Major trauma in older patients is becoming more prevalent, with a median age of 63.6 years for major trauma cases in the UK between 2012 and 2017. Falls are the most common cause of traumatic injuries in this demographic, with approximately 20% of falls resulting in injuries and being the leading cause of traumatic mortality. Concerns have arisen regarding the transfusion of only packed red blood cells (PRBC) potentially exacerbating traumatic coagulopathy. Although various trials have sought optimal transfusion component ratios, and the Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma recommends a "high" ratio, there is limited literature on its application specifically in older populations.

## Clinical Point
Does FFP:PRBC ratio in massive transfusion for trauma affect survival in older adults?

## SGEM Bottom Line
1:1 transfusion of packed red blood cells with fresh frozen plasma is associated with lower odds of mortality than other transfusion ratios.

## Case Resolution
You ask the transfusion tech to send pRBCs and FFP in a 1:1 ratio.

## Clinical Application
I will be taking this information back to my home institution to review and discuss our Massive Transfusion Protocol ratio of 1:1 for pRBC and FFP in older adults.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You seem to be bleeding from your chest as well as your broken leg. We need to transfuse some blood to replace the blood you’ve lost. We will transfuse two important parts of blood in equal quantities.
