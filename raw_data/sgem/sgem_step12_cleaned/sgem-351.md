## Summary
The document discusses the challenge of managing geriatric falls in emergency departments (EDs), highlighting the lack of effective tools for predicting recurrent falls and the need for guideline-based assessments. It reviews two scoping studies: one on fall prevention interventions and another on risk stratification for patients over 60 presenting with falls. The studies suggest that multifactorial interventions, particularly those involving exercise, may reduce recurrent falls, but emphasize the need for more research to identify effective intervention combinations. The Geriatric Emergency care Applied Research (GEAR) network prioritizes research on EMS roles, optimal ED assessment tools, patient-prioritized interventions, standardizing fall ascertainment, and ideal intervention components. The document concludes that while there is no definitive tool or intervention, comprehensive geriatric assessments and multifactorial approaches are recommended.

## Case
Mid-shift, you realize that the next patient you are about to see is the third in a row aged over 70 who has fallen at home, and that this is her third attendance for a fall in the last two months. You wonder if any emergency department (ED)-based interventions would help her and people like her be safe.

## Background
Geriatric falls are a significant clinical concern, particularly as they are the most common cause of traumatic injury leading older adults to present at emergency departments (EDs), with nearly three million adults aged 65 and over visiting American EDs annually due to falls. Approximately 20% of these falls result in injuries, and they are the leading cause of traumatic mortality in this age group. Despite the prevalence and risks associated with geriatric falls, a 2014 systematic review by Dr. Chris Carpenter highlighted the lack of effective tools for predicting recurrent falls in ED patients. The SAEM Geriatric Emergency Medicine Task Force has prioritized fall prevention for over a decade. The Geriatric Emergency care Applied Research (GEAR) network aims to enhance emergency care for older adults, including those with cognitive impairments, by identifying research gaps and supporting relevant studies. GEAR 2.0 has been launched with funding opportunities, building on previous work that includes publications on delirium prevention, care transitions, and elder abuse screening.

## Clinical Point
In older patients presenting to ED with falls do risk stratification or fall prevention interventions influence patient-centered or operational outcomes?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Patients may (or may not) benefit from falls screening and interventions. There is inadequate evidence to support a specific tool or intervention across the board, but it is likely that multifactorial interventions are most effective.

## Case Resolution
You refer your patient to the Frailty outreach service, where she will undergo a comprehensive geriatric assessment.

## Clinical Application
Many centers have falls screening or prevention programs; encourage yours to get involved in the GEAR-Falls priority research areas (or the equivalent in your locality).

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
Previous falls are a predictor of future falls – we don’t know the best way to support you to reduce this risk, but we think the most useful approach is looking at all those different factors that might contribute, so we have a team that can do this.
