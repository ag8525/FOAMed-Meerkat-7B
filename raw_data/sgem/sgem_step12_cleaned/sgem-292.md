## Summary
In the case of a 53-year-old man presenting with acute ischemic stroke symptoms and a high NIHSS score suggestive of a large vessel occlusion (LVO), imaging confirmed a clot in the left proximal middle cerebral artery with a large penumbra. The patient was eligible for both endovascular therapy and systemic thrombolysis with alteplase. However, recent evidence, including the MR CLEAN trial and subsequent studies, suggests that endovascular thrombectomy alone may be non-inferior to combined therapy with systemic thrombolytics in LVO strokes, particularly when performed within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. The Yang et al. study supports this, showing no significant difference in functional outcomes between the two approaches, thus questioning the added benefit of thrombolytics given the associated risks. Consequently, the decision was made to proceed with endovascular therapy alone, resulting in successful reperfusion and a favorable recovery trajectory for the patient. This case underscores the need for further research into the role of systemic thrombolytics in the context of endovascular treatment for LVO strokes.

## Case
A 53-year-old previously healthy man presents with 1.5 hours of right sided weakness as well as slurred speech. A rapid bedside assessment gives you a National Institute of Health Stroke Score/Scale (NIHSS) of 9 and you are concerned about a large vessel occlusion (LVO) based on the high NIHSS as well as the presence of both an upper extremity drift and the speech abnormality. A non-contrast CT shows no evidence of intracranial hemorrhage. A CT angiogram plus CT perfusion demonstrate a clot in the left proximal middle cerebral artery (MCA) with a small infarcted area and a large penumbra. Based on your institution’s current guidelines, the patient is a candidate for endovascular therapy, but they are also within the current window for the administration of alteplase. You wonder if you should give the alteplase while waiting for your neurointerventional team?

## Background
Thrombolytics for stroke, particularly acute ischemic strokes, have been debated since the 1995 NINDS trial, with skepticism about their net patient-oriented benefit. The MR CLEAN trial in 2015 marked a significant advancement by demonstrating the benefit of endovascular treatment for patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) presenting within six hours of symptom onset. This trial spurred further research to refine and expand treatment windows. A key aspect of LVO management involves using systemic thrombolytics within the thrombolytic treatment window before endovascular intervention. However, the benefit of systemic thrombolytics in improving outcomes remains uncertain, as they may increase risks and costs. Current evidence questions the efficacy of combining thrombolytics with endovascular therapy, highlighting the need for further research into thrombolytic dosing and application.

## Clinical Point
Is endovascular therapy alone non-inferior to endovascular therapy plus systemic thrombolytics in the treatment of patients with large vessel occlusion strokes presenting within 4.5 hours of onset?

## SGEM Bottom Line
There does not appear to be a role for systemic thrombolysis in acute ischemic stroke for appropriate patients when EVT is readily available.

## Case Resolution
Based on the best available evidence and the potential for harm, you decide to hold off on administration of systemic thrombolytics. Your neurointerventional team calls for the patient 20 minutes after you get the report from radiology and you bring him up to the interventional suite. The patient has a clot retriever deployed which returns circulation to the affected area. Although they have a long road to recovery, the patient is discharged to a rehab setting with only a mild weakness in the arm and fully recovered speech.

## Clinical Application
Additional studies are needed but, given the potential harms of systemic thrombolytics, there should be careful consideration about their use in patients with LVO strokes who are proceeding to endovascular therapy.
