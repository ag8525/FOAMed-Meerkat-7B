## Summary
The document discusses the management of unconscious survivors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, focusing on targeted temperature management. It references a study comparing cooling to 33°C versus 36°C, concluding that there is no significant difference in mortality or neurological outcomes between the two temperatures. The study, involving 939 patients across multiple ICUs, found no benefit in targeting a lower temperature of 33°C over 36°C. The document emphasizes the importance of maintaining a target temperature of 36°C post-resuscitation, aligning with current best practices and international guidelines. The clinical application suggests adopting a 36°C cooling protocol, and the patient communication strategy involves explaining the cooling process to improve survival and neurological outcomes.

## Case Scenario
A 72-year-old man has a witnessed arrest at the Goderich beach. Bystander CPR is started, and he is shocked out of ventricular fibrillation by emergency medical services but does not regain consciousness. His temperature on arrival to the emergency department is 36°C.

## Background
Therapeutic hypothermia is a medical intervention used post-cardiac arrest to provide neuroprotection, as evidenced by two significant randomized control trials published in 2002. These studies demonstrated improved neurological outcomes with the use of cooling methods, such as a cooled air mattress, showing a 55% versus 39% good outcome at six months in one trial and a 49% versus 26% good outcome at hospital discharge in another. The number needed to treat (NNT) for mild therapeutic hypothermia to achieve neuroprotection following cardiopulmonary resuscitation is calculated to be six. The Cochrane Collaboration's 2012 review supports the use of conventional cooling methods, affirming their efficacy in enhancing survival and neurological outcomes, aligning with the International Resuscitation Guidelines.

## Point
Does cooling to a target temperature of 33°C improve survival to hospital discharge and neurological outcome in unconscious survivors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause?

## SGEM Bottom Line
This study did not demonstrate a benefit of a targeted temperature of 33°C vs. 36°C for the survival of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

## Case Resolution
The 72-year-old man with the out-of-hospital cardiac arrest who arrived at 36°C. He was actively cooled to maintain this temperature but was not cooled further to 33°C. You plan to check in on him in the intensive care unit on your next shift.

## Clinical Application
I will start cooling patients to 36°C and admit them to the intensive care unit for further management based on our own local protocols.

## What Do I Tell the Patient
I will tell the patient’s wife that her husband had a cardiac arrest. We were able to bring him back but he is still unconscious. We are going to cool him to improve his chances of survival and a good neurological outcome.
