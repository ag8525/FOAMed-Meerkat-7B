## Summary
The document discusses the safety and efficacy of using topical 1.0% tetracaine for 24 hours in treating uncomplicated corneal abrasions. Despite traditional concerns about delayed healing and potential corneal damage from topical anesthetics, a double-blind, randomized clinical trial found that tetracaine is safe and perceived as more effective for pain relief compared to saline. The study showed no significant difference in healing between the tetracaine and placebo groups, with no complications reported at 1 month. However, the study was underpowered to detect differences in pain scores, and potential unblinding due to local irritation from tetracaine was noted. The findings suggest that tetracaine can be used safely for short-term pain management in uncomplicated corneal abrasions, with follow-up recommended to monitor healing.

## Case Scenario
47-year-old man is playing Marco Polo in the pool with his daughters. He is accidentally hit in the eye and sustains an uncomplicated corneal abrasion.

## Point
Is the use of topical 1.0% tetracaine for 24 hours safe and effective for the treatment of uncomplicated corneal abrasions?

## Background
Corneal abrasions are a frequent and painful condition encountered in emergency departments. The clinical concern with prescribing topical anesthetics for home use in patients with corneal injuries is based on the potential risks of delayed healing, impaired detection of foreign bodies, and the development or exacerbation of keratitis. These concerns are partly derived from studies involving animal models and the effects of local anesthetics injected into the anterior chamber during cataract surgery, which have demonstrated corneal endothelial toxicity. Notable studies include those by Duffin and Olson on tetracaine toxicity, Judge et al. on the corneal endothelial toxicity of topical anesthesia, and Guzey et al. on the effects of bupivacaine and lidocaine on the corneal endothelium. The debate continues on whether these risks are applicable to the use of topical anesthetics for simple corneal abrasions.

## Case Resolution
You offer the Marco Polo playing dad some 1% tetracaine drips to use as needed every two hours for the next 24 hours. The drops sting and burn when used but make it much easier to get to sleep that night. He is seen in the emergency department for follow-up in 48 hours. The pain is gone and the abrasion has completely healed.

## Clinical Application
Topical anesthetics are better at patient-perceived pain relief compared to oral pain medications and saline eye drops. Evidence is not robust, but indicates when topical anesthetics are used appropriately, and for a short duration of time (24 hours) there are no corneal complications.

**What Do I Tell My Patients?**
You have scratched your cornea. Here are some eye drops to help treat the pain. It is safe to use for 24 hours. Your vision is important so we have arranged to see you back in the emergency department in two days. Please come back earlier if you have increased pain, decreased vision, or are otherwise concerned.
