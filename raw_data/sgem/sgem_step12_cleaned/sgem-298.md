## Summary
A meta-analysis by Ebell et al. evaluated the accuracy of individual signs and symptoms for diagnosing community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) in adolescents and adults, using chest X-ray (CXR) as the gold standard. The study found that no single sign or symptom could independently rule in or out pneumonia, with the overall clinical impression being the most reliable indicator, having a positive likelihood ratio of 6.32. Egophony was the best individual sign for ruling in pneumonia, while the absence of abnormal vital signs was most effective for ruling it out. The study emphasizes the importance of clinical judgment and suggests that teaching high-value physical exam elements can improve diagnostic accuracy and reduce unnecessary imaging and antibiotic use. The findings highlight the need for clinicians to refine their diagnostic skills through experience and follow-up, as clinical diagnosis alone is not infallible.

## Case
A 67-year-old woman with no previous health problems presents with fever, cough, and myalgias. You are working with a medical student on their very first rotation, and you want to spend some time teaching them about the history and physical exam. However, being an evidence-based medicine enthusiast, you wonder what aspects of the patient’s presentation are going to be truly helpful in making a diagnosis.

## Background
Fever and cough are common symptoms in emergency departments, particularly during certain times of the year, and distinguishing pneumonia from viral illnesses is crucial due to the risks associated with antibiotic overuse and the inefficiency and potential harm of routine chest x-rays. Pneumonia is a significant clinical concern, and accurate diagnosis is essential to avoid unnecessary treatments and interventions. A meta-analysis has shown that the absence of pneumonia can be effectively ruled out with normal vital signs and a normal lung exam, while a physician's overall clinical impression is moderately accurate in diagnosing pneumonia. However, there is a lack of recent meta-analyses evaluating the diagnostic value of individual signs and symptoms for pneumonia.

## Clinical Point
What is the accuracy of individual signs and symptoms for diagnosing community acquired pneumonia?

## SGEM Bottom Line
No individual sign or symptom is good enough to either rule in or rule out community acquired pneumonia. Decisions should be made based on the clinician’s overall judgement. However, individual clinicians may want to improve their expertise by reviewing radiology results, and following patients up clinically, so that they can learn from their mistakes over time.

## Case Resolution
You review the entire history and physical exam with your student, as everyone needs to learn the basics. You explain to the patient that based on your clinical expertise, you think it is unlikely that they have pneumonia, and so they don’t need to be exposed to CXR at this time. However, you explain to the patient that no test is perfect, so if she is getting worse and is worried that you may have missed a pneumonia, she should come back for a recheck.

## Clinical Application
Depending on where you are in your career, reviewing these numbers may help you develop the expertise required to accurately diagnose pneumonia, although clinical diagnosis alone will never be perfect.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
Based on the symptoms you have, your normal vital signs, and the fact that your lung exam is normal, I think it is very unlikely that you have pneumonia today, so we don’t have to expose you to a CXR. However, we can never be 100% certain, so if you are getting worse, please come back so we can recheck you.
