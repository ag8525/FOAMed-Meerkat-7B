## Summary
The document discusses the potential benefits of using an automated head-up CPR device (ACE-CPR) in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) scenarios, based on a study by Moore et al. The study, a multi-centre prospective observational analysis, compared ACE-CPR with conventional CPR (C-CPR) and found no significant improvement in survival to hospital discharge, although a subgroup analysis suggested better outcomes with rapid ACE-CPR application. Despite the association between ACE-CPR and improved survival, the study's design limits causal conclusions, and potential biases exist due to industry funding and conflicts of interest. The document concludes that current evidence does not support the purchase of ACE-CPR devices, recommending instead proven interventions like automated external defibrillators.

## Case
You are the Chief of your local Fire and EMS Department, and an individual contacts you saying he saw a piece on TV about a *“Heads Up”* CPR device, and he wants to donate half the cost and has his checkbook out.

## Background
Out of Hospital Cardiac Arrest (OHCA) is a critical medical condition characterized by the cessation of cardiac activity outside a hospital setting, with a notably low resuscitation success rate, necessitating improved intervention strategies. Standard approaches to OHCA management include the use of epinephrine, target temperature management, mechanical CPR, supraglottic airways, and steroids. Recent animal studies suggest that elevating the head during CPR may enhance resuscitation outcomes by reducing intracranial pressure and facilitating venous return from the head and neck to the thorax. However, the translation of these findings into clinical practice requires robust evidence from human studies to confirm the proposed physiological benefits and avoid medical reversals.

## Clinical Point
Does the rapid use of an automated head up device as part of a CPR bundle improve survival from OHCA?

## SGEM Bottom Line
We cannot recommend the purchase of an ACE-CPR device at this time.

## Case Resolution
The generous offer to help purchase an ACE-CPR device is declined.

## Clinical Application
Current research has not adequately shown a difference in outcomes between high-quality conventional CPR and ACE-CPR. There is a potential that earlier application of the ACE-CPR device, along with ITD and ACD-CPR or mechanical CPR may improve outcomes. However, it is too soon to adopt this technology. The authors mention further studies being conducted and we look forward to critically appraising those publications when available.

**What Do I Tell the Donor?**
I would thank the individual for the generous offer but there lacks high-quality evidence the ACE-CPR device saves lives. Our suggestion would be for them to purchase something that has been proven to save lives like an automated external defibrillator for the local community center.
