## Summary
The document discusses the application of the Ottawa Knee Rule (OKR) in assessing knee injuries in children, specifically its ability to exclude knee fractures. The OKR, originally validated in adults, requires x-rays only if specific criteria are met, and has shown 100% sensitivity in detecting fractures. A systematic review and meta-analysis involving 1130 children with a fracture prevalence of 8.4% suggests that the OKR can be safely applied to children over 5 years old, reducing unnecessary radiographs. However, there is insufficient evidence to support its use in children under 5 years. The study highlights the potential of the OKR to decrease x-ray usage by 28% in appropriate pediatric populations.

## Case Scenario
Ten-year-old Johnny was playing hockey last night. He twisted his knee but could not finish the game. His mom brings him in after asking for an x-ray.

## Point
Can the Ottawa knee rules safely exclude knee fractures in children?

## BEEM Bottom Line
The Ottawa Knee Rule can be safely used in children over the age of 5 years.
