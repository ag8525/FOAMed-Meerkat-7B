## Summary
In this clinical case, a 62-year-old male with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) was admitted to the hospital, presenting with symptoms such as shortness of breath, purulent cough, and fever. Diagnostic evaluation included a chest X-ray revealing a left lower lobe infiltrate, and the patient was risk stratified using the PORT score, indicating moderate risk. The clinical Point addressed whether β-Lactam monotherapy or β-Lactam plus macrolide combination therapy is more effective in achieving clinical stability in hospitalized patients with moderately severe CAP. A referenced study by Garin et al. found that β-Lactam monotherapy was not non-inferior to combination therapy, particularly in patients with atypical pathogens or higher pneumonia severity index scores. The study highlighted the importance of combination therapy in achieving better clinical outcomes, reaffirming current practice guidelines to use β-Lactam plus macrolide empirically in such cases. The patient was treated with ceftriaxone and azithromycin, showing clinical improvement, and was later discharged on cefuroxime after blood cultures confirmed sensitivity.

## Case
62-year-old man presents to your emergency department with a four-day history of increasing shortness of breath, purulent cough, fever, and generally feeling quite unwell. You measure his vitals, and he is mildly tachycardic and tachypneic, normotensive, O2 Sats are 96% on room air and his temperature is 38.2C.
You obtain routine blood work including a lactate level and blood cultures. A chest x-ray reveals a left lower lobe infiltrate in his lungs suggestive of pneumonia, and the decision is made to admit him to your general medicine floor. You are about to start him on empiric antibiotics and wonder whether to use β-Lactam monotherapy or β-Lactam plus macrolide combination therapy.

## Background
Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is a prevalent condition leading to numerous emergency department visits and hospital admissions. It necessitates the use of clinical decision tools, such as the PORT score or pneumonia severity index (PSI), to assess illness severity and guide treatment location and duration. These tools help stratify patients for outpatient or inpatient management based on factors like age, sex, and vital signs. The choice of empiric antibiotics for CAP remains contentious due to rising antimicrobial resistance, exemplified by the increase in macrolide-resistant Streptococcus pneumoniae in Canada from 3.7% to 19.0% between 1995 and 2005, primarily driven by antibiotic overuse and misuse.

## Clinical Point
In adult patients admitted to hospital with moderately severe CAP, which is better at achieving clinical stability: β-Lactam monotherapy or β-Lactam plus macrolide combination therapy?

## SGEM Bottom Line
In patients presenting to the emergency department with community acquired pneumonia severe enough to warrant admission, we should continue to use β-Lactam + macrolide empiric therapy.

## Case Resolution
You decide to start the patient empirically on ceftriaxone IV + azithromycin PO. Two days later his blood cultures come back showing strep pneumo sensitive to cefuroxime.
At this point, he has improved clinically. You make the decision to write him a script for cefuroxime to complete his course of antibiotics, and discharge him home.

## Clinical Application
According to the Ontario *“Anti-infective Guidelines for Community-acquired Infections”* – an evidence-based, peer-reviewed publication – community acquired pneumonia is divided into three categories: mild to moderate, severe requiring hospitalization, and severe requiring intensive care unit.
Notice how none of these categories align 100% with the article’s *“moderately severe community acquired pneumonia”*. Recommended empiric therapies also differ. β-Lactam monotherapy is only recommended for mild to moderate community acquired pneumonia patients safely treated as out-patients.
Once admitted, the only monotherapy recommended is a fluoroquinolone. However, at our hospital, we are trying to decrease our fluoroquinolone use, as our antibiogram shows increasing resistance to it and also because it is associated with a higher risk of C. difficile infection compared to other options.
For our community acquired pneumonia patients sick enough to be admitted, we empirically use β-Lactam + macrolide, which is supported by the results of this study.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You have community-acquired pneumonia and we need to admit you to the hospital for treatment. You will be started on two different antibiotics to treat the infection (one oral and one intravenous). We will keep a close eye on you and hope to see some improvement in the next 24-48 hours. Some of the blood tests we took may be able to tell us how to better treat your pneumonia, but they will take a few days to get final results.
