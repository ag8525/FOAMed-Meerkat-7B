## Summary
Ketorolac is evaluated as a treatment for acute migraine headaches in the emergency department, with a systematic review of eight studies involving 321 adult patients. The review suggests ketorolac is an effective alternative for pain relief, comparable to meperidine and more effective than sumatriptan, though potentially less effective than metoclopramide/phenothiazine agents. However, the evidence is limited by small study sizes, high or unclear bias, and inconsistent outcome reporting. The review highlights the variability in migraine treatment practices and the potential influence of administration routes on placebo effects. Despite these limitations, ketorolac is considered a reasonable second-line agent, with dexamethasone noted for reducing recurrence and ED bounce-backs. The clinical application suggests using ketorolac as a backup if initial standard treatments fail.

## Case Scenario
A thirty-two year-old woman presents with her usual migraine headache.

## Background
Migraines affect over 10% of the population, with a higher prevalence in women (18%) compared to men (6%), and are a significant contributor to medical expenses and reduced productivity, with direct costs around $17 billion and indirect costs approximately $15 billion annually. A notable clinical issue is the high rate of patients returning to the emergency department (ED) shortly after initial treatment, with up to 50% experiencing this "bounce-back" phenomenon. Dexamethasone has been evaluated in randomized controlled trials as a potential intervention to reduce these bounce-backs, as reviewed by Giuliano et al. in Postgraduate Medicine.

## Point
Does ketorolac work well for acute migraine headache treatment?

## Bottom Line
Ketorolac is a reasonable second-line agent in the treatment of acute migraine.

## Clinical Application
I will tend to use ketorolac only as a second-line agent in the treatment of acute migraine.

## Case Resolution
I can see you are in pain and that is important to me. We will try some standard treatments first that have been shown to work. I will check back with you in 30-60 minutes to see how you are doing. If your pain is not controlled there is a plan B.
