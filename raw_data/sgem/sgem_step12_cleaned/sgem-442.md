## Summary
A systematic review by Kruse et al. highlights the scarcity of clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) for transgender and gender-diverse (TGD) individuals in emergency department (ED) settings, identifying eight key recommendations to improve care. These include offering HIV testing and prophylaxis, immediate HIV care referral, creating medical homes for TGD youth, training ED staff in culturally competent care, and addressing substance use and mental health with a trauma-informed approach. The study underscores the need for more patient-oriented research and the involvement of the queer community in guideline development. Despite the limited scope of existing guidelines, the recommendations are actionable and emphasize the importance of culturally sensitive care and appropriate referrals for TGD patients.

## Case
A 16-year-old nonbinary youth on testosterone blockers and oestrogen has come into your emergency department (ED) having twisted their ankle while playing soccer. You learn they are ordering hormones online as they do not have any gender-affirming primary care. You don’t see a large number of trans patients in your ED and you wonder if there might be specific recommendations that can guide your care of this adolescent.

## Background
Trans and gender diverse (TGD) individuals constitute 0.3% to 0.6% of the general population and up to 4.1% of adolescents in North America. This group faces significant health challenges, including increased risks of violence, substance use, homelessness, and mental health issues, which heighten their need for emergency services. However, TGD individuals often avoid emergency departments (ED) due to discrimination and report negative experiences when seeking care. There is a notable deficiency in research and clinical guidelines to effectively support TGD patients in emergency settings. To address this gap, researchers, including those from the queer community, are conducting a systematic review and synthesis of clinical practice guidelines and best practice statements to improve ED care for TGD individuals.

## Clinical Point
What clinical practice guidelines (CPGs) exist to inform the care of transgender and gender diverse (TGD) patients in the emergency department?

## SGEM Bottom Line
There are limited high-quality, emergency care-specific guidelines with which to inform the care of trans and gender-diverse patients in the ED and there is a need for more patient-oriented research in this area.

## Case Resolution
You assess and treat your patient’s ankle injury as you would for anyone else, but while ensuring that they have appropriate follow-up you learn they have no primary care professional so make a referral from the ED to a primary care team that is appropriately trained in the competent care of gender diverse individuals.

## Clinical Application
The recommendations arising out of this review are largely actionable in any setting. Although the one recommendation to refer to a primary care team is, perhaps a bit optimistic for most of Canada and perhaps elsewhere.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
While caring for your patient including while discussing them with other care team members, you use their correct name and pronouns in keeping with the cultural competency training your hospital makes available to staff. You say “Hey I noticed you don’t have a primary care team. I might add something to the effect that I’m worried the HRT you’re ordering off the internet may not be safe, and may not work as well as you want it to… We have a clinic in the community that sees many of our trans and nonbinary patients and they sometimes will pick up patients who we refer to them. Would you like me to send a referral to them for ongoing primary care?”
