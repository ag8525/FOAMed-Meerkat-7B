## Summary
An 18-year-old female presented to the ED after a snowmobile accident with a mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), scoring 2 on the ACE-ED tool for headache and feeling foggy, with a GCS of 15. The case highlights the management of concussions, emphasizing the use of the Canadian CT Head Rule for imaging decisions and the importance of early light exercise over prolonged rest to prevent persistent symptoms. A study by Macnow et al. in JAMA Pediatrics 2021 explored the impact of screen time on concussion recovery, finding that abstaining from screen time may shorten symptom duration, though the study's limitations prevent definitive recommendations. The SGEM conclusion aligns with the authors, suggesting that while screen time limitation might be beneficial, evidence is insufficient to mandate it, and standard discharge instructions do not currently include screen time restrictions.

## Case
An 18-year-old female presents to the emergency department (ED) after falling off a moving snowmobile and hitting her head on the ground. It was a witnessed fall; she was wearing a helmet at the time and there was no loss of consciousness. There were no other injuries reported and she is found to have a GCS score of 15 after the injury. The Acute Concussion Evaluation–Emergency Department (ACE-ED) Tool is used, and she scores a 2 for headache and feeling foggy. She knows about taking it easy physically for the next couple of days but wonders if she must stay off her computer as well?

## Background
Concussions, or mild traumatic brain injuries (mTBI), are frequently diagnosed in emergency departments, with most patients recovering within a week, though 15-30% experience persistent post-concussive symptoms. The Canadian CT Head Rule, developed by Dr. Ian Stiell, aids in determining the necessity of advanced imaging for minor head injuries. In pediatric cases, a study by Thomas et al. suggests that two days of rest followed by a gradual return to activity is more beneficial than five days of strict rest, as prolonged rest may exacerbate post-concussive symptoms. In adults, early light exercise post-concussion does not significantly differ from rest in preventing persistent symptoms, but may be encouraged as tolerated. The Acute Concussion Evaluation–Emergency Department (ACE-ED) tool assists clinicians in diagnosing concussions and identifying risk factors for prolonged recovery, while symptom scoring tools like the Postconcussion Symptom Scale and the Rivermead Post-concussion Symptom Questionnaire provide quantitative assessments for ongoing management.

## Clinical Point
Does screen time in the first 48 hours after concussion have an impact on the duration of concussive symptoms?

## SGEM Bottom Line
We do not know what impact screen time has on post-concussion symptoms.

## Case Resolution
Patient is given standard discharge instructions for a concussion which does not include a recommendation about screen time.

## Clinical Application
Based on the limitations we have discussed for this study and the paucity of research in this area, we cannot make screen time recommendations at this point.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You have a mild concussion. In the vast majority of patients, your symptoms should resolve within 7 days if not sooner. Light activity seems ok after a concussion. We do not have good information to tell you to stay off your computer, but in my practice I tell patients to limit screen time as much as possible, take breaks if you are unable to limit screen time, and if screen time is making symptoms worse, take a break.
