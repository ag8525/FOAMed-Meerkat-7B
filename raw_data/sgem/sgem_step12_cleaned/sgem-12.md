## Summary
Ondansetron is highlighted as the only antiemetic with consistent efficacy in reducing vomiting in children with gastroenteritis, thereby decreasing the need for intravenous fluids and hospital admissions. A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials demonstrated that ondansetron significantly prevents further emesis, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 5 to prevent both further vomiting and the need for IV fluids. The study emphasizes that ondansetron should be administered as needed rather than on a regular schedule to avoid exacerbating diarrhea. Despite the limitation of including only English language studies, the findings align with other systematic reviews, including a Cochrane review. In the case of a four-year-old with vomiting, ondansetron was effectively used alongside oral rehydration therapy, resulting in no further vomiting.

## Case Scenario
A four-year-old child presents with a one day history of vomiting. They are otherwise healthy, immunized, no fever, no rash, no recent travel, no abdominal pain, no cough, and no sick contacts.

## Background on Dehydration in Children
Dehydration in children, often due to gastroenteritis, is a prevalent issue in emergency departments, characterized by acute diarrhea, potentially accompanied by nausea, vomiting, fever, and abdominal pain. Annually, the United States reports 20-40 million episodes of diarrhea in children, leading to 2-4 million physician visits and accounting for 10% of hospital admissions for children under five. The severity of dehydration is categorized into mild, moderate, and severe, as outlined by Goldman et al. in 2008. Most gastroenteritis cases are mild and self-limiting, with oral rehydration therapy being the standard treatment approach, supported by guidelines from the Canadian Pediatric Society.

## Point
What can we use to prevent vomiting/IV fluids/admissions in children with gastroenteritis?

## BEEM Bottom Line
Ondansetron is an effective anti-emetic preventing further vomiting, intravenous fluids, and admissions for children with gastroenteritis.

## Case Resolution
The four-year-old was given 0.1mg/kg of ondansetron orally. They were observed for one hour with a trial of oral rehydration therapy. There was no further vomiting. The dosing is typically between 0.1-0.15mg/kg.
