## Summary
In the clinical scenario of a 70-year-old man presenting with signs of sepsis, including hypotension, tachycardia, hypoxia, and fever, the management involves rapid sequence intubation (RSI) and sepsis protocol initiation. The use of etomidate for RSI in septic patients is controversial due to its association with increased mortality and adrenal insufficiency, as highlighted by a meta-analysis showing a relative risk increase in all-cause mortality and adrenal insufficiency. Despite these concerns, etomidate was used for RSI in this case, followed by standard sepsis management steps including fluid resuscitation, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and ICU admission. The decision to use etomidate should be carefully considered, given the potential risks, and alternative sedatives may be preferred in septic patients.

## Case Scenario
70-year-old man arrives by ambulance from home, complaining of being weak and dizzy. He has no specific complaints of chest pain, shortness of breath, abdominal pain, or focal weakness. He has a history of hypertension, benign prostatic hypertrophy, dyslipidemia, and osteoarthritis. His vital signs are blood pressure 76/48mmHg, heart rate 110bpm, oxygen saturation 86%, and temperature of 39.5C. He is difficult to rouse, and you decide he needs rapid sequence intubation.

## Point
Can you use Etomidate for RSI in septic patients?

## Background
Sepsis is a life-threatening condition resulting from the body's extreme response to infection, leading to tissue damage, organ failure, and potentially death. It has gained increased attention recently, highlighted by initiatives such as the Surviving Sepsis Campaign and Early Goal-Directed Therapy, which aim to improve outcomes through early recognition and treatment. The clinical significance of sepsis is underscored by its high morbidity and mortality rates. Standard management guidelines have been developed by organizations such as the American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) and the Canadian Association of Emergency Physicians (CAEP) to optimize the treatment of severe sepsis, emphasizing the importance of timely intervention.

## BEEM Bottom Line
This review updates the controversy on using single-dose etomidate for RSI in septic patients, and raises more concerns about increased in-hospital mortality. However, this is based on a single large observational substudy of a failed RCT, and it is not clear why these trial results were so different from others. This is enough information to give PAUSE to routine use of etomidate in septic RSI, but not to abandon it completely.

## Case Resolution
You initiate 1L normal saline bolus. Do a rapid sequence intubation using etomidate, draw laboratory tests including blood/urine cultures, start broad spectrum antibiotics, and call the ICU for admission.
