## Summary
In the study by Prekker et al., the efficacy of video laryngoscopy (VL) versus direct laryngoscopy (DL) for tracheal intubation in critically ill adults was evaluated, revealing that VL resulted in a higher first pass success (FPS) rate compared to DL (85.1% vs 70.8%). This multi-center, pragmatic randomized controlled trial included adult patients requiring intubation in emergency departments or ICUs, excluding those with clear indications or contraindications for either method. The study highlighted that while VL improves visualization, it may complicate tube passage, especially with hyperangulated blades. Despite the higher FPS with VL, the choice of intubation method should consider the clinician's experience and comfort with the device, as operator familiarity significantly influences success rates. The study underscores the importance of using the most familiar and practiced technique, particularly for clinicians with limited intubation experience.

## Case
You are an emergency physician caring for a 65-year-old male with a history of hypertension and diabetes who presented with altered mental status, fever, and labored breathing. You’ve found that he is septic and in respiratory failure and needs intubation. Your assessment is that the patient will likely be a physiologically, but not anatomically, difficult airway. You’ve assured appropriate physiologic optimization and pharmacologic preparation and are ready to intubate. Should you go with video or direct laryngoscope?

## Background
Intubation is a critical procedure for securing the airway in emergent situations, with the choice of technique and tools impacting patient outcomes. The use of etomidate as an induction agent in critically ill patients remains controversial regarding its effect on survival with good neurologic function. In out-of-hospital cardiac arrests, emphasis is placed on high-quality CPR and early defibrillation over the type of supraglottic airway device used. The use of a bougie has been shown to increase first pass success (FPS) rates, which is associated with fewer adverse events such as hypoxia, hypotension, and cardiac arrest. Point of care ultrasound (POCUS), particularly transtracheal sonography, is a promising adjunct for confirming endotracheal tube placement. Traditionally, direct laryngoscopy (DL) has been used for intubation, but video laryngoscopy (VL) has been developed to improve visualization of the laryngeal structures, although it may complicate tube passage. The debate between DL and VL continues, with mixed evidence regarding FPS and outcomes. While VL generally improves visualization, it may take longer and potentially increase mortality in severe head injuries, necessitating further research to clarify these findings.

## Clinical Point
In adult patients needing intubation in an ED or ICU, is FPS higher with DL or VL?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Clinicians should use the device they are most comfortable and experienced with. If they’re highly experienced with DL and very successful with it, they should likely continue to use it.

## Case Resolution
Jeff would usually reach for VL and Ken would usually reach for DL.

## Clinical Application
Video laryngoscopes have added a new tool to the clinician’s airway management toolbox. VL, particularly with hyperangulated blades, make visualization easier but may make tube passage more challenging. Techniques for tube passage are different with these blades and clinicians must learn these techniques. DL obviously works as well as it always has and if that’s the technique, you’re familiar and successful with, keep doing what you’re good at. I’d caution against subjective assessment of the provider’s success, though. When it comes to intubation, almost all clinicians are like the children from Lake Wobegon… *“we’re all above average”.*

**What Do I Tell the Emergency Physician?**
You should use the device they are most experienced and successful with. If you have limited (<100) prior intubations and most of those were with VL, you should almost certainly use VL. If you have lots of experience with DL, and little experience with VL, DL is probably still a better option for you.
