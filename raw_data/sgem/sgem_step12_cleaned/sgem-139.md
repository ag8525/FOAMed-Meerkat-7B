## Summary
Idarucizumab, a monoclonal antibody fragment, has been approved by the FDA as the first reversal agent for dabigatran, a non-vitamin K antagonist anticoagulant used in atrial fibrillation and venous thromboembolism. In a prospective observational trial, idarucizumab demonstrated a 100% median maximum reversal of dabigatran's anticoagulant effect within four hours, as measured by dilute thrombin time and ecarin clotting time. However, the study's lack of a control group and small cohort size limit the ability to draw definitive conclusions about its clinical efficacy and safety, particularly in urgent bleeding or procedural contexts. Despite rapid normalization of coagulation parameters, the clinical application remains uncertain, and the trial's interim results, funded by the manufacturer, warrant cautious interpretation.

## Case
A 67-year-old man presents with a history of atrial fibrillation and hypertension. He takes metoprolol 50mg twice daily and dabigatran 150mg twice daily. He had been having mild epigastric pain for about one week and had one episode of coffee ground emesis that night. He arrives via ambulance with a blood pressure of 120/70, heart rate of 74 beats per minute and O2 saturation of 98% on room air.

## Background
Dabigatran is a non-vitamin K antagonist anticoagulant that inhibits thrombin and is used for the prevention and treatment of venous thromboembolism and to prevent stroke in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation. A significant clinical concern with dabigatran is the risk of life-threatening bleeding, for which there was previously no effective reversal method, unlike warfarin. Hemodialysis, the only method to remove dabigatran, is impractical in most clinical scenarios. However, the FDA has approved idarucizumab, a monoclonal antibody fragment developed by Boehringer Ingelheim, as the first reversal agent for dabigatran, although it has not yet been approved in Canada.

## Clinical Point
Is idarucizumab safe and effective treatment adjunct for patients taking dabigatran with serious bleeding or who require an urgent procedure?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Idarucizumab is here (USA) and probably works but its patient-oriented efficacy and safety are still pending.

## Case Resolution
You establish two large bore intravenous lines and group and cross him for four units of blood. Laboratory studies are sent off stat and gastroenterology is called to the emergency department. He is taken to the procedure suite and found to have a bleeding duodenal ulcer.

## Clinical Application
It is too early to know how to clinically apply this preliminary research.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You are having a serious bleed. It is made worse because of the blood thinner you take to prevent strokes. We are getting some blood for you. The gastroenterologist is on their way to take you to the operating room. If you become unstable I can give you a new drug just approved that seems effective and seems to work very quickly. However, we don’t have much information on how well it works and there may be safety issues we have not yet identified.
