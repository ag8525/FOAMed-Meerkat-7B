## Summary
An 11-year-old snowboarder presented with a concussion after a fall, characterized by brief loss of consciousness and amnesia, but with a normal examination, leading to a decision against CT imaging. The case highlights the use of the PECARN decision rule for pediatric head trauma, which aids in minimizing unnecessary radiation exposure while maintaining high sensitivity for significant intracranial injuries. A study by Thomas DG et al. evaluated the effects of strict rest post-concussion, finding no added benefit and potential harm from a five-day strict rest compared to a two-day rest followed by gradual activity resumption. The study suggests that a two-day rest period is sufficient, aligning with the SGEM conclusion that prolonged rest may exacerbate post-concussive symptoms. The patient was advised to rest for two days before gradually returning to activities.

## Case
11-year-old snowboarder fails to land epic jump. She was wearing a helmet. There was a brief loss of consciousness and she is amnestic to the event. The only complaint is a mild headache. Her examination is normal and a shared decision is made to observe her rather than getting a CT scan. She is ultimately diagnosed with a concussion. When leaving the department she wants to know when can she go back shreddin’ the gnar?

## Background
Pediatric traumatic brain injury (TBI) is a significant cause of mortality and morbidity, recognized by the CDC as a national health issue, with approximately 500,000 emergency department visits, over 35,000 hospitalizations, and more than 7,000 deaths annually in the USA. TBI is classified as mild in 75% of cases, yet poses diagnostic challenges in emergency settings due to the need to balance the detection of significant intracranial lesions against the risks of ionizing radiation from imaging. The Pediatric Emergency Care Applied Research Network (PECARN) provides a validated clinical decision tool to guide neuroimaging decisions, outperforming the CHALICE and CATCH tools. PECARN's rules, derived from a study of 34,000 patients and validated with an additional 9,000, offer 97% sensitivity and 59% specificity for children over 2 years, and 99% sensitivity and 54% specificity for those under 2 years, with a clinically important TBI (ciTBI) prevalence of 0.9% and a neurosurgery requirement of 0.14%.

## Clinical Point
Is there benefit to recommending strict rest after a child has a concussion?

## SGEM Bottom Line
In children with concussion, two days of rest followed by a gradual return to activity is preferred over five days of rest followed by a gradual return to activity. The longer strict rest period appears to cause more post-concussive symptoms.

## Case Resolution
You send the shredder home with your hospital’s standard concussion information.

## Clinical Application
In children presenting with concussion, we can limit the rest period, post-concussion, to two days followed by gradual return to activity.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
Your child has a concussion and they need to take two days off school and sports. They can slowly return to activity after that period of time.
