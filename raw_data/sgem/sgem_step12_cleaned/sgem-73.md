## Summary
In patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), the management of elevated blood pressure is critical, yet the optimal target remains debated. The INTERACT 2 study explored whether intensive blood pressure lowering (target <140 mmHg) within 1 hour improves outcomes compared to standard care (<180 mmHg). The study found no significant difference in the primary outcome of death or severe disability at 90 days between the two groups, although an ordinal analysis suggested improved functional outcomes with intensive treatment. Despite these findings, intensive blood pressure lowering was deemed safe but not necessarily superior, leading to the recommendation of maintaining blood pressure below 180 mmHg without aggressively targeting normal levels. This approach aligns with current guidelines and emphasizes the importance of careful blood pressure management in conjunction with neurosurgical consultation.

## Case Scenario
A 54-year-old man presents with acute onset of headache. His blood pressure is 210/110 mmHg. CT scan shows an acute bleed.

## Point
Does rapid lowering of blood pressure improve outcomes in patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage?

## Background
Hypertensive emergencies, particularly those involving end organ damage, are critical medical conditions characterized by severe elevations in blood pressure that can lead to significant morbidity and mortality. Key organs affected include the heart, brain, kidneys, and eyes, with potential outcomes such as myocardial infarction, intracerebral hemorrhage, renal failure, and vision impairment. Acute spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is notably severe, with poor patient outcomes often linked to elevated blood pressure levels. The American Heart Association/American Stroke Association (AHA/ASA) guidelines from 2010 recommend blood pressure reduction in such cases. The INTERACT study, published in 2008, suggested that early intensive blood pressure-lowering treatment is feasible, well-tolerated, and may reduce hematoma growth in ICH, though further large-scale trials are necessary to assess its impact on broader clinical outcomes, such as survival and neurological function.

## Bottom Line
Intensive blood pressure lowering in patients with ICH is safe but not necessarily better.

## Case Resolution
In this patient with ICH and a blood pressure of 210/110 mmHg, you begin to lower his patient’s blood pressure to <180mmHg as per the guidelines. You do not worry about dropping the BP fast or to <140mmHg. Neurosurgery is contacted.

## Clinical Application
In patients with acute ICH, I will continue to attempt to lower their blood pressure below 180mmHg. However, I will not be worried if the blood pressure drops to normal levels.

**What do I tell my patients?**
You are having a bleeding stroke which is very serious. You also have high blood pressure with the stroke. There is some weak evidence that aggressive lowering of the blood pressure may help and does not appear dangerous. Based upon this evidence, we will try to carefully lower your blood pressure and get the neurosurgeons to see you as fast as we can.
