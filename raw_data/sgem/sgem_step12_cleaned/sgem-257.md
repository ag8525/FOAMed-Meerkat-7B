## Summary
The document discusses the implications of the Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA) on psychiatric emergencies, highlighting that nearly 20% of civil monetary penalties for EMTALA violations involve psychiatric cases. These penalties are notably higher for psychiatric emergencies, averaging 2.6 times more than those for non-psychiatric cases, with significant settlements concentrated in specific regions and states. The study underscores the necessity for hospitals to improve access and quality of care for psychiatric emergencies to avoid substantial financial penalties. The document also emphasizes the importance of conducting thorough medical screenings and stabilizing emergent needs in the emergency department, with a focus on appropriate transfers to higher care levels when necessary.

## Case
You are working in your emergency department at a hospital that has an on-site psychiatric unit. You are holding several patients in the department who have been placed on involuntary holds for suicidal ideation while a bed search occurs at facilities elsewhere in the region. Your charge nurse tells you that she has learned the psychiatric unit has open beds that currently aren’t being used.

## Background
The Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act (EMTALA), enacted in 1986, is a federal US law designed to ensure that uninsured patients receive timely and adequate emergency medical care. It requires emergency departments to conduct a medical screening evaluation, stabilize emergent conditions, and arrange transfers to facilities with the necessary capabilities if needed. Receiving hospitals are obligated to accept transferred patients if they have the appropriate on-call specialists. EMTALA's provisions extend to psychiatric emergencies, as clarified by the Center for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS). Non-compliance with EMTALA can result in the termination of Medicare provider agreements, as evidenced by actions against 12 hospitals, including four related to psychiatric emergencies, and may also incur civil monetary penalties.

## SGEM Bottom Line
Civil monetary penalties for EMTALA violations involving psychiatric patients are increasing, and very expensive for hospitals. Institutions need to have protocols in place to avoid inadequate stabilization, screening, and inappropriate transfer of patients.

## Case Resolution
You call on the on-call psychiatrist who arranges for nursing staff to open up the remaining beds in the psychiatric unit.

## Clinical Application
Do an appropriate medical screening exam on all ED patients including psychiatric patients. Stabilize any emergent needs and arrange transfer of any patient to a higher level of care if necessary.
