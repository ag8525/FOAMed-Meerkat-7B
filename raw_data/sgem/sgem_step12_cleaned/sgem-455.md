## Summary
The document discusses the use of a cranial accelerometry (CA) headset device, specifically the Harmony 5000, for prehospital detection of large vessel occlusion (LVO) strokes, which are a significant cause of stroke-related morbidity and mortality. The study compared the CA device's diagnostic accuracy to the Los Angeles Motor Scale (LAMS) in a prospective, multicenter observational study involving 594 suspected acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients. The CA device demonstrated higher sensitivity (84.6%) without sacrificing specificity (82.6%) compared to LAMS, which had a sensitivity of 38.5%. The study concluded that the CA headset is feasible for prehospital use and improves LVO detection, although the potential for data overfitting was noted. The case resolution involved a patient with suspected stroke symptoms, ultimately diagnosed with a small acute lacunar infarct, highlighting the challenges in differentiating stroke from mimics in prehospital settings. Further research is needed to confirm the CA device's utility in improving prehospital stroke triage.

## Case
The family of a 69-year-old woman activated emergency medical services (EMS) for slurred speech that they noticed when she woke up a couple of hours before. The patient has a history of hypertension, diabetes, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and dyslipidemia and lives alone but speaks with her son daily. The son reported she seemed fine yesterday evening when they went to dinner, perhaps more tired than usual. The patient had some slurred speech but no obvious facial droop or asymmetric limb weakness.

## Background
Stroke is a major global health concern, with large vessel occlusion (LVO) strokes constituting about one-third of acute ischemic strokes (AIS) in the United States. LVO strokes are particularly significant due to their high contribution to stroke-related disability and mortality, being responsible for nearly two-thirds of post-stroke dependence and over 90% of stroke deaths. The standard treatment for LVO strokes with moderate to severe symptoms is endovascular thrombectomy (EVT), which necessitates rapid identification and transport to an EVT-capable center to improve patient outcomes. However, prehospital identification of LVO strokes poses a challenge for emergency medical services (EMS), as traditional stroke scales like the Los Angeles Motor Scale (LAMS), the Cincinnati Stroke Triage Assessment Tool (C-STAT), and the Rapid Arterial Occlusion Evaluation (RACE) scale, while highly specific, often lack sensitivity, leading to missed diagnoses.

## Clinical Point
Can a cranial accelerometry (CA) headset device be used by paramedics in the prehospital setting to accurately detect patients with a large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Early stroke identification is important but can be difficult in the pre-hospital setting even with the existing tools.

## Case Resolution
Based on the patient’s symptoms, EMS activates a stroke alert and transports the patient to the nearest stroke center. The patient undergoes a CT and CT angiogram of the head which reveals a small acute lacunar infarct but no large vessel occlusion or hemorrhage. The patient, family, neurologist, and emergency physician engage in shared decision-making.

## Clinical Application
Diagnosis of an acute stroke and differentiation between a stroke and a stroke mimics can be difficult. Further investigation is needed to understand if the Harmony 5000 can improve diagnostic accuracy for stroke in the prehospital setting to triage their transportation to an appropriate receiving hospital.
