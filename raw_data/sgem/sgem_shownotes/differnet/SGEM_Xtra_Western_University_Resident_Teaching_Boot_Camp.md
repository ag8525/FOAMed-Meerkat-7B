**Date:** November 8th, 2017

I had the pleasure of demonstrating how to teach evidence-based medicine yesterday to Western University residents. It was part of a Boot Camp put on by the Schulich School of Medicine and Dentistry. It is an annual event designed to teach residents how to be better teachers.

The conference is officially called Boot Camp for Resident Teachers. Program Directors nominate exemplary residents with an interest in teaching. It is described as *“an intensive 2-day program focusing on cross-disciplinary teaching skills including one-minute preceptors, giving and receiving feedback, orienting clerks, case-based teaching, and managing the difficult learner.”* For more information about the Boot Camp you can click on this [link](http://www.schulich.uwo.ca/communications/the_pulse/2017/october/resident_boot_camp_training_the_trainers_to_enrich_learning.html).

You can download a PDF copy of my slides using this [link](http://thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/Western-Boot-Camp-PDF.pdf). We covered five basic training components for teaching clerks and five basic training points of evidence-based medicine. The conclusion was we should try and have a NNT (Number Needed to Teach) of one to help one clerk in their medical education. The ultimate goal is for patients to get the best care based on the best evidence and cut the knowledge translation window down from over ten years to less than one year.

**![palm five](https://i0.wp.com/thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/palm-five-150x150.jpg?resize=150%2C150)Five Basic Training Components for Teaching Clerks:**

1. Stimulating and Conducive Learning Environment
2. Get the Learners Involved
3. Respect the Learner
4. Recognize Limitations
5. Encourage Skepticism

**[![EBM triad](https://i0.wp.com/thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Screen-Shot-2017-09-29-at-4.03.39-PM-150x150.png?resize=150%2C150)](http://www.bmj.com/content/312/7023/71.full)Five Basic Training Points of Evidence-Based Medicine:**

1. Define[Evidence Based Medicine](http://www.bmj.com/content/312/7023/71.full) (EBM)
2. Use [P.I.C.O.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PICO_process) to formulate your clinical question
3. Search for the [BEST](http://thesgem.com/2014/03/make-it-so-beem-appraisal-tools/) evidence
4. Critically [APPRAISE](http://www.cebm.net/critical-appraisal/) the evidence
5. How to apply the evidence with [Shared Decision Making](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3445676/) (SDM)
