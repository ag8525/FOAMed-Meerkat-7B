**Date:** Sept 30th, 2017

I had the pleasure of presenting at the CALS Conference on Rural Emergency Care held in beautiful Minneapolis, Minnesota yesterday.  Thank you to Dr. Todd Grant and the conference committee for inviting me. Everyone made me feel very welcome.

The keynote address was called [Looking Back/Looking Ahead](http://thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/CALS-looking-back.pdf). I was asked to look back over my career providing rural emergency care and look ahead to the future of rural emergency care. There were five objectives to the presentation:

![EBM triad](https://i0.wp.com/thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Screen-Shot-2017-09-29-at-4.03.39-PM-150x150.png?resize=150%2C150)

EBM Triad

**1) Define Evidence Based Medicine (EBM):** *“The conscientious, explicit, and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about the care of individual patients.”  (*[David Sackett](http://www.bmj.com/content/312/7023/71.full) et al BMJ 1996)*.* The evidence based medicine triad can be illustrated in a Venn diagram. EBM encourages the health care professionals to use their clinical judgment. It also includes the patients values and preferences. The third part of the EBM triad is the relevant scientific evidence (literature), which should guide the practitioners care not dictate the care.

![Leaky Pipe Model](https://i0.wp.com/thesgem.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/06/leaky-pipe-150x150.png?resize=150%2C150)

Leaky Pipe Model

**2) Understand the Problem of Knowledge Translation:** It can take over ten years for high-quality, clinically-relevant information to reach the patients bedside ([Morris et al](http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1258/jrsm.2011.110180) JRSM 2011). A model to explain this was put forth by [Pathman et al](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/8792778) Med Care 1996.  This model was illustrated in the leaky pipe model published by [Diner et al](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1553-2712.2007.tb02381.x/epdf) AEM 2007. These authors described seven leaks in the pipe:

* Awareness, Acceptance, Applicable, Able,  Act On, Agree and Adhere

**4) Identify Some of the Big Changes in the Practice of Emergency Medicine:** I was asked to reflect back over my twenty plus years of practicing rural emergency medicine. There were a five examples of big studies that significantly impacted emergency medicine. The top movie, top pop song and other big technology stories were identified for the year a major paper was published.

Each paper was briefly presented and how it related to rural emergency care. Then some more recent literature was presented that either supported or refuted the original study claims. Finally, I looked ahead to consider what the future may (or may not) be for those topics.

The five major papers were NINDS [NEJM 1995](http://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199512143332401#t=article) ([SGEM#70](http://thesgem.com/2014/04/sgem70-the-secret-of-ninds-thrombolysis-for-acute-stroke/)), EGDT [NEJM 2001](http://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa010307#t=article) ([SGEM#92](http://thesgem.com/2014/10/sgem92-arise-up-arise-up-egdt-vs-usual-care-for-sepsis/)), Therapeutic Hypothermia [NEJM 2001](http://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa012689#t=article) ([SGEM#82](http://thesgem.com/2014/07/sgem82-melt-with-you-targeted-temperature-management/)), OPALS [NEJM 2004](http://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa040325#t=article) ([SGEM#64](http://thesgem.com/2014/03/sgem64-classic-em-papers-opals-study/)) and CT to Rule out SAH [BMJ 2011](http://www.bmj.com/content/343/bmj.d4277) ([SGEM#48](http://thesgem.com/2013/10/sgem48-thunderstruck-sah/)). In the brackets are the SGEM episodes on the classic original paper or a SGEM critical review of a publication that came out after on the same topic.
