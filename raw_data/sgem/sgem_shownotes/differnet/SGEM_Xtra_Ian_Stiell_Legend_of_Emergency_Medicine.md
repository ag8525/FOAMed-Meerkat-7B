**Date:**July 13th, 2017

**Guest Skeptic:**Dr. Ian Stiell is Professor, Department of Emergency Medicine, University of Ottawa. He is internationally recognized for his research in emergency medicine with a focus on the development of clinical decision rules. Ian is best known for the development of the [Ottawa Ankle Rules](http://thesgem.com/2012/09/podcast3-to-xray-or-not-to-xray/) and Canadian C-Spine Rule, and as the Principal Investigator for the landmark [OPALS](http://thesgem.com/2014/03/sgem64-classic-em-papers-opals-study/) Studies for pre-hospital care.

**Legends of Emergency Medicine:** This is the forth episode in the summer series of [SGEM Xtras](http://thesgem.com/the-sgem-xtra/) called Legends of Emergency Medicine. These episodes highlight some of the pioneers and leaders of our specialty.  I hope these episodes will give the next generation of emergency physicians access to some super stars in our field via social media.

The format of the show will be similar. We will start by finding out some a non-medical thing about the legend: What song they picked as their music for the episode and why?  Then we will tell the back story of how we first met. The more formal part of the episode asks the legend five questions. Listen to the podcast on [iTunes](https://itunes.apple.com/ca/podcast/skeptics-guide-to-emergency/id564247833) to hear their responses.

**Five Questions for Ian:**

2. What is the biggest negative change you have seen in emergency medicine over your career?
3. What contribution to emergency medicine that you made are you most proud of so far?
4. What advice would you give to someone who is interested going into emergency medicine?
5. How do you picture emergency medicine looking in the next ten years?

**Ottawa Rules Application:** You can get many of the clinical decision instruments on a free app from the iTunes store. *“The Ottawa Rules are a set of clinical decision rules developed by Dr. Ian Stiell and his research team at The Ottawa Hospital Research Institute and the University of Ottawa. The rules have been demonstrated to decrease unnecessary diagnostic imaging and emergency room wait times which enhances patient comfort and reduces health care costs.”*
