**Summary:**
In the management of uncomplicated gonococcal infections, particularly in patients with cephalosporin allergies or concerns about cephalosporin-resistant strains, alternative treatment regimens have been explored. A study evaluated the efficacy of azithromycin combined with either gentamicin or gemifloxacin, demonstrating high microbiological cure rates of 100% and 99.5%, respectively. These regimens offer viable options for patients unable to receive standard ceftriaxone treatment, with gentamicin and azithromycin showing better tolerability and antimicrobial susceptibility. The study underscores the need for alternative therapies amidst rising resistance, emphasizing the importance of patient history and safe sexual practices in managing sexually transmitted infections.

**Case Scenario:**
28-year-old male who presents to your emergency department with a three-day history of urethral discharge and moderate to severe dysuria. He lives with his wife and denies any other sexual contacts. He reports experiencing a similar problem occurring shortly after his bachelor party last summer. On physical exam, you note the presence of a yellowish urethral discharge and bilateral inguinal adenopathy, which is moderately tender to palpation. A swab of the urethral discharge is collected, but the results of the gram stain will not be made available for a few days. You note that his chart states that he has an allergy to ceftriaxone, and when you Point him further regarding the type of reaction that occurred, he states that the last time he was treated for this, he developed an anaphylactic reaction to the drug. You would like to empirically treat him for gonorrhea and chlamydia.

**Point:**
What viable options do we have in the empiric treatment of gonorrhea in the patient who has a cephalosporin allergy or if there are concerns related to cephalosporin-resistant infections?

**Background:**
Gonorrhea, a sexually transmitted infection caused by the bacterium Neisseria gonorrhoeae, has become a significant public health concern due to the development of antimicrobial resistance. Over the past century, treatment strategies have evolved as resistance emerged to penicillins, sulfonamides, tetracyclines, and fluoroquinolones. In 2011, the CDC recommended increasing the dose of ceftriaxone to 250 mg due to treatment failures and decreased susceptibility, particularly for pharyngeal infections. Rising minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) to cefixime led the CDC to advise against using oral cephalosporins. By 2013, gonorrhea was classified as an urgent threat in the U.S., prompting calls for increased public awareness, alternative treatment regimens, and the development of new therapeutic agents.

**Case Resolution:**
You offer your 28-year-old patient empiric treatment for his gonococcal infection, and highlight that two antimicrobial regimens may be used as a potential cure for his infection: two oral agents as a single dose or one parenteral and one oral agent as one-time doses.

You discuss with him that although the combination of these agents have not had widespread utilization for the treatment of this condition, they have been shown in small populations to be associated with cure of his infection, which may be especially helpful in his case given his previous history of infection as well as his allergy to cephalosporins, the standard treatment of this condition.

You counsel him related to the potential for adverse effects, mainly gastrointestinal in nature, and he decides to choose the combination of parenteral gentamicin and oral azithromycin for empiric therapy. He receives treatment with no adverse effects noted, and prior to discharge, you advise him related to safe sexual practices.

You also inform him that it is essential that he notify his sexual partner regarding the need for evaluation and treatment for the same infection.

**Clinical Application:**
This study provides us with potential alternative options that may be used for the management of suspected uncomplicated gonococcal infection in patients who may be at increased risk for resistance or in those patients who have a cephalosporin allergy.

**What Do I Tell Patients?**
The combination of azithromycin with either gentamicin or gemifloxacin administered as one-time doses will likely cure your suspected gonococcal infection, as the combination of agents has been found to be safe and effective in otherwise healthy patients.
