**Summary:**
The document discusses the Quick-Wee method, a novel approach to expedite clean catch urine collection in infants suspected of having a urinary tract infection (UTI). The method involves cleaning the genital area and stimulating the suprapubic region with cold saline-soaked gauze to induce urination within five minutes. A randomized controlled trial demonstrated that the Quick-Wee method significantly increased the success rate of obtaining a urine sample compared to usual care, with a 19% absolute increase in voiding within five minutes and higher satisfaction among parents and clinicians. The study noted no adverse events and suggested potential for reduced contamination, although this was not statistically significant. Despite some limitations, such as lack of blinding and potential selection bias, the Quick-Wee method offers a practical solution in pediatric emergency settings to improve urine sample collection efficiency.

**Case:**
You are working the afternoon shift in the paediatric emergency department. It has been the usual busy after-school, after-work time. Your shift is almost over and the department finally seems to be under control. The next patient you see is a five-month old girl who has been brought by her parents with a history of fever and vomiting in the last 24 hours. She has not had any diarrhoea and her parents have noticed she seems particularly miserable when she is producing wet nappies (diapers), which she is doing more frequently than often. You wonder whether it might be a urinary tract infection (UTI) causing her symptoms and you explain to the parents the importance of a clean catch urine sample. They look at you in despair – all three of them are tired and they really want to go home. You know that it can take some time to get a urine sample in this age-group and wonder, is there a quicker way to get the wee?

**Background:**
Urinary tract infections (UTIs) in pediatric patients, particularly those presenting with symptoms such as vomiting, fever, and abdominal pain, are commonly assessed in emergency settings. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) guidelines emphasize the necessity of urinalysis and urine culture for UTI diagnosis to ensure appropriate antibiotic use and prevent complications like renal scarring. Invasive urine collection methods, such as urethral catheterization or supra-pubic aspiration, are recommended for ill-appearing children requiring immediate antimicrobial therapy. For well-appearing febrile infants not at low risk for UTI, a two-step approach is advised, starting with non-invasive collection methods like bagged urine specimens, followed by invasive methods if initial results suggest a UTI. This approach reduces unnecessary catheterization without compromising diagnostic accuracy. Non-invasive methods are preferred unless the child is at risk for neonatal sepsis or appears ill. For low-risk febrile infants, urine testing may be omitted with proper clinical follow-up. A new method, Quick-Wee, has been proposed to expedite clean catch urine sample collection.

**Clinical Point:**
Does the Quick-Wee method increase the success rate of getting a clean catch urine collection in infants suspected of having a UTI?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
If you have five minutes to spare, you can increase the proportion of infants in whom a clean catch urine sample can be obtained using the Quick-Wee method.

**Case Resolution:**
You suggest to the parents that, now that the department is a little less busy, you could try the Quick-Wee method to make their infant pass urine a little bit faster. After a brief explanation of what’s involved they agree. After about 90 seconds of cold saline suprapubic stimulation you successfully have a clean catch urine sample.

**Clinical Application:**
This certainly seems like a relatively quick and reasonably effective way of increasing your chances of obtaining a clean catch urine within five minutes. If you have staff to spare to spend five minutes rubbing the suprapubic area of your patients with cold-saline-soaked gauze then great. However, we might not get to five minutes of uninterrupted stimulation in the emergency department before something more urgent comes through the door.

**What Do I Tell My Patient (Parent/Care Giver)?**
It’s very important that we get a clean catch urine sample from your infant. We want to make sure the urine we get does not have any contamination from the skin. This means if we get a sample with no signs of infection we know the fever is most likely not due to a UTI. If there are signs of infection in the sample we will need to put a catheter inside your infant’s bladder to get a better sample of urine. This can be uncomfortable for your infant. This is why we really like to try to get a clean catch sample.

However, waiting to get a clean catch sample can be a long process. But there is a new method called the Quick-Wee method that can increase the chance we get some urine in less than five minutes. It involves rubbing over their bladder area with a cold gauze pad. There is no harm to your infant other than they might get fussy and cry. So, if you are happy with this we can give it a wee go!
