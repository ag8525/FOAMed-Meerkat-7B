**Summary:**
A systematic review and meta-analysis by Chu DK et al. evaluated the effects of liberal versus conservative oxygen therapy in acutely ill adults, revealing that liberal oxygen therapy increases mortality without improving other patient-important outcomes. The study included 25 randomized control trials with 16,037 patients suffering from conditions like sepsis, stroke, and myocardial infarction. Results indicated that a liberal oxygen strategy increased the risk of death compared to a conservative approach, with no significant differences in morbidity outcomes such as disability or hospital-acquired infections. The authors concluded that supplemental oxygen might become unfavorable above an SpO2 range of 94–96%, advocating for a conservative administration of oxygen therapy. The study highlights the importance of treating oxygen as a drug, emphasizing careful dosing to avoid potential harm.

**Case:**
You’re working a shift in a rural emergency department when a 68-year-old man presents with a two-day course of worsening cough, shortness of breath, and fever. Their workup reveals a multifocal pneumonia with signs of sepsis. The patient has an oxygen saturation of 98% on room air and your nurses ask if you want the patient to receive supplemental oxygen.

**Background:**
Supplemental oxygen therapy, traditionally used liberally in acutely ill adults, is being re-evaluated, particularly in non-hypoxic patients with myocardial infarction, as evidenced by trials like DETO2X-AMI and the 2015 AVOID study, which suggested increased myocardial infarction size with high oxygen flow in STEMI patients. While beneficial for conditions such as acute desaturation, respiratory distress, and carbon monoxide poisoning, there is concern over "indication creep," where oxygen is used excessively without clear benefit. In neonatal resuscitation, oxygen is carefully dosed to minimize harm, a practice not yet widely adopted in adult care, where liberal use remains common despite potential risks.

**Clinical Point:**
Is liberal oxygen therapy vs. conservative oxygen therapy for acutely ill adults effective and safe?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The goal of oxygen therapy should not usually be 100% in critically ill patients but rather aim for the mid 90’s%.

**Case Resolution:**
As the patient is breathing easily and has adequate oxygen saturation, I request nursing only supply additional oxygen if the patient begins to have low oxygen saturations. The patient is admitted to the medical ward on room air.

**Clinical Application:**
This is another study reminding us that oxygen is a drug and just like any other drug it can have potential benefit and potential harm depending on how and when it is used. Multiple recent studies support a conservative supplemental oxygen therapy approach should be used when treating critically ill patients.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
We will be continuously monitoring your body and your vital signs during your hospitalization with us. It doesn’t appear that additional oxygen will be helpful to you at this time, but if it becomes needed, we’ll supply it at once.
