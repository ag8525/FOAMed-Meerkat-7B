**Summary:**
In this clinical scenario, a 39-year-old woman with a history of migraines was successfully treated in the emergency department with IV fluids, DHE, and metoclopramide. The document discusses the high prevalence and economic burden of migraines, noting that up to half of patients may return to the ED shortly after initial treatment. A meta-analysis by Coleman et al. evaluated the efficacy of parenteral dexamethasone in preventing migraine recurrence within 24-72 hours post-treatment, showing a 26% relative reduction in recurrence with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 9. Dexamethasone was associated with minimal adverse effects, making it a viable option to reduce ED bounce-backs. Despite some limitations in the study's reporting, the findings suggest that adding dexamethasone to standard abortive therapy can effectively decrease early migraine recurrences. The patient in the case was informed about this option and received 15mg of IV dexamethasone, with plans to monitor for any recurrence.

**Case Scenario:**
A thirty-nine year-old woman known to your emergency department with a long history of migraine headaches presents in her usual way. There is nothing to suggest anything other than her typical migraine headache. You treat her successfully with IV fluids, DHE, and metoclopramide. She is feeling much better and is ready for discharge.

**Background:**
Migraines affect over 10% of the population, with a higher prevalence in women (18%) compared to men (6%), and are associated with substantial medical and economic burdens, including approximately $17 billion in direct costs and $15 billion in indirect costs annually due to lost productivity. A significant clinical challenge is the high rate of patients returning to the emergency department (ED) shortly after initial treatment. Dexamethasone has been evaluated in randomized controlled trials as a potential intervention to reduce these ED return visits, with a comprehensive review by Giuliano et al. published in Postgraduate Medicine.

**Point:**
Can dexamethasone prevent migraine patients from bouncing back to the emergency department in the next few days?

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
For patients successfully aborted for a migraine attack, a single parenteral dose of dexamethasone ≥15mg will significantly reduce early recurrences (NNT=9) with no significant side effects.

**Case Resolution:**
You discussed dexamethasone treatment with the patient. She decided it was worth a try and you give her 15mg of IV dexamethasone. You plan on checking to see if she re-presents in the next week.
