**Summary:**
A cluster randomized trial conducted in France evaluated the impact of family presence during cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) on psychological outcomes and medical efforts. The study involved 570 family members of patients experiencing cardiac arrest at home, with 60% witnessing the resuscitation. Results indicated that family presence was associated with a significant reduction in PTSD-related symptoms and anxiety compared to those who did not witness CPR. Importantly, family presence did not interfere with medical efforts, increase healthcare team stress, or lead to medico-legal conflicts. The study suggests that offering family members the opportunity to witness CPR may provide psychological benefits without compromising medical care, although the findings are primarily applicable to the European context.

**Case Scenario:**
You are working in a busy ED when a young new paramedic crew brings in a post cardiac arrest that they are resuscitating. They tell you that the patient collapsed at home during a family event and that a family member immediately started CPR while the rest of the family bore witnesses. The family is now en route to the ED and the paramedics are concerned that they did the wrong thing by allowing the family to watch.

**Background:**
Family presence during cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) is a debated topic in medical literature, with discussions on its impact on both family members and medical teams. While it may provide closure and a chance for final goodbyes, concerns exist about the potential for increased stress and emotional burden on both families and healthcare providers. Research on this issue is limited, with most data derived from feedback and small observational studies, and only one randomized controlled trial, which was terminated early. Despite these limitations, major international CPR guidelines suggest that family-witnessed resuscitation is supported by available evidence and is considered reasonable and generally useful.

**Case Resolution:**
The patient survives to hospital admission but like the majority of patients does not survive to hospital discharge. You reassure the paramedic that they did the right thing by allowing the family to witness the resuscitation.
