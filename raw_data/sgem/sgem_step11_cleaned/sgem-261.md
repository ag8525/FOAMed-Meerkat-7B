**Summary:**
The document discusses the validation of the CriSTAL tool, designed to predict short-term mortality in elderly patients admitted to the emergency department, with a focus on facilitating end-of-life care discussions. The study involved patients over 65 from Australia and Ireland, excluding those with severe cognitive impairment or critical illness without a surrogate. The tool demonstrated good discriminant power, with suggested cut-offs of seven for Australia and six for Ireland, based on AUC statistics. However, concerns about inter-rater reliability, overfitting, and external validity were noted, and the tool's clinical utility compared to clinician judgment remains uncertain. The study emphasizes the importance of accurate prognosis in guiding end-of-life discussions, though further validation and comparison to clinician gestalt are needed.

**Case:**
An 83-year-old man with early dementia, congestive heart failure (CHF), prior myocardial infarction (MI) with three stents, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), and atrial fibrillation is transferred to the hospital because the nursing home thinks he might have a urinary tract infection (UTI). On arrival, he is febrile, confused, with an alternating level of consciousness, tachycardia, and a rapid respiratory rate. According to the family with him, he had never had an end-of-life conversation with his physicians. You think such a conversation is important to guide your care in the next few hours, but you wonder if there is a tool to help you predict this gentleman’s chance of dying during this visit or shortly after.

**Background:**
End-of-life care discussions in emergency medicine are crucial for determining appropriate interventions, balancing life-saving measures with the avoidance of aggressive treatment in patients unlikely to recover. Prognostication in this setting is challenging due to the emergency department's fast-paced nature, and existing decision tools lack rigorous validation and accuracy. Basic demographic data are inadequate for predicting individual patient risk. This study aims to validate the Criteria for Screening and Triaging to Appropriate aLternative care (CriSTAL), a personalized risk score designed for older patients in the emergency department, to improve prognostic accuracy and guide care decisions.

**Clinical Point:**
To establish the predictive ability of individual and combined parameters in the CriSTAL tool to predict short-term post-discharge death in an elderly population.

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Accurate prognosis is an important component of medical advice, especially in elderly populations. The CriSTAL tool, or its components, may help physicians open an important end-of-life discussion.

**Case Resolution:**
You open up a conversation about end-of-life care with the patient and their family. It is the first time they have been approached about the topic.

**Clinical Application:**
We are unable to apply the CriSTAL tool at this time. We would like to see further trials looking at its validation, implementation, and comparing it to clinician gestalt.

**What Do I Tell My Patient:**
You probably have an infection in your urine. Given your age and all of your other medical conditions, it could be serious. By serious, I mean you could die from the infection. Have you ever thought about or talked about end-of-life care? We want to make sure we respect your wishes. If your heart or lungs stopped, would you like us to do CPR, put a tube down your throat to help you breathe, shock your heart if necessary, and admit you to an intensive care unit? Or would you like us to allow a natural death?
