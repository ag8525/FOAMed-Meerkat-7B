**Summary:**
The document discusses the evaluation of the Convergent Functional Information for Suicidality (CFI-S) as a screening tool for suicidal ideation in emergency department (ED) patients, comparing it to physician gestalt. The study involved 338 participants, with 9.5% experiencing a suicidality spectrum event within six months. The CFI-S demonstrated moderate accuracy, similar to physician gestalt, with an area under the curve of 0.77 compared to 0.75 for gestalt, indicating no significant improvement. The authors suggest that while the CFI-S may aid in detecting high-risk patients, it does not outperform physician gestalt, which remains the most efficient screening method. The study highlights the challenge of integrating additional screening tools in busy ED settings without clear evidence of superiority over clinical judgment.

**Case:**
A 32-year-old woman presents to the emergency department after spraining her ankle playing basketball. Although she has no other health problems, and no other complaints, you are aware of data that indicates there is a high level of psychiatric illness and suicidal ideation among emergency department patients and wonder what is the best way to approach this problem?

**Background:**
Suicidal ideation is a significant clinical concern, accounting for approximately 1% of emergency department visits, equating to about 1.4 million visits annually in the United States. Despite the availability of several validated screening tools, such as the PHQ9, ED-Safe Patient Safety Screener, and the Suicide Behaviors Questionnaire–Revised (SBQ-R), these tools have not been compared to physician gestalt and are not widely implemented in clinical practice. The Convergent Functional Information for Suicidality (CFI-S) is another validated tool for assessing suicidal ideation, though it has not been evaluated in an emergency department setting. A current trial seeks to determine the accuracy of the CFI-S in the ED by comparing it to an existing screening tool and physician gestalt.

**Clinical Point:**
Can the CFI-S improve on clinician gestalt for screening of all adults to an emergency department for suicidal ideation?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Physician gestalt is probably still the most accurate and efficient manner of screening of psychiatric disease in the emergency department.

**Case Resolution:**
After a brief discussion, you have no concerns about psychiatric problems, and discharge the patient home without any formal testing.

**Clinical Application:**
Unless demonstrated to be better than clinical gestalt, it isn’t clear how an extra screening tool for suicidal ideation can help all comers in the emergency department.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You have sprained your ankle. We use a tool called the Ottawa ankle rule to decide if you need an x-ray. Based on this tool an x-ray is not needed and I can confidently say you have not broken your ankle. We will put a tensor bandage around your ankle, and you can take acetaminophen or ibuprofen for the pain. The nurse will give you a standard information sheet for managing a sprained ankle. Obviously, if you have any other concerns about your health we are always happy to see you again in the emergency department.
