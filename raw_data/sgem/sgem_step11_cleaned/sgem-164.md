**Summary:**
A recent multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial investigated the efficacy of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP/SMX) in treating uncomplicated cutaneous abscesses post-incision and drainage (I&D), particularly in settings with prevalent MRSA. The study included patients over 12 years with abscesses larger than 2 cm, excluding those with complicating factors such as immunodeficiency or recent antibiotic use. Results showed a significant increase in clinical cure rates with TMP/SMX compared to placebo, with a 6.9% difference in the modified intention-to-treat analysis and a 7.2% difference in the per-protocol analysis, indicating a number needed to treat (NNT) of 14. Adverse events were similar between groups, primarily mild gastrointestinal issues. The study supports the use of TMP/SMX in enhancing cure rates for uncomplicated abscesses, advocating for shared decision-making in clinical practice.

**Case:**
A 40 year-old male with a history of MRSA presents complaining of an area of redness and swelling consistent with an abscess developing on his arm. He says that he has heard that antibiotics may not be necessary after his friend had an incision and drainage (I&D) and they did not give her any antibiotics. He denies any allergies to antibiotics and has taken Sulfa drugs in the past without complication.

**Background:**
Cutaneous abscesses are a prevalent condition encountered in emergency departments, characterized by localized collections of pus within the skin. The clinical management of these abscesses often involves incision and drainage (I&D), but the necessity of additional interventions such as packing, irrigation, and antibiotic use has been debated. Historically, routine packing and irrigation post-I&D have been deemed unnecessary. The use of antibiotics following I&D, particularly in the context of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), has also been contentious due to a lack of high-quality evidence. A 2007 review highlighted the need for a robust clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy of antibiotics in this setting. Recently, a multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial has been conducted to provide more definitive guidance on the use of antibiotics for simple cutaneous abscesses.

**Clinical Point:**
Does TMP/SMX offer a higher clinical cure rate than placebo in patients with a drained uncomplicated cutaneous abscess?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The addition of TMP/SMX to the treatment of uncomplicated cutaneous abscesses represents an opportunity for shared decision-making.

**Case Resolution:**
You discuss with your patient that in the past there was some limited evidence suggesting that antibiotics may not be of benefit but that there were limitations due to the studies small size. However, a newer, larger study demonstrates that antibiotics are effective in increasing the cure rate for abscesses. After shared decision-making including the risks, benefits, and potential complications of antibiotics, the patient agrees to receive TMP/SMX for his abscess after the incision and drainage is complete.

**Clinical Application:**
TMP/SMX should be considered as part of the treatment regimen for the management of cutaneous abscesses.

**What do I tell my patient?**
You are right, until recently we did not have good evidence that antibiotics were helpful in treating abscesses. However, a new well-done study has shown antibiotics can increase the cure rate by 7%. That means we need to treat 14 patients for one more patient to be cured. Antibiotics are not without risk though and there are certain populations that were not included in the study. We should consider TMP/SMX in the treatment of your abscess.
