**Summary:**
The document discusses the impact of using scribes in emergency departments to enhance physician productivity and patient throughput. It highlights the challenges posed by electronic medical records (EMRs), which consume significant physician time and contribute to burnout, potentially affecting patient care quality. A multicenter randomized trial in Australia demonstrated that scribes increased the number of patients seen per hour by 15.9% and reduced door-to-discharge times by 19 minutes, without compromising patient safety. The study suggests that scribes can alleviate the clerical burden on physicians, allowing them to focus more on patient care, and recommends further exploration of scribe use in various healthcare settings. Despite potential biases and the need for better EMR systems, scribes offer a pragmatic solution to improve efficiency in high-volume emergency departments.

**Case:**
The emergency department is backing up. You have ambulances ramping and patients queuing at triage. Your medical team is great, but you notice that the busier you all become, the more you see your docs at their computers, rather than at patient bedsides. You are frustrated that whilst you frantically fill in data in the Electronic Medical Record (EMR) from your last consultation, your neighbor is in your waiting room with a dislocated shoulder and you haven’t been able to get to her yet. Is there a better way of working than this way?

**Background:**
Emergency Medicine consultations constitute a significant portion of healthcare interactions in high-income countries, with electronic medical records (EMRs) being a prevalent tool among emergency physicians. The use of EMRs, while essential for documenting patient history and examinations, has expanded to include numerous tasks, resulting in increased time demands and a notable decrease in medical productivity. Research indicates that physicians spend more time on EMRs than on direct patient care, contributing to physician burnout, which is associated with diminished quality and safety of care. To mitigate these challenges, the use of medical scribes has been introduced. Scribes assist by handling clerical tasks such as documentation, information retrieval, and discharge preparation, which can enhance physician productivity and are generally well-received by both physicians and patients. While scribes have been utilized in the United States for some time, their adoption is now growing in countries like Canada and Australia.

**Clinical Point:**
What is the impact of scribes on emergency medicine physicians’ productivity and patient throughput.

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Scribes could have a positive impact on productivity and patient throughput depending on your practice environment.

**Case Resolution:**
You decide to implement a scribe program at your hospital. The IT and processes still remain tedious but you have offloaded many of them. You rediscover what it is like to be a physician who is enabled to walk from one room to another, seeing patients, providing high-quality/evidence-based care and moving rapidly to where you are needed. You go home less tired despite a bigger patient load.

**Clinical Application:**
When working with a scribe, enjoy your work and get used to being enabled to see more patients, whilst feeling like you have done less. Enable your scribe to speak up if they see anything untoward happening in the department, they may be the only one to notice it. Check every time you open the EMR that you have selected the correct patient.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
This is John, he is what is called a scribe. His job is to organize everything you need today and write down all my instructions for the emergency team. This will help me focus on being your physician.
