**Summary:**
The document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the effectiveness of parachutes in preventing death or major injury when jumping from aircraft. Despite the trial's findings that parachute use did not significantly reduce death or major injury, the study was limited by its design, as participants jumped from stationary aircraft on the ground, not from high altitudes. Key limitations included selection bias, lack of blinding, and the fact that parachutes were not deployed during the trial. The authors caution against extrapolating these results to high-altitude jumps. The clinical recommendation remains to use parachutes when jumping from moving aircraft, as they are believed to reduce morbidity and mortality despite the lack of high-quality evidence.

**Case:**
A 32-year-old woman with no previous medical history calls you while a passenger on a crashing plane. She has been offered a parachute by the flight attendant but is unsure whether jumping from the plane is wise. You quickly scour the literature for evidence to inform her decision.

**Background:**
Parachutes are devices designed to slow the descent of an object through the atmosphere by creating drag, primarily used to prevent fatal outcomes from falls. Despite their widespread use for safety and recreational purposes, a 2003 systematic review in the BMJ highlighted the absence of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to definitively prove their effectiveness in preventing morbidity and mortality. The review humorously proposed a double-blinded RCT involving parachutes and sham parachutes (backpacks) to provide conclusive evidence. Although observational data suggest parachutes can fail, and some free falls do not result in death, the lack of RCTs underscores the challenges in applying evidence-based medicine to certain interventions. Researchers eventually managed to conduct a trial with volunteers jumping from planes with either a parachute or a backpack.

**Clinical Point:**
Do parachutes reduce death or major injury when jumping from aircraft?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Wear a parachute if jumping out of a moving aircraft in the air to prevent morbidity and mortality.

**Case Resolution:**
Despite the lack of high-quality evidence demonstrating the efficacy of parachutes, you advise your friend to use the parachute being offered by the flight attendant.

**Clinical Application:**
Based on your understanding of physics and reality, you would recommend people use parachutes if jumping out of an aircraft that is flying. While it does not guarantee you will not be injured or die, it is the best evidence we have on the topic. In addition, more research is not needed to determine if parachutes prevent morbidity or mortality due to gravitational challenges.

**What Do I Tell the Passenger?**
Accept the parachute being provided by the flight attendant.
