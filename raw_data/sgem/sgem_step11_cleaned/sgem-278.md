**Summary:**
The study by Belisle et al. investigates the effectiveness of video discharge instructions compared to paper handouts for managing acute otitis media (AOM) in children. A randomized controlled trial was conducted with parents of children aged 6 months to 17 years, presenting with otalgia and a clinical diagnosis of AOM. The primary outcome was the AOM Symptom Severity Score (AOM-SOS) on day three post-discharge, which was significantly lower in the video group (p=0.004), indicating better symptom management. Secondary outcomes showed no significant differences in knowledge acquisition or functional outcomes. The study highlights the potential of video instructions to improve symptom management in pediatric AOM, though it notes limitations such as the single-center design and exclusion of non-English-speaking populations. The authors suggest that video instructions could be a valuable tool in pediatric emergency settings, particularly for English-speaking families.

**Case:**
An 18-month-old, previously healthy female presents to the emergency department with 24 hours of fever. The past few days the parents note there has been some rhinorrhea and cough. She looks well, immunizations are up to date and her examination reveals right sided acute otitis media (AOM). When discussing discharge instructions for her AOM, you wonder whether having the parents watch a video will be more beneficial for the child’s symptoms, rather than giving the parents oral instructions with a paper handout.

**Background:**
Acute otitis media (AOM) is the second most commonly diagnosed illness in children and the leading reason for antibiotic prescriptions, imposing significant healthcare costs. Clinically, AOM is associated with symptoms such as pain and fever, with over one-third of children experiencing these symptoms three to seven days post-treatment. Approximately 75% of parents cite pain and disturbed sleep as major burdens of AOM. There is considerable parental uncertainty regarding AOM treatment, with less than 30% of U.S. parents receiving adequate instructions on analgesia, leading to medication errors and suboptimal care. Standardized discharge instructions can mitigate these errors, and parents generally prefer them over verbal summaries. While video discharge instructions are favored in various pediatric contexts, their effectiveness for AOM has not been specifically studied.

**Clinical Point:**
Are video discharge instructions superior to a paper handout with respect to the Acute Otitis Media – Symptom Severity Score (AOM-SOS)?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Consider using video discharge instructions for parents of children with acute otitis media.

**Case Resolution:**
After a brief discussion with the parent, you have them watch a video of discharge instructions for their child. You then return and Point their questions prior to discharge.

**Clinical Application:**
This study provides support for use of video discharge instructions for AOM.

**What Do I Tell the Parents?**
Your child has an ear infection. It has been shown that your child’s pain and symptoms will be better managed 3 days after discharge if we have you watch a 5-minute video about ear infection treatment before you leave. We’ll also give the video link so you can watch it again at home and I’ll Point any questions you have after watching prior to your discharge.
