**Summary:**
The document discusses a 73-year-old man with a COPD exacerbation, highlighting the potential use of nebulized furosemide as an adjunctive treatment. COPD, primarily caused by smoking, is managed through smoking cessation, bronchodilators, anticholinergics, steroids, and pulmonary rehabilitation. A study by Vahedi et al. explored nebulized furosemide's efficacy, showing improved FEV1 and dyspnea scores compared to conventional therapy alone. However, the study's methodology was criticized for poor design and questionable clinical significance, leading to the conclusion that nebulized furosemide should not be used for COPD exacerbations until further data is available. The patient was managed with standard therapy, smoking cessation support, and a flu vaccination.

**Case Scenario:**
73yo man presents to the emergency department with an exacerbation of his COPD. He quit smoking two days ago when his breathing got worse. He has a history of hypertension but no history of congestive heart failure. He has been taking all his medication and puffers as prescribed. Vital signs are BP165/85, HR 95, Temp 37.2C, RR 22 and O2 Sat 92% on room air.

**Background:**
Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is defined by the World Health Organization as a lung disease characterized by chronic obstruction of lung airflow that interferes with normal breathing and is not fully reversible, encompassing conditions previously known as chronic bronchitis and emphysema. COPD is a significant, under-diagnosed, life-threatening condition, primarily caused by cigarette smoking in the western world. Although there is no cure, effective treatments include smoking cessation, bronchodilators, anticholinergics, steroids, and pulmonary rehabilitation programs. Research has suggested that nebulized furosemide might be beneficial in COPD by modulating vagal afferent activity, reducing dyspnea, and aiding patients with related respiratory conditions.

**Case Resolution:**
You treat his COPD exacerbation with conventional therapy, encourage him to stay a non-smoker, and give him a flu shot.
