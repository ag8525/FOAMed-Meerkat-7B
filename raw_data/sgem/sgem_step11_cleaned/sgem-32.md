**Summary:**
In the case of a 46-year-old male with a history of kidney stones presenting with renal colic, the clinical Point addressed is whether high volume oral/IV fluids or diuretics aid in passing kidney stones. The Cochrane review analyzed two small studies (n=118) and found no significant difference in pain relief, stone clearance, or need for surgical intervention between those receiving high volume fluids or diuretics and those receiving placebo or standard hydration. The authors concluded there is no reliable evidence supporting the use of these interventions for acute ureteric colic, and potential risks such as renal impairment or ureteric rupture were noted. Consequently, routine use of high volume fluids or diuretics is not recommended. In this case, the patient was treated with IV fluids and ketorolac, with symptom resolution and follow-up instructions provided.

**Case Scenario:**
A 46-year-old man presents to the emergency department doing the renal colic shuffle (not the Harlem Shake). He has a history of kidney stones. Nothing in his physical examination or investigations suggest anything other than another renal colic attack. He wants to know if there is a way to flush the stone out.

**Point:**
Does pushing oral/IV fluids or diuretics help in passing kidney stones?

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
You don’t need to push fluids (oral/IV) or use diuretics to pass kidney stones

**Case Resolution:**
You treat him with some IV fluids and 30mg ketorolac IV. His symptoms resolve. Imaging demonstrates a small, distal stone with no hydronephrosis. He is instructed to return if new symptoms, existing symptoms get worse or he is worried. His follow-up is with his primary care provider or urologist.
