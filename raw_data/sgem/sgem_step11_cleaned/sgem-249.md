**Summary:**
Transtracheal ultrasonography is highlighted as a rapid and accurate method for confirming endotracheal tube placement, particularly when traditional methods like quantitative waveform capnography are unavailable or unreliable, especially in cardiac arrest scenarios. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 studies involving 1,595 patients demonstrated that transtracheal ultrasound has a sensitivity of 98.7% and specificity of 97.1%, with a mean confirmation time of 13 seconds. Despite the high esophageal intubation rate of 15% in the studies, ultrasound offers a faster alternative to capnography, which can take up to 60 seconds. The review acknowledges potential biases, including publication bias and selection bias due to convenience sampling, but supports the use of ultrasound as a valuable adjunct in emergency settings. The integration of POCUS in clinical practice is encouraged as it becomes more accessible and affordable, enhancing the accuracy and speed of endotracheal tube placement confirmation.

**Case:**
A 48-year-old male is in cardiac arrest and is not being successfully oxygenated by bag valve mask or with a supraglottic airway. While preparing to intubate the patient, you consider ways of quickly confirming endotracheal tube placement. You have a colleague in the room who is proficient at ultrasound and asks if there is a role for bedside ultrasound in this situation.

**Background:**
Endotracheal intubation is a critical medical procedure that, if improperly performed, can result in fatal outcomes, necessitating rapid confirmation of endotracheal tube placement. The American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) has issued guidelines on this matter, highlighting several methods for confirmation, including physical examination, direct visualization or videolaryngoscopy, pulse oximetry, chest x-ray, esophageal detector devices, and end-tidal carbon dioxide detection through various capnography techniques. Despite these methods, evidence suggests that commonly used endpoints for rapid confirmation may be inaccurate, with quantitative waveform capnography confirming tube placement correctly only about two-thirds of the time during cardiac arrest. Point of care ultrasound (POCUS) has gained popularity for its ease of use and accuracy, and although small studies have explored its application in confirming tube placement, these studies have been limited by small sample sizes and wide confidence intervals.

**Clinical Point:**
What is the accuracy of ultrasonography for confirmation of endotracheal tube placement?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Transtracheal sonography represents a potential fast and accurate way to help confirm endotracheal tube placement in conjunction with other methods.

**Case Resolution:**
Your colleague is familiar with the application of POCUS for endotracheal tube placement and agrees to assist you in your intubation attempt. While you directly visualize the passage of the endotracheal tube through the vocal cords, she is able to see the appropriate findings consistent with successful placement. Waveform capnography is used in addition and further supports the appropriate placement. With this successful intubation, the patient’s oxygen saturations improve.

**Clinical Application:**
POCUS for confirmation of endotracheal tube placement is another example of emergency medicine embracing this new technology. It represents another potential tool that can be used in combination with existing methods to verify correct tube placement. As these bedside devices become pocket size and more affordable, it will be interesting to see how clinicians continue to include POCUS in their practice.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
Since the patient is intubated, there really is not much to tell them. However, you may tell your team that to help rapidly confirm endotracheal intubation you will use transtracheal ultrasound along with other measures such as direct visualization of the tube’s passage through the cords, auscultation, and waveform capnography.
