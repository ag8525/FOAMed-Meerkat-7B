**Summary:**
A retrospective cohort study by Harrison et al. examined the association between first emergency department (ED) presentations related to alcohol and subsequent mortality in adolescents and young adults aged 12-29 in Ontario from 2009-2015. The study found that alcohol-related ED visits were significantly associated with increased 1-year mortality, with an adjusted odds ratio of 3.07, particularly in young adults, those with concurrent mental health or substance use disorders, and those with severe initial presentations. The primary outcome showed a 1-year mortality rate of 0.35% in the alcohol group compared to 0.1% in the non-alcohol group. The study underscores the importance of recognizing the heightened risk of mortality following alcohol-related ED visits in this demographic and suggests the need for targeted interventions and support services to mitigate these risks.

**Case:**
It’s a Friday evening at the end of the academic year in the Paediatric Emergency Department (ED) and you are with the parents of a 15-year-old girl who has been brought in acutely intoxicated from an unofficial "School’s Out" party. Although your patient has recovered and is now fit for discharge, her parents are very worried that this may mean she is at more risk in the future.

**Background:**
Alcohol-related problems are a significant cause of global mortality and morbidity, with emergency department (ED) visits due to alcohol on the rise. Adults presenting to the ED with alcohol-related issues face an elevated risk of death within the subsequent year, with a correlation between the frequency of alcohol-associated ED visits and increased mortality. The impact of alcohol on specific vulnerable age groups remains an area requiring further exploration.

**Clinical Point:**
Is a first ED presentation related to alcohol associated with increased mortality in adolescents and young adults?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
A first ED presentation related to alcohol is associated with increased mortality in adolescents and young adults.

**Case Resolution:**
You explain to the teenager and her parents that there is an increased risk associated with this alcohol-related presentation, but that their daughter’s age and gender reduce this risk. You also signpost them to the local alcohol harm reduction and family support services.

**Clinical Application:**
You work with your ED education team to ensure that staff are aware of this risk and able to support patients in addressing their alcohol issues.
