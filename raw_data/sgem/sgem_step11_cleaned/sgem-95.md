**Summary:**
In the management of febrile children, the focus should be on improving comfort rather than solely normalizing body temperature, as fever itself is not harmful to generally healthy children. The American Academy of Pediatrics emphasizes that antipyretic therapy should aim to enhance the child's overall well-being. A systematic review suggests that combined or alternating acetaminophen and ibuprofen therapy may reduce fever more effectively than monotherapy, but evidence on improving child comfort remains inconclusive. While alternating therapy may decrease absenteeism from daycare, the primary goal should be comfort, with caution advised to avoid overdosing when using both medications. Further research is needed to better understand the benefits of these therapies on comfort.

**Case:**
2-year-old presents with flu-like symptoms and a fever.

**Background:**
Fever in children is a common concern among parents, often leading to unnecessary emergency department visits due to misconceptions about its dangers. According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, fever itself does not pose a threat to generally healthy children and may even be beneficial. The primary objective of antipyretic treatment is not to reduce body temperature to normal levels but to enhance the child's comfort and overall well-being.

**Point:**
Should parents combine/alternate acetaminophen and ibuprofen?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Parents and caregivers should focus on patient comfort instead of normalizing a temperature in febrile children. Alternating therapy may be beneficial for comfort, but more research is required to address this specific Point.

**Clinical Application:**
In febrile children, alternating or combining antipyretics may be helpful in controlling temperature, but this is of limited usefulness. Comfort may also benefit, but this requires more research.

**What Do I Tell My Patient:**
Treat comfort not fever. If one medication is not working try the other. Be careful if using both as not to accidentally overdose on one or the other.
