**Summary:**
The study by Han et al. explores the impact of telemental health (TMH) versus in-person mental health consultations in the Veterans Administration emergency department setting, focusing on 30-day outcomes and care processes. The exploratory retrospective cohort study included 496 veterans, predominantly middle-aged males, with 70% receiving TMH. The primary outcome, a composite of 30-day return ED visits, rehospitalizations, and death, showed no significant difference between TMH and in-person consultations. However, TMH was associated with longer ED stays and reduced involuntary psychiatric holds. These findings suggest TMH as a viable alternative when in-person consultations are unavailable, though the study's external validity may be limited due to the specific veteran population. Further research is needed to confirm these results and understand the underlying mechanisms.

**Case:**
You are moonlighting at the Veterans Administration emergency department (ED) and are caring for an Iraq veteran complaining of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and severe anxiety. You desire a psychiatric consult and learn that you don’t have in-person consult availability at this facility, but instead use telehealth services. You wonder about how this compares to an in-person consult.

**Background:**
Mental health and behavioral complaints frequently present in emergency departments (EDs), but the limited availability of mental health providers often necessitates patient transfers, even when discharge might be appropriate. Telemental health (TMH) has demonstrated effectiveness in non-Veterans Administration settings by improving access to mental health care, increasing the rate of patient discharges to home, and reducing the need for patient transfers. However, the impact of TMH on post-evaluation care processes, including medication adjustments, patient disposition, length of stay, involuntary holds, and the use of chemical or physical restraints, remains insufficiently studied.

**Clinical Point:**
What is the effect of TMH, versus in-person consult, on 30-day outcomes and processes of care during the visit?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Telemental health consults did not significantly change the primary outcome but did have some interesting process differences when compared with in-person consults in the American Veteran’s Administration system.

**Case Resolution:**
You connect to a TMH provider who assesses your patient, makes some medication changes, and discharges the patient home to follow up within a week.

**Clinical Application:**
Telemental health may serve as a good option for mental health consultation when in-person evaluation isn’t available.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
“I’m going to set you up for a mental health evaluation. You’ll talk to a provider, but they won’t be here in the building. Instead, we’re going to use a videoconferencing app.”
