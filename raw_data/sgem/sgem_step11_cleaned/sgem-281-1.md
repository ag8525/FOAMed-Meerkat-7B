**Summary:**
In this episode of "Statistically Significant," the focus is on the critical role of balancing prognostic factors in randomized controlled trials (RCTs), using the PreVent trial as a case study. Prognostic factors, which predict patient outcomes, must be balanced to avoid bias in treatment effect measures. Randomization is crucial as it ensures that both known and unknown prognostic factors are evenly distributed across study groups, thus minimizing indication bias. The discussion highlights that statistical hypothesis testing for comparing prognostic factors in RCTs is inappropriate, as randomization assumes the null hypothesis of group equivalence, and any observed differences are likely due to chance, risking a type 1 error. This underscores the importance of trusting the randomization process to achieve balanced prognostic factors, ensuring valid and unbiased trial results.

**Background:**
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) aim to balance prognostic factors, which are characteristics indicating a patient's likelihood of experiencing an outcome, to accurately assess treatment effectiveness. Without accounting for these factors, treatment effect measures can be biased, as seen in non-randomized designs where indication bias may occur. Randomization ensures that patient characteristics are evenly distributed across study groups, making the randomization process the sole determinant for treatment allocation. This balance includes both known and unknown prognostic factors, and any statistical differences observed at baseline are due to chance, assuming a valid randomization process. Statistical hypothesis testing to compare prognostic factors in RCTs is inappropriate, as the null hypothesis of group equivalence is inherently assumed, and observed differences are attributed to random variation, avoiding type 1 errors.
