**Summary:**
A study by Vincendeau et al. evaluated the efficacy of tamsulosin 0.4 mg daily compared to placebo in 129 adult patients with distal ureteral stones, finding that tamsulosin did not significantly accelerate stone expulsion. The study, a multicenter, randomized, double-blind trial, was well-tolerated but did not demonstrate a benefit over placebo, raising questions about the utility of alpha-blockers in this context. Despite previous studies suggesting potential benefits, this trial's findings align with concerns about publication bias and methodological limitations in earlier research. A Cochrane systematic review is underway to further investigate the role of alpha-blockers in managing ureteral stones. The BEEM commentary highlights the need for more robust statistical analyses, such as relative risk reduction or NNT, to better assess treatment efficacy.

**Case Scenario:**
A 49-year-old man presents to the ED with his typical problem of renal colic.

**Point:**
Is an alpha blocker safe and effective for the treatment of kidney stones?

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
Tamsulosin 0.4 mg OD does not seem to work for renal colic beyond the placebo effect.
