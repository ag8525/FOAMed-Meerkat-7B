**Summary:**
A 60-year-old man with severe sepsis from pneumonia is managed in the ICU with intravenous fluids and antibiotics. The case highlights the controversial use of a vitamin C cocktail (vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine) for sepsis, popularized by Dr. Paul Marik's retrospective study, which suggested a significant reduction in mortality from 40.4% to 8.5% in patients with severe sepsis or septic shock. Despite these promising results, the study's design limits its ability to establish causation, and the findings require further validation through randomized controlled trials. Current clinical practice should remain guided by established evidence, and while the vitamin C protocol shows potential, it is not yet a standard treatment for sepsis.

**Case:**
A 60 year-old man is admitted to the ICU with severe sepsis from pneumonia. He has a history of hypertension and diabetes. You are providing him with intravenous fluids and appropriate antibiotics. When speaking with the family his son asks about a vitamin C cure he just read about?

**Background:**
Sepsis is a life-threatening condition characterized by a dysregulated immune response to infection, leading to organ dysfunction and high mortality rates. Recent interest has emerged around a vitamin C cocktail, comprising vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine, as a potential treatment for sepsis, following anecdotal reports of successful recoveries. Dr. Paul Marik, a critical care physician, initially treated three septic shock patients with this combination, observing dramatic recoveries, which prompted him to conduct a retrospective study. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of the cocktail, as conducting a randomized controlled trial was deemed unethical due to the potential life-saving nature of the treatment. Despite the promising outcomes, the initial cases were not included in the study data, and the results remain hypothesis-generating rather than conclusive evidence of efficacy.

**Clinical Point:**
Does a vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine protocol decrease mortality in patients with severe sepsis or septic shock?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine was associated with lower mortality in severe septic and septic shock patients in this one small, single center retrospective before-after study but causation has yet to be demonstrated.

**Case Resolution:**
You continue with your intravenous fluids and antibiotics and the man recovers from his pneumonia and is discharged out of the ICU after four days doing well and expected to make a full recovery.

**Clinical Application:**
While this provides some weak evidence of the effectiveness of a vitamin C cocktail, it is not strong enough to reject the null hypothesis that there is no effect. Therefore, I will not be providing this treatment at this time based on this information.

**What do I tell my patient?**
You tell the son you have read the original published study. It was a small observational study done at one hospital showing unusually good results. It demonstrated association not causation of vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine reducing mortality. This is weak evidence but encouraging. Even the lead author of the study said the results were preliminary and needed to be confirmed. Our hospital is going to look at this information and decide if it is in the best interest of patients to use the treatment or not. For now, your dad will get the best medical care based on the best evidence.
