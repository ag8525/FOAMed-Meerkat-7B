**Summary:**
In a study examining the efficacy of calcium administration during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), a randomized clinical trial by Vallentin et al. found no significant improvement in sustained return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) with calcium compared to saline. The trial included adults who received epinephrine during OHCA, excluding cases like traumatic arrests and known pregnancies. Despite theoretical benefits of calcium's inotropic and vasopressor effects, the primary outcome showed a 19% ROSC rate in the calcium group versus 27% in the saline group, with no significant differences in secondary outcomes such as survival and neurological status at 30 and 90 days. The study, conducted in Denmark, highlighted potential limitations in external validity due to differences in EMS systems and bystander CPR rates compared to other regions. The findings do not support routine calcium use in OHCA, aligning with the authors' conclusions and reinforcing current clinical practice guidelines.

**Case:**
An EMS crew arrives at the home of a 68-year-old suffering from a witnessed out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA). They have a history of hypertension, elevated cholesterol, and smoked cigarettes for 50+ years. Bystander CPR is being performed. The monitor is hooked up. The paramedics performed high-quality CPR and follow their ACLS protocol. Intraosseous access is quickly obtained, and a dose of epinephrine is provided. CPR is continued while a supraglottic airway is placed successfully. The patient is transported to the emergency department with vital signs absent (VSA).

**Background:**
Calcium is theorized to benefit patients experiencing cardiac arrest due to its inotropic and vasopressor effects, which could potentially aid in the return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC). Despite this theoretical advantage, previous small randomized controlled trials have not demonstrated a significant superiority of calcium in achieving ROSC, although the point estimates in these studies have shown a slight favor towards calcium.

**Clinical Point:**
Does administration of calcium during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest improve sustained return of spontaneous circulation?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The routine use of calcium in an OHCA is not supported by the available evidence.

**Case Resolution:**
Three rounds of epinephrine are eventually provided without ever achieving ROSC. ECG shows no electrical activity, pupils are fixed and dilated, and POCUS shows no cardiac activity. The patient is pronounced deceased in the ED.

**Clinical Application:**
We have not and will continue to not routinely give calcium to adult patients with OHCAs.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You tell the patients family that they had a cardiac arrest. The paramedics did great CPR, put in an airway to help breathing and gave epinephrine to try and restart the heart. Despite everyone’s efforts we were not able to get their heart going again and they have died.
