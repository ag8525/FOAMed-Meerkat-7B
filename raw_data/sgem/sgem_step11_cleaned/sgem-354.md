**Summary:**
The document discusses the potential implementation of mobile stroke units (MSUs) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) management, referencing a study by Grotta et al. that suggests better 90-day outcomes with MSUs compared to standard EMS care. The study found that MSUs led to higher rates of tPA administration and improved utility-weighted modified Rankin Scale (uw-mRS) scores at 90 days. However, the document highlights significant concerns about the study's methodology, including multiple protocol changes, limited operational hours, and potential biases such as the Hawthorne effect. It questions the cost-effectiveness and generalizability of MSUs, suggesting that improving EMS-ED handoffs might be more beneficial. The conclusion advises against investing in MSUs, recommending instead the allocation of resources to enhance overall EMS capabilities.

**Case:**
The Mayor of your community consults you as an expert in public health, EMS, and as an EM physician on whether they should purchase a mobile stroke unit (MSU) ambulance.

**Background:**
Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is a critical medical condition where timely intervention is essential to improve patient outcomes. Thrombolysis, with or without endovascular therapy (EVT), is a standard treatment approach for AIS. Secondary stroke prevention and pre-hospital care, such as the early administration of nitroglycerin by paramedics, have been explored to enhance neurologic outcomes, though the RIGHT-2 trial found no significant improvement in functional outcomes at 90 days as measured by the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). Mobile stroke units (MSUs) have been evaluated through a systematic review and meta-analysis of seven randomized controlled trials and four observational studies involving 21,297 patients, showing improved neurologic outcomes at seven days post-treatment compared to conventional care, though not at one day.

**Clinical Point:**
Should mobile stroke units be purchased and deployed in your community?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
We still cannot recommend the use of MSU even with the addition of the BEST-MSU publication.

**Case Resolution:**
You tell the mayor that given the choice of spending lots of money on one MSU ambulance vs. two to three regular ambulances you would advise the latter. This would not just improve response times for stroke patients but for all patients who call 911.

**Clinical Application:**
We do not have high-quality evidence to support the use of MSUs and these initiatives should not be supported by EM or EMS physicians.

**What Do I Tell the Mayor?**
I would tell a mayor, EMS chief, or health system that MSU’s are an expensive boondoggle that won’t change the health of the population they serve. I would point out that this study suggests that there may be issues with the handoff between EMS and the ED regarding suspected stroke patients that could be improved on to increase favorable outcomes.
