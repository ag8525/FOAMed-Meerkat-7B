**Summary:**
This document discusses the use of corticosteroids as an adjunct treatment for sore throat, highlighting a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials. The analysis included 1,426 patients and found that corticosteroids, particularly dexamethasone, provided modest pain relief without a significant increase in adverse effects. However, the studies varied in dosing and administration routes, and the term "low-dose" was questioned due to inconsistencies in steroid regimens. The review emphasized the need for cautious use of corticosteroids, especially given the potential for overuse and the lack of rigorous safety data. The authors concluded that while corticosteroids may offer some benefit, their use should be carefully considered, particularly in the context of avoiding unnecessary antibiotic prescriptions.

**Case:**
A 50-year-old man presents with a one-day history of sore throat, cough, and low-grade fever. He is otherwise healthy with only sports-related injuries. The ibuprofen did not help, and he is requesting antibiotics so he can get back to work sooner.

**Background:**
Sore throat is a common complaint leading to approximately 6.6 million annual visits to primary care providers and emergency departments in the US. Despite guidelines from NICE and ESCMID advising against routine antibiotic use due to concerns about antimicrobial resistance, antibiotics are frequently prescribed. Alternative treatments for symptomatic relief include acetaminophen and NSAIDs, though these can have adverse effects. Corticosteroids have shown moderate benefit in treating sore throats by reducing inflammation through the inhibition of pro-inflammatory mediators in airway endothelial cells. However, the efficacy of corticosteroids is difficult to assess due to confounding factors such as the concurrent use of antibiotics and analgesics in studies.

**Clinical Point:**
Are corticosteroids effective and safe as an adjunct treatment for sore throat in addition to standard care compared with standard care alone?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Steroids appear to provide a modest benefit to patients presenting to the emergency department with a sore throat.

**Case Resolution:**
Using some shared decision-making, you discuss the evidence for different treatments. This includes over-the-counter analgesics and NSAIDs, not needing antibiotics, and considering corticosteroids. He decides to try 10mg of dexamethasone.

**Clinical Application:**
I am concerned that corticosteroids are going to be used indiscriminately for sore throats. Given the potential side effects, cumulative doses, and the use in children, I am going to be cautious. This means I will not be routinely giving corticosteroids for sore throats.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You have a sore throat, and it is most likely from a virus. Taking acetaminophen or ibuprofen can help. Antibiotics do not treat viruses and can lead to diarrhea and an allergic reaction. We could try one dose of a steroid medication. There is some evidence it can shorten your illness and get you feeling better quicker.
