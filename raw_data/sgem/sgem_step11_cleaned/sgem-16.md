**Summary:**
In the context of acute upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, the use of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) prior to endoscopy has been evaluated for its impact on patient-oriented outcomes. Despite the common practice of administering PPIs to reduce gastric acid, a systematic review and meta-analysis of six randomized controlled trials involving 2,223 participants found no significant difference in primary outcomes such as all-cause mortality, rebleeding, or the need for surgery within 30 days when compared to placebo or no treatment. While PPIs may reduce the proportion of patients with stigmata of recent hemorrhage and the need for endoscopic therapy, these benefits do not translate into improved clinically important outcomes. Consequently, routine PPI administration before endoscopy in the emergency department setting for acute upper GI bleeds is not supported by current evidence, emphasizing the need for judicious use of resources and treatments that directly benefit patient outcomes.

**Case Scenario:**
A jolly old man presents to the emergency department vomiting bright red blood all over his fur lined winter coat. You quickly assess his A,B,Cs. The nurse places two big IVs, you bring the advanced airway cart to the bedside and the laboratory draw appropriate blood work. The nurse then asks if you want to give 80mg pantoprazole IV bolus followed by an 8mg/hr drip.

**Background on Upper GI Bleeds:**
Upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding is a prevalent and serious medical condition, characterized by significant morbidity and mortality, and is the most common cause for emergency room admissions. It primarily results from peptic ulcers and affects approximately 170 per 100,000 adults annually. The condition incurs substantial healthcare costs, estimated at $750 million per year in the United States, and demands extensive medical resources. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are commonly used in the management of upper GI bleeds to reduce gastric acid, although the treatment's cost-effectiveness and impact on patient-centered outcomes require further validation.

**Point:**
Does the use of PPIs prior to endoscopy in acute upper GI bleeds change patient oriented outcomes?

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
Routine use of proton pump inhibitors is not required in the emergency department setting for acute upper GI bleeds.

**Case Scenario Conclusion:**
Jolly old St. Nick was stabilized in the emergency department. He went for endoscopy where the GI docs gave him a pre-scope dose of IV PPI anyways. He did well and was told to make some lifestyle changes.
