**Summary:**
A 26-year-old male with an uncomplicated boxer’s fracture of the fifth metacarpal neck presents with concerns about traditional cast immobilization interfering with work. Recent evidence from a randomized controlled trial suggests that buddy taping may offer similar functional outcomes to plaster casting for such fractures, with no significant difference in QuickDASH scores at 12 weeks, pain, satisfaction, or quality of life. However, buddy taping allowed for a quicker return to work. Despite these findings, the study's limitations, including potential selection bias and loss to follow-up, suggest that further validation is needed before changing standard practice. Clinicians may consider discussing buddy taping as an alternative, especially for patients concerned about work disruption, while awaiting further evidence and consensus from specialists.

**Case:**
A 26-year-old right-handed male presents to the emergency department on Friday night with a swollen right hand after punching a wall. The x-ray confirms an uncomplicated boxer’s fracture. You explain to him the traditional management which includes adequate pain control, immobilization with a cast, and referral to a hand surgeon. He does not want any opioids because a friend was addicted to oxycocet. He is fine with going to see a hand surgeon in clinic, but asks if he really needs a cast. He is concerned that it will interfere with going to work on Monday morning.

**Background:**
A boxer's fracture is a common hand injury characterized by a fracture of the neck of the fifth metacarpal, typically resulting from punching a solid object with a closed fist. Clinically significant due to its prevalence, the management of uncomplicated boxer's fractures, defined as minimally displaced closed fractures with angulation up to 70 degrees, remains controversial. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Poolman et al. (Cochrane 2005) involving 252 patients across five studies found insufficient evidence to recommend a specific treatment modality, as the studies were of poor quality and did not assess functional outcomes. Another review by Dunn et al. (Orthopedics 2016) concluded that cast immobilization is not superior to a soft wrap without reduction in most cases. No studies have yet compared the efficacy of buddy taping versus casting for functional outcomes in these fractures.

**Clinical Point:**
Is buddy taping an uncomplicated boxer’s fracture just as effective as a plaster cast?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Consider offering patients with uncomplicated boxer’s fractures buddy taping.

**Case Resolution:**
You inform him there is another treatment option called buddy taping and inform him of the limitations of the evidence. He decides to try buddy taping instead of a plaster cast.

**Clinical Application:**
This small trial provides some evidence that buddy taping uncomplicated 5th metacarpal fractures is reasonable. It is not enough to change my routine practice of putting patients in an ulnar gutter. The orthopedic surgeon or plastic surgeon can decide how to manage these patients once they see them in clinic. If the patient requests not having a cast, I will bring up the buddy taping idea. We think this type of treatment needs to be validated and confirmed before offering this routinely to patients.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
There is another option besides casting. One small study of less than 100 patients done in Australia put half the people in casts and half of them had their 4th and 5th finger buddy taped together. Both groups had the same functional outcome at three months. The study needs to be confirmed here and our hand surgeons need to be on board. The traditional way is still to cast these broken bones. Let me know If you really want to try buddy taping.
