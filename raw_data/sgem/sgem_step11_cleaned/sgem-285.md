**Summary:**
A randomized control trial by Rajendran et al. investigated the efficacy of adult therapeutic coloring as a non-pharmacological intervention for managing significant anxiety in emergency department (ED) patients. The study included patients over 15 years old with moderate to severe anxiety, as indicated by a Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale Anxiety (HADS-A) score greater than 6. Participants were randomized to receive either a coloring pack or a placebo pack. The primary outcome was the change in HADS-A score after two hours. Results showed a significant reduction in anxiety levels in the coloring group, with a mean HADS-A decrease of 3.7 compared to 0.3 in the control group. Secondary outcomes indicated high satisfaction with the coloring intervention. The study supports the use of adult coloring books as a low-risk, effective non-pharmacologic option for anxiety management in the ED setting.

**Case:**
One night during an overnight shift, you are taking care of a patient who presented to the emergency department (ED) due to anxiety and vague suicidal ideation. The process for medical clearance and psychiatric evaluation can take quite a while, and you notice that this patient seems stressed and anxious. You wonder if there’s a way to assist them during the prolonged wait without resorting to sedative medication.

**Background:**
Psychological disorders frequently lead to emergency department (ED) visits, with anxiety disorders being the most prevalent. The ED environment can exacerbate anxiety, often resulting in prolonged patient stays. Emergency physicians commonly use benzodiazepines, such as lorazepam or diazepam, to manage anxiety. There is also interest in non-pharmacological treatments, such as art therapy, with adult coloring books being explored for their potential to provide cognitive easing.

**Clinical Point:**
Can colouring decrease anxiety in adult patients presenting to the emergency department?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Art therapy in the form of coloring may be a useful non-pharmacologic alternative treatment for ED patients with anxiety.

**Case Resolution:**
You provide your patient with an adult coloring book and coloring pencils. Two hours later, they seem calmer, and their ED visit is almost over. They thank you for providing them something to ease their mind during their stay.

**Clinical Application:**
Adult coloring books are a low risk and potentially rewarding non-pharmacologic way to treat anxiety in the ED.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You seem anxious, and this visit may take some time. Some people have found that being able to spend some time colouring can help them cope with the stress of an ED visit. Would you like some supplies and try doing some colouring?
