**Summary:**
In a prospective, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy controlled trial, Nguyen et al. compared the efficacy of nebulized ketamine (0.75 mg/kg) versus intravenous sub-dissociative dose ketamine (0.3 mg/kg) for acute pain management in the emergency department. The study involved 150 patients with a mean pain score of 8.2, primarily presenting with abdominal, flank, and musculoskeletal pain. Both ketamine administration routes significantly reduced pain scores at 30 minutes, with no statistically significant difference between them (pain scores reduced to 3.8 in the nebulized group and 3.6 in the IV group). Secondary outcomes showed a higher need for rescue analgesia in the nebulized group, but adverse events were similar across both groups. The study highlights the potential of nebulized ketamine as an effective alternative to IV administration, though further multi-center trials are needed to confirm these findings and explore patient preferences.

**Case:**
You’re working your usual day shift in the emergency department (ED) from 9 am to 5 pm on a Tuesday. The next patient you pick up is a 38-year-old male with a history of kidney stones, presenting with severe flank pain radiating to the groin. He has a history of difficult intravenous (IV) access, is hemodynamically stable, and has no signs of infection. His allergies to acetaminophen, non-steroidal anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs), and opioids limit your pain management options. A bedside sonogram shows no significant hydronephrosis. You’re considering ketamine for pain relief but wonder if you should choose IV sub-dissociative ketamine or nebulized ketamine?

**Background:**
Ketamine is a versatile drug in emergency medicine, primarily used for analgesia and procedural sedation due to its unique pharmacological profile as a non-competitive NMDA receptor antagonist. It induces dissociative anesthesia, providing analgesia and amnesia without loss of consciousness. In emergency departments, ketamine is increasingly used for acute pain management at sub-dissociative doses (0.1–0.3 mg/kg IV), which effectively manage pain with minimal adverse effects such as respiratory depression and hypotension, making it a viable alternative to opioids. Ketamine can be administered via various routes, including intravenous, intranasal, and nebulized forms, with ongoing research into their efficacy and safety. The ketamine breath-actuated nebulizer (K-BAN) has shown effective analgesic properties at doses of 0.75 mg/kg, 1 mg/kg, and 1.5 mg/kg for up to 120 minutes, although its efficacy compared to intravenous sub-dissociative ketamine has not been directly compared.

**Clinical Point:**
Which ketamine regimen is superior for treating moderate to severe acute pain in the emergency department, 0.75 mg/kg nebulized or 0.3 mg/kg intravenously?

**Case Resolution:**
You decide to go with the K-BAN and after 30 minutes, your patient is pain-free and ready for discharge home.

**Clinical Application:**
It is a reasonable option to try K-BAN on a patient for pain relief if you have the resources.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
I can see you are in severe pain. Ketamine is a safe and effective analgesic that can help reduce your pain. We can give it to you by an IV or nebulized to breathe in using a mask. Which treatment would you prefer?
