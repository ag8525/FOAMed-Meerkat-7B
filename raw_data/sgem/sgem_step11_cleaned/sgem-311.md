**Summary:**
In the management of uncomplicated skin abscesses in the emergency department, the LOOP technique, which involves placing a vessel loop through the abscess cavity, is compared to standard packing with sterile ribbon gauze. A randomized controlled trial by Ladde et al. found no significant difference in treatment failure rates between the two methods in adults, but the LOOP technique showed significantly fewer failures in children. Additionally, the LOOP technique was associated with less pain and greater patient satisfaction over the 36-hour post-procedure period. While both techniques have similar efficacy in adults, the LOOP method offers advantages in terms of comfort and ease of care, particularly in pediatric cases. The study suggests that the LOOP technique may be a preferable option for uncomplicated abscess drainage, warranting consideration in clinical practice.

**Case:**
A 52-year-old previously healthy woman presents to your emergency department (ED) with an abscess on her left forearm. She is systemically well and there is no sign of tracking, so you decide to perform incision and drainage in the ED. When you ask your nursing colleague to set up the equipment, he wants to know if you will be using standard packing or a vessel loop drainage technique.

**Background:**
An abscess is a localized collection of pus within tissue, often requiring incision and drainage (I&D) for treatment. Clinical practices surrounding I&D have evolved, with studies suggesting that routine packing post-I&D may not be necessary, and irrigation after the procedure is likely unnecessary. The use of antibiotics, specifically trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP/SMX), in treating uncomplicated cutaneous abscesses has been shown to be beneficial, allowing for shared decision-making between clinicians and patients. An alternative technique to traditional packing is the loop technique, which involves placing vessel loops through the abscess cavity via small incisions, offering the advantage of not requiring replacement or change, unlike packing.

**Clinical Point:**
In uncomplicated abscesses drained in the ED, does the LOOP technique reduce treatment failure?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Consider putting in a vessel LOOP on your next uncomplicated abscess.

**Case Resolution:**
You ask the nurse to set up for the LOOP technique and the patient leaves after the procedure with follow up planned.

**Clinical Application:**
Using the LOOP technique can result in less pain and easier care for the patient in the 36 hours following I&D.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
There are two techniques for draining an abscess, which have similar failure rates, but leaving a small piece of rubber through the wound rather than filling it with cloth ribbon makes it more comfortable over the next 36 hours.
