**Summary:**
The document discusses the evaluation of the TWIST score for diagnosing testicular torsion in pediatric patients presenting with acute scrotal pain. Testicular torsion is a critical condition requiring prompt surgical intervention to prevent ischemia. The TWIST score, ranging from 0-7, includes factors such as testicular swelling, hard testicle, absent cremasteric reflex, nausea/vomiting, and high riding testicle. The study found that a TWIST score of 7 had high specificity and positive predictive value for torsion, but sensitivity was low. Despite the TWIST score's potential to expedite care, the study highlights limitations such as fair inter-rater reliability and the need for further validation before replacing ultrasound as the diagnostic standard. The recommendation is to use ultrasound for confirmation unless the TWIST score is 7, due to the risk of false negatives and the study's limitations.

**Case:**
Brian is a 14-year-old male who presents to the emergency department (ED) complaining of acute onset testicular pain. He has vomited twice, but there is no history of any fever or trauma. On examination, you find a firm, swollen right testicle, and the cremasteric reflex is notably absent on that side. Your index of suspicion for testicular torsion is high. Might his examination be enough to convince the urologists to take him straight to the operating room without a preceding diagnostic ultrasound?

**Background:**
Acute onset testicular pain can result from various causes, including testicular torsion, epididymitis, and traumatic hematomas, with testicular torsion being particularly critical due to its potential to cause ischemia from reduced blood supply. Prompt surgical intervention is crucial for testicular salvage in cases of torsion. Diagnosis can be challenging as clinical signs are not always specific, and while ultrasound imaging is the gold standard, it may delay treatment. The Testicular Workup for Ischemia and Suspected Torsion (TWIST) score is a tool developed to assess the risk of torsion in patients under 18 with acute scrotum, based on testicular swelling, hard testicle, absent cremasteric reflex, nausea or vomiting, and high riding testicle, with a total score ranging from 0 to 7. Although validated by urologists and emergency medical personnel, it has not been validated among Emergency Department physicians.

**Clinical Point:**
Can the TWIST score risk stratify pediatric patients presenting with acute scrotal pain and reduce the time to surgical intervention by eliminating the need for ultrasound diagnosis?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The TWIST score needs further multi-center validation among emergency department patients presenting with acute testicular pain and swelling before implementation into a protocol.

**Case Resolution:**
Our patient has a TWIST score of 4, receiving 2 points for testicular swelling, 1 point for absent cremasteric reflex, and 1 point for vomiting. Despite your high level of suspicion, you are going to send your patient for an ultrasound examination to confirm the diagnosis of torsion before committing him to surgical intervention.

**Clinical Application:**
Unless your patient is hitting a TWIST score of 7, you should strongly consider obtaining a diagnostic ultrasound to confirm the diagnosis of testicular torsion if that ultrasound can be performed in a reasonable time frame.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
Brian, your pain could be due to a twisting of your testicles (balls) called torsion. This can cut off the blood supply and permanently damage your testicle. You will need urgent surgery if the blood supply has been blocked. There is a scoring system we use with 0 being the lowest (less worried) and 7 the highest (most worried). Your score is in the middle at 4 which means it could be something else. To find out the right diagnosis we have ordered an urgent ultrasound. While we get that arranged would you like some pain medication?
