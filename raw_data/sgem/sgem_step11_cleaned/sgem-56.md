**Summary:**
The document discusses the Best Evidence in Emergency Medicine (BEEM) project, which aims to provide emergency medicine practitioners with the best clinical evidence by screening and rating articles for clinical relevance. The BEEM rater score, a validated tool, is used to predict the future citation rate of articles, indicating their impact on the field. A study involving 605 articles found a positive correlation between BEEM rater scores and citation rates, although the correlation with journal impact factors was minimal. The BEEM rater score significantly predicted citation rates in initial models but lost significance when adjusted for other bibliometric variables. The study highlights the BEEM rater score's reliability and potential as a measure of clinical relevance, despite limitations such as selection bias and language restrictions. The newly graduated EM resident can assess her paper's impact through traditional metrics and social media altmetrics.

**Case Scenario:**
An newly graduated EM resident gets her research paper published in an emergency medicine journal.

**Point:**
How can she know what kind of impact her paper will have on the profession?

**Background:**
Best Evidence in Emergency Medicine (BEEM) is a project initiated at McMaster University to provide emergency medicine practitioners with high-quality clinical evidence to enhance patient care. Given the vast number of articles published daily, BEEM employs a validated method to filter relevant information by screening approximately 200 journals monthly, identifying 10-20 pertinent articles related to emergency medicine. These articles are organized in a survey format and evaluated by over 100 emergency physicians using a seven-point Likert scale to determine their clinical importance. Only articles rated highly are further appraised by BEEM faculty using standardized evidence-based medicine tools to produce a critical appraisal and a BEEM bottom line. BEEM's audience rating tool is the only validated instrument for emergency medicine continuing medical education, as demonstrated by the inter-rater reliability assessment published in Academic Emergency Medicine.

**Case Resolution:**
There were be a number of ways to measure her impact factor both traditional and via social media altmetrics.
