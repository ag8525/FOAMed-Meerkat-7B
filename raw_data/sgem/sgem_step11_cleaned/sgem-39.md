**Summary:**
In the presented case of a 40-year-old male with a swollen and painful right knee, the differential diagnosis includes septic arthritis, which requires prompt identification and treatment to prevent morbidity. The document emphasizes the importance of history, physical examination, and diagnostic testing, particularly synovial fluid analysis, in diagnosing septic arthritis. While recent joint surgery or cellulitis overlying a prosthetic joint significantly alters the probability of septic arthritis, extreme synovial white blood cell counts can increase its likelihood. However, the document notes limitations in current diagnostic accuracy and highlights the potential of synovial lactate as a promising marker. In this case, synovial fluid analysis revealed urate crystals, leading to a diagnosis of gout rather than septic arthritis.

**Case Scenario:**
40yo construction worker presents to the emergency department with a swollen and painful right knee. No history of injury. No significant past medical history. Palpable effusion on examination with no overlying redness or warmth to touch. Any movement of the joint is very painful. He is taking no medications. Vital signs are normal.

**Point:**
What history, physical or diagnostic studies can help in the diagnosis of septic arthritis?

**Background:**
Monoarticular arthropathies, which affect a single joint, can arise from various causes such as trauma, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, and infections including viral, fungal, and bacterial origins. Septic arthritis, a bacterial infection of the joint, is clinically significant with an incidence rate of 10 per 100,000 annually in the USA. The risk is elevated in individuals with prosthetic joints, pre-existing rheumatoid arthritis, or those who are immunocompromised, such as patients with HIV.

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
When it comes to the accuracy of history, physical examination, serum tests, or synovial tests for the diagnosis of septic arthritis in ED patients, we just don’t know, but synovial lactate looks promising.

**Case Resolution:**
You successfully tap the knee and send the synovial fluid off for examination. Orthopedics is consulted. Three days later, you get the lab tests back describing urate crystals consistent with gout.
