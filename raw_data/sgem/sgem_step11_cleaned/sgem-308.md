**Summary:**
The document discusses the integration of advanced practice providers (APPs), such as nurse practitioners and physician assistants, into emergency department (ED) staffing to address increased patient volumes. The study by Pines et al. evaluates the impact of APPs on productivity, safety, and patient flow in 94 EDs across the USA. Findings indicate that APPs handle less complex cases and see fewer patients per hour compared to physicians, with no significant adverse effects on ED flow, safety, or patient experience. Although APPs do not reduce staffing costs due to lower productivity, they enable physicians to focus on higher-acuity cases. The study suggests that while APPs can effectively manage lower-acuity patients, they should not fully replace physician hours, and their integration should be optimized to complement physician roles.

**Case:**
You are the medical director of a medium sized urban emergency department (ED). Volumes have increased over the past few years and you’re considering adding an extra shift or two. Your hospital has asked you to consider adding some advanced practice providers (APPs) instead of physician hours.

**Background:**
Advanced practice providers (APPs), including nurse practitioners (NPs) and physician assistants (PAs), are increasingly utilized in U.S. emergency departments to address staffing needs, primarily due to their lower hourly wages compared to physicians. The economic viability of this model depends on APPs achieving productivity levels comparable to physicians, despite differences in billing rates. However, there is limited data on the comparative productivity, safety, patient flow, and experiences in emergency medicine involving APPs. The American Academy of Emergency Medicine (AAEM) and the American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) have issued position statements and documents regarding the role of APPs in emergency departments. Concerns have been raised about the adequacy of post-graduate training for NPs and PAs in emergency settings, prompting a joint statement from several professional organizations, including AAEM/RSA, ACEP, ACOEP/RSO, CORD, EMRA, and SAEM/RAMS, in September.

**Clinical Point:**
How does the productivity of advanced practice providers compare to emergency physicians and what is its impact on emergency department operations?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Increasing advanced practice provider coverage has minimal effect on emergency department productivity, flow and safety outcomes.

**Case Resolution:**
You continue the discussion with hospital administration, understanding that APP hours need to be added in such a way as to utilize their skillsets best, but not as a full replacement for physician hours. You suggest considering a higher number of APP hours to replace one physician hour.

**Clinical Application:**
APPs can be utilized to “offload” lower acuity cases, while allowing physicians to care for higher acuity patients. Physicians overall had higher levels of productivity, both as measured by patients/hour and RVUs/hour.
