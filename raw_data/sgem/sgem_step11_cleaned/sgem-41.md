**Summary:**
In this clinical scenario, a 66-year-old man with symptoms suggestive of meningitis requires a lumbar puncture (LP) for diagnosis. The document discusses the utility of ultrasound guidance in improving the success rate of LPs and epidural catheterizations. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Shaikh et al. indicate that ultrasound imaging significantly reduces the risk of failed or traumatic LPs, with a relative risk reduction of 79% for failed procedures and 73% for traumatic procedures. The number needed to treat (NNT) to prevent one failed procedure is 16, and for traumatic procedures, it is 17. The use of small-gauge atraumatic needles, reinsertion of the stylet, and patient mobilization post-LP are recommended to decrease post-LP headache risk. Despite the lack of blinding in the studies, which may influence subjective outcomes, the objective benefits of ultrasound in reducing failed and traumatic attempts are clear.

**Case Scenario:**
A 66-year-old man presents with a 48-hour history of fever, lethargy, and headache. No significant past medical history. On physical examination, he has a temperature of 38.8C, GCS 15, stiff neck on flexion, and no rash. Urinalysis and chest x-ray are normal. Laboratory testing reports an elevated white blood cell count with a left shift. You decide he needs a lumbar puncture to check for meningitis.

**Point:**
Can ultrasound be used to improve successful LP attempts?

**Background:**
Post-lumbar puncture (LP) headache is a common complication following a lumbar puncture procedure. To mitigate the risk of this condition, several procedural strategies are recommended. Utilizing small-gauge atraumatic needles can reduce the incidence of headaches by minimizing dural puncture size and subsequent cerebrospinal fluid leakage. Reinserting the stylet before removing the spinal needle helps maintain the integrity of the dural fibers, further decreasing the likelihood of headache. Additionally, mobilizing patients after the completion of the LP may contribute to reducing the risk of post-LP headache. These approaches are integral to improving patient outcomes following lumbar puncture.
