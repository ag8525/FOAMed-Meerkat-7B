**Summary:**
The document discusses the management of acute uncomplicated appendicitis (AUA) in pediatric patients, focusing on the potential for non-operative treatment (N.O.T.) using intravenous antibiotics as an alternative to appendectomy. A meta-analysis by Georgiou et al. suggests that N.O.T. is effective in 97% of cases, with a 14% recurrence rate, but highlights the need for further research to assess long-term outcomes and cost-effectiveness. The study's limitations include variability in antibiotic protocols, diagnostic challenges, and a lack of high-quality randomized controlled trials. Despite the promising results, the document concludes that N.O.T. is not yet ready for widespread clinical application in children, emphasizing the need for more robust evidence before it can replace surgical intervention. The case resolution recommends appendectomy for a 12-year-old patient with AUA, ensuring a quick recovery and return to normal activities.

**Case:**
It is 7 pm on a quiet evening in the emergency department, and Bobby comes in. He’s 12 years old and complains of a belly ache, pointing to his right iliac fossa pain. It has been going on for about 36 hours now, initially peri-umbilical and associated with nausea, poor appetite, and malaise. It has increased in severity and is now localized in his right iliac fossa. He is particularly worried as he is the ice hockey captain. Their hockey team is playing in the regional finals in two weeks’ time. His blood work shows an elevated WBC count, and the ultrasound is consistent with acute uncomplicated appendicitis (AUA).

**Background:**
Appendicitis is the most common pediatric surgical emergency, with a lifetime risk exceeding 7% and a peak incidence in the second decade of life. Acute, uncomplicated appendicitis, if diagnosed promptly, is traditionally treated with an appendectomy, a surgical procedure that has been refined since its initial description in 1886. This approach typically involves a small incision or laparoscopic technique, depending on the patient's gender, to minimize scarring and expedite recovery. Recently, there has been interest in Non Operative Treatment of Appendicitis (N.O.T.A) in adults, which involves the use of intravenous antibiotics to avoid surgery-related risks and facilitate a quicker return to normal activities. However, pediatric surgery is distinct from adult surgery, and treatment approaches in adults may not directly apply to children, highlighting the need for specialized pediatric surgical practices.

**Clinical Point:**
Is non-operative treatment (N.O.T.) for acute uncomplicated appendicitis safe and effective in children?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Non-operative treatment of acute uncomplicated appendicitis is not ready for prime time.

**Case Resolution:**
As the surgeon, I would come and discuss the diagnosis of acute uncomplicated appendicitis with the patient and family. I would recommend appendicectomy and encourage them if all goes well Bobby should recover quickly and he should be back on the ice in time for the finals.

**Clinical Application:**
Non-operative treatment of acute uncomplicated appendicitis is interesting. More and better-quality evidence is needed before applying non-operative treatment in the pediatric population.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
I would tell Bobby and his parents that he has appendicitis. The appendix has not ruptured and he needs to be seen by the surgeon and they will probably recommend taking out his appendix today. If all goes well he should be able to play in the hockey finals.
