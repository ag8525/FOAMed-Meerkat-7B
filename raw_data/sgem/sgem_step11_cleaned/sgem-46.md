**Summary:**
Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS) is characterized by recurrent nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain in patients with long-term cannabis use, often alleviated by hot showers. Diagnosis is supported by major and supportive clinical criteria, as identified in a case series of 98 patients, predominantly young males, with an average age of 32. The condition, first recognized in 2004, highlights the paradoxical effect of chronic cannabis use causing cyclical vomiting. Management may include antiemetics like haloperidol, although evidence is limited to case reports, and cessation of cannabis use is advised. Extensive diagnostic workups are generally unnecessary once CHS is suspected, as highlighted by the BEEM Bottom Line.

**Case Scenario:**
22yo man presents for the third time in a week with vomiting. He was worked up extensively in the previous two visits and no cause was found. Nothing seems to stop the vomiting. He had a similar bout of cyclical vomiting a few months ago. You go to assess him and he is very anxious and all he wants to do is take a hot shower.

**Point:**
How do you diagnose cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome?

**Background:**
Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS) is a medical condition characterized by cyclical vomiting associated with chronic marijuana use. Marijuana, the most widely used illegal drug globally, contains the psychoactive compound tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), which interacts with CB1 receptors in the brain and CB2 receptors in peripheral tissues. While marijuana is used medically to alleviate nausea and vomiting, paradoxically, chronic use can lead to CHS, as first identified by Allen et al. in 2004 and further corroborated by Roche and Foster in 2005. The condition highlights the complex effects of marijuana, which can persist in the body due to THC's lipophilic nature.

**BEEM Bottom Line:**
You may not need to do an extensive work-up in patients with suspected cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome.

**Case Resolution:**
You give him lorazepam 1mg IV and it does not work. You choose wisely and decide not to repeat another extensive/expensive workup. Then you remember a case report about haloperidol and cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome. You give it a try and he stops vomiting. However, you are skeptical with an n=1 and know the cyclic vomiting could have stopped on its own. The young man is discharged home with the advice to stop smoking so much pot.
