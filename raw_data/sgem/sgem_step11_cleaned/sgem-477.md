**Summary:**
The document discusses the optimal preoxygenation strategies for patients with acute hypoxic respiratory failure (AHRF) requiring intubation, focusing on a network meta-analysis comparing various methods. The study highlights that high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) is considered the safest and most effective preoxygenation strategy compared to non-invasive ventilation (NIV) and conventional oxygen therapy (COT), although the evidence is not definitive. The analysis reveals that while NIV shows a meaningful reduction in severe hypoxia during intubation, HFNC is associated with fewer post-intubation complications, though the effect sizes are not statistically significant. The study underscores the need for further research due to limitations such as low evidence levels and lack of a prespecified primary outcome, suggesting that while HFNC and NIV offer advantages over COT, the optimal approach remains uncertain. In clinical practice, the choice of preoxygenation strategy should be tailored to the patient's specific condition, as demonstrated in the case resolution where NIV was chosen for its potential benefits in providing positive pressure.

**Case:**
A 68-year-old woman presents in acute respiratory distress. She is febrile, hypoxemic, and meets criteria for sepsis. A chest x-ray reveals multilobar pneumonia. After managing her sepsis, her oxygenation remains poor, with saturations in the 88-92% range despite supplemental oxygen via a nonrebreather mask, and she now shows signs of worsening fatigue. You determine she requires endotracheal intubation, but note that she is at high risk for peri-intubation complications or even death, and wonder if there is a strategy you can utilize to reduce this risk?

**Background:**
Acute hypoxic respiratory failure (AHRF) is a critical condition characterized by inadequate pulmonary gas exchange, leading to insufficient oxygenation, often resulting from conditions such as pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), sepsis, and chronic lung disease exacerbations. It is clinically significant due to its life-threatening nature and the high risk of profound desaturation during emergency intubation, which occurs in approximately 25% of cases. Preoxygenation is essential in airway management to extend the "safe apnea time" and reduce peri-intubation hypoxia, a predictor of morbidity and mortality. Standard preoxygenation methods include conventional oxygen therapy (COT) with non-rebreather masks, high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC), non-invasive ventilation (NIV), and combinations of these strategies. Despite their widespread use, there is ongoing clinical uncertainty about the optimal preoxygenation approach, prompting numerous randomized controlled trials to determine the most effective and safe strategy for managing AHRF.

**Clinical Point:**

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
While both NIV and HFNC appear to offer advantages over COT, there is a lack of clear data as to which approach is optimal, and further research is needed.
