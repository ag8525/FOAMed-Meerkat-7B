**Summary:**
A study evaluated the efficacy of a 5-minute virtual reality (VR) program, "Take Pause," in reducing situational anxiety among adolescents aged 13-17 in the pediatric emergency department (ED) with mild to moderate acute pain. This prospective, randomized, single-blind trial compared the VR intervention to a passive distraction technique using an iPad with pre-downloaded games. The primary outcome was the change in situational anxiety levels measured by the Spielberger State – Trait Anxiety Inventory (STAI: Y-6 item), with secondary outcomes including changes in pain scores, heart rate, and respiratory rate. Results showed a statistically significant greater reduction in anxiety in the VR group compared to the iPad group, though the clinical significance of this difference was questioned. Both interventions effectively reduced anxiety, suggesting that VR can be a viable non-pharmacological option for anxiety management in pediatric ED settings.

**Case:**
A 15-year-old male presents to the pediatric emergency department (ED) with right ankle pain sustained while twisting his ankle during dance practice. The right ankle is swollen and tender. He rates his pain a 5 on the FACES scale and is awaiting examination by the treating clinician.

**Background:**
Pediatric emergency department visits often cause pain and anxiety in children, and effective pain management is crucial for reducing distress, improving physician rapport, enhancing compliance with discharge instructions, and increasing satisfaction for both patients and caregivers. Despite these benefits, children frequently experience inadequate analgesia, a condition known as oligoanalgesia. Distraction techniques are commonly employed to alleviate pain and anxiety, though there is a lack of prospective randomized trials evaluating the use of virtual reality as a distraction method during these visits.

**Clinical Point:**
Does a 5-minute virtual reality program reduce situational anxiety in the pediatric ED?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The virtual reality **"Take Pause"** immersion tool is another possible non-pharmaceutical intervention to reduce anxiety of pediatric patients in the ED.

**Case Resolution:**
The patient is offered either a mindfulness-based virtual reality program or an iPad with preprogrammed games while awaiting clinician evaluation.

**Clinical Application:**
Reducing anxiety is important in the emergency department. There are several non-pharmacologic techniques that can be used. This small study suggests the VR Take Pause immersion tool can be one of those techniques.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
There are several ways to reduce anxiety in the pediatric emergency department. Both a mindfulness-based virtual reality program and playing games on an iPad may help reduce your anxiety.
