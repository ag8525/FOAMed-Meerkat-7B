**Summary:**
In a randomized clinical trial by Moran et al., the efficacy of cephalexin plus trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) versus cephalexin alone was evaluated for the treatment of uncomplicated cellulitis. The study concluded that the addition of TMP-SMX did not significantly improve clinical cure rates compared to cephalexin alone in patients without systemic signs of infection or risk factors for MRSA. The trial involved 500 participants, with a primary outcome of clinical cure assessed 14-21 days post-enrollment. Secondary outcomes showed no significant differences between the treatment groups, and adverse events were mostly mild. The study suggests that cephalexin alone is sufficient for treating uncomplicated cellulitis in low-risk MRSA patients, although further research may be needed to explore the potential benefits of TMP-SMX in different dosing regimens or patient populations.

**Case:**
A 22-year-old male with no significant past medical history arrives to your department for an area of tender erythema to the right forearm for two days that has grown in size without purulence or drainage. With point of care ultrasound, you diagnose cellulitis without the presence of an abscess affecting a 6cm diameter area. When deciding how to treat for this condition, you have read recently that cephalexin could be used alone and that trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) may not be needed to cover for methicillin-resistant staphylococcus aureus (MRSA).

**Background:**
Cellulitis is a common bacterial skin infection characterized by redness, swelling, and pain, typically caused by Beta-hemolytic streptococci, although MRSA is a frequent cause in purulent skin infections. The condition is clinically significant due to its potential to cause severe complications if untreated. Diagnosis is often challenging as cultures are rarely helpful, necessitating empirical treatment. For uncomplicated cellulitis without systemic infection signs or risk factors for MRSA, the Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) recommends antibiotics targeting streptococci. TMP-SMX may be an alternative to clindamycin for patients without major comorbidities.

**Clinical Point:**
Does cephalexin plus TMP-SMX result in higher clinical cure rates in uncomplicated cellulitis versus cephalexin alone?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
In patients with uncomplicated cellulitis the routine addition of TMP-SMX is not required to improve the clinical cure rate.

**Case Resolution:**
You discuss the situation with the patient further and he seems to be at low risk for MRSA and do not believe that coverage with TMP-SMX is needed. After a careful and detailed discussion, you and the patient decided to use cephalexin alone to treat his cellulitis.

**Clinical Application:**
Cephalexin can be used in patients’ who are low-risk for MRSA on its own but other antibiotics that will cover for strep and staph as well should be considered. This could still be TMP-SMX but other options include clindamycin for single antibiotic coverage. This paper will not change my clinical practice at this time.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
We are going to prescribe you an oral antibiotic called cephalexin for your cellulitis. Sometimes we have to use two oral antibiotics for patients with MRSA. This is a certain type of infection that the regular oral antibiotics do not work. However, you are at low risk for a MRSA infection. If you do not improve or get worse, it could be MRSA and we can add another oral antibiotic called TMP-SMX. Another option if that happens is to switch to another single oral antibiotic called clindamycin that treats MRSA but can give you more diarrhea. Sometimes we even have to use intravenous antibiotics to treat these skin infections. Let’s just see how the cephalexin works and please come back if you are getting worse, not getting better or are worried.
