**Summary:**
The document discusses the use of a topically applied analgesic, specifically lidocaine-epinephrine-tetracaine (LET) gel, to reduce pain in children undergoing laceration repair with tissue adhesive. A randomized controlled trial demonstrated that applying LET gel 45 minutes prior to the procedure significantly decreased pain levels compared to a placebo, with 51.6% of children experiencing no pain versus 28.3% in the placebo group. The study also found improved wound hemostasis in the LET group, although there was no significant difference in the difficulty of repair or follow-up visits. Despite limitations such as lack of intention-to-treat analysis and potential unblinding, the study supports the routine use of LET gel for pain management in pediatric laceration repairs.

**Case Scenario:**
A two-year-old child presents to the emergency department with a simple laceration on his forehead. You decide to use a tissue adhesive rather than sutures.

**Point:**
Does a topically applied analgesic decrease pain in children undergoing laceration repair with tissue adhesive?

**Background:**
Oligoanalgesia is defined as inadequate pain management due to the underuse of analgesics, a significant issue in emergency departments where many patients receive insufficient pain relief. This condition is clinically significant as it affects various patient groups, including children, the elderly, certain ethnicities, individuals with mental illness, and those lacking health insurance, highlighting disparities in healthcare delivery.

**Bottom Line:**
LET gel should be used routinely prior to repairing simple lacerations in children.

**Case Resolution:**
You get the LET out and apply it before using tissue adhesive on this two-year-old with a forehead laceration.

**Clinical Application:**
Use LET in children with lacerations in the emergency department.
