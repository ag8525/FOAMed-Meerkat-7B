**Summary:**
In the context of prehospital trauma resuscitation for patients with hemorrhagic shock, the RePHILL trial evaluated the efficacy of blood products versus saline. The trial, which included 432 participants, found no superiority of prehospital administration of packed red blood cells and lyophilized plasma over saline in terms of mortality or lactate clearance. The primary outcome, a composite of mortality and lactate clearance, showed no significant difference between the groups, and secondary outcomes, including mortality and adverse events, were also similar. The study suggests that while blood products are traditionally favored in trauma resuscitation, their prehospital use does not demonstrate a clear benefit over saline, highlighting the need for further research to identify specific patient populations that might benefit from early blood product administration. The trial's findings emphasize the importance of rapid transport to definitive care and the integration of prehospital and hospital-based trauma management.

**Case:**
You are working in the emergency department (ED) and receive a call from the Advanced Care Paramedics who are at the scene of a stabbing. Apparently, two rival gangs (Jets and Sharks) had a rumble. The young man has been stabbed in the abdomen and lost a lot of blood. The patient is tachycardic (120 beats/minute), hypotensive (80/60 mmHg) and looks very pale. They have two large bore intravenous (IV) access and are planning to bring them to your ED as soon as possible. The paramedic asks you, *“we have saline, and we also have red-cells and this fancy new lyophilised plasma. Should we give our shocked patient saline or plasma / red cells en route to the ED?”* What do you advise him?

**Background:**
Trauma resuscitation involves the use of fluids to manage patients with significant injuries, particularly those at risk for hemorrhagic shock. The COMBAT trial, a randomized study conducted in Denver and published in 2018, investigated the use of plasma in prehospital settings but found no significant mortality benefit within 28 days post-injury. In contrast, the PAMPer trial, also published in 2018, demonstrated that prehospital administration of thawed plasma was safe and associated with reduced 30-day mortality in patients at risk for hemorrhagic shock. Traditional trauma management emphasizes replacing blood with blood products rather than saline alone, suggesting potential benefits from using plasma and blood in initial resuscitation efforts.

**Clinical Point:**
In the resuscitation of pre-hospital trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock is there a patient-oriented benefit to using blood and plasma over 0.9% saline?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
The results of RePHILL do not justify the expense and logistical difficulties associated with the use of lyophilised plasma and red cell use in prehospital ambulance services for adult trauma patients.

**Case Resolution:**
I will advise the prehospital team to use whatever fluid they have and feel is necessary to resuscitate the patient whilst expediting transfer to the ED. A trauma call is put out to activate the lab, surgical teams and radiology in order to provide rapid care to this very sick patient.

**Clinical Application:**
The choice of pre-hospital fluids remains an open Point. We simply do not know if there is a real benefit to early infusion of blood products in the field.
There are certainly some patients who will benefit from the use of blood products – although at this time it seems that we can limit the use of blood products to the ED and hospital phase of care. Further research is needed to define the group patient whom may benefit from earlier blood in the prehospital environment.
Having said that – trauma is a team sport, and having a seamless system of care between the prehospital providers, the ED, and the operating room is crucial.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You have been stabbed and lost a lot of blood. We are going to start some intravenous fluids and get you to the hospital as quick as possible. They will be standing by with a team to help you on arrival.
