**Summary:**
The document discusses the management of acute ischemic stroke, particularly focusing on the role of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) prior to mechanical thrombectomy in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO). Two randomized clinical trials, SKIP and DEVT, are highlighted, comparing endovascular treatment (EVT) alone versus EVT combined with IVT. The SKIP trial, conducted in Japan, found that EVT alone was not non-inferior to EVT plus IVT, with a noted increase in intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) in the combination group. The DEVT trial concluded that EVT alone was non-inferior to EVT plus IVT, with similar findings regarding ICH. Both studies suggest that while EVT is effective, the addition of IVT may not significantly improve outcomes and could increase bleeding risks. The trials emphasize the need for careful consideration of thrombolytic use, especially given the potential for increased adverse events without clear functional benefit.

**Case:**
A 74-year-old woman presents to your emergency department with 1.5 hours of right-sided weakness, aphasia, and neglect. On rapid bedside assessment you calculate the National Institute of Health Stroke Score/Scale (NIHSS) of 11 and a Field Assessment for Stroke Triage for Emergency Destination (FAST-ED) score of 7; you suspect a large vessel occlusion (LVO) based on the high NIHSS and FAST-ED score >3. A non-contrast CT shows no evidence of intracranial hemorrhage. A CT angiogram plus CT perfusion demonstrate a clot in the left proximal middle cerebral artery (MCA) with a small infarcted area and a large penumbra. Based on your institution’s current guidelines, the patient is a candidate for endovascular therapy, but they are also within the current window for the administration of thrombolytics. You wonder if you should give the thrombolytics while waiting for your neurointerventional/endovascular team?

**Background:**
Acute ischemic stroke management has evolved significantly, particularly with the advent of endovascular therapy (EVT) for large vessel occlusion (LVO) strokes, which gained prominence following the MR. CLEAN trial in 2015. This trial demonstrated a 14% absolute risk reduction in achieving good neurologic function, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 7. Subsequent trials, such as SWIFT PRIME, EXTEND-IA, REVASCAT, and ESCAPE, further supported EVT's efficacy, though concerns about bias due to early trial termination persist. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Badhiwala et al. in 2015 indicated a favorable outcome for EVT, but the potential bias from early trial stoppage was noted. Additional trials like DAWN and DEFUSE in 2018 also reported low NNTs but were similarly stopped early. Despite impressive NNTs, the actual benefit may be overstated due to denominator neglect, as a large number of patients must be screened to identify eligible candidates. The role of systemic thrombolytics, such as tPA, remains contentious, with mixed evidence on its efficacy when combined with EVT. A 2020 RCT by Yang P et al. suggested no significant benefit of adding tPA to EVT, indicating that systemic thrombolysis may not be necessary when EVT is available. This ongoing debate highlights the complexity of optimizing stroke management strategies.

**Clinical Point:**
Does intravenous thrombolysis prior to mechanical thrombectomy increase the percentage of patients with LVO who achieve mRS of 0-2 (functional independence) at 90 days?
