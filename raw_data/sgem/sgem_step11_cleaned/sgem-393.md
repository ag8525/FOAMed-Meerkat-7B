**Summary:**
The document discusses the evolving role of advanced practice providers (APPs) in emergency departments (EDs), highlighting their increasing involvement in high-acuity patient care due to physician shortages and rising ED visit volumes. An observational study from 2013 to 2019 showed a significant rise in independently billed high-acuity encounters by APPs, particularly in rural areas, where their billing increased from 7.3% to 16.4%, compared to urban areas, which saw an increase from 4.8% to 8.8%. Despite the American College of Emergency Physicians' guideline against unsupervised APP care, the study indicates that APPs are essential in maintaining ED operations, especially in rural settings. The findings suggest a need for strategic staffing that includes both APPs and emergency medicine physicians to ensure patient safety and effective care delivery.

**Case:**
You are an administrator responsible for staffing emergency departments (EDs) in a health care system comprising both urban and rural locales. The hiring pool includes emergency medicine trained physicians, non-emergency trained physicians, and advanced practice providers (physician assistants and nurse practitioners). Prior to your hiring search, you wonder how many patient encounters are being seen by each type of physician or advanced practice provider. You also wonder the breakdown of visit acuity being seen by the different provider types.

**Background:**
Advanced practice providers (APPs), including physician assistants (PAs) and nurse practitioners (NPs), are increasingly integral to the emergency medicine (EM) workforce, addressing both low and high-acuity patient needs due to rising emergency department (ED) visit volumes and physician shortages. In the U.S., policies now allow APPs more independent practice, though the impact of this shift on patient care quality remains unassessed. The American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) advises against unsupervised APP care in EDs, yet workforce constraints necessitate their involvement to maintain 24/7 operations in some areas. Similarly, Canadian EDs face staffing challenges, with APPs potentially playing a larger role as shortages persist.

**Clinical Point:**
How has the role of APPs in the provision of emergency care changed in recent years?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
APPs are becoming an increasing part of the emergency department workforce in the United States and billing for more high acuity patient encounters.

**Case Resolution:**
You determine that your hiring strategy will include APPs as well as physicians. APPs will be providing critical care and be seeing high acuity patients. You use this information to balance hiring of APPs and EM physicians in your urban and rural sites.

**What Do I Tell the Staff?**
We are looking how to safely staff our urban and rural emergency departments. This will be a difficult process and adapt over time. We need to ensure that patients get the right care, by the right clinician. Their safety is a top priority, and we are all on “Team Patient”. A variety of metrics will be followed to monitor this implementation and ensure we get the right balance of APPs and physicians.
