**Summary:**
Fatigue among emergency physicians is a significant concern, impacting both personal health and patient care. A study by Fowler et al. assessed fatigue in emergency physicians using actigraphy to measure sleep quality, duration, and efficiency, revealing that physicians were fatigued for nearly 24% of their shifts. The study highlighted that night shifts were particularly associated with higher fatigue scores. Despite the limited sample size, the findings underscore the need for system-level interventions to mitigate fatigue and its potential impact on clinical performance. The study calls for further research to explore effective strategies to reduce fatigue and improve outcomes for both physicians and patients.

**Case:**
You arrive at 7am to relieve your colleague after a night shift. You find her at the desk, asleep with her face on the keyboard, patient documentation half finished. As she tries to wipe the drool out of the keyboard, you ask how she has been sleeping recently. She confides that she is worried that fatigue might be impacting her care of patients.

**Background:**
Shift work, particularly among emergency physicians, is associated with significant health risks and impacts on clinical performance due to poor sleep. This condition is linked to increased rates of cancer, cardiovascular disease, and accidents, with fatigue affecting patient care by impairing cognitive function and increasing error rates. Studies suggest that fatigue can impair performance to a degree comparable to alcohol intoxication, with industrial data showing a 30-50% increase in errors during night shifts. Although research in medicine is limited, existing studies highlight the connection between fatigue and clinical errors, reduced empathy, and interpersonal conflict, underscoring the critical need for addressing sleep-related issues in healthcare settings.

**Clinical Point:**
What is the percentage of time that emergency physicians spend in a fatigued state?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Fatigue is an issue for many emergency physicians, and perhaps an issue many of us still need to take more seriously, for our own sakes and for our patients.

**Case Resolution:**
You listen compassionately to your colleague but realize that 7am after a night shift is not a great time for further discussion, so take handover as quickly as possible to get her home to rest. However, you do set up departmental rounds to discuss strategies to improve sleep quality and manage the difficulties of shift work.

**Clinical Application:**
Part of being an emergency clinician is acknowledging the impact that fatigue has on our performance, and consciously working to minimize that risk.

**What Do I Tell Patients?**
I am honestly not sure if patients want to know exactly how bad my brain is working at 4 am. Luckily for this podcast, I don’t have a patient, and so can leave that tricky discussion for another time.
