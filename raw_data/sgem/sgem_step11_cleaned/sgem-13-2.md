**Summary:**
In the management of simple cutaneous abscesses (SCA), routine packing post-incision and drainage (I&D) is likely unnecessary, as suggested by a single-center randomized controlled trial. The study involved 48 patients, with 23 receiving packing and 25 without, and found no significant difference in the need for further intervention at 48 hours between the two groups. The trial challenges the traditional practice of packing, indicating that it may not contribute to improved outcomes and can be painful. The study's limitations include potential biases and lack of comprehensive prognostic data, but it encourages reconsideration of standard practices in emergency medicine. Current recommendations for SCA management emphasize buffered lidocaine for analgesia, I&D without routine antibiotics, and avoiding packing, with follow-up for wound evaluation.

**Case:**
Healthy 45-year-old man presents with a 3cm abscess under left arm. There is no surrounding cellulitis. He is not an intravenous drug user and never had an abscess before. You make the diagnosis of an uncomplicated superficial cutaneous abscess. You know that antibiotics are probably not necessary after incision and drainage (I&D). However, the patient gets nervous after you describe the I&D process. He wants to know if you can do anything to make this procedure less painful and if packing is really necessary?

**Background:**
Simple cutaneous abscesses (SCA) are localized infections characterized by a collection of pus within the skin, frequently presenting in emergency departments. In 2005, there were 3.3 million emergency department visits for SCA, with the incidence rising more rapidly than the overall number of emergency department visits.

**Point:**
Is routine packing of simple cutaneous abscesses necessary after an I&D?

**Bottom Line:**
Routine packing of simple cutaneous abscesses may not be necessary.

**Summary for I&D of Simple Cutaneous Abscesses:**
- Buffer your lidocaine
- I&D is the solution (better out than in) and not routine antibiotics
- No packing required

**Case Resolution:**
You try to address the problem of oligoanalgesia in the ED by buffering your lidocaine. You have an informed discussion about antibiotics and make the mutual decision not to subject him to the harm with limited support for benefit. You explain the incomplete data doesn’t support packing and duh? he decides to forego wound packing. You discharge him with instructions to return in 2-days for a wound re-evaluation. At the same time, you wonder whether someday routine follow-up might be proven not to be necessary too.
