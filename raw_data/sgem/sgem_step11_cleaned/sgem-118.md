**Summary:**
The document discusses the use of age-adjusted D-dimer thresholds to rule out pulmonary embolism (PE) in non-high risk emergency department patients, particularly in older adults. Traditional D-dimer testing has a high false positive rate, especially in the elderly, due to naturally increasing D-dimer levels with age. The age-adjusted D-dimer, calculated as the patient's age multiplied by 10, has been shown to increase specificity without compromising sensitivity, thereby reducing unnecessary imaging and over-testing. The ADJUST-PE study demonstrated that using this method increased the proportion of patients in whom PE could be safely excluded from 28.2% to 39.8%, with a low subsequent risk of venous thromboembolism. This approach is recommended for integration into clinical practice to enhance diagnostic efficiency and reduce the burden of over-testing in geriatric populations.

**Case:**
An 84-year-old woman presents to the ED with vague symptoms of shortness of breath and mild chest discomfort for the last two days. She does not say “pain” but rather just an ache when taking a deep breath. Her past medical history includes hypertension, dyslipidemia, type-2 diabetes, and osteoarthritis. Her vital signs are normal. She is Well’s low but PERC positive due to her age. You do not want to order a D-dimer because of the high false positive rate but pulmonary embolism (PE) is on your differential.

**Background:**
Pulmonary embolism (PE) can be safely ruled out in non-high-risk patients using a negative D-dimer test, which is highly sensitive but has low specificity, leading to a high false positive rate that increases with age due to naturally rising D-dimer levels. This results in excessive diagnostic imaging in older patients, contributing to a global issue of over-testing, over-diagnosis, and over-treatment, driven by incomplete diagnostic evidence, malpractice concerns, and the challenge for clinicians to stay updated with research. To address this, age-adjusted D-dimer thresholds have been proposed, with Douma et al. (2010) suggesting a cut-off of the patient's age multiplied by 10 for those over 50, which increases the proportion of older patients in whom PE can be safely excluded. This approach was validated by studies from van Es et al. (2011) and Penaloza et al. (2012), and a systematic review by Schouten et al. (2013) confirmed that age-adjusted D-dimer values enhance specificity without affecting sensitivity, thereby improving the test's clinical utility in patients aged 50 or older with a non-high clinical probability of PE.

**Clinical Point:**
Can an age-adjusted D-dimer threshold safely exclude PE in non-high risk ED patients with suspected PE?

**Clinical Application:**
This is ready for prime time. We should incorporate age-adjusted D-dimer into medical education, continuing medical education, electronic medical record protocols, and patient shared decision-making instruments.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
Blood clots in the lung are called pulmonary embolisms. They can be hard to diagnose sometimes. We have a blood test for people like you who are not at high risk. We adjust the test based on your age. If the test is below your age-adjusted level you most likely do not have a blood clot in your lung and no further testing is needed. If the test is above your age-adjusted level we will need to do a CT scan to check for a blood clot.
