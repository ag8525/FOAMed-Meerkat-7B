**Summary:**
A 22-year-old female with a history of oral contraceptive use and recent immobilization presents with bilateral pulmonary embolism (PE) and right ventricular dilatation, confirmed by CTPA, and elevated troponin levels. Current guidelines suggest systemic thrombolysis for acute PE with hypotension, but this patient is normotensive. The PEITHO trial indicates that systemic thrombolysis in intermediate-risk PE does not improve long-term mortality or morbidity and increases the risk of major bleeding. The patient was managed with low molecular weight heparin and monitored for potential thrombolysis if clinical deterioration occurred. The clinical recommendation is to reserve thrombolysis for hypotensive patients or within clinical trials, as it does not confer long-term survival benefits in submassive PE.

**Case:**
A 22-year-old patient presents to the emergency department with sudden onset shortness of breath. She takes the oral contraceptive pill and was placed in a below-knee plaster cast for a fibula fracture 2 weeks ago. She is alert and talking, with a systolic blood pressure of 110 mmHg, but CTPA demonstrates bilateral pulmonary artery thrombus with right ventricular dilatation, and her troponin is raised. When you explain this, she asks if there isn’t something she could have to break up the clot.

**Background:**
Acute pulmonary embolism (PE) associated with hypotension, defined as a systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, is a critical condition where systemic thrombolytic therapy is recommended for patients without a high risk of bleeding, according to the 2015 Chest Guidelines. This approach is preferred over no thrombolytic therapy (Grade 2B) and is suggested to be administered via a peripheral vein rather than catheter-directed thrombolysis (CDT) (Grade 2C), unless the patient has a higher bleeding risk and access to CDT expertise. Clinical trials, such as MAPPET-3 and MOPETT, indicate that systemic thrombolytics can reduce clinical decompensation and long-term pulmonary hypertension. The PEITHO trial demonstrated that fibrinolytic therapy in intermediate-risk PE patients prevents hemodynamic decompensation but increases the risk of major bleeding and stroke, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 33 to prevent death or hemodynamic decompensation within 7 days, and a number needed to harm (NNH) of 11 for major bleeding.

**Clinical Point:**
Does systemic thrombolysis in patients with submassive PE improve long-term mortality or morbidity?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
Systemic thrombolysis in submassive PE cannot be recommended to reduce long-term mortality or morbidity at this time.

**Case Resolution:**
The patient was treated with low molecular weight heparin, and admitted to a high care area so that thrombolysis could be reconsidered if she deteriorated.

**Clinical Application:**
In patients with submassive PE, treat with low molecular weight heparin, unless you can recruit them to a well-designed trial of thrombolysis.

**What do I tell my patient?**
Although it sounds sensible to give you a drug to break up the clot, the evidence we have at the moment is that it will not improve your survival or how well you are in the long term. We may need to reconsider that if your blood pressure drops.
