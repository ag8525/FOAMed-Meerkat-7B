**Summary:**
In the context of asymptomatic coronary artery disease (CAD), the document discusses the management approach for stable CAD, highlighting a meta-analysis comparing initial coronary stent implantation with medical therapy alone. The analysis, which included 7,229 patients across eight trials, concluded that stenting offers no additional benefit over medical therapy in preventing death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, unplanned revascularization, or angina. Despite the widespread use of percutaneous coronary interventions (PCI), the procedure carries risks such as stroke, kidney failure, heart attack, and death, with an estimated 2% complication rate. The document also touches on the concept of "VIP Syndrome," where the care of high-profile patients, like the former President who underwent stenting, may deviate from standard practice, potentially leading to suboptimal outcomes.

**Case Scenario:**
67-year-old former President is getting his annual physical. He is known to be very physically fit and active. There is no suggestion of symptomatic coronary artery disease (CAD) suggested.

**Point:**
What is the best treatment for asymptomatic CAD?

**Background:**
Heart disease, particularly coronary artery disease (CAD), is the leading cause of death in the United States, responsible for approximately 600,000 deaths annually. CAD affects about 6% of the American population, highlighting its significant clinical impact.

**Case Resolution:**
President Bush had an angiogram and stent placed.
