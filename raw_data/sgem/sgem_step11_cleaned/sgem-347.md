**Summary:**
In critically ill patients, particularly those at risk for acute kidney injury, the choice between balanced crystalloids (such as Plasmalyte 148) and 0.9% saline for fluid resuscitation has been debated. The BaSICS trial, a large, double-blind, multi-center randomized study, found no significant difference in 90-day mortality between patients receiving balanced solutions versus saline. Despite some secondary outcomes indicating potential harm with balanced solutions, these findings were considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. The study suggests that in ICU settings, the type of crystalloid solution and infusion rate may not significantly impact mortality outcomes, especially when administered in modest volumes. This aligns with the clinical application that, in emergency settings, the choice of crystalloid may not critically affect short-term survival, allowing for flexibility based on availability and clinical judgment.

**Case:**
A 66-year-old woman is brought in by EMS from home with lethargy and hypotension. Chest x-ray is clear, labs are remarkable for a leukocytosis of 16,000 with left shift; exam is notable for left flank pain and costovertebral tenderness. Straight catheter urinalysis is grossly cloudy, and pyuria is present on microscopy. Blood pressure is 85/50 mm Hg. You wonder which intravenous (IV) fluid should you order?

**Background:**
The choice of intravenous (IV) solutions for critically ill patients, including those with trauma or sepsis, remains controversial, particularly regarding the use of normal saline versus balanced crystalloids like Lactated Ringer’s or Plasmalyte 148. Normal saline is a hypertonic acidotic fluid, and its use has been questioned due to potential adverse effects. Recent studies, including the SMART and SALT-ED trials, have suggested that balanced crystalloids may reduce the risk of major adverse kidney events within 30 days (MAKE-30) compared to saline. However, these trials, which were unblinded and single-center, did not show statistical differences in individual components of the composite outcomes, such as in-hospital death, new renal replacement therapy, or significant increases in creatinine levels. The BaSICS trial is a large, double-blind, multi-center randomized trial designed to further investigate whether balanced solutions offer superior outcomes compared to saline in critically ill patients.

**Clinical Point:**
Does administration of a balanced solution (Plasma-Lyte 148) during intensive care unit (ICU) stay, compared with saline solution, result in improved 90-day survival in critically ill patients?

**SGEM Bottom Line:**
In adult ICU patients at risk for kidney injury, administering modest volumes of Plasmalyte 148 versus normal saline, at fast or slow infusion rates, did not influence 90-day mortality.

**Case Resolution:**
You only have ready access to 0.9% saline in your emergency department. You administer a total of one liter before the patient is transferred to the ICU.

**Clinical Application:**
When resuscitating critically ill patients in the emergency department, the type of crystalloid and rapidity of infusion do not likely influence 90-day mortality, at least at low total volumes administered.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You have a severe urine infection. We are going to give you some IV antibiotics to treat the infection. Your blood pressure is low and that can be dangerous. We are going to also give you some IV fluids to bring your blood pressure up. You will be admitted to the intensive care unit where they can continue the treatment and watch you closely.
