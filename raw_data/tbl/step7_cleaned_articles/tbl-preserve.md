## Summary

The PRESERVE trial investigated whether sodium bicarbonate and acetylcysteine could prevent major adverse events and acute kidney injury in high-risk patients undergoing angiography. This multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study included 5177 patients with impaired kidney function. The primary outcome was a composite of death, dialysis, or a significant increase in creatinine levels, assessed 90-104 days post-procedure. Results showed no significant benefit of sodium bicarbonate or acetylcysteine over placebo in reducing these outcomes. The study concluded that these agents do not prevent major adverse events or contrast-associated nephropathy, supporting the current practice of not using them prophylactically in this context.

## Clinical Point

In high-risk patients undergoing angiography, does the administration of bicarbonate and/or acetylcysteine, compared with placebo, prevent major adverse events and acute kidney injury?

## Background

Contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) is defined as renal impairment occurring within 48 hours of intravascular radiographic contrast material administration, not attributable to other causes. CIN is a significant clinical issue, potentially leading to death, long-term dialysis, and increased healthcare costs. Despite widespread use, high-quality evidence supporting the benefit of intravenous sodium bicarbonate and oral acetylcysteine in preventing CIN is lacking. Previous studies, including the Acetylcysteine for Contrast-Induced Nephropathy Trial (ACT), found no benefit, and the association between modern intravenous contrast and nephropathy remains controversial.

## Bottom Line

In a predominantly male, high-risk population undergoing angiography, sodium bicarbonate and/or acetylcysteine did not prevent major adverse events. The incidence of contrast-associated nephropathy was low, supporting current practice of not administering these agents before contrast administration.
