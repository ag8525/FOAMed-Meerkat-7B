## Summary

The PROWESS-SHOCK trial investigated the efficacy of Activated Protein C (drotrecogin alfa or DrotAA) in reducing mortality among patients with septic shock. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study, conducted across 208 sites globally, found no significant difference in 28-day or 90-day mortality rates between the DrotAA and placebo groups. The trial highlighted a higher incidence of non-serious bleeding events in the DrotAA group. Despite targeting severely ill patients, the study concluded that DrotAA does not improve survival outcomes in septic shock, leading to its withdrawal from the market. The trial's strengths included its robust design and adaptive recruitment strategy, while weaknesses involved potential bias due to manufacturer funding and unexplained low recruitment rates for severe sepsis.

## Clinical Point

In patients with septic shock, does Activated Protein C (drotrecogin alfa or DrotAA) compared with placebo reduce mortality?

## Bottom Line

DrotAA does not reduce mortality in patients with septic shock and is no longer available as the drug has been withdrawn.
