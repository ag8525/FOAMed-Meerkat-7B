## Summary

The SCARLET trial investigated the effect of recombinant human soluble thrombomodulin (rhsTM) on 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis-associated coagulopathy. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III trial included 800 patients across 319 sites in 27 countries. Despite the potential efficacy suggested by a prior Phase 2b study, rhsTM did not significantly reduce mortality compared to placebo, with a 2.55% absolute risk reduction that was not statistically significant. The trial highlighted lower coagulation markers in the rhsTM group but also noted similar rates of serious adverse events and major bleeding between groups. The study's strengths included its robust design and international scope, while weaknesses involved a heterogeneous patient population and a sample size potentially insufficient to detect smaller mortality differences. The findings do not support rhsTM use in this context, underscoring the need for further research.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with sepsis-associated coagulopathy, does the administration of human recombinant thrombomodulin (rhsTM) compared to placebo reduce 28-day mortality?

## Background

Sepsis-associated coagulopathy is linked to increased hospital mortality. Previous trials with Activated Protein C (Drotrecogin Alfa) have been controversial. ART-123, a recombinant human soluble thrombomodulin, acts as a co-factor with thrombin in activating Protein C. A Phase 2b study suggested safety and potential efficacy of rhsTM.

## Bottom Line

This study does not support the use of rhsTM in sepsis-associated coagulopathy. Continued research is needed due to the association with hospital mortality.
