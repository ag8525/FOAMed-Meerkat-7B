## Summary

The HOT-ER trial investigated whether humidified high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) reduces the need for advanced respiratory support compared to standard oxygen therapy in hypoxic adults in the emergency department. Conducted as a single-centre, randomised controlled trial in New Zealand, it found no significant difference in conversion rates to non-invasive or invasive ventilation between HFNO and standard oxygen therapy. Secondary outcomes showed higher intolerance to HFNO and no differences in length of stay or 90-day mortality. The study was underpowered, with methodological limitations such as non-blinding and significant loss-to-follow-up, suggesting the need for a larger trial to draw definitive conclusions.

## Clinical Point

In hypoxic adults presenting to the emergency department, does nasal high-flow oxygen compared to standard oxygen therapy reduce the need for advanced respiratory support?

## Bottom Line

This trial did not demonstrate a difference between nasal high-flow oxygen and standard oxygen therapy in hypoxic and dyspneic patients in the emergency department. Methodological weaknesses mean no firm conclusion can be drawn, and a false negative result may have occurred.
