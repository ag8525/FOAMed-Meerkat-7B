## Summary

The study investigates the impact of prehospital epinephrine administration on patients experiencing out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, revealing that while epinephrine significantly increases the likelihood of return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) before hospital arrival, it is associated with decreased survival at one month and poorer neurological outcomes. Conducted as a large, prospective, non-randomized cohort study in Japan, the research highlights the potential drawbacks of adrenaline use, despite its historical inclusion in cardiac arrest management protocols. The study's strengths include its large sample size and consistent findings across analytical models, though it is limited by its non-randomized design and lack of data on in-hospital CPR and adrenaline dosing. The findings suggest that if adrenaline were a new drug, its inclusion in guidelines might be reconsidered due to the adverse outcomes associated with its use.

## Clinical Point

In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, is the use of adrenaline associated with improved mortality compared to not using adrenaline?

## Bottom Line

This study found an association between adrenaline use in cardiac arrest and decreased survival and poor neurological outcomes. Despite being non-randomised, the large sample size warrants consideration. If adrenaline were a new drug, it might not be included in guidelines, but it remains due to historical use.
