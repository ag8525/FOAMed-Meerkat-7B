## Summary

The ACORN trial compared the effects of cefepime and piperacillin-tazobactam (pip-taz) on acute kidney injury (AKI) and mortality in acutely ill adults in the ED or ICU. This pragmatic, open-label, randomized trial found no significant difference in the incidence of AKI or death by day 14 between the two groups, with an odds ratio of 0.95. However, cefepime was associated with more neurological dysfunction, including delirium and coma. The study's strengths include its large sample size and robust randomization process, though its single-center design and unblinded nature may limit generalizability. Clinically, pip-taz is preferred unless cefepime is specifically indicated, with careful monitoring for neurotoxicity when using cefepime.

## Clinical Point

In acutely ill adults in the ED or ICU, does the administration of cefepime or piperacillin-tazobactam result in an increased incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) or death by day 14?

## Background

- Sepsis is a common reason for ICU admission, necessitating frequent antibiotic use.
- Anti-pseudomonal antibiotics are often recommended, but concerns exist about pip-taz and renal dysfunction, and cefepime and neurotoxicity.
- Limited randomized trials evaluate antibiotic-associated harm.

## Bottom Line

In practice, pip-taz is preferred unless specific indications for cefepime exist. When using cefepime, monitor for potential neurotoxicity, especially in critically ill patients.
