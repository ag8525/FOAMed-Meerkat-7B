## Summary

The study evaluates the effectiveness of three ultrasound-guided transducer orientation approaches—short-axis (SAX), long-axis (LAX), and oblique-axis (OAX)—for internal jugular venous cannulation. Conducted in a single Spanish hospital, the randomized trial involved 220 patients and found that both OAX and SAX approaches had higher first needle pass success rates compared to LAX, with OAX showing a significant advantage over LAX (OR 3.7). While SAX was quicker, it had a higher incidence of mechanical complications, notably posterior wall puncture. The study concludes that OAX is a safe and effective method, combining the benefits of SAX and LAX, though it necessitates further training and evaluation.

## Clinical Point

In patients undergoing internal jugular cannulation under ultrasound guidance, does the use of an oblique approach compared to short- and long-axis approaches increase success rate and decrease complications?

## Bottom Line

This study supports that ultrasound-guided IJV cannulation is quicker and technically less demanding in SAX compared to LAX but carries a higher risk of complications. OAX combines advantages of SAX and LAX but requires further evaluation and training.
