## Summary

The HALT-IT trial investigated the efficacy of high-dose tranexamic acid (TXA) in reducing mortality from acute gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding compared to placebo. Despite previous suggestions of potential benefits, this large, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial involving 12,009 patients across 164 hospitals found no significant difference in death due to bleeding within 5 days between the TXA and placebo groups. Secondary outcomes, including all-cause mortality, re-bleeding rates, and thromboembolic events, also showed no significant differences. The study's strengths include its pragmatic design, high validity, and comprehensive international scope, though it faced limitations such as late amendments to primary outcomes and population heterogeneity. The authors concluded that TXA should not be used as a standard treatment for GI bleeding, and further research is needed to explore its side effect profile.

## Clinical Point

In patients with gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, does high-dose tranexamic acid (TXA) compared to placebo reduce mortality from GI bleeding?

## Background

- GI bleeding mortality can be as high as 10%, with a fourfold increase in risk of death if re-bleeding occurs.
- TXA, an anti-fibrinolytic, has shown benefits in early administration for traumatic and postpartum hemorrhage.
- A 2012 Cochrane Review suggested a potential mortality reduction in upper GI bleeding with TXA, but recommended further trials due to validity concerns.

## Bottom Line

This large, well-designed trial indicates no benefit of TXA in GI bleeding management. Future trials (e.g., PATCH) will further explore TXA's side effect profile.
