## Summary

The RePHILL trial investigated whether prehospital administration of packed red blood cells (PRBC) and lyophilised plasma (LyoPlas) in patients with trauma-related haemorrhagic shock improves outcomes compared to normal saline. Conducted as a multicentre, open-label RCT across four UK prehospital critical care services, the study included 432 participants with traumatic injury and hypotension. The primary outcome, a composite of mortality and failure to achieve lactate clearance, showed no significant difference between the PRBC-LyoPlas group and the saline group. Secondary outcomes also revealed no significant differences in mortality, lactate clearance, or vital signs, although the intervention group had higher haemoglobin levels upon emergency department arrival. The trial concluded that prehospital transfusion with PRBC and LyoPlas did not improve patient outcomes, highlighting the need for further research to identify specific patient groups that might benefit from such interventions.

## Clinical Point

In patients with trauma-related haemorrhagic shock, does prehospital administration of packed red cells and lyophilised plasma (LyoPlas) compared with normal saline reduce the composite outcome of mortality and failure to reach lactate clearance?

## Background

- Early transfusion in traumatic haemorrhagic shock is expanding from military to civilian settings.
- Evidence on prehospital transfusion benefits is limited and of low quality.
- Previous RCTs (PAMPER and COMBAT) showed discordant results on prehospital plasma transfusion.
- The PROPPR trial (2015) showed no significant mortality difference in hospital settings but reduced death from exsanguination with a 1:1:1 plasma, platelets, and RBCs ratio.

## Bottom Line

The logistical challenges and costs of carrying packed cells and LyoPlas for prehospital administration in trauma-related haemorrhagic shock are substantial. In this trial, prehospital transfusion did not benefit patient outcomes. Further trials are needed to identify specific patient groups that may benefit.
