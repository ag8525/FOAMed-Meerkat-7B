## Summary

This cluster-randomized, crossover trial compared the use of Lactated Ringer’s (LR) solution versus Normal Saline (NS) in hospitalized patients to assess the impact on death or readmission within 90 days. Conducted across seven hospitals in Ontario, the study included 43,626 patients and found no significant difference in the primary outcome, with death or readmission rates of 20.3% for LR and 21.4% for NS. Secondary outcomes, including mortality, readmission, dialysis initiation, and length of stay, also showed no significant differences. Despite the large sample size and comprehensive data collection, the trial's early cessation and limited geographic scope may affect generalizability. The findings suggest no compelling evidence to alter current fluid management practices, though LR showed a non-significant trend towards better outcomes. Further research is recommended to explore strategies for reducing hospital fluid usage.

## Clinical Point

In hospitalised patients, does the use of Lactated Ringer’s (LR) versus Normal Saline (NS) lower the incidence of death or readmission to the hospital within 90 days after the index admission?

## Background

Fluid choice between balanced solutions and NS has been widely studied, with mixed results. Concerns exist that the chloride load with NS may impair renal perfusion, leading to adverse outcomes. Previous studies like SMART showed decreased rates of major adverse kidney events with balanced solutions, but little work has assessed fluid choice outside the ICU and ED.

## Bottom Line

While the use of LR did not significantly reduce mortality or readmissions within 90 days, point estimates favored LR. The trial does not provide compelling evidence to change current practice. Further work on strategies to reduce overall hospital fluid usage is recommended.
