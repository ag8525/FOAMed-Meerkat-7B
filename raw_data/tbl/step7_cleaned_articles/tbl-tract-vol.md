## Summary

The study investigated whether a 30 ml/kg blood transfusion is superior to a 20 ml/kg transfusion in reducing 28-day mortality among African children with severe anaemia. Conducted as an open-label, multi-centre, randomised trial across hospitals in Uganda and Malawi, the study included children aged 2 months to 12 years with severe anaemia, excluding those with chronic diseases or prior transfusions. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in 28-day mortality between the two transfusion volumes, with a hazard ratio of 0.76 (p = 0.12). Secondary outcomes indicated shorter hospital stays and fewer repeat transfusions in the 30 ml/kg group, but no difference in adverse events or long-term mortality. The study concluded that increasing transfusion volume does not improve mortality outcomes, suggesting no change in current WHO guidelines.

## Clinical Point

In African children presenting to hospital with severe anaemia, is a blood transfusion volume of 30 ml/kg superior to 20 ml/kg in reducing 28-day mortality?

## Background

- Severe anaemia is a major cause of death and morbidity in sub-Saharan African children.
- Blood products are scarce in most African countries.
- WHO guidelines recommend a 20 ml/kg blood transfusion for children with profound anaemia (<4 g/dL) or life-threatening severe anaemia (4 to 6 g/dL).
- This study investigates whether a 30 ml/kg transfusion is superior to 20 ml/kg.

## Bottom Line

A transfusion volume one-third higher than WHO recommendations did not reduce 28-day mortality in sub-Saharan African children with anaemia. Current practice should not change based on this data.
