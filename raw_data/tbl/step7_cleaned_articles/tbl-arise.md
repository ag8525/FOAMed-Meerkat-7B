## Summary

The ARISE trial evaluated the efficacy of early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) versus standard care in adult patients with septic shock, focusing on 90-day mortality outcomes. Conducted across multiple centers, the study included 1600 patients who met specific criteria for septic shock, such as refractory hypotension or hypoperfusion. The EGDT group received a structured treatment protocol involving continuous ScvO2 monitoring and targeted interventions, while the control group received standard care without ScvO2 measurement. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in 90-day mortality between the EGDT and usual care groups (18.6% vs. 18.8%, respectively). Secondary outcomes also revealed no substantial differences in hospital or ICU length of stay, although vasopressor use was higher in the EGDT group. The study concluded that EGDT does not reduce mortality in septic shock, emphasizing the importance of high-quality sepsis care, including early recognition and appropriate management.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with septic shock, does early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) compared with standard therapy reduce mortality at 90 days?

## Bottom Line

EGDT, including continuous central venous co-oximetry and liberal blood transfusion, does not reduce mortality in septic shock. High standards of sepsis care, including early recognition, source control, antimicrobial therapy, fluid resuscitation, and escalation, remain essential.
