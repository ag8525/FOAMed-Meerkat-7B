## Summary

The ACTT-1 trial investigated the efficacy of remdesivir in hospitalized adults with COVID-19 and lower respiratory tract infection, comparing it to a placebo. This double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial demonstrated that remdesivir significantly reduced the median time to recovery from 15 to 11 days, particularly in patients requiring supplemental oxygen. Despite these findings, there was no significant difference in mortality rates between the remdesivir and placebo groups. The study's strengths include its multinational design and stratified randomization, while limitations involve changes to the primary outcome during the study, potential blinding issues, and lack of long-term outcome data. Overall, remdesivir may benefit recovery time, but its clinical relevance remains uncertain due to these limitations.

## Clinical Point

In hospitalised adult patients with evidence of lower respiratory tract infection due to Covid-19, is remdesivir superior to placebo in shortening the time to recovery?

## Background

- SARS-CoV-2 identified in December 2019, responsible for a global pandemic.
- No effective interventions other than supportive care for hospitalised Covid-19 patients.
- Remdesivir, an antiviral, inhibits viral RNA polymerase, reducing viral RNA production.
- Shown activity against SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV in animal models.
- Previous RCT found no difference in time to clinical improvement but was stopped early.

## Bottom Line

Remdesivir may shorten recovery time in hospitalised Covid-19 patients, especially those needing oxygen. Clinical relevance uncertain due to study limitations and lack of long-term data.
