## Summary

The Manchester Acute Coronary Syndromes (MACS) Decision Rule is designed to aid in the rapid exclusion of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in patients presenting with suspected cardiac chest pain in the Emergency Department. This rule was derived and validated through prospective observational diagnostic cohort studies, incorporating eight key variables such as high sensitivity troponin T and ECG ischaemia. The studies demonstrated high sensitivity (99.4% in derivation and 98% in validation) and a low negative likelihood ratio, indicating the rule's effectiveness in identifying low-risk patients who could be safely discharged. The MACS rule also effectively identified high-risk patients, with a 100% MACE rate in the derivation study. Despite its strengths, including validation in a separate center and minimal follow-up loss, the study's limitations include its single-center design and moderate sample size. A randomized trial comparing the MACS rule with standard treatment is anticipated to further validate its clinical utility.

## Clinical Point

Can a clinical decision rule be derived and validated for use in the Emergency Department that allows immediate exclusion of acute coronary syndrome (ACS)?

## Bottom Line

The MACS decision rule shows promise in identifying patients with suspected cardiac chest pain who can be safely discharged. A randomised trial comparing the MACS rule with standard treatment is planned, and results are eagerly awaited.
