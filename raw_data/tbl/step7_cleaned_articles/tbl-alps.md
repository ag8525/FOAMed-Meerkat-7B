## Summary

The ALPS trial investigated the efficacy of amiodarone and lidocaine compared to placebo in adult patients experiencing nontraumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest with shock-refractory ventricular fibrillation or pulseless ventricular tachycardia. This double-blind, randomized controlled trial included 3026 patients across 55 EMS agencies in North America. The primary outcome, survival to hospital discharge, showed no statistically significant improvement with amiodarone or lidocaine over placebo, although amiodarone demonstrated a non-significant 3% increase in survival. Secondary outcomes included survival with favorable neurological outcomes, which were also not significantly different. Subgroup analysis suggested potential benefits in witnessed arrests, particularly with amiodarone. Despite these findings, the study concluded that current cardiac arrest protocols should remain unchanged, and amiodarone should continue to be used for VF/pVT in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with nontraumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and shock-refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) or pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT), does amiodarone or lidocaine, along with standard care, improve survival and neurological outcomes compared to placebo?

## Bottom Line

Amiodarone did not show a statistically significant benefit over placebo in refractory VF/pVT in OHCA. However, the 3% difference in survival to discharge may be clinically important. Amiodarone and lidocaine were superior in witnessed OHCA, but caution is advised with subgroup analysis. Current arrest protocols should not change, and amiodarone should continue to be used for VF/pVT in OHCA.
