## Summary

This meta-analysis evaluates the impact of thrombolysis in addition to anticoagulation on mortality and bleeding complications in patients with pulmonary embolism (PE). The study found that thrombolysis significantly reduces all-cause mortality (OR: "0.53) and recurrent PE (OR: 0.40) compared to anticoagulation alone, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 59 for mortality. However, thrombolysis is associated with a higher risk of major bleeding (OR: 2.73) and intracranial hemorrhage (OR: 4.63), with a number needed to harm (NNH) of 18 for major bleeding. Subgroup analysis indicates that thrombolysis does not increase major bleeding risk in patients aged ≤65 years. The findings suggest that thrombolysis may be beneficial in reducing mortality in intermediate-risk PE patients, but the increased bleeding risk necessitates careful patient selection."

## Clinical Point

In patients with pulmonary embolism (PE), does the addition of thrombolysis to anticoagulation, compared with anticoagulation alone, affect mortality and bleeding complications?

## Bottom Line

Thrombolysis for PE is associated with lower all-cause mortality compared to anticoagulation, especially in intermediate-risk PE, despite increased major bleeding risk. For patients ≤65 years, thrombolysis does not increase major bleeding risk. The meta-analysis supports thrombolysis use in submassive PE, considering individual patient risk factors.
