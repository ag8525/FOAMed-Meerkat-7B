## Summary

The SALT-ED study investigated whether balanced crystalloids, compared to normal saline, reduce hospital-free days in non-critically ill adults admitted from the emergency department. Conducted at Vanderbilt University Hospital, this single-centre, randomised, open-label study included 13,347 patients who received at least 500 mL of IV isotonic crystalloid. The primary outcome showed no difference in hospital-free days between the groups. However, secondary outcomes indicated a lower incidence of Major Adverse Kidney Events within 30 days in the balanced crystalloids group. Despite these findings, the study's single-centre design and unblinded fluid administration limit its external validity, suggesting the need for further multi-centre trials to confirm these results.

## Clinical Point

In non-critically ill adult patients admitted to the hospital from the Emergency Department, does the administration of a balanced salt solution compared with normal saline reduce hospital-free days to day 28?

## Background

- Over one million patients receive intravenous (IV) crystalloid solutions daily in hospitals.
- Previous studies have shown synthetic colloids are harmful, highlighting the need to establish the safety of IV crystalloids.
- 0.9% sodium chloride can cause hyperchloraemic acidosis and potentially reduce renal blood flow, leading to acute kidney injury.
- Balanced salt solutions may mitigate some risks associated with 0.9% saline.

## Bottom Line

Results need replication in a multi-centre RCT before concluding on the effects of 0.9% saline. Until then, balanced salt solutions or saline can be used based on clinical context.
