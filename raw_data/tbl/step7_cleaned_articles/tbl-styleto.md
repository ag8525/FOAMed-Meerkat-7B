## Summary

In a multicentre, randomised clinical trial conducted across 32 ICUs in France, the use of an intubating stylet was compared to a tracheal tube alone to assess its impact on first-attempt intubation success in critically ill adults requiring emergency mechanical ventilation. The study found that the stylet group had a significantly higher first-attempt success rate (78.2%) compared to the control group (71.5%), with no significant differences in complication rates or serious adverse events between the groups. Despite the lack of blinding and some limitations in achieving recruitment targets, the trial's findings suggest that using a stylet can enhance intubation success without increasing adverse outcomes, supporting its use in ICU settings where intubation is performed by experienced operators.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill adults, does the use of an intubating stylet compared to a tracheal tube alone increase first-attempt successful intubation?

## Background

- Acute respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation is a leading cause of ICU admission.
- Intubation in the ICU has a higher complication rate compared to the operating theatre.
- Various devices and strategies have been developed to improve intubation success rates.
- A preformed stylet has been proposed to increase first-attempt success rates.

## Bottom Line

Successful intubation is the result of complex interactions between operator and devices. The use of a stylet increases first-attempt success rates, but the overall success rate remains low. Operator experience did not affect the primary outcome. Current practice may include using a bougie or stylet depending on the situation.
