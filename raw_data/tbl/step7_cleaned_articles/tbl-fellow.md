## Summary

The FELLOW trial investigated whether apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy could improve the lowest arterial oxygen saturation in critically ill ICU patients undergoing intubation. Conducted as a single-centre, randomized controlled trial at Vanderbilt University Medical Center, the study included 150 patients and compared high-flow nasal cannula oxygenation to usual care. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in the lowest SpO₂ between the two groups, with secondary outcomes also indicating no significant differences in hypoxemia incidence, desaturation, or changes in saturation from baseline. The study concluded that apneic oxygenation did not enhance oxygen saturation during intubation, though it was a pragmatic trial reflecting real-world conditions. Despite its strengths, the trial's small size and single-centre design limit its external validity.

## Clinical Point

In ICU patients undergoing intubation, does apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy increase the lowest arterial oxygen saturation experienced by patients?

## Bottom Line

In this medical ICU setting, apneic oxygenation did not improve lowest arterial oxygen saturation compared to usual care during intubation by experienced fellows. No harm was demonstrated.
