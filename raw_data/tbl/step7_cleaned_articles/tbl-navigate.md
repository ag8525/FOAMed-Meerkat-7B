## Summary

The NAVIGATE trial investigated the efficacy of early noninvasive ventilation (NIV) in preventing the progression of mild acute respiratory failure (ARF) to severe ARF in general ward settings. Conducted across 11 hospitals in Italy, Greece, and Kazakhstan, this multicentre, open-label, randomized controlled trial included 524 patients with mild ARF, excluding those with severe hypoxaemia or hypercapnoeic acidaemia. The intervention group received 2-hour cycles of NIV every 8 hours, while the control group received standard care. The primary outcome showed a reduction in progression to severe ARF in the early NIV group (18.5% vs. 28.3% in usual care), with a relative risk of 0.65. However, there were no significant differences in secondary outcomes such as length of hospital stay, mortality, or ICU admissions. The trial suggests that early NIV can reduce the progression of ARF but does not significantly impact other clinical outcomes, and its cost-effectiveness remains uncertain.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with mild acute respiratory failure (ARF), does a regime of 2-hour cycles of noninvasive ventilation (NIV), delivered on general wards every 8 hours, reduce the progression to severe acute respiratory failure?

## Background

- ARF is a significant cause of ICU referrals and admissions, with high morbidity and mortality.
- The UK has fewer critical care beds per capita, making prevention of severe ARF crucial.
- NIV can maintain alveolar recruitment and reduce work of breathing but may also worsen shunt and secretion clearance.
- Continuous NIV is standard for COPD and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema but not recommended for community-acquired pneumonia and asthma.

## Bottom Line

Early NIV may reduce progression to severe ARF in mild cases but lacks impact on more patient-centred outcomes. Cost-effectiveness remains uncertain.
