## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the effect of low-dose hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone on mortality in patients with septic shock and relative adrenal insufficiency. Conducted across 19 ICUs in France, the study found that in non-responders to a corticotropin test, the steroid treatment significantly improved 28-day survival, with a hazard ratio of 0.67 and a number needed to treat of 7. The intervention also reduced the median time to vasopressor withdrawal. However, no significant mortality benefit was observed in responders or the overall population. The study's strengths include its randomized, double-blind design, but limitations such as the primary outcome measure and external validity were noted. The findings suggest potential benefits of steroids in high-risk, non-responding septic shock patients, though routine use is not recommended pending further evidence from ongoing studies like ADRENAL.

## Clinical Point

In patients with septic shock and relative adrenal deficiency, do low-dose corticosteroids improve 28-day mortality?

## Bottom Line

Conflicting results from major studies on steroids in septic shock. Routine use not recommended, but may be considered in high-risk, non-responding patients. Awaiting results from the ADRENAL study for further guidance.
