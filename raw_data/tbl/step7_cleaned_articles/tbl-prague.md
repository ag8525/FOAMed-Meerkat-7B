## Summary

The Prague OHCA study investigated whether early intra-arrest transport, extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR), and immediate invasive assessment improve neurological outcomes in patients with witnessed refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA). Conducted as a single-centre, randomized clinical trial, it included adults aged 18-65 with presumed cardiac etiology OHCA and no return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) after ≥5 minutes of advanced life support (ALS). The study found no statistically significant improvement in 180-day survival with minimal or no neurological impairment (CPC 1 or 2) in the invasive strategy group compared to the standard strategy group, although secondary outcomes suggested potential benefits of ECLS in specific cohorts. The trial was terminated early due to futility, and its findings highlight the need to consider local logistical constraints when implementing ECLS programs.

## Clinical Point

In patients with witnessed refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), does early intra-arrest transport, extracorporeal CPR (ECPR), and invasive assessment improve neurological outcomes at 180 days?

## Background

Refractory cardiac arrest is associated with poor clinical outcomes. ILCOR has incorporated ECPR into ALS guidelines for patients with suspected reversible causes of arrest where conventional measures fail (weak recommendation). The ARREST trial (2020) showed early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation improved survival to hospital discharge in refractory shockable rhythms.

## Bottom Line

This study did not show a statistically significant improvement in 180-day favourable neurological outcomes with a hyperinvasive approach to refractory cardiac arrest. However, secondary outcomes and subgroup analyses suggest potential benefits of ECLS in specific patient cohorts. Implementation of ECLS programs should consider local geographical and logistical constraints.
