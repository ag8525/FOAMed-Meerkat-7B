## Summary

The THAPCA-OH trial investigated whether therapeutic hypothermia improves survival with good neurobehavioral outcomes in children after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest compared to normothermia. Conducted across 38 sites in the USA and Canada, the randomized controlled trial included children aged 48 hours to 18 years who required mechanical ventilation post-return of spontaneous circulation. The study found no significant difference in survival with good neurobehavioral outcomes at 12 months between the hypothermia and normothermia groups, although hypothermia was associated with longer survival over time. Adverse events were similar between groups, but hypothermia was linked to more hypokalemia and thrombocytopenia. The trial's authors concluded that therapeutic hypothermia cannot be recommended based on these findings, noting potential under-powering and wide confidence intervals as limitations.

## Clinical Point

In children suffering an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does hypothermia compared to normothermia increase survival with a good neurobehavioural outcome?

## Bottom Line

Therapeutic hypothermia after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in children cannot be recommended based on this trial. Although no difference was found between normothermia and hypothermia, a false negative is possible, and confidence intervals are wide. Longer survival with hypothermia may suggest delayed risk of death.
