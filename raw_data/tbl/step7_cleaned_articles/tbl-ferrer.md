## Summary

This retrospective observational cohort study, utilizing data from the Surviving Sepsis Campaign, investigates the impact of antibiotic timing on in-hospital mortality in patients with severe sepsis or septic shock. Conducted across 165 ICUs in Europe, the US, and South America, the study analyzed 17,990 patients and found that administering antibiotics within the first hour of diagnosis significantly reduces mortality. Mortality rates increased with each hour of delay, with the most substantial rise observed after a 6-hour delay. The study underscores the critical importance of prompt antibiotic administration in managing severe sepsis and septic shock, highlighting a direct correlation between treatment delay and increased mortality risk. Despite its strengths, such as a large dataset and validated statistical methods, potential biases and data quality issues were noted.

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe sepsis or septic shock, is the timing of the first administration of antibiotics associated with the in-hospital mortality rate?

## Bottom Line

This observational study provides strong evidence that the time between diagnosis of severe sepsis or septic shock and the first dose of antibiotics is critical. Delays beyond 2 hours are associated with higher hospital mortality.
