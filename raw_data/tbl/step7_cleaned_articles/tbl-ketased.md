## Summary

The KETASED trial compared the effects of etomidate and ketamine on organ failure scores in critically ill adults requiring rapid sequence intubation. Conducted across multiple French EMS and ICU settings, the study found no significant difference in maximum Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scores between the two groups, with a mean difference of 0.7 (p = 0.056). Despite lower basal cortisol levels and higher adrenal insufficiency in the etomidate group, mortality rates did not differ significantly between ACTH test responders and non-responders. The study suggests ketamine as a safe alternative to etomidate, particularly in septic patients, though the potential for a type II error is noted. The trial's strengths include its robust design and state funding, while weaknesses involve the complexity of statistical analysis and applicability in regions without etomidate availability.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill adults, does a single induction dose of etomidate compared to ketamine affect organ failure scores during the subsequent first 3 days?

## Bottom Line

This study addresses an important clinical Point and finds no significant difference in organ failure scores between ketamine and etomidate for induction. There is a trend suggesting potential benefit from ketamine in septic patients, but the likelihood of a type II error is high.
