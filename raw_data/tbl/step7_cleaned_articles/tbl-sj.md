## Summary

The study investigates the efficacy of high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) compared to standard face-mask oxygen in preventing oxygen desaturation during rapid sequence induction (RSI) for emergency surgery. Conducted as an unblinded international prospective randomized controlled trial across six European centers, it included 349 adult patients undergoing elective surgery with planned RSI. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in the incidence of oxygen desaturation between the HFNO and facemask groups, with both maintaining similar mean oxygen saturations. Secondary outcomes revealed higher desaturation rates in Switzerland compared to Sweden, attributed to longer apnoea and intubation times. Despite being well-conducted, the study was underpowered due to a lower than expected desaturation incidence, and its findings suggest that HFNO is a viable alternative to facemask pre-oxygenation, though no clear advantage was demonstrated.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients undergoing rapid sequence intubation (RSI) for emergency surgery, does pre-oxygenation with high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) compared with standard face-mask oxygen reduce the incidence of oxygen desaturation?

## Background

Rapid sequence induction aims to minimize the apnoeic period between pre-oxygenation and intubation. Hypoxia is a recognized complication of RSI. HFNO may offer continuous oxygen delivery during apnoea, potentially reducing desaturation.

## Bottom Line

This study shows no significant benefit of HFNO over facemask in reducing desaturation during RSI. Clinicians should use the technique they are most comfortable with until further evidence suggests a change in practice.
