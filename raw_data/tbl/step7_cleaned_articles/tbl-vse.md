## Summary

The VSE trial investigated whether the combination of vasopressin, epinephrine, and steroids during CPR improves neurologically favorable survival after in-hospital cardiac arrest compared to epinephrine alone. Conducted in three Greek tertiary centers, this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial included 300 patients, with significant findings showing improved survival to discharge with good neurological outcomes in the intervention group (13.9% vs. 5.1%, OR 3.28, p = 0.02). Secondary outcomes also favored the intervention, with higher rates of ROSC and improved outcomes in patients with post-resuscitation shock. Despite these promising results, the study's limitations, such as its single geographical setting and small sample size for good neurological outcomes, suggest the need for further validation before altering clinical practice.

## Clinical Point

In patients experiencing in-hospital cardiac arrest, does the combination of vasopressin and epinephrine during CPR, along with steroid supplementation during and after CPR, improve survival to discharge with a good neurological outcome compared to epinephrine alone?

## Bottom Line

This study shows a significant improvement in survival to discharge with a good neurological outcome when using a combination of steroids, vasopressin, and epinephrine compared to epinephrine alone. However, further validation in different settings is needed before routine practice changes.
