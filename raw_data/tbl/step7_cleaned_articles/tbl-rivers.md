## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the effectiveness of early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) in reducing mortality among adults with severe sepsis or septic shock. Conducted at a single academic hospital, the study found that EGDT significantly lowered in-hospital mortality compared to usual care, with an absolute risk reduction of 15.13% and a number needed to treat of 7. The EGDT protocol involved aggressive management including fluid resuscitation, vasopressors, and inotropic support to maintain specific hemodynamic targets. Despite its strengths, such as a high completion rate, the study's limitations include its single-center design and potential conflicts of interest. The trial's findings have influenced the adoption of EGDT protocols, although subsequent studies have prompted modifications to the original care bundle.

## Clinical Point

In adults with severe sepsis or septic shock, does the use of early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) reduce mortality?

## Bottom Line

Until ProCESS, ARISE, and ProMISe, Rivers’ trial represented the best evidence for EGDT and demonstrated a strong benefit. Concerns and criticism have led to modified but widespread adoption of the 'bundle' of care in severe sepsis and septic shock. Until individual elements are tested, the specific goals are not unreasonable to target, although the algorithm is perhaps less robust.
