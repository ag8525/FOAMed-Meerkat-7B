## Summary

In a multicenter randomized controlled trial conducted across four tertiary centers in Australia, the use of prophylactic inferior vena cava (IVC) filters in severely injured trauma patients with contraindications to anticoagulation was evaluated for its efficacy in reducing pulmonary embolism (PE) incidence. The study found no significant difference in the composite outcome of symptomatic PE or death at 90 days between the intervention group (13.9%) and the control group (14.4%). Secondary outcomes revealed that while the intervention group had a lower incidence of symptomatic PE in patients without anticoagulation within 7 days, complications related to IVC filters were notable, including clots on the filter and difficulties in removal. The trial concluded that early prophylactic placement of IVC filters did not reduce the incidence of symptomatic PE or mortality, suggesting that individualized anticoagulation remains the preferred management strategy. The study's limitations included a high recruitment rate from a single site, unblinded design, and potential underpowering due to outdated PE incidence data.

## Clinical Point

In severely injured trauma patients where anticoagulation is contraindicated, does the use of prophylactic inferior vena cava (IVC) filters reduce the incidence of pulmonary embolism (PE)?

## Background

Venous thromboembolism is common after trauma, with 18% of severely injured patients developing DVT and 11% developing PE. A significant portion of symptomatic PEs occur within the first 4 days post-trauma. Previous meta-analyses have suggested that IVC filters may reduce PE incidence but increase DVT risk without affecting mortality.

## Bottom Line

The early use of IVC filters in trauma has limited benefit. Individualised anticoagulation remains the preferred practice.
