## Summary

This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial evaluated the efficacy of hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) as post-exposure prophylaxis for COVID-19 among individuals with high or moderate-risk exposure. Conducted in the USA and Canada, the study included 821 participants who were exposed to confirmed COVID-19 cases. Participants were randomized to receive either HCQ or placebo, with the primary outcome being the incidence of symptomatic illness confirmed by assay or epidemiological criteria. The trial found no significant difference in COVID-19 incidence between the HCQ and placebo groups, with HCQ showing 11.8% versus 14.3% in the placebo group (p=0.35). Secondary outcomes, including hospitalization and death rates, were similarly unaffected. The study concluded that HCQ is not effective for COVID-19 post-exposure prophylaxis and noted a higher incidence of adverse effects, primarily gastrointestinal, in the HCQ group. The trial's early termination and limited PCR testing were noted as weaknesses.

## Clinical Point

In individuals with high-risk or moderate-risk exposure to confirmed COVID-19 cases, does hydroxychloroquine post-exposure prophylaxis reduce the incidence of new illness compatible with COVID-19 compared to placebo?

## Background

- COVID-19, caused by SARS-CoV-2, emerged in Wuhan, China, in December 2019 and was declared a pandemic by WHO in March 2020.
- Healthcare workers (HCWs) are particularly vulnerable due to occupational exposure.
- Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) has been considered for COVID-19 prophylaxis due to its potential to inhibit viral entry and endocytosis.

## Bottom Line

Based on this trial, hydroxychloroquine is not recommended for post-exposure prophylaxis against COVID-19 due to lack of efficacy and high incidence of adverse effects.
