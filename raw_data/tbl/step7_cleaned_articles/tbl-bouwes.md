## Summary

This prospective cohort study investigates the prognostic value of clinical examination, neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and somatosensory-evoked potentials (SEPs) in predicting poor outcomes in comatose patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest. Conducted across ten Dutch ICUs, the study found that absent pupillary light or corneal reflexes at 72 hours, or bilaterally absent cortical N20 SEPs, are reliable indicators of poor prognosis. NSE levels greater than 81.8 µg/L at 48 hours post-collapse showed high specificity but low sensitivity. Despite the study's strengths, such as its prospective design and blinding, limitations include potential biases due to non-blinded repeat SEP results and moderate sample size. The findings support a multimodal prognostic approach as recommended by ERC/ESICM guidelines.

## Clinical Point

In patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest, can clinical examination, neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and median nerve somatosensory-evoked potentials (SEPs) predict poor outcomes?

## Bottom Line

In patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest, clinical examination at 72 hours, neuron-specific enolase, and somatosensory-evoked potentials can help predict poor outcomes. Due to methodological limitations and wide confidence intervals, a multimodal approach is recommended as per ERC/ESICM guidelines.
