## Summary

The CLASSIC trial investigated whether a restrictive intravenous fluid strategy in adult ICU patients with septic shock would reduce 90-day mortality compared to standard care. Conducted across 31 European ICUs, this randomized controlled trial included 1554 patients and found no significant difference in mortality between the restrictive and standard fluid groups (42.3% vs 42.1%). The study highlighted that while fluid management is critical in septic shock, a restrictive approach did not confer a mortality benefit. Despite its strengths, such as a robust multi-center design and minimal loss to follow-up, the trial faced limitations including unblinded treatment assignments and potential biases due to protocol violations and large pre-randomization fluid volumes. The findings suggest that while fluid restriction is feasible, it does not improve survival outcomes in septic shock, emphasizing the need for individualized fluid management strategies.

## Clinical Point

In adult ICU patients with septic shock, does a restrictive approach to fluid therapy compared to standard care result in fewer deaths at day 90?

## Background

Fluid therapy in critically ill patients has been extensively studied. Higher fluid volumes in sepsis and septic shock have been associated with harm, though evidence quality is low. The ANDROMEDA SHOCK trial indicated limited fluid responsiveness in septic shock patients after 2 hours. The CLASSIC pilot study showed feasibility of a restrictive fluid strategy, with reduced fluid use in the first 5 ICU days and a non-significant reduction in 90-day mortality.

## Bottom Line

This study did not show superiority of a restrictive fluid strategy over standard management for 90-day mortality in septic shock. Vigilant fluid management remains crucial in ICU care.
