## Summary

The 3SITES study, a multi-centre randomized controlled trial conducted in French ICUs, evaluated the impact of central venous catheter (CVC) insertion site on complication rates in critically ill patients. The study compared subclavian, internal jugular, and femoral sites, finding that subclavian catheterization was associated with the lowest risk of catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI) and symptomatic deep-vein thrombosis (DVT), but a higher risk of mechanical complications, particularly pneumothorax. Femoral and internal jugular sites had higher rates of CRBSI and DVT, with femoral showing the highest complication rates. Despite these findings, the study's unblinded design and variability in ultrasound use and antiseptic protocols were noted as limitations. The results suggest that while subclavian access minimizes infection risk, the choice of site should consider the balance between infection risk and mechanical complications, with a preference for ultrasound-guided internal jugular access when feasible.

## Clinical Point

In intensive care patients requiring a central venous catheter (CVC), does the choice of insertion site affect complication rates?

## Bottom Line

Subclavian vein catheterisation has a lower infection risk but higher pneumothorax risk. This study supports conventional wisdom but does not change my practice; I prefer ultrasound-guided internal jugular lines and would choose femoral over subclavian if needed.
