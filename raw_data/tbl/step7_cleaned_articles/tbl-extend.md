## Summary

The EXTEND trial investigated the efficacy of alteplase in patients with acute ischemic stroke and salvageable brain tissue, administered between 4.5 to 9 hours post-onset, compared to placebo. The study found that alteplase significantly improved functional outcomes at 90 days, with 35.4% achieving excellent functional recovery versus 29.5% in the placebo group, although the trial was terminated early due to external influences. Key diagnostic criteria included perfusion imaging to identify viable brain tissue, and critical management involved administering alteplase at 0.9 mg/kg. Despite the positive findings, the trial's early termination, changes in statistical analysis, and imbalances in baseline characteristics necessitate further research to confirm these results. Safety concerns included a higher incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in the alteplase group.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute ischaemic stroke and salvageable brain tissue, does alteplase compared with placebo, administered between 4.5–9 hours post-stroke onset, improve functional outcome at 90 days?

## Background

- Intravenous thrombolysis for acute ischaemic stroke is typically limited to within 4.5 hours of onset.
- Perfusion imaging (CT/MRI) can identify viable brain tissue beyond 4.5 hours.
- Endovascular therapy has shown benefits up to 24 hours post-stroke in patients with salvageable tissue.

## Bottom Line

This trial reported a significant improvement in functional outcomes at 90 days for patients with salvageable brain tissue treated with alteplase 4.5–9 hours post-stroke onset compared to placebo. However, due to early termination and statistical analysis changes, further trials are required before recommending this treatment.
