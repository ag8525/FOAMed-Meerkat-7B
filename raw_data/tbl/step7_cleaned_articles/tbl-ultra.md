## Summary

The ULTRA trial investigated the efficacy of ultra-early tranexamic acid (TXA) administration in adults with subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) to improve clinical outcomes at six months. Despite the theoretical benefit of TXA in reducing re-bleeding, the trial found no significant improvement in the primary outcome of a good clinical outcome (mRS 0-3) at six months, with 60% in the TXA group versus 64% in the control group. Secondary outcomes also showed no significant differences in mortality, serious adverse effects, or re-bleeding rates. The study's strengths include its randomized design and low loss to follow-up, but it was limited by its unblinded nature and single-country setting. The authors concluded that routine use of TXA in SAH is not recommended due to the lack of demonstrated clinical benefit.

## Clinical Point

In adults presenting with subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH), does ultra-early short-term treatment with tranexamic acid improve clinical outcomes at 6 months?

## Background

Tranexamic acid (TXA) has been considered effective in reducing bleeding, but recent studies suggest its use needs refinement. Patients with SAH are at risk of re-bleeding, often within 24 hours of the initial event. TXA may reduce re-bleeding rates, but its impact on overall clinical outcomes remains unclear.

## Bottom Line

This study does not show any clear clinical benefit from tranexamic acid use in SAH. While there may be potential future use, given the risk of delayed cerebral ischaemia, routine use is not recommended.
