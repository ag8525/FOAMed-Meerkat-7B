## Summary

The COACT trial investigated whether immediate coronary angiography improves 90-day survival in patients resuscitated from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest without ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). This randomized, open-label trial conducted in the Netherlands found no significant difference in survival between immediate and delayed angiography groups, with survival rates of 64.5% and 67.2%, respectively. Secondary outcomes, including cerebral performance and ICU discharge survival, also showed no differences. The study suggests that immediate angiography is not superior to delayed intervention in this patient population, and supportive management without immediate coronary intervention is reasonable unless ischemic symptoms or significant cardiac history are present. The trial's strengths include its focus on a critical clinical Point and robust design, while limitations include its limited external validity and the unrealistic predicted survival improvement.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients who have sustained an out-of-hospital arrhythmia-associated cardiac arrest without ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), is immediate angiography superior to delayed angiography in improving 90-day survival?

## Background

- Percutaneous coronary intervention improves mortality in STEMI patients.
- The benefit of early angiography in post-cardiac arrest patients without STEMI is uncertain.
- Observational studies suggest early coronary intervention may provide a survival benefit.
- This is the first RCT assessing early angiography in patients without STEMI after cardiac arrest.

## Bottom Line

In patients without STEMI after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, supportive management without immediate coronary intervention is reasonable. Early angiography may be considered for those with ischaemic symptoms or significant cardiac history. Awaiting results from ACCESS and DISCO studies for further guidance.
