## Summary

The UK-REBOA RCT evaluated the impact of Resuscitative Endovascular Balloon Occlusion of the Aorta (REBOA) in trauma patients with exsanguinating hemorrhage compared to standard care alone. Conducted across 16 UK trauma centers, this multicenter, open-label, Bayesian adaptive RCT was halted early due to indications of harm, with 90 patients enrolled. The study found that REBOA did not reduce 90-day mortality and was associated with higher mortality rates at 3, 6, and 24 hours post-intervention. Additionally, the REBOA group experienced longer times to definitive hemorrhage control and fewer definitive hemorrhage control procedures. The trial's findings suggest that REBOA, as implemented, may increase mortality, highlighting the need for caution and ensuring no delays in achieving definitive hemorrhage control in future applications.

## Clinical Point

In trauma patients with exsanguinating hemorrhage, does the use of the REBOA device with standard care in the emergency department, compared with standard care alone, impact 90-day mortality?

## Background

- Trauma is a leading cause of death globally, with hemorrhage being the most common mechanism.
- REBOA is a novel catheter inserted via the femoral artery to occlude the aorta, potentially controlling distal bleeding.
- Previous studies were limited to retrospective and prospective cohort studies and case series.
- This trial is the first RCT assessing REBOA's use.

## Bottom Line

REBOA, as used in this trial, likely increases mortality. Future use should be cautious, ensuring no delays in definitive hemorrhage control.
