## Summary

The CLOVERS trial investigated whether a restrictive fluid strategy with early vasopressor use compared to a liberal fluid strategy affects mortality in patients with sepsis-induced hypotension. Conducted across 60 US centers, the trial included adult patients with sepsis-induced hypotension, randomizing them to either a restrictive or liberal fluid management approach. The primary outcome, mortality before discharge by day 90, showed no significant difference between the two groups. Secondary outcomes, including organ support-free days and ICU stay duration, also showed no significant differences. The trial concluded that both fluid management strategies are safe, supporting a multi-modal approach to fluid and vasopressor management in septic patients. Despite its strengths, such as high internal validity and protocol adherence, the trial's unblinded design and early cessation may limit its external validity.

## Clinical Point

In patients with sepsis-induced hypotension, does a restrictive fluid strategy (with early vasopressor usage) compared to a liberal fluid strategy result in lower mortality before discharge by day 90?

## Background

- The 2021 SSC Guidelines recommend large volumes of fluid (30 ml/kg) during initial resuscitation of septic shock, based on low-quality evidence.
- Practice varies widely; an Australian study showed a median of 4.2L of fluid given to septic patients within the first 24 hours.
- The CLASSIC trial showed no difference in 90-day mortality between restrictive fluid strategy and standard care, but randomisation occurred post-initial fluid resuscitation.

## Bottom Line

This trial complements the CLASSIC trial regarding fluid restriction timing in critically ill septic patients. Both approaches appear safe, and a multi-modal approach to fluid and vasopressor management remains appropriate.
