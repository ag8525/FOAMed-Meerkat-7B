## Summary

The ANDROMEDA-SHOCK trial investigated whether targeting peripheral perfusion status via capillary refill time (CRT) is more effective than serum lactate levels in reducing 28-day mortality among patients with early septic shock. Conducted across 28 hospitals in South and Central America, this randomized controlled trial included 424 patients and found no significant difference in 28-day mortality between the CRT-targeted group and the lactate-targeted group, although the CRT group showed trends towards better outcomes, such as reduced fluid administration and lower SOFA scores. Despite these findings, the study was underpowered to detect the pre-specified mortality difference, and further research is needed to confirm CRT's utility as a resuscitation target, particularly in resource-limited settings.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with early septic shock, does peripheral perfusion-targeted resuscitation, compared to lactate level-targeted resuscitation, improve all-cause mortality at 28 days?

## Background

- The Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends lactate normalization during resuscitation.
- Issues with lactate as a target include non-hypoperfusion causes, delayed normalization, and limited availability.
- Capillary refill time (CRT) is a potential alternative, offering rapid response, ease of use, and independence from resources.

## Bottom Line

CRT is a potential alternative to lactate as a resuscitation target in sepsis, especially in resource-limited settings. Further studies are needed to validate CRT as an exclusive target.
