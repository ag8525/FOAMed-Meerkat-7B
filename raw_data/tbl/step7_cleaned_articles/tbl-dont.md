## Summary

This study investigates the transit time and complication rate of ingesting small toy parts, specifically Lego heads, in adults, comparing it to historical data on children ingesting coins. Conducted as a prospective, observational cohort study across five centers, it involved six adult healthcare professionals ingesting a Lego head. The primary outcome, the Found and Retrieval Time (FART) score, showed that 5 out of 6 Lego heads were retrieved within an average of 1.71 days, with no significant complications. The study concluded that small objects typically pass safely within 1 to 3 days, aligning with existing data on similar ingestions in children. Despite its strengths, such as standardization and gender balance, the study's limitations include a small sample size, non-randomization, and the use of unvalidated scoring methods.

## Clinical Point

In adults who ingest a small toy part, compared to historical controls of children who have ingested coins, is there a difference in object transit time and complication rate?

## Background

Children often ingest non-nutritional objects during developmental exploration. Coins, the most commonly ingested objects, typically pass safely within 3-5 days. There is limited data on the second most commonly swallowed objects, toy parts.

## Bottom Line

In this trial of 6 adults ingesting a standard Lego head, 5 were found within 3 days. This supports existing data that swallowing small objects is generally safe for most individuals. There is no need for undue concern or extensive searching through faeces.
