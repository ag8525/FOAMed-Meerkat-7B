## Summary

The REACT 2 trial investigated whether immediate total-body CT scanning in patients with severe trauma reduces hospital mortality compared to conventional imaging with selective CT scanning. Conducted across five level 1 trauma centers, the study found no significant difference in in-hospital mortality between the immediate CT group (15.9%) and the control group (15.7%). However, the immediate CT group experienced increased radiation exposure and reduced time to imaging completion and diagnosis. Despite these differences, the trial concluded that immediate whole-body CT scanning does not improve mortality outcomes but does increase radiation exposure, suggesting that focused CT scanning often leads to comprehensive imaging due to sequential scans.

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe trauma, does immediate total-body CT scanning compared with standard workup with selective CT scanning reduce hospital mortality?

## Bottom Line

For patients with suspected major trauma in high-functioning level 1 trauma centres, immediate whole-body CT scanning does not change mortality but increases radiation exposure. Focused CT scanning often results in a total-body CT scan due to segmental scans.
