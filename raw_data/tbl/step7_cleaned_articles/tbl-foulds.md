## Summary

This study evaluates the effectiveness of the McGrath® series 5 videolaryngoscope compared to the Macintosh laryngoscope in patients with cervical spine immobilisation. Conducted as a prospective, randomised cross-over trial, it involved 50 patients undergoing elective procedures requiring tracheal intubation. The primary outcome was the laryngeal view, with the McGrath® providing a significantly better view (92% Grade 1) compared to the Macintosh (6% Grade 1), and a higher intubation success rate (100% vs. 72%). No significant differences were found in time to intubation or complications. The study concludes that the McGrath® videolaryngoscope offers superior laryngeal visualization and intubation success in this setting, though it notes limitations such as single-centre design and non-blinded methodology.

## Clinical Point

In patients with cervical immobilisation, does the McGrath® videolaryngoscope compared to the Macintosh laryngoscope provide a better view of the larynx?

## Bottom Line

In this study, in patients immobilised with a semi-rigid cervical collar, the grade of laryngoscopic view and rate of successful intubations was greater when using a McGrath® series 5 videolaryngoscope compared to a Macintosh blade, in the hands of two anaesthetists experienced in both techniques.
