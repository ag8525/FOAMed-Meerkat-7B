## Summary

The TICH-2 trial investigated the efficacy of tranexamic acid (TXA) in adults with acute spontaneous intra-cerebral haemorrhage (ICH) to improve functional status at 90 days compared to placebo. This double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial included 2,325 participants across 124 hospital sites in 12 countries. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in functional status at 90 days between the TXA and placebo groups. However, TXA demonstrated potential benefits in reducing haematoma expansion and early death, particularly in subgroups with systolic blood pressure below 170 mmHg and baseline haematoma volume between 30-60 ml. Safety outcomes indicated fewer serious events in the TXA group, with no significant increase in venous thromboembolism or seizures. Despite the lack of significant improvement in the primary outcome, the trial suggests that early TXA administration may be beneficial in specific patient subgroups, warranting further research.

## Clinical Point

In adults with acute, spontaneous intra-cerebral haemorrhage (ICH), does the administration of tranexamic acid (TXA) compared to placebo improve functional status at 90 days?

## Background

Spontaneous ICH is associated with high mortality and morbidity, often due to haematoma expansion, which occurs frequently within the first three hours but can occur later. Previous interventions like early surgery and intensive blood pressure lowering have not reduced death or severe disability. Recombinant Factor VII has been studied but showed no significant reduction in death or disability. TXA had not been widely studied in this context before TICH-2.

## Bottom Line

The trial did not show a statistically significant benefit of TXA in improving functional status at 90 days. However, given the safety profile and potential benefits in certain subgroups, further research is warranted. Early administration of TXA in ICH patients may be reasonable.
