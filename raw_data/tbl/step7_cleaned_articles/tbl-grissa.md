## Summary

A randomized controlled trial conducted in a Tunisian university hospital compared the efficacy of acupuncture versus intravenous morphine for managing acute pain in the emergency department. The study included 300 adult patients with moderate to severe pain from musculoskeletal issues, abdominal pain, or primary headaches. Results showed that acupuncture achieved a 50% pain reduction in 92% of patients within one hour, compared to 78% in the morphine group, with a statistically significant mean difference favoring acupuncture. Additionally, acupuncture led to faster pain resolution and fewer side effects. Despite these findings, the study's non-blinded design, single-center setting, and cultural factors suggest the need for further research before acupuncture can be widely recommended as a primary treatment for acute pain in emergency settings.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute pain in the Emergency Department, is acupuncture more effective than intravenous morphine in reducing pain by ≥50%?

## Bottom Line

This non-blinded single-centre RCT found acupuncture to be quicker and more effective than intravenous morphine for acute pain in the ED. However, methodological weaknesses suggest further blinded, multi-centre international trials are needed before widespread adoption.
