## Summary

The REDUCE trial investigated the efficacy of short-term (5 days) versus conventional (14 days) systemic glucocorticoid therapy in patients with acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Conducted as a randomized, double-blind, non-inferiority trial across five Swiss hospitals, the study included patients over 40 years with a significant smoking history and excluded those with asthma or severe comorbidities. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in exacerbation rates at 6 months between the two groups, with the short-term group experiencing a significant reduction in cumulative steroid exposure. Secondary outcomes, including mortality, clinical performance, and steroid-related complications, were also similar between groups. The findings suggest that a 5-day glucocorticoid regimen is non-inferior to a 14-day course, supporting its use to minimize steroid exposure in COPD exacerbations.

## Clinical Point

Is short-term (5 days) systemic glucocorticoid treatment in patients with COPD exacerbation as effective as conventional (14 days) treatment in clinical outcomes, and does it decrease overall steroid exposure?

## Bottom Line

This study reinforces the practice of prescribing short courses of steroids in COPD exacerbations, supporting a 5-day glucocorticoid treatment regimen.
