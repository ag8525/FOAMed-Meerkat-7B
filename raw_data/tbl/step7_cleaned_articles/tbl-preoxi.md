## Summary

The PREOXI trial investigated the efficacy of noninvasive ventilation (NIV) compared to an oxygen mask for preoxygenation in critically ill patients undergoing emergency intubation. Conducted across multiple centers, the trial found that NIV significantly reduced the incidence of hypoxemia (SpO₂ <85%) during intubation, with a notable difference of -9.4% compared to the oxygen mask group. Secondary outcomes also favored NIV, showing lower rates of severe hypoxemia and cardiac arrest. Despite concerns about potential aspiration risks with NIV, the study reported similar safety profiles between the groups. The trial's pragmatic design and large sample size support its generalizability, although regional practice variations and the surrogate nature of hypoxemia as an outcome are noted limitations. Overall, the study concludes that NIV is more effective than an oxygen mask for preoxygenation in emergency intubation scenarios.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill patients undergoing tracheal intubation, does preoxygenation with noninvasive ventilation (NIV) compared to an oxygen mask reduce the incidence of hypoxemia during intubation?

## Background

Emergency intubation is common in the ED and ICU, with high adverse event rates. Hypoxemia is a frequent complication, associated with cardiac arrest. Preoxygenation aims to denitrogenate the lungs, providing a buffer during apnoea. Concerns exist about NIV causing gastric insufflation and aspiration risk. Evidence on optimal preoxygenation technique is limited.

## Bottom Line

This study suggests NIV is more effective than an oxygen mask for preoxygenation in reducing hypoxemia during emergency intubation. However, applicability may vary based on local practices.
