## Summary

The CASS trial investigated the effects of induced hypothermia in patients aged 50 or older with septic shock and respiratory failure, comparing 24 hours of hypothermia followed by 48 hours of normothermia to standard care. Conducted across 10 ICUs in Denmark, the Netherlands, and the USA, the study found no statistically significant improvement in 30-day mortality with hypothermia, which was actually associated with higher morbidity, including increased days on ventilators and renal replacement therapy. Despite strong randomization and allocation concealment, the trial was stopped early due to futility, and the results suggest that induced hypothermia may increase mortality and morbidity in this patient population, indicating no change in current management practices is warranted.

## Clinical Point

In septic and mechanically ventilated patients aged 50 or over, does 24 hours of induced hypothermia followed by 48 hours of normothermia improve 30-day mortality compared to routine care?

## Background

The search for effective treatments to improve sepsis outcomes has been ongoing, with therapeutic hypothermia showing promise in animal models. This study investigates whether similar benefits are observed in humans with severe sepsis or septic shock.

## Bottom Line

Induced hypothermia did not improve mortality or organ dysfunction in patients over 50 with severe sepsis on a ventilator. The results suggest increased mortality and morbidity, and current management should not change until further evidence is available.
