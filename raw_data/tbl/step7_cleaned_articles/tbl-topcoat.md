## Summary

The TOPCOAT trial investigated the efficacy of tenecteplase plus heparin versus heparin alone in patients with submassive pulmonary embolism (PE), focusing on patient-oriented outcomes. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial included 83 patients with submassive PE and right ventricular strain, excluding those with contraindications to thrombolysis. The primary outcome was a composite of adverse events within 5 days, including PE-related and treatment-related complications. Results showed a significant reduction in adverse outcomes in the tenecteplase group (15% vs. 37%, p = 0.017), with a notable improvement in self-assessed health at 90 days. Despite these findings, the study's small size, early termination, and complex composite outcomes limit its applicability, suggesting caution in using tenecteplase for submassive PE based solely on this trial.

## Clinical Point

In patients with submassive pulmonary embolism (PE), does the use of tenecteplase plus heparin, compared to heparin alone, improve patient-oriented outcomes?

## Bottom Line

In patients with submassive PE, tenecteplase reduced adverse outcomes based on a complex composite outcome. However, the only significant independent variable was self-assessed overall health at 90 days. The study's small size and incomplete nature limit its conclusions. I would not be persuaded to thrombolyse a patient with submassive PE based on this study.
