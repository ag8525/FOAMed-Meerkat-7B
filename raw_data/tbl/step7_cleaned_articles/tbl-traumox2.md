## Summary

The TRAUMOX2 trial investigated whether an early restrictive oxygen strategy, compared to a liberal one, reduces death and major respiratory complications in adult trauma patients. Conducted across multiple centers in Denmark, the Netherlands, and Switzerland, the trial included 1,979 patients with blunt or penetrating trauma. The restrictive strategy aimed to maintain SpO₂ at 94%, while the liberal strategy used higher oxygen levels. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in death or major respiratory complications between the two groups. Secondary outcomes indicated more hypoxaemic episodes but fewer surgical site infections in the restrictive group. The study suggests that targeting normoxia (SpO₂ 94-98%) in early trauma care is reasonable, though the short intervention duration and potential biases are noted limitations.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients experiencing blunt or penetrating trauma, does an early restrictive oxygen strategy compared to a liberal oxygen strategy reduce death and/or major respiratory complications within 30 days?

## Background

ATLS guidelines recommend supplemental oxygen for severely injured patients initially. Hyperoxaemia is common in trauma patients, but evidence supporting this practice is limited, especially regarding optimal oxygen targets in trauma.

## Bottom Line

Targeting normoxia (SpO₂ 94-98%) in the early phase for patients with moderate to severe trauma seems reasonable based on this trial.
