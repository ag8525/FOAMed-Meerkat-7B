## Summary

In the management of acute intermediate high-risk pulmonary embolism (PE) with right heart strain, a study compared the effects of volume expansion versus diuretic therapy on troponin levels and other cardiac biomarkers. The randomized controlled trial found no significant difference in the time to normalisation of troponin levels between the two groups. However, diuretic therapy, specifically intravenous furosemide, was associated with a faster reduction in BNP levels and a greater decrease in systolic pulmonary artery pressure and inferior vena cava diameter, suggesting potential benefits in right ventricular function. Despite these findings, the study did not demonstrate a direct improvement in clinical outcomes, indicating the need for further research to confirm these results and guide clinical practice. The study highlights the complexity of managing PE with right heart strain and suggests a tailored approach that may include diuretics, fluids, and other supportive therapies.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with acute pulmonary embolism and right heart strain, does a strategy of volume expansion or early diuretic therapy result in a more rapid resolution of troponin levels?

## Background

- Acute pulmonary embolism (PE) is a common cause for hospital presentation.
- Right heart strain is associated with increased mortality in patients with PE.
- Current ESC 2019 guidelines recommend consideration of ‘cautious fluid loading’ if CVP is low.
- Right ventricular (RV) dilatation may compress the left ventricle, compromising cardiac output, potentially ameliorated by diuretic therapy.

## Bottom Line

Routine ECHO in PE patients shows high prevalence of RV dysfunction (33% in this study). Diuretics likely reduce right atrial and pulmonary artery pressures but unclear if this changes outcomes. A tailored approach to maintain cardiac output in PE patients is recommended, potentially involving fluid or diuretics, inodilators, vasodilators, and anticoagulants.
