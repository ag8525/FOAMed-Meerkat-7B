## Summary

The document discusses the validation of the MIRACLE2 score for predicting neurological outcomes in patients after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA). The study, conducted at a single tertiary cardiac center in the UK, retrospectively analyzed data from 219 patients to compare the MIRACLE2 score with other prognostic tools like the CAHP, OHCA, and TTM scores. The MIRACLE2 score, which includes variables such as unwitnessed arrest, non-shockable rhythm, and age, showed a high area under the curve (AUC) of 0.93 for predicting poor neurological outcomes at discharge, comparable to other scores. Despite its high specificity and sensitivity, the study's limitations, including its single-center design and missing data, suggest that the MIRACLE2 score should not be the sole basis for clinical decisions regarding treatment or escalation.

## Clinical Point

In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA), how does the MIRACLE2 score compare with previously reported scoring systems for predicting poor neurological outcomes at hospital discharge?

## Background

- OOHCA has an ~8% survival rate to hospital discharge.
- Among those admitted to intensive care post-OOHCA, ~50% survive, with hypoxic brain injury being the leading cause of death.
- Current guidelines recommend prognostication after 72 hours following cardiac arrest.
- The MIRACLE2 score was developed for early prediction of neurological outcomes.
- This study aimed to externally validate the MIRACLE2 score and compare it with other scoring systems.

## Bottom Line

The MIRACLE2 score reported high accuracy for determining neurological prognostication at admission. However, due to this being a single-centre retrospective validation cohort with only small numbers of patients that had a high MIRACLE2 score, and >20% of patients having missing data, decisions regarding treatment or escalation should not currently be made solely on the MIRACLE2 score at admission.
