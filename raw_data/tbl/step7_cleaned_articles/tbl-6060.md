## Summary

The document discusses the use of echocardiographic features, specifically the 60/60 sign and McConnell sign, in diagnosing acute pulmonary embolism (APE). The 60/60 sign, characterized by a pulmonary valve acceleration time of ≤60ms and a tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient of ≤60mmHg, and McConnell sign, indicating normokinesia/hyperkinesia of the RV apical segment, are evaluated for their diagnostic accuracy. The study, conducted in a single tertiary center, found that while these signs are specific, they are not highly sensitive for APE. In patients without pre-existing cardiorespiratory disease, both signs showed high specificity. The study highlights that in high-probability APE cases where CT is unavailable, these echocardiographic signs can enhance diagnostic confidence, although a negative result does not significantly alter the diagnostic probability.

## Clinical Point

In patients with suspected acute pulmonary embolism (APE), do echocardiographic features of disturbed right ventricular ejection allow accurate diagnosis?

## Background

- APE often causes right ventricular (RV) pressure overload, detectable by echocardiography.
- In patients with pre-existing cardiorespiratory disease, RV overload may be chronic.
- The 60/60 sign may aid in diagnosing APE in patients with pre-existing pulmonary hypertension.

## Bottom Line

In a tertiary referral centre with high pre-test probability of APE, McConnell’s Sign has a high positive likelihood ratio. In patients without previous cardiorespiratory disease, the 60/60 sign also shows a high positive likelihood ratio. A negative result for either sign adds little diagnostic information. In high-probability APE cases where CT is not immediately available, bedside echo for 60/60 and McConnell’s signs can increase diagnostic confidence.
