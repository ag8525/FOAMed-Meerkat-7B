## Summary

The MAPPETT 3 trial investigated the efficacy of adding thrombolysis with alteplase to heparin therapy in patients with submassive pulmonary embolism (PE) characterized by right ventricular dysfunction or pulmonary hypertension. This randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study found that the combination therapy significantly reduced the composite outcome of in-hospital death or clinical deterioration requiring treatment escalation compared to heparin alone, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 7.5. However, there was no significant difference in mortality rates between the groups. The trial highlighted a reduction in the need for secondary thrombolysis in the intervention group, though it was underpowered due to early termination. Despite these findings, the study concluded that further evidence is needed before recommending thrombolysis for submassive PE, as it did not impact mortality or major bleeding events significantly.

## Clinical Point

In patients with submassive pulmonary embolism (PE), does the use of thrombolysis plus heparin, compared to heparin alone, reduce the composite outcome of mortality or clinical deterioration?

## Bottom Line

Thrombolysis for submassive PE decreased the requirement for rescue thrombolysis but did not affect mortality or adverse events. Further evidence is required before this treatment can be recommended.
