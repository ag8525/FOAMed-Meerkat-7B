## Summary

The ANTHARTIC trial investigated whether empirical antibiotics could prevent early ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in post-out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA) patients undergoing targeted temperature management. Conducted across 16 French ICUs, this double-blind, placebo-controlled study randomized 198 patients to receive either a 2-day course of amoxicillin-clavulanate or placebo. Results indicated a significant reduction in early VAP incidence in the antibiotic group (19% vs 34%), though no differences were observed in late VAP, mortality, or ICU stay length. The study highlights the potential benefit of short-term antibiotic prophylaxis in reducing early VAP without impacting other clinical outcomes, suggesting a possible need for practice reassessment in this patient population.

## Clinical Point

In post out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients treated with targeted temperature management, does the use of empirical antibiotics prevent early ventilator-associated pneumonia?

## Background

- Survival to hospital discharge and good neurological outcomes post-OOHCA remain poor.
- Targeted temperature management aims to improve neurological outcomes but may increase secondary infection risk.

## Bottom Line

This trial shows that a short course of antibiotics in OOHCA patients with shockable rhythm reduces VAP rates without affecting mortality or ICU stay length. Current practice may need review in light of these findings.
