## Summary

The TRUST study evaluates an accelerated diagnostic protocol (ADP) using high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-cTnT) to identify low-risk patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) suitable for early discharge. Conducted in a UK emergency department, the study included adult patients with chest pain suggestive of ACS, excluding those with significant ECG changes, age ≥80, or non-ACS causes. The ADP, which combines a modified Goldman score, non-ischaemic ECG, and hs-cTnT <14 ng/L, demonstrated a sensitivity of 98.8% for ruling out myocardial infarction (MI) and 99% for major adverse cardiac events (MACE) within 30 days. The protocol could potentially allow early discharge for 40% of patients, though further multi-centre validation is needed. Limitations include its single-centre design and exclusion of older patients, affecting generalizability.

## Clinical Point

In patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS), does a novel accelerated diagnostic protocol (ADP) identify low-risk patients suitable for discharge after a single high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-cTnT)?

## Bottom Line

The TRUST ADP is a promising tool that may enable clinicians to rapidly exclude ACS in low-risk patients. A multi-centre validation trial is required to confirm these findings, ideally comparing the TRUST ADP with the MACS rule.
