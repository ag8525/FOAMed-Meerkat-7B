## Summary

The MOPETT trial investigated the efficacy of low-dose thrombolysis combined with anticoagulation in patients with moderate pulmonary embolism (PE) compared to anticoagulation alone. The study found that thrombolysis significantly reduced the incidence of pulmonary hypertension at 28 months, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of 2.5, and decreased mean pulmonary artery systolic pressure (PASP) from 43 mmHg to 28 mmHg. Secondary outcomes showed a reduction in the composite of pulmonary hypertension and recurrent PE, although mortality differences were not statistically significant. The trial, while demonstrating the potential benefits of low-dose thrombolysis, was limited by its single-centre design, small sample size, and lack of blinding for patients and clinicians. Further research is needed to confirm these findings and evaluate the broader clinical implications.

## Clinical Point

In patients with moderate pulmonary embolism (PE), does low-dose thrombolysis combined with anticoagulation reduce pulmonary artery pressure at 28 months compared to anticoagulation alone?

## Bottom Line

Low-dose thrombolysis in moderate PE significantly reduces pulmonary artery systolic pressure at 28 months. Further studies are needed to assess clinical relevance and potential risks.
