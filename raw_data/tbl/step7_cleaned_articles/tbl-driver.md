## Summary

The document discusses the use of esmolol in patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) after standard cardiopulmonary resuscitation has failed. It is based on a retrospective observational study conducted in a single tertiary emergency department in the USA, involving 25 patients who remained in cardiac arrest despite receiving multiple defibrillation attempts, amiodarone, and adrenaline. The study found that the use of esmolol was associated with a non-significant improvement in survival with good neurological outcomes compared to controls. However, no significant differences were observed in rates of return of spontaneous circulation or survival to discharge. The study's limitations include its small sample size, retrospective design, and potential confounding factors such as the use of therapeutic hypothermia and emergent cardiac catheterisation. The authors suggest that beta-blockade, specifically esmolol, should be considered in refractory VF, and further prospective studies are warranted to explore its efficacy.

## Clinical Point

In patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation, does the use of esmolol improve mortality?

## Bottom Line

The use of esmolol for refractory VF was associated with a dramatic, but non-significant, improvement in mortality. This association may be due to a causal effect of esmolol. However, the small sample size or confounding variables such as the use of therapeutic hypothermia and emergent cardiac catheterisation may also account for the differences found. Further studies are required. In the meantime, if I have a patient with refractory VF, with no other treatment options available such as cardiac catheterisation, I will use esmolol prior to stopping resuscitation. Survival with good neurological outcome can be achieved with prolonged CPR (56–68 minutes).
