## Summary

The OPTALYSE PE trial investigated the efficacy of low-dose, short-duration ultrasound-facilitated catheter-directed thrombolysis (USCDT) in patients with intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism (PE). The study found that USCDT significantly reduced the right ventricular to left ventricular (RV: LV) diameter ratio at 48 hours across all treatment groups, with reductions ranging from 23% to 26%. The trial, conducted across 17 centers in the USA and Europe, included patients aged 18-75 with symptomatic, intermediate-risk acute PE. Despite the absence of a control group, the study demonstrated a low incidence of major bleeding and no mortality in most groups, although one group was discontinued early due to an intracranial hemorrhage. The findings suggest that USCDT may be a beneficial treatment for intermediate-risk PE, but further research with patient-centered outcomes and control groups is necessary to confirm these results.

## Clinical Point

In patients with intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism (PE), do low doses of tPA and short durations of ultrasound-facilitated catheter-directed thrombolysis (USCDT) result in a reduction in RV:LV diameter at 48 hours?

## Background

Systemic thrombolysis in intermediate-risk PE has not shown long-term mortality benefits and is associated with increased bleeding. USCDT, a form of pharmaco-mechanical thrombolysis, uses ultrasound waves to enhance thrombolysis. Previous trials have shown improvements in RV:LV diameter ratio with USCDT compared to anticoagulation alone.

## Bottom Line

In intermediate-risk PE, USCDT with low-dose, short-duration thrombolysis significantly reduces RV:LV diameter ratio at 48 hours. Further trials with patient-centred outcomes and a control group are needed to determine the benefit of USCDT.
