## Summary

The AIRWAYS-2 trial investigated whether using a supraglottic airway (SGA) device compared to tracheal intubation as the initial advanced airway management strategy in adult patients with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest improves functional neurological outcomes. Conducted as a multicenter, cluster-randomized trial across four ambulance services in England, the study included 9,296 patients. The primary outcome, measured by the Modified Rankin Scale at 30 days, showed no significant difference between the SGA and tracheal intubation groups. Secondary outcomes also revealed no significant differences in survival, regurgitation, aspiration, or return of spontaneous circulation, although initial ventilation success was higher in the SGA group. The study concluded that both airway management strategies resulted in comparable functional outcomes, emphasizing the importance of familiarity and proficiency with the chosen airway device.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does a supraglottic airway device compared with tracheal intubation as the initial advanced airway management strategy improve functional neurological outcome?

## Background

- Optimal airway management during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest is uncertain due to limited high-quality research.
- Tracheal intubation is considered a definitive airway management technique, but supraglottic airway (SGA) devices offer a simpler, faster alternative requiring less training.
- Another study (PART) assessing laryngeal tube insertion vs endotracheal intubation was published in the same JAMA issue.

## Bottom Line

In out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, SGA and tracheal intubation strategies had comparable functional outcomes at 30 days. Familiarity and proficiency with the airway device are crucial.
