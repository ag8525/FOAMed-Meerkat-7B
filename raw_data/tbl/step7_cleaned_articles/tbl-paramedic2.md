## Summary

The PARAMEDIC2 trial investigated the efficacy of epinephrine in adult patients experiencing out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA) compared to placebo. This large, randomized, double-blinded trial conducted by NHS ambulance services in the UK found that epinephrine administration increased 30-day survival rates (3.2% vs. 2.4% with placebo) but was associated with worse neurological outcomes. The study highlighted a significant absolute risk increase in survival with an adjusted odds ratio of 1.47, though the number needed to treat was 112. Despite the survival benefit, the potential for increased neurological impairment raises ethical considerations regarding the balance between survival and quality of life. The trial underscores the importance of further research and emphasizes the need for improved access to early CPR and defibrillation.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA), does the administration of epinephrine compared to placebo improve survival?

## Background

Epinephrine is used in cardiac arrest to increase coronary blood flow by raising aortic diastolic pressure. However, it may also increase myocardial oxygen demand and platelet activation, potentially worsening cerebral ischemia. Previous studies have shown mixed results regarding its efficacy, prompting the need for a large-scale RCT.

## Bottom Line

Epinephrine improves 30-day survival in OOHCA but may worsen neurological outcomes. This trial highlights the need for further research and better access to early CPR and defibrillation.
