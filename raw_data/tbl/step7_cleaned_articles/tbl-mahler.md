## Summary

The HEART Pathway Randomised Trial evaluated the effectiveness of the HEART Pathway in reducing objective cardiac testing in emergency department patients presenting with chest pain, compared to usual care. The study, conducted at a single tertiary academic center, involved 282 patients and demonstrated that the HEART Pathway significantly decreased the rate of cardiac testing (56.7% vs. 68.8%) and increased early discharge rates (39.7% vs. 18.4%) without increasing major adverse cardiac events (MACE) within 30 days. The intervention involved using the HEART score, which includes history, ECG, age, risk factors, and troponin levels, to identify low-risk patients suitable for early discharge. Despite its strengths, such as being a randomized control trial with clinically relevant endpoints, the study's limitations include its single-center design and small sample size. The findings suggest that the HEART Pathway could be a valuable tool for risk stratification in the ED, though further research is needed for broader application.

## Clinical Point

In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with chest pain, does the HEART Pathway compared with usual care reduce objective cardiac testing?

## Bottom Line

The HEART pathway may be a useful tool for risk stratification in the ED, reducing unnecessary testing and length of stay while maintaining safety. Further studies in different populations are needed before widespread adoption.
