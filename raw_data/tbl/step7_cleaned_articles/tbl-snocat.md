## Summary

The SNOCAT trial investigated the effect of sodium nitrite on survival to hospital admission in patients experiencing out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. This double-blind, randomized phase 2 clinical trial involved 1500 patients and compared two doses of sodium nitrite (45 mg and 60 mg) against a placebo. The primary outcome, survival to hospital admission, showed no significant difference between the sodium nitrite groups and the placebo group, with survival rates of 41%, 43%, and 44% respectively. Secondary outcomes, including rates of rearrest, return of spontaneous circulation, and survival to hospital discharge, also showed no significant differences. The study concluded that sodium nitrite does not improve survival outcomes in this context, highlighting the importance of publishing negative results to inform clinical practice.

## Clinical Point

In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does sodium nitrite, compared with placebo, improve survival to hospital admission?

## Background

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest has a high risk of morbidity and mortality. Nitrate therapy may mitigate cellular injury and death following reperfusion after ischemia, showing cytoprotective effects in animal models. Previous phase 1 trials indicated potential benefits of sodium nitrite at specific serum levels during early reperfusion.

## Bottom Line

This well-designed study provides convincing data that sodium nitrite is not beneficial during resuscitation from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.
