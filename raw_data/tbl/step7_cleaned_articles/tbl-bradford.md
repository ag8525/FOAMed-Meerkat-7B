## Summary

The Bradford Trial investigated whether induced hypermagnesaemia could reduce cerebral artery vasospasm in patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH). This randomized controlled trial, conducted in two Australian tertiary centers, involved 162 patients and compared high serum magnesium levels (1.60–2.50 mmol/L) to standard levels (0.65–1.05 mmol/L). The primary outcome, incidence of vasospasm diagnosed by digital subtraction angiography, showed a non-significant reduction in the high magnesium group (Odds Ratio 0.51, p = 0.06). Secondary outcomes, including Glasgow Outcome Score and Modified Rankin Score at 90 days, showed no significant differences. The study was underpowered, and while it suggested a potential benefit of hypermagnesaemia, larger trials are necessary to confirm these findings and assess their impact on clinical outcomes.

## Clinical Point

In patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH), does induced hypermagnesaemia reduce the incidence of cerebral artery vasospasm?

## Bottom Line

This underpowered study found a non-significant reduction in vasospasm post-SAH with hypermagnesaemia. Larger trials are needed to confirm any significant differences and potential improvements in patient outcomes.
