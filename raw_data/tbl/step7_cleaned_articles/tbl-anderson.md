## Summary

The document discusses a study comparing low-dose versus standard-dose intravenous alteplase in patients with acute ischemic stroke, focusing on non-inferiority regarding death and disability at 90 days. Conducted as part of the ENCHANTED study, this international, multicenter trial involved 3,310 patients and found no significant difference in poor outcomes (mRS 2–6) between the two dosing regimens. However, the low-dose group experienced a reduced incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and lower mortality at 7 days. Despite these findings, low-dose alteplase was not proven non-inferior to the standard dose, and the study's authors recommend continuing the use of standard-dose alteplase in clinical practice. The study's strengths include its large, diverse population and blinded outcome assessment, while limitations involve its predominantly Asian cohort and exclusion of patients unable to receive thrombolysis within 4.5 hours.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute ischaemic stroke, is low-dose thrombolysis therapy with intravenous alteplase non-inferior to standard-dose therapy regarding death and disability at 90 days?

## Bottom Line

Previous meta-analyses suggest increased risk of symptomatic and fatal intracranial haemorrhage with thrombolysis. This trial does not demonstrate non-inferiority (or superiority) of low-dose alteplase compared to standard-dose. Standard-dose alteplase should remain the practice in institutions capable of providing thrombolytic therapy for acute ischaemic stroke.
