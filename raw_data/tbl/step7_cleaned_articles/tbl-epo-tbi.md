## Summary

The EPO-TBI trial investigated the efficacy of erythropoietin (EPO) in improving neurological outcomes in patients with moderate to severe traumatic brain injury (TBI). Conducted across 29 hospitals in seven countries, this double-blind, randomized controlled trial included 606 patients who received either EPO or placebo. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in neurological status at six months between the EPO and placebo groups. Secondary outcomes, including mortality and thrombotic events, also showed no significant differences, although a sensitivity analysis suggested a potential reduction in mortality with EPO. Despite the trial's robust design, it concluded that EPO does not support improved outcomes in TBI, highlighting the need for further research into alternative treatments.

## Clinical Point

In patients with moderate or severe traumatic brain injury, does the administration of erythropoietin compared with placebo improve neurological outcomes at 6 months post-injury?

## Bottom Line

This trial does not support erythropoietin use in moderate/severe TBI. It highlights a high incidence of proximal DVT in this group, though clinical significance is unexplored.
