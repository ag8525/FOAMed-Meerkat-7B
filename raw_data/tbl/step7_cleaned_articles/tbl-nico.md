## Summary

The document discusses a randomized clinical trial evaluating the effects of withholding intubation in comatose patients with acute poisoning, specifically those with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) ≤ 8. The study, conducted across multiple centers in France, found that delaying intubation unless emergency criteria were met resulted in improved outcomes, including reduced ICU and hospital stays, without increasing mortality or pneumonia rates. The trial's primary outcome, a hierarchical composite of in-hospital death, ICU, and hospital length of stay, favored the restricted intubation group with a significant win ratio. Despite the study's strengths, such as its focus on patient-centered outcomes and pragmatic design, limitations include its unblinded nature and broad exclusion criteria, which may affect generalizability. The findings suggest that in cases of simple acute poisoning, delaying intubation can be beneficial, reducing ICU admissions and length of stay.

## Clinical Point

In adults with decreased consciousness from suspected acute poisoning, does withholding intubation versus routine practice reduce in-hospital death, ICU stay, and hospital stay length?

## Background

The decision to intubate poisoned patients with GCS < 9 is debated due to risks of aspiration versus intubation risks. No high-level evidence exists to determine the optimal strategy.

## Bottom Line

In patients with GCS < 8 due to simple acute poisoning without other adverse features, delaying intubation for up to 4 hours reduces ICU admissions and LOS.
