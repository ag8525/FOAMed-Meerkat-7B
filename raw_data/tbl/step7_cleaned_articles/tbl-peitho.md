## Summary

The PEITHO trial investigated the efficacy of tenecteplase in patients with intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism (PE) compared to heparin alone. The study found that tenecteplase reduced the composite outcome of death or haemodynamic decompensation within 7 days, with an odds ratio of 0.44, but did not significantly reduce mortality at 30 days. However, the use of tenecteplase was associated with a higher risk of major extracranial bleeding and haemorrhagic stroke, with odds ratios of 5.55 and a significant increase in haemorrhagic stroke incidence, respectively. The trial's findings suggest that while tenecteplase may offer haemodynamic benefits, its increased bleeding risk limits its routine use in intermediate-risk PE patients.

## Clinical Point

In patients with intermediate-risk/sub-massive pulmonary embolism (PE), does the addition of tenecteplase, compared with heparin alone, improve the composite outcome of death and haemodynamic decompensation?

## Bottom Line

In patients with sub-massive PE, tenecteplase offers modest haemodynamic benefits but no mortality reduction at 30 days. Its use is associated with increased risks of major bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage, questioning its routine use in this setting.
