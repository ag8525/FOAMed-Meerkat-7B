## Summary

The PROPPR trial investigated whether a 1: "1:1 transfusion ratio of plasma, platelets, and red blood cells (RBCs) compared to a 1:1:2 ratio improves mortality in patients with severe trauma requiring massive transfusion. Conducted across 12 North American Level 1 trauma centers, the study found no significant difference in 24-hour or 30-day mortality between the two groups. However, a post-hoc analysis revealed a reduction in death from exsanguination within the first 24 hours in the 1:1:1 group. Despite being underpowered to detect smaller mortality differences, the trial supports the use of a 1:1:1 ratio to potentially reduce early mortality from bleeding, with no increase in complications compared to the 1:1:2 ratio."

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe trauma predicted to require massive transfusion, does a transfusion protocol using a 1:1:1 ratio of plasma to platelets to red blood cells (RBCs) compared to 1:1:2 improve mortality?

## Bottom Line

Among patients with severe trauma and major bleeding, there was no significant difference in 24-hour or 30-day mortality between those receiving a 1:1:1 ratio of blood products compared to a 1:1:2 ratio. However, the study was underpowered for the observed mortality difference. A 1:1:1 ratio reduced mortality from exsanguination within the first 24 hours, with similar complication rates to a lower ratio protocol. Therefore, aiming for a 1:1:1 ratio in this population is reasonable.
