## Summary

In a randomized controlled trial conducted across five US emergency departments, the efficacy of trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) was compared to placebo in patients with uncomplicated skin abscesses following incision and drainage. The study found that TMP-SMX significantly increased clinical cure rates at 14-21 days post-treatment, with a cure rate of 92.9% compared to 85.7% in the placebo group, yielding a number needed to treat (NNT) of 14. Secondary outcomes also favored TMP-SMX, showing reduced rates of additional surgical drainage and new skin infections. Despite a higher incidence of gastrointestinal side effects in the TMP-SMX group, the antibiotic was deemed more effective in achieving clinical cure and preventing recurrent infections. The study highlights the importance of considering TMP-SMX in the management of uncomplicated skin abscesses, particularly in settings with high MRSA prevalence, while also emphasizing the need for clinical judgment in cases with minimal surrounding erythema.

## Clinical Point

In patients with drained cutaneous abscesses, does trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) compared with placebo increase clinical cure rates?

## Bottom Line

In patients with drained cutaneous abscesses, TMP-SMX significantly improved clinical cure rates compared with placebo and had a benign safety profile. Clinicians should use clinical judgment, especially in abscesses with minimal surrounding erythema.
