## Summary

The SAFE study, a large-scale, double-blinded, randomized controlled trial conducted across 16 ICUs in Australasia, compared the effects of fluid resuscitation with 4% albumin versus 0.9% saline on 28-day mortality in ICU patients. The study found no significant difference in overall mortality between the two groups, with a relative risk of death of 0.99. However, subgroup analyses suggested potential differences in outcomes for specific conditions: albumin was associated with higher mortality in patients with brain injuries and a non-significant trend towards benefit in severe sepsis. The study concluded that while both fluids are generally equivalent, albumin should be avoided in patients with head injuries due to increased mortality risk. Further research is recommended for patients with severe sepsis.

## Clinical Point

Does fluid resuscitation with albumin, compared to saline, affect mortality for patients in the ICU?

## Bottom Line

Overall, there is no difference in outcomes when using 4% albumin compared to 0.9% sodium chloride for fluid resuscitation. However, 4% albumin should be avoided in patients with head injuries. Further studies, such as the ALBIOS study, are needed for patients with severe sepsis.
