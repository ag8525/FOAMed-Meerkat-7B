# Randomized Comparison of Intravenous Procainamide vs. Intravenous Amiodarone for the Acute Treatment of Tolerated Wide QRS Tachycardia (PROCAMIO)

## Clinical Question
In patients with wide QRS (probably ventricular) tachycardia, does intravenous procainamide compared with intravenous amiodarone reduce major adverse cardiac events?

## Design
- Multi-centre, prospective, open-label randomised control trial
- Block randomisation in groups of ten on a 1:1 basis
- Allocation concealment through sealed envelopes
- Unblinded
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 26 hospitals in Spain (16 recruited)
- Cardiology and Emergency Departments
- Study period: 6 years (stopped early due to decreasing recruitment rate)

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults with regular wide complex tachycardia
- Heart rate ≥120 bpm, QRS ≥120 ms
- Haemodynamically stable: BP ≥90 mmHg; no dyspnoea at rest, severe angina, or signs of peripheral hypoperfusion

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Treatment with either study drug in the preceding 24 hours
- Poor haemodynamic tolerance
- Irregular tachycardia
- Tachycardia deemed supraventricular (based on response to vagal manoeuvres or adenosine)
- Contraindications to study drugs

**Enrollment:**
- 74 patients randomised
- 12 patients excluded post-randomisation (non-sustained VT, prior study drug use, protocol violation, other reasons)

## Intervention

**Procainamide Group:**
- 10 mg/kg over 20 minutes

## Control

**Amiodarone Group:**
- 5 mg/kg over 20 minutes

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Incidence of Major Cardiac Adverse Events (MACE):**  
  - Procainamide: 9%  
  - Amiodarone: 41%  
  - Odds Ratio (OR): 0.1 (95% CI: 0.03–0.6), p = 0.006  
  - Fragility index: 3 patients  
  - Most patients with MACE required immediate Direct Current Cardioversion  

**MACE Defined As:**
- Clinical signs of hypoperfusion
- Signs of heart failure
- Severe hypotension (≤70 mmHg SBP if pre-treatment BP ≤100 mmHg and ≤80 mmHg if pre-treatment BP >100 mmHg)
- Tachycardia acceleration >20 bpm
- Fast polymorphic VT

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Termination of VT:**  
  - Procainamide: 67%  
  - Amiodarone: 38%  
  - OR: 3.3 (95% CI: 1.2–9.3), p = 0.026  
  - Fragility index: 1 patient  

- **Total Adverse Events:**  
  - No significant difference: 24% vs. 48% (OR: 0.34, 95% CI: 0.12–1, p = 0.052)  

- **Adverse Events during 24-hour Observation:**  
  - No significant difference: 18% vs. 31% (OR: 0.49, 95% CI: 0.15–1.6, p = 0.24)  

**Subgroup Analysis:**
- Patients with structural heart disease showed lower MACE and higher termination rates in the Procainamide group.

## Authors’ Conclusions
For the acute treatment of sustained monomorphic well-tolerated wide QRS tachycardia (probably VT), procainamide is associated with fewer major cardiac adverse events and a higher rate of tachycardia termination within 40 minutes compared to amiodarone.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised control trial
  - Multi-centre
  - Clinically relevant question and endpoints
  - Trial registered at clinicaltrials.gov

- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded
  - Did not reach estimated sample size for power calculation
  - Low fragility index
  - Procainamide not widely available in UK Emergency Departments
  - No comparison with electrical cardioversion
  - High adverse effects rate in amiodarone arm, possibly due to administration over 20 minutes (ALS guidelines recommend 20-60 minutes for stable VT)

## Bottom Line
The trial suggests an improved safety profile and efficacy of 10 mg/kg intravenous procainamide over 20 minutes compared to 5 mg/kg intravenous amiodarone over 20 minutes in tolerated wide complex tachycardia.
