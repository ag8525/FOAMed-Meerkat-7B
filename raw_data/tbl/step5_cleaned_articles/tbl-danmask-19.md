  The DANMASK-19 trial investigated whether recommending surgical mask use to adults in Denmark, who spent significant time outside during the early COVID-19 pandemic, reduced SARS-CoV-2 infection rates. Conducted as a non-blinded, randomized controlled trial, participants were divided into a mask group, provided with surgical masks, and a control group with no mask recommendation. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in infection rates between the groups, with an odds ratio of 0.82. The authors concluded that mask recommendations did not significantly protect wearers, but the study was not designed to assess masks' effectiveness in source control, and its methodological flaws limit its utility in guiding public health policy.

# Effectiveness of Adding a Mask Recommendation to Other Public Health Measures to Prevent SARS-CoV-2 Infection in Danish Mask Wearers: A Randomized Controlled Trial

## Clinical Question
In adults in Denmark who spent three hours or more outside the home during the early stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, did a recommendation to wear a surgical face mask, compared with no recommendation, reduce SARS-CoV-2 infection at one month?

## Background
- SARS-CoV-2 is primarily transmitted through droplets and aerosols.
- Observational studies suggest universal mask-wearing reduces transmission.
- The study aimed to assess if wearing a surgical mask protects the wearer from infection.

## Design
- Non-blinded, randomised controlled trial
- Participants recruited via media advertisements and contacts with companies and organizations
- Random assignment to mask or control group, stratified by Denmark's five regions
- Sample size calculation: 6,000 participants for 80% power to detect a 50% absolute risk reduction
- Participants self-tested for SARS-CoV-2 using finger prick tests at baseline and one month

## Setting
- Nationwide in Denmark, April to June 2020
- Masks were not universally recommended in Denmark during this period

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 or older without current or prior COVID-19 symptoms or diagnosis
- Reported being outside the home among others for at least 3 hours per day
- Did not wear masks during daily work

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Evidence of previous COVID-19 infection (positive antibody test at baseline)

**Enrollment:**
- 17,258 responded; 6,024 completed baseline survey and met eligibility
- 4,862 completed the study

## Intervention

**Mask Group:**
- Instructed to wear a mask outside the home for one month
- Provided with 50 three-layer, disposable surgical masks

## Control
- No mask-wearing instruction or provision

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- SARS-CoV-2 infection: no significant difference
  - Mask group: 1.8%
  - Control group: 2.1%
  - Odds Ratio: 0.82 (95% CI: 0.54–1.23)

### Secondary Outcomes
- PCR evidence of other respiratory viruses: no significant difference

## Authors’ Conclusions
The recommendation to wear a surgical mask did not significantly reduce SARS-CoV-2 infection in mask wearers in a setting with existing public health measures. The findings should not be used to conclude that community mask recommendations are ineffective for source control.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Timely and relevant investigation
  - Patient-centered endpoint
  - Adequately powered trial
  - High participant completion rate
- **Weaknesses**
  - Not designed to assess masks' role in source control
  - Potential selection bias due to recruitment method
  - Short follow-up period
  - No ethical approval or data safety monitoring board oversight
  - Reliance on self-testing and reporting

## Bottom Line
This trial did not demonstrate a protective effect of masks against SARS-CoV-2 infection in a society without a mask mandate. Methodological flaws mean it should not guide public health policy. The study does not address masks' impact on source control.
