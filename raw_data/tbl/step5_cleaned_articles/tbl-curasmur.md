  The CURASMUR trial investigated whether rocuronium is non-inferior to suxamethonium for successful first-pass intubation in out-of-hospital emergency settings. # Effect of Rocuronium vs Succinylcholine on Endotracheal Intubation Success Rate Among Patients Undergoing Out-of-Hospital Rapid Sequence Intubation (CURASMUR Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients requiring emergency out-of-hospital endotracheal intubation, is rocuronium non-inferior to suxamethonium in providing a successful first-pass attempt?

## Background
- Suxamethonium and rocuronium are commonly used paralytic agents for emergency intubation.
- Suxamethonium has a higher side effect profile (e.g., hyperkalaemia, malignant hyperthermia).
- Rocuronium at 1.2 mg/kg is thought to provide comparable intubating conditions to 1 mg/kg of suxamethonium.
- Previous Cochrane review suggested rocuronium was slightly less effective than suxamethonium for intubating conditions.
- This trial is the first RCT comparing rocuronium and suxamethonium in prehospital emergency care.

## Design
- Nationwide, single-blinded, randomised, non-inferiority study
- Physicians were non-blinded to study arm
- Sample size: 1204 based on a 75% success rate in the suxamethonium group, with a non-inferiority margin of 7%
- Per-protocol analysis conducted
- Data recorded by intubating physician and verified by clinical research associate
- Follow-up until hospital admission

## Setting
- Pre-hospital setting throughout France
- 17 out-of-hospital emergency medical units
- Study period: January 2014 to August 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients in the pre-hospital setting requiring emergency intubation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Cardiac arrest
- Patients <18 years
- Pregnant women
- Contraindication to rocuronium or suxamethonium

**Enrollment:**
- 1326 patients assessed; 78 excluded; 1248 randomised

## Intervention

**Rocuronium Group:**
- Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg based on estimated weight

## Control

**Suxamethonium Group:**
- Suxamethonium 1 mg/kg based on estimated weight

**Common Management:**
- Recommended induction agents: etomidate (0.3 mg/kg) or ketamine (2 mg/kg)
- Cricoid pressure optional
- Intubation 60 seconds post-paralytic administration
- Single-use Macintosh blades (sizes 3 and 4)
- Alternative procedures per French guidelines if needed
- Sugammadex available for rocuronium group
- Standard management for physiological deterioration

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Successful first-pass intubation:  
  - Rocuronium: 455/610 (74.6%)  
  - Suxamethonium: 489/616 (79.4%)  
  - Absolute difference: -4.8% (1-sided CI -9% to infinity)  
  - Below non-inferiority margin of 7%

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in:
  - Cormack and Lehane airway grade
  - Intubation difficulty (Intubation Difficulty Scale)
  - Intubation conditions (Copenhagen score)
  - Use of alternative intubation techniques
  - Intubation-related complications within 15 minutes

## Authors’ Conclusions
Rocuronium did not demonstrate non-inferiority to suxamethonium for first-attempt intubation success in out-of-hospital emergency care.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Pragmatic study with a heterogeneous patient group
  - Relevant clinical question
  - Appropriate power calculation and non-inferiority design
  - Minimal loss to follow-up and protocol violations
- **Weaknesses**
  - Etomidate, not commonly used in UK/Australia, was the main sedative
  - First-pass success and complications were physician-reported and unblinded
  - Baseline differences in chronic disease prevalence
  - Lower first-pass success rate compared to other settings
  - Primary outcome is a surrogate measure
  - No video laryngoscope or bougie used
  - Rocuronium's lower side-effect profile not demonstrated due to study design
  - No data on physician experience

## Bottom Line
Rocuronium at 1.2 mg/kg remains a reasonable choice for emergency intubations, but study limitations prevent firm conclusions on superiority. The debate on intubating conditions versus rare adverse reactions continues.
