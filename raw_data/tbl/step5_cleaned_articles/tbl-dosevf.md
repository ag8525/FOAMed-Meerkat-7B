  The DOSEVF trial investigated the efficacy of Double Sequential External Defibrillation (DSED) and Vector-change (VC) Defibrillation compared to standard defibrillation in patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. # Defibrillation Strategies for Refractory Ventricular Fibrillation (DOSEVF Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF), does Double Sequential External Defibrillation (DSED) or Vector-change (VC) Defibrillation compared to standard defibrillation improve survival to hospital discharge?

## Background
Refractory VF occurs in up to half of patients following an out-of-hospital VF arrest. Anti-arrhythmics have shown limited efficacy in improving survival. Case series suggest potential benefits of DSED or VC defibrillation in treating refractory VF by targeting different parts of the ventricle.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- 3-group cluster randomisation with cross-over
- Randomisation at the paramedic service level
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Blinded outcome assessors
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov

## Setting
- 6 paramedic services in Ontario, Canada
- Data collection: March 2018 – May 2022
- Study paused April – September 2020 due to COVID-19

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years
- Out-of-hospital VF arrest of presumed cardiac cause
- Refractory VF (VF/VT present after 3 rhythm checks and standard defibrillations)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Non-VF/VT as presenting rhythm
- Non-cardiac cause (e.g., trauma, drowning)
- DNAR order
- Initial treatment by non-participating agencies

**Enrollment:**
- 405 patients randomised

## Intervention

**Vector-change (VC) Defibrillation:**
- Subsequent defibrillations with Anterior-Posterior pad placement
- 144 patients assigned, 113 received VC defibrillation

**Double Sequential External Defibrillation (DSED):**
- Subsequent shocks using DSED with a second defibrillator
- 125 patients assigned, 107 received DSED

## Control
- Standard defibrillation with anterior-lateral pad placement
- 136 patients assigned, 135 received standard defibrillation

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital discharge:**
  - DSED: 30.4% vs. Standard: 13.3% (RR 2.21, 95% CI: 1.33–3.67)
  - VC: 21.7% vs. Standard: 13.3% (RR 1.71, 95% CI: 1.01–2.88)

### Secondary Outcomes
- **DSED vs. Standard:**
  - Termination of VF: 84.0% vs. 67.6% (Adjusted RR 1.25, 95% CI: 1.09–1.44)
  - ROSC: 46.4% vs. 26.5% (Adjusted RR 1.72, 95% CI: 1.22–2.42)
  - Good neurological outcome: 27.4% vs. 11.2% (Adjusted RR 2.21, 95% CI: 1.26–3.88)

- **VC vs. Standard:**
  - Termination of VF: 79.9% vs. 67.6% (Adjusted RR 1.18, 95% CI: 1.03–1.36)
  - No significant difference in ROSC or good neurological outcome

## Authors’ Conclusions
Survival to hospital discharge was higher with DSED and VC defibrillation compared to standard defibrillation in patients with refractory VF. However, the trial was stopped early, and the effect size may be overestimated.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial with crossover design
  - Assessed CPR quality
  - Standardised resuscitation protocol
  - Blinded outcome assessment
- **Weaknesses**
  - Early termination and low number of outcome events may overestimate effect size
  - Outcomes assessed only until hospital discharge
  - DSED requires two defibrillators, potentially introducing bias
  - Results from pilot trial included in analysis
  - Baseline differences between treatment groups

## Bottom Line
DSED and VC defibrillation showed higher survival to hospital discharge in refractory VF. However, due to early trial termination and low event numbers, further research is needed. DSED or VC defibrillation may be reasonable options for refractory VF, but outcome benefits remain unclear.
