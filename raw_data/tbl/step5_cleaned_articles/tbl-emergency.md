# Emergency Department Resuscitative Endovascular Balloon Occlusion of the Aorta in Trauma Patients with Exsanguinating Hemorrhage (UK-REBOA RCT)

## Clinical Question
In trauma patients with exsanguinating hemorrhage, does the use of the REBOA device with standard care in the emergency department, compared with standard care alone, impact 90-day mortality?

## Background
- Trauma is a leading cause of death globally, with hemorrhage being the most common mechanism.
- REBOA is a novel catheter inserted via the femoral artery to occlude the aorta, potentially controlling distal bleeding.
- Previous studies were limited to retrospective and prospective cohort studies and case series.
- This trial is the first RCT assessing REBOA's use.

## Design
- Multicentre, open-label, group-sequential RCT
- Bayesian adaptive design
- Random assignment in a 1:1 ratio
- Allocation concealment via web-based randomization
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Sample size: Planned 120 patients; stopped at 80 due to harm indication

## Setting
- 16 UK trauma centers
- Enrollment: October 2017 to March 2022
- Follow-up: 90 days

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Trauma patients >16 years with suspected life-threatening torso hemorrhage amenable to REBOA

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant patients
- Unsurvivable injuries

**Enrollment:**
- 90 patients randomized
  - 46 to REBOA + standard care
  - 44 to standard care

## Intervention

**REBOA + Standard Care Group:**
- 46 patients randomized
- 19 had REBOA placed and balloon inflated
  - Zone 1: 10 patients
  - Zone 3: 9 patients
- Median time to inflation: 32 minutes (IQR 20-47)
- Median inflation duration: 29 minutes (IQR 19-64)

## Control

**Standard Care Group:**
- 44 patients randomized
- 42 received standard care
- 2 received REBOA and balloon inflated

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **90-day mortality:**
  - REBOA + SC: 25/46 (54%)
  - Standard Care: 18/46 (42%)
  - Odds Ratio: 1.58 (95% CrI: 0.72-3.52)
  - Posterior probability of OR > 1: 86.9%

### Secondary Outcomes
- **3-hour mortality:** Higher in REBOA group (24% vs 5%)
- **6-hour mortality:** Higher in REBOA group (28% vs 9%)
- **24-hour mortality:** Higher in REBOA group (37% vs 23%)
- **Time to definitive hemorrhage control:** Longer in REBOA group (83 min vs 64 min)
- **Definitive hemorrhage control procedure:** Fewer in REBOA group (30% vs 43%)
- Similar transfusion requirements between groups

## Authors’ Conclusions
In trauma patients with exsanguinating hemorrhage, REBOA with standard care does not reduce and may increase mortality compared to standard care alone.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - First RCT testing REBOA in trauma
  - Bayesian design allowed adaptation based on accumulating data
  - Robust internal validity with allocation concealment and intention-to-treat analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Care model may delay definitive hemorrhage control
  - Unblinded study design
  - Baseline imbalances (e.g., lower blood pressure in REBOA group)
  - Less than 50% of REBOA group had successful REBOA insertion and inflation
  - Other potential harms of REBOA not reported

## Bottom Line
REBOA, as used in this trial, likely increases mortality. Future use should be cautious, ensuring no delays in definitive hemorrhage control.
