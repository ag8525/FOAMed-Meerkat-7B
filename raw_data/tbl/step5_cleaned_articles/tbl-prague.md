# Effect of Intra-arrest Transport, Extracorporeal Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation, and Immediate Invasive Assessment and Treatment on Functional Neurological Outcome in Refractory Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest

## Clinical Question
In patients with witnessed refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), does early intra-arrest transport, extracorporeal CPR (ECPR), and invasive assessment improve neurological outcomes at 180 days?

## Background
Refractory cardiac arrest is associated with poor clinical outcomes. ILCOR has incorporated ECPR into ALS guidelines for patients with suspected reversible causes of arrest where conventional measures fail (weak recommendation). The ARREST trial (2020) showed early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation improved survival to hospital discharge in refractory shockable rhythms.

## Design
- Single-centre, randomised clinical trial
- Randomisation via web-based system during active cardiac arrest management
- Crossover allowed under specific conditions
- Power calculations based on varying increases in favourable survival rates
- Independent Data Safety and Monitoring Board reviewed data regularly
- Study terminated early due to futility

## Setting
- Single centre in Prague
- Study period: March 2013 to October 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18–65
- Witnessed OHCA of presumed cardiac aetiology
- ≥5 minutes of ALS without ROSC
- Unconscious (GCS < 8)
- ECPR team available at cardiac centre

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Unwitnessed cardiac arrest
- Presumed non-cardiac cause
- Suspected or confirmed pregnancy
- ROSC within 5 minutes of initial resuscitation
- Obvious life-limiting comorbidities
- Bleeding diathesis
- Known CPC > 3
- Suspected or confirmed acute stroke

**Enrollment:**
- 358 patients without ROSC assessed; 264 randomised
- 124 to invasive strategy (9 received standard strategy)
- 132 to standard strategy (11 received invasive strategy)
- 94 excluded due to logistical issues or exclusion criteria

## Intervention

**Invasive Strategy:**
- Intra-arrest nasal cooling (stopped in 2016)
- Immediate transfer to cath lab with ongoing CPR
- ECLS if ROSC not achieved en-route or on admission
- ECLS criteria: No ROSC or ROSC with shock, <60 minutes from initial call, consensus of ECLS team
- ECLS exclusion: Signs of death, irreversible organ damage, bleeding diathesis, inadequate vessels for cannulation

## Control

**Standard Strategy:**
- Treatment per standard ALS guidelines
- Transport to hospital if ROSC achieved, with early invasive strategy encouraged

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Survival with minimal or no neurological impairment (CPC 1 or 2) at 180 days
  - Invasive: 31.5%
  - Standard: 22.0%
  - Absolute difference: 9.5% (95% CI: -1.3 to 20.1, p = 0.09)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in cardiac recovery at 30 days
- Significantly greater survival with minimal or no neurological impairment at 30 days in invasive group
  - Invasive: 30.6%
  - Standard: 18.2%
  - Absolute difference: 12.4% (95% CI: 1.9–22.7%, p = 0.02)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with refractory OHCA, the bundle of early intra-arrest transport, ECPR, and invasive assessment did not significantly improve survival with a neurologically favourable outcome compared to standard resuscitation.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised trial with intention-to-treat analysis
  - Pre-published statistical analysis plan
  - Consistent neurological outcome assessment by a single neurologist
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre study; results may not generalise to larger cities
  - High rates of bystander CPR may influence overall outcomes
  - Possibly underpowered due to early cessation
  - Higher than expected favourable survival in standard group

## Bottom Line
This study did not show a statistically significant improvement in 180-day favourable neurological outcomes with a hyperinvasive approach to refractory cardiac arrest. However, secondary outcomes and subgroup analyses suggest potential benefits of ECLS in specific patient cohorts. Implementation of ECLS programs should consider local geographical and logistical constraints.
