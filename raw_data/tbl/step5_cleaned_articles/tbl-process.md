  The ProCESS trial investigated whether protocol-based care for early septic shock in adult patients, compared to usual care, reduces 60-day mortality. Conducted across 31 academic hospitals in the U.S., the trial randomized 1,351 patients into three groups: Early Goal-Directed Therapy (EGDT), protocol-based standard therapy, and usual care. The study found no significant differences in 60-day mortality rates among the groups, with EGDT at 21.0%, protocol-based standard therapy at 18.2%, and usual care at 18.9%. Secondary outcomes, including 90-day and 1-year mortality and morbidity markers, also showed no differences. The trial concluded that protocol-based resuscitation offers no mortality or morbidity advantage over physician-directed bedside care. Despite its robust design, the trial's power calculations and protocol adherence rates may have influenced the lack of observed differences.

# A Randomized Trial of Protocol-Based Care for Early Septic Shock (ProCESS Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with sepsis, does protocol-based care compared to usual care reduce death within 60 days?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial with three groups in a 1:1:1 ratio
- Non-blinded design
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 31 academic hospitals in the United States
- Study period: March 2008 to May 2013

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults arriving in the emergency department (ED) with suspected sepsis
  - Refractory hypotension or lactate > 4 mmol/L with two or more SIRS criteria

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Presence of another primary diagnosis, such as acute myocardial infarction

**Enrollment:**
- 12,707 patients screened, 1,351 randomised

## Intervention

**Early Goal-Directed Therapy (EGDT) Group:**
- Strict protocolised care based on the Rivers study for 6 hours
- Dedicated doctor, nurse, and research assistant provided prompts

**Protocol-Based Standard Therapy Group:**
- Relaxed protocolised care based on published expert opinions for 6 hours
- Dedicated doctor, nurse, and research assistant provided prompts

## Control

**Usual Care Group:**
- No extra staffing; care directed by bedside physician for 6 hours
- Research assistant collected data but provided no prompting

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No differences in 60-day mortality between groups:
  - EGDT: 21.0%
  - Protocol-based standard therapy: 18.2%
  - Usual care: 18.9%
  - P-values ranged from 0.31 to 0.89

### Secondary Outcomes
- No differences in 90-day or 1-year mortality between groups
- No differences in markers of significant morbidity

## Authors’ Conclusions
There is no significant advantage, in terms of mortality or morbidity, of protocol-based resuscitation over bedside care provided according to the treating physician’s judgement.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed, pragmatic methodology
  - Methods and statistical analysis defined and published a priori
  - Adequate recruitment for 80% power to detect a 6–7% mortality reduction with alpha 0.05
- **Weaknesses**
  - Inclusion criteria changed during the trial (reduced fluid bolus required before meeting "refractory hypotension" criteria)
  - Mortality ~20%, but initial power calculation based on 30–46%; interim adjustment made and recruitment target reduced
  - Adherence to protocol was 88.1% in EGDT group and 95.6% in protocol-based standard therapy group, which may reduce between-group differences

## Bottom Line
Adults with sepsis in the ED have approximately 20% 60-day mortality, and care by a dedicated team following either a strict or relaxed protocol makes no difference to this outcome.
