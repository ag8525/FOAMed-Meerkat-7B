# Everything is Awesome: Don’t Forget the LEGO

## Clinical Question
In adults who ingest a small toy part, compared to historical controls of children who have ingested coins, is there a difference in object transit time and complication rate?

## Background
Children often ingest non-nutritional objects during developmental exploration. Coins, the most commonly ingested objects, typically pass safely within 3-5 days. There is limited data on the second most commonly swallowed objects, toy parts.

## Design
- Prospective, observational, non-blinded cohort study
- Standardised toy part (Lego Head) used
- Ingestion occurred between 0700-0900
- Bowel habit standardised using the Stool Hardness And Transit (SHAT) score, based on the Bristol Stool Chart over 3 days

## Setting
- 5 centres: 3 in the UK, 2 in Australia
- Conducted over an unspecified 2-week period in 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Healthcare professionals working in paediatric hospital care

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous gastrointestinal surgery
- Inability to ingest foreign objects
- Aversion to searching through faecal matter

**Enrollment:**
- 6 adult participants (50% male)
- Mean age: 36.2 years (range 27-45)

## Intervention
- Ingestion of a standard Lego mini-figure head

## Control
- Historical controls from previous studies of ingested coins (Spitz, 1971)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Found and Retrieval Time (FART) score:**
  - 5 out of 6 figures retrieved
  - Mean bowel motions to retrieval: 2 (range 1-3)
  - Average FART score: 1.71 days (range 1.14 to 3.04 days)
  - One participant searched 13 stools unsuccessfully over 2 weeks

### Secondary Outcome
- **Comparison of SHAT and FART scores:**
  - Pre-study SHAT score (n=6): range 3 to 5.67
  - Post-study SHAT score (n=5): range 2.96 to 7.76
  - No significant difference between pre and post-study SHAT scores
  - No correlation between SHAT and FART scores

## Authors’ Conclusions
Small objects, such as those swallowed by children, are likely to pass within 1 to 3 days without complication.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Important research question
  - Standardisation of toy object and stool scoring
  - Gender balance
  - Authors' commitment to self-experimentation
- **Weaknesses**
  - Non-randomised and non-blinded
  - Small sample size limits estimation of true complication rate
  - Adult population differs from the target population (children)
  - Unvalidated scoring methods (SHAT and FART scores)
  - One head was lost to follow-up

## Bottom Line
In this trial of 6 adults ingesting a standard Lego head, 5 were found within 3 days. This supports existing data that swallowing small objects is generally safe for most individuals. There is no need for undue concern or extensive searching through faeces.
