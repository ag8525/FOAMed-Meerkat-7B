  The AVERT Shock study investigates the impact of low-dose arginine vasopressin (AVP) on blood product transfusion requirements in trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock. Conducted as a randomized controlled trial at a single urban level 1 trauma center, the study included adult trauma patients who received more than six units of blood products within 12 hours of admission. The intervention group received a bolus and continuous infusion of AVP, while the control group received a saline placebo. Results demonstrated a significant reduction in blood product transfusion volume in the AVP group compared to placebo, with a median difference of -1.00L. No significant differences were observed in secondary outcomes such as crystalloid volume, fluid balance, vasopressor requirements, or mortality. The study suggests that early AVP administration can decrease blood product use without increasing complications, though further multicenter trials are needed to confirm these findings.

# Effect of Low-Dose Supplementation of Arginine Vasopressin on Need for Blood Product Transfusions in Patients with Trauma and Hemorrhagic Shock (AVERT Shock)

## Clinical Question
In trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock, does the use of low-dose arginine vasopressin compared to placebo reduce the volume of blood products transfused?

## Background
- Traumatic hemorrhagic shock is a leading cause of death among adults under 45 years.
- Associated with neuroendocrine dysfunction, particularly AVP deficiency.
- AVP replacement may improve vasomotor tone, hemostasis, and vasoconstriction.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Computerized block randomization
- Blinding of clinical team, research personnel, patients, and families
- Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov

## Setting
- Single urban level 1 trauma centre, USA
- Data collected from 2013 to 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult trauma patients receiving >6 units of any blood product within 12 hours of admission

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Interhospital transfers, prehospital CPR, emergency department thoracotomy
- Recent corticosteroid use, chronic renal insufficiency, significant coronary artery disease
- Traumatic brain injury requiring neurosurgical intervention, pregnancy, incarcerated patients

**Enrollment:**
- 257 patients identified; 157 excluded (mainly for insufficient blood transfusion or ED thoracotomy)
- 101 randomized; 1 post-randomization refusal

## Intervention

**AVP Group:**
- 4U bolus of AVP post-randomization
- 0.04U/min infusion started after bolus
- Titrated to maintain MAP ≥65 for 48 hours

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Bolus of normal saline in equivalent volume as AVP
- Infusion of saline placebo of equivalent volume

**Common Management:**
- 1:1:1 transfusion strategy suggested
- Crystalloid and blood products at physician's discretion
- Additional vasopressors at physician's discretion

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Cumulative volume of blood products transfused within 48 hours:
  - AVP: 1.7L (IQR: 0.7–3.1)
  - Placebo: 3.0L (IQR: 1.4–5.2)
  - Median Difference: -1.00L (95% CI: -2.03 to 0.00L, p = 0.03)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Volume of crystalloid transfused
  - Fluid balance
  - Total vasopressor requirements
  - 30-day mortality
  - Hospital/ICU length of stay
  - Organ dysfunction

**Adverse Events:**
- DVT significantly reduced in AVP group: 20% vs. 39%, p = 0.05

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early administration of AVP during resuscitation of trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock decreases blood product use at 48 hours without increased complications.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomized control trial with appropriate blinding
  - Balanced pragmatic vs. protocolized design
  - Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses
  - Physiologic basis for findings well supported
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-center trial limits generalizability
  - Predominantly young male population with penetrating trauma
  - Not adequately powered for secondary endpoints or safety markers
  - Sample size calculation based on higher baseline blood product volume than observed

## Bottom Line
Trauma patients with hemorrhagic shock may benefit from low-dose vasopressin to reduce blood transfusion needs. Larger multicentre trials are needed to confirm efficacy and safety.
