# One-Year Outcomes after PCI Strategies in Cardiogenic Shock (CULPRIT-SHOCK Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock and multi-vessel disease, does immediate multi-vessel PCI compared to culprit lesion-only PCI (with staged revascularisation) improve outcomes?

## Background
- Early revascularization reduces mortality in acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock.
- Most patients with cardiogenic shock have multi-vessel coronary artery disease, associated with higher mortality.
- ESC and AHA/ACC 2014 guidelines recommend PCI of all high-grade lesions in cardiogenic shock, but the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial challenged this by showing lower risk of death or severe renal failure with culprit-lesion-only PCI.

## Design
- Prospective, randomised, international multicentre trial
- Open-label
- 1:1 randomisation
- Block randomisation (4 to 6 patients) using an internet-based programme
- Stratification by study centre
- Power calculation: 706 patients targeted for 80% power to detect a difference

## Setting
- 83 European centres
- Study period: April 2013 to April 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Acute myocardial infarction (STEMI or NSTEMI)
- Planned early revascularization by PCI
- Multi-vessel coronary artery disease (>70% stenosis in ≥2 major vessels)
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg for >30 min or catecholamines required
- Signs of pulmonary congestion and impaired organ perfusion

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Resuscitation >30 minutes
- No intrinsic cardiac activity
- Assumed neurological devastation
- Indication for urgent CABG
- Mechanical or noncardiogenic shock
- Onset of shock >12 hours before randomization
- Age >90 years
- Massive pulmonary embolism
- Severe chronic kidney disease
- Life expectancy <6 months

**Enrollment:**
- 1075 patients screened, 706 randomised, 686 provided full informed consent
- 2 lost to follow-up by 1 year

## Intervention

**Culprit-Lesion Only PCI Group:**
- Treat culprit lesion first
- Other lesions left untreated initially
- Staged revascularisation based on clinical status and residual ischaemia

**Multi-Vessel PCI Group:**
- Treat culprit lesion first
- All major coronary arteries with >70% stenosis treated

## Control
- Standard PCI technique with drug-eluting stents
- Maximum contrast material: 300 mls
- Post-intervention ICU transfer for continuous monitoring
- Other therapies at clinician’s discretion

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Composite of 30-day all-cause mortality or renal failure requiring replacement therapy
  - Lower in culprit-lesion-only PCI (45.9% vs 55.4%; ARR 9.5%, CI 2–17%, p=0.01, NNT=11)

### Secondary Outcome at 1 Year
- No significant difference in:
  - Death from any cause
  - Renal replacement therapy
  - Recurrent myocardial infarction
  - Bleeding rates
- Higher rates of staged or urgent repeat revascularisation and rehospitalisation for heart failure in culprit-lesion-only PCI group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Culprit-lesion-only PCI significantly lowers 30-day risk of death or renal replacement therapy compared to multi-vessel PCI in cardiogenic shock. However, at 1 year, mortality difference is not significant, but rehospitalisation and repeat revascularisation are more frequent with culprit-lesion-only PCI.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre
  - Relevant clinical question
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - 1-year analysis exploratory; trial powered for 30-day analysis
  - Open-label design may introduce bias
  - Cross-over and chronic occlusion revascularisation may affect outcomes
  - Neurological status not assessed as a primary outcome

## Bottom Line
In acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock, a targeted PCI strategy may be more beneficial, though further interventions may be needed. Supports recent ESC guideline changes downgrading non-culprit lesion intervention.
