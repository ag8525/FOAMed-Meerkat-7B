  The COACT trial investigated whether immediate coronary angiography improves 90-day survival in patients resuscitated from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest without ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). This randomized, open-label trial conducted in the Netherlands found no significant difference in survival between immediate and delayed angiography groups, with survival rates of 64.5% and 67.2%, respectively. Secondary outcomes, including cerebral performance and ICU discharge survival, also showed no differences. The study suggests that immediate angiography is not superior to delayed intervention in this patient population, and supportive management without immediate coronary intervention is reasonable unless ischemic symptoms or significant cardiac history are present. The trial's strengths include its focus on a critical clinical question and robust design, while limitations include its limited external validity and the unrealistic predicted survival improvement.

# Coronary Angiography after Cardiac Arrest without ST-Segment Elevation (COACT Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients who have sustained an out-of-hospital arrhythmia-associated cardiac arrest without ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), is immediate angiography superior to delayed angiography in improving 90-day survival?

## Background
- Percutaneous coronary intervention improves mortality in STEMI patients.
- The benefit of early angiography in post-cardiac arrest patients without STEMI is uncertain.
- Observational studies suggest early coronary intervention may provide a survival benefit.
- This is the first RCT assessing early angiography in patients without STEMI after cardiac arrest.

## Design
- Investigator-initiated, randomised, open-label, multicentre trial
- Patients randomised 1:1 using a web-based system
- Study powered at 85% to detect a 40% relative difference in 90-day survival
- Adaptive design allowed for sample size increase if needed

## Setting
- 19 centres in the Netherlands
- Major recruitment in Amsterdam and Rotterdam
- Study period: January 2015 to July 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with out-of-hospital arrest, initial shockable rhythm, unconscious post-ROSC

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Signs of STEMI on ECG
- Hemodynamic instability unresponsive to therapy
- Non-coronary cause of arrest
- Severe renal dysfunction (GFR < 30 ml/min)
- Pregnancy, intracranial bleeding, stroke
- Therapy limitations or DNR order
- Pre-arrest Cerebral Performance Category 3 or 4
- >4 hours from ROSC to screening
- Refractory ventricular arrhythmia
- Inability to complete 90-day follow-up

**Enrollment:**
- 538 patients randomised

## Intervention

**Immediate Angiography Group:**
- Coronary angiography within 2 hours post-randomisation

## Control

**Delayed Angiography Group:**
- Coronary angiography after neurological recovery, typically post-ICU discharge

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Urgent angiography for cardiogenic shock, recurrent arrhythmias, or ischaemia
- Anticoagulant and revascularisation strategy at physician's discretion
- Treatment for unstable coronary lesions
- Post-resuscitation care per international guidelines
- Targeted temperature management initiated per local protocol

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistical difference in 90-day survival
  - Immediate: 64.5% survival
  - Delayed: 67.2% survival
  - Odds ratio: 0.89 (95% CI: 0.62–1.72); p = 0.51

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in survival with good cerebral performance
- No difference in ICU discharge survival, major bleeding, arrhythmia recurrence, inotrope support, mechanical ventilation duration, or renal replacement therapy
- Immediate group took longer to achieve target temperature (5.4 vs 4.7 hours)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Immediate angiography was not superior to delayed angiography for 90-day survival in patients resuscitated from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest without STEMI.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses an important research area in cardiac arrest management
  - Appropriately powered for patient-centred outcomes
  - Well-matched baseline characteristics
  - Standardised coronary intervention approach
  - Robust randomisation and minimal crossover
- **Weaknesses**
  - Limited external validity (conducted only in the Netherlands)
  - Blinding not possible
  - Time to target temperature management differed between groups
  - Predicted survival improvement was unrealistic
  - Acute unstable coronary lesions found in only 20% of patients
  - Narrower inclusion criteria might identify higher-risk patients

## Bottom Line
In patients without STEMI after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, supportive management without immediate coronary intervention is reasonable. Early angiography may be considered for those with ischaemic symptoms or significant cardiac history. Awaiting results from ACCESS and DISCO studies for further guidance.
