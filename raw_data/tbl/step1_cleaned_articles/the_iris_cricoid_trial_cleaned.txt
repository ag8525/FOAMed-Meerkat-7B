# Title
Effect of Cricoid Pressure Compared with a Sham Procedure in the Rapid Sequence Induction of Anesthesia (The IRIS Cricoid Trial)  
**Aurélie Birenbaum.**  
JAMA Surg 2018; published online October 17, 2018. doi:10.1001/jamasurg.2018.3577

# Clinical Question
In patients undergoing rapid sequence induction (RSI) of anesthesia, does the use of cricoid pressure versus a sham procedure prevent pulmonary aspiration?

# Background
Cricoid pressure involves applying digital pressure against the cricoid cartilage to compress the esophagus and prevent gastric aspiration. First described by Sellick in 1961, it is often considered a standard of care in RSI, though its efficacy is debated.

# Design
- Randomised, double-blinded, non-inferiority trial
- Patients allocated 1:1 to Sellick (cricoid pressure) or Sham group via computer-generated randomisation
- Follow-up: up to 28 days or until hospital discharge
- Standardised anesthesia and intubation procedures per French guidelines

# Setting
- Conducted across 10 urban educational centers in France
- Enrollment: February 2014 to February 2017

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients undergoing anesthesia with RSI
- >18 years old with a full stomach (<6 hours fasting) or ≥1 risk factor for pulmonary aspiration (e.g., emergency conditions, BMI >30, previous gastric surgery)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Refusal, age <18 years, pregnancy
- Contraindication to cricoid pressure or use of succinylcholine
- Pneumonia, pulmonary contusions, or respiratory tract abnormalities
- Requirement for alternative laryngoscopy technique

**Enrollment:**
- 3472 patients enrolled
- Mean age: 51 years; 51% male

# Intervention
- Sham cricoid pressure

# Control
- Cricoid pressure: 30 N applied with 3 fingers on the cricoid cartilage
- Operators trained monthly to ensure technique consistency

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in pulmonary aspiration incidence:
  - Cricoid pressure: 0.6%
  - Sham: 0.5%
  - Non-inferiority not demonstrated due to wide confidence intervals

## Secondary Outcomes
- Higher incidence of difficult intubation in cricoid group (not statistically significant)
- Increased Cormack-Lehane grade (3 and 4) and time to intubation in cricoid group
- No difference in pneumonia, aspiration difficulty, or traumatic complications

# Authors’ Conclusions
The study did not demonstrate non-inferiority of the sham procedure in preventing pulmonary aspiration. The benefits of cricoid pressure remain unproven, and potential harms include interference with intubation.

# Strengths
- Large, randomised, double-blinded trial
- Standardised procedures and repeated operator training

# Weaknesses
- Exclusion of pregnant women and children
- Conducted in controlled environments, limiting generalisability to emergency settings
- Heterogeneous risk factors for aspiration without weighting
- Wide confidence intervals due to low primary outcome incidence

# Bottom Line
This RCT failed to demonstrate the non-inferiority of a sham procedure compared with cricoid pressure in preventing pulmonary aspiration during RSI. The study highlights the unproven benefits and potential harms of cricoid pressure, suggesting further research is needed, especially including pregnant women.
