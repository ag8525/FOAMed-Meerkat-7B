# Title
Trial of Continuous or Interrupted Chest Compressions during CPR (ROC-CCC)  
**Nichol et al.**  
NEJM 2015; published online first. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1509139

# Clinical Question
In adult patients experiencing cardiac arrest, do uninterrupted chest compressions compared to chest compressions interrupted for manual ventilation improve survival, neurological recovery, or reduce adverse events?

# Design
- Cluster randomised, controlled, cross-over trial
- EMS grouped into clusters, randomised to intervention or control
- Run-in phase for adherence and performance
- Cross-over to alternative trial arm twice a year
- Unblinded EMS providers; outcome assessment blinding not specified
- Huber-White sandwich adjustment for cluster design
- Adjustments for interim analyses
- 90% power to detect survival rates of 8.1% in control vs 9.4% in intervention

# Setting
- 8 Resuscitation Outcome Consortium sites with 114 EMS agencies
- USA and Canada
- Study period: June 2011 to May 2015

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- EMS witnessed arrest
- Traumatic injury, asphyxial cause, uncontrolled bleeding
- Pregnancy, existing tracheostomy, or advanced airway management prior
- Manual CPR by non-study EMS or mechanical CPR by any EMS prior
- Opted out of resuscitation research or known prisoner status

**Enrollment:**
- 35,904 patients screened, 26,148 eligible
- 23,711 actively enrolled
- 2,437 in run-in phase included in safety analysis only

# Intervention

**Continuous Chest Compressions Group:**
- Chest compressions at 100/min
- Asynchronous positive pressure ventilation at 10/min within 2 minutes
- Ventilation via "oral airway" for first 3 cycles (~2 minutes each)
- Advanced airway inserted after 3rd cycle if no ROSC or CPR termination
- Continuous compressions and asynchronous ventilation continued post-airway insertion

# Control

**Interrupted Chest Compressions Group:**
- Chest compressions at 100/min, interrupted for ventilation at 30:2 ratio
- Positive pressure ventilation during 5-second pause
- Ventilation via "oral airway" for 3 cycles (~2 minutes each)
- Advanced airway inserted after 3rd cycle if no ROSC or CPR termination
- Continuous compressions and asynchronous ventilation continued post-airway insertion

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- No statistically significant difference in survival to hospital discharge
  - Continuous: 9.7%
  - Interrupted: 9.0%
  - Adjusted difference: -0.7% (95% CI: -1.5 to 0.1); p = 0.07

## Secondary Outcomes
- **Per-protocol survival to discharge:** Interrupted group favored
  - Continuous: 7.6%
  - Interrupted: 9.6%
  - Adjusted difference: -1.3% (95% CI: -2.5 to -0.1); p = 0.04

- **Neurological outcome at discharge:** No significant difference
  - Continuous: 7.0%
  - Interrupted: 7.7%
  - Adjusted difference: -0.6% (95% CI: -1.4 to 0.1); p = 0.09

- **Hospital-free survival at 30 days:** Continuous group had less
  - Continuous: 1.3 days
  - Interrupted: 1.5 days
  - Adjusted difference: -0.2 days (95% CI: -0.3 to -0.1); p = 0.004

- **Transfer to hospital rate:** Lower in continuous group
  - Continuous: 52.8%
  - Interrupted: 54.9%
  - Adjusted difference: -2.0% (95% CI: -3.6 to -0.5); p = 0.01

# Authors’ Conclusions
Continuous chest compressions with asynchronous ventilation do not improve survival or neurological recovery compared to standard interrupted compressions in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

# Strengths
- Pragmatic design suitable for community emergencies
- Cluster and cross-over design with run-in phase for training
- Statistical corrections for cluster design
- High internal validity with large screening and enrollment

# Weaknesses
- Short duration of intervention before advanced airway insertion (~6 minutes)
- Potential bias due to non-study differences in hospital transfer rates
- High-quality CPR in both groups may not reflect real-world resuscitation

# Bottom Line
This trial did not demonstrate a benefit of continuous chest compressions over interrupted compressions in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. Continuous compressions may not affect outcomes compared to conventional interrupted compressions.
