# Title
Initiation of Inappropriate Antimicrobial Therapy Results in a Fivefold Reduction of Survival in Human Septic Shock  
**Kumar A.**  
Chest 2009; 136(5): 1237-1248. doi: 10.1378/chest.09-0087

# Clinical Question
In patients with septic shock, what is the relationship between the appropriateness of initial empiric antimicrobial therapy and survival?

# Design
- Retrospective observational study
- Pre-determined rules for assessing appropriateness of antimicrobial agents
- Review by two blinded infectious disease/critical care physicians for unanticipated scenarios

# Setting
- 22 hospitals in Canada, USA, and Saudi Arabia
- Study period: 1996 to 2006

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with septic shock defined by:
  - Documented or suspected infection
  - Persistent hypotension requiring pressor therapy
  - ≥2 of the following: HR >90, RR >20 or PCO₂ <32 mmHg, Temp <36°C or >38°C, WCC <4 or >12 or >10% band forms

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- None defined

**Enrollment:**
- 5715 patients

# Intervention

**Appropriate Antimicrobial Therapy:**
- Defined as positive if:
  - The first new antimicrobial agent given following recurrent/persistent hypotension or within 6 hours of the first new antimicrobial had in vitro activity appropriate for the isolated pathogen
  - For culture-negative shock, antimicrobial consistent with accepted norms modified for local flora

# Control

**Inappropriate or No Antimicrobial Therapy:**
- Patients who did not receive appropriate antimicrobial therapy

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Survival to hospital discharge (including discharge to chronic care facility): 43.7%

## Secondary Outcomes
- Appropriate antimicrobials initiated in 80.1%
- Survival to hospital discharge significantly improved with appropriate antimicrobials (52% vs. 10.3%, OR 9.45, 95% CI: 7.74–11.54, p<0.0001)
- Multivariable logistic regression analysis confirmed improved survival (OR 8.99, 95% CI: 6.60–12.23, p<0.0001)

## Subgroup Analysis
- Significantly improved mortality with appropriate antimicrobials in:
  - Culture-positive cases (OR 6.95, 95% CI: 3.11–15.52)
  - Gram-positive cocci (n=1356), p<0.0001
  - Gram-negative bacilli (n=2002), p<0.0001
  - Anaerobes (n=156), p=0.0048
  - Yeast (n=443), p<0.0001

# Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with septic shock, approximately 20% are initially treated with inappropriate antimicrobial therapy, which is associated with a fivefold decrease in survival.

# Strengths
- Large sample size, multi-centre study
- Pre-determined rules for determining appropriateness of antimicrobials
- Adjustments for known biases through multivariate analyses
- Large effect size (OR 6.95-9.45), clinically relevant
- Consistent results across subgroups

# Weaknesses
- Retrospective analysis, potential for multiple biases
- Significant differences in patient characteristics between groups
- No data on patients lost to follow-up
- Weak gold standard for culture-negative infections
- Historical data with high overall mortality based on current standards

# Bottom Line
In adult patients with septic shock, inappropriate initial empiric antibiotic therapy is associated with a ~5-fold absolute reduction in survival, even after correcting for co-morbidities, clinical presentation, and organism. The effect size is greatest in patients with primary bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, and infections due to anaerobes or yeasts.
