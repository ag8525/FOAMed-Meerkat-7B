# Title
Intra-aortic Balloon Counterpulsation in Acute Myocardial Infarction Complicated by Cardiogenic Shock (IABP-SHOCK II)  
**IABP-SHOCK II Trial Investigators.**  
NEJM 2012; 367(14): 1287-1296. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1208410

# Clinical Question
In patients with acute myocardial infarction (MI) and cardiogenic shock, does intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation (IABP) compared to medical therapy alone improve mortality?

# Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Non-blinded
- Central internet-based randomisation
- Stratified by centre
- Intention-to-treat analysis

# Setting
- 37 centres in Germany
- Study period: June 2009 to March 2012

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Acute MI (with or without ST elevation)
- Cardiogenic shock defined by:
  - BP < 90 mmHg for > 30 min or requiring catecholamine infusion to maintain BP > 90 mmHg
  - Clinical signs of pulmonary congestion
  - Impaired end-organ perfusion (e.g., altered mental status, cold/clammy skin, urine output < 30 ml/hr, lactate > 2)
- Early revascularisation (PCI or CABG) planned

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- CPR > 30 minutes
- Coma with fixed dilated pupils not induced by drugs
- Mechanical cause of cardiogenic shock
- No intrinsic heart action
- Onset of shock > 12 hours before screening
- Massive pulmonary embolism
- Age > 90
- Contraindication to IABP insertion

**Enrollment:**
- 600 patients randomised

# Intervention

**IABP Group:**
- Intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation
- Inserted before or immediately after PCI at physician’s discretion
- Initiated with 1:1 triggering
- Weaned when BP > 90 mmHg for > 30 minutes without support
- Median duration of IABP support: 3 days
- 13 patients (4.3%) did not receive IABP, mostly due to death prior to insertion

# Control
- No IABP
- 30 patients (10%) subsequently received IABP, mostly within 24 hours

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- 30-day all-cause mortality: no significant difference
  - IABP: 39.7%
  - Control: 41.3%
  - Relative risk: 0.96 (95% CI: 0.79–1.17), p = 0.69

## Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Re-infarction in hospital: 3% vs. 1.3%, p = 0.16
  - Peripheral ischaemic complications requiring intervention: 4.3% vs. 3.4%, p = 0.53
  - Life-threatening or severe bleeding: 3.3% vs. 4.4%, p = 0.51
  - Sepsis: 15.7% vs. 20.5%, p = 0.15
  - Mortality in patients with pre- vs. post-revascularisation IABP: 36.4% vs. 36.8%, p = 0.96
- SAPS II score improved at day 2 in IABP group (p = 0.02), but not at day 4 (p = 0.14)

# Authors’ Conclusions
The use of IABP compared to conventional therapy did not reduce mortality in patients with cardiogenic shock following acute MI.

# Strengths
- Randomised controlled trial
- Allocation concealment maintained
- Minimal loss to follow-up
- Multi-centre study

# Weaknesses
- Non-blinded
- High crossover rate between groups
- No specific criteria for left ventricular assist device use

# Bottom Line
In patients with cardiogenic shock following acute MI, IABP did not improve mortality or long-term secondary outcomes. The use of IABP did not significantly increase complications.
