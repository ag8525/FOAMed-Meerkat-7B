# Title
Advanced Reperfusion Strategies for Patients with Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest and Refractory Ventricular Fibrillation (ARREST)  
**D Yannopoulos et al.**  
The Lancet 2020; DOI:10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32338-2

# Clinical Question
In adults suffering out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) with an initial rhythm of VF or pulseless VT, does early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation improve survival to hospital discharge compared to standard ACLS resuscitation?

# Background
- OHCA has a poor survival rate.
- PARAMEDIC 2 trial showed a 30-day survival rate of 2.8% for all-cause OHCA.
- Half of OHCA patients with VF have refractory VF, with worse prognosis.
- Evidence for ECMO in OHCA is from observational studies; no prior randomised trials.
- ELSO registry (2016) showed 29% survival to discharge for refractory OHCA.
- Other studies show survival rates from 5.5% to 45% for all-cause OHCA.

# Design
- Phase 2, single-centre, open-label RCT.
- Randomisation verified on hospital arrival.
- Computer-generated randomisation (blocks of 2, 4, 6).
- Functional assessment at 3 and 6 months by blinded assessors.
- Pre-specified secondary outcomes: survival and favourable neurological outcome (mRS ≤3, CPC ≤2) at 3 and 6 months.
- Emergency consent process; written consent within 24 hours of waking.
- Mixed Bayesian and Frequentist statistical analysis.
- Primary outcome analysed every 30 participants using Bayesian statistics.
- Sample size: 174 patients (ECMO survival 37%, ACLS 12%, 90% power, 0.05 type 1 error).

# Setting
- University of Minnesota Medical Center, USA.
- August 2019 – June 2020.

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults 18–75 years old.
- Initial rhythm of VF or pulseless VT.
- No ROSC after 3 shocks.
- Body habitus supporting mechanical CPR.
- Estimated transport time <30 minutes.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Valid DNACPR.
- Mechanisms: drowning, trauma, burns, overdose.
- Pregnancy, terminal cancer, active bleeding.
- Prisoner or nursing home resident.
- Cath lab unavailable, contraindications to angiography.

**Enrollment:**
- 36 assessed, 30 enrolled (15 per arm).
- Baseline demographics similar between ECMO and ACLS groups.

# Intervention

**ECMO Group:**
- Immediate access to cardiac catheterisation lab.
- ECMO initiated if criteria met (EtCO2, PaO2, SpO2, lactate).
- Peripheral veno-arterial ECMO and coronary angiogram performed.
- Time from 999 call to ECMO: 59 min.

# Control

**ACLS Group:**
- Standard ACLS in ED.
- Treatment continued for 15 min or >60 min post-911 call.
- Transferred for angiography if ROSC achieved.

# Management Common to Both Groups
- Post-angiography care per local protocol in Cardiac ICU.
- Included 24 hours of TTM (34°C), no neuro-prognostication for 72 hours, CTB on admission and at 72 hours, continuous EEG until awakening.

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital discharge:**
  - ECMO: 6/14 (43%)
  - ACLS: 1/15 (7%)
  - Posterior probability of ECMO superiority: 0.9861

## Secondary Outcomes
- Cumulative survival better with ECMO (HR 0.16, p<0.0001).
- 6-month survival: 43% ECMO vs. 0% ACLS.
- Good functional outcomes in ECMO survivors (mRS 1.3, CPC 1.16).
- Length of ICU stay for ECMO survivors: 21.5 days.
- Length of hospital stay for ECMO survivors: 25.5 days.
- Adverse events: 166 vs. 47 (ECMO vs. ACLS).

# Authors’ Conclusions
Early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation significantly improves survival to hospital discharge in OHCA with refractory VF compared to standard ACLS.

# Strengths
- Well-conducted RCT in a complex cohort.
- Randomisation minimises selection bias.
- Fast ECMO initiation times.
- Excellent safety reporting.
- Adaptive randomisation based on Bayesian analysis.

# Weaknesses
- Single-centre study limits external validity.
- High bystander CPR rates and fast shock times.
- Small patient numbers.
- Early trial cessation may affect outcomes.
- No cost analysis despite being a secondary outcome.

# Bottom Line
This trial shows early ECMO initiation results in 43% survival to discharge for refractory shockable OHCA. Further multicentre trials are needed to validate these findings and assess generalisability.
