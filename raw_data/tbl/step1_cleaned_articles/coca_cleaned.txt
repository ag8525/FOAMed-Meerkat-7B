# Title
Effect of Intravenous or Intraosseous Calcium vs Saline on Return of Spontaneous Circulation in Adults With Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Randomized Clinical Trial  
**MF Vallentin, @LarsWAndersen1.**  
JAMA 2021; Published online November 30, 2021. doi:10.1001/jama.2021.20929

# Clinical Question
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does the administration of calcium compared with placebo improve sustained return of spontaneous circulation?

# Background
Calcium plays a crucial role in myocardial contractility, exhibiting both inotropic and vasopressor effects. Previous small RCTs in 1985 suggested potential benefits of calcium in cardiac arrest, though results were not statistically significant.

# Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Double-blinded
- Placebo-controlled
- Variable block sizes (2, 4, 6) in a 1:1 ratio
- Stratified by mobile emergency care unit stations
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

# Setting
- Pre-hospital services in central Denmark
- Study period: January 2020 to April 2021

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years
- Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest
- Received at least 1 dose of adrenaline

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Traumatic cardiac arrest (including drowning and external asphyxia)
- Known/suspected pregnancy
- Received adrenaline before arrival of prehospital personnel with the study drug
- Clinical indication for calcium administration

**Enrollment:**
- 397 patients randomised (197 to calcium, 200 to saline)
- 6 patients excluded post-randomisation due to exclusion criteria

# Intervention

**Calcium Group:**
- 5 mmol (10 ml ampoule) of calcium chloride administered IV or IO immediately after the 1st dose of adrenaline and again after the 2nd dose if needed

# Control

**Saline Group:**
- Normal saline administered similarly to the calcium group

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Sustained return of spontaneous circulation (palpable pulses or signs of circulation without chest compressions for >20 minutes)
  - Calcium: 19%
  - Saline: 27%
  - Risk ratio: 0.72 (95% CI: 0.49–1.03), p = 0.09

## Secondary Outcomes
- **Survival at 30 days:**
  - Calcium: 5.2%
  - Saline: 9.1%
  - Risk ratio: 0.57 (95% CI: 0.27–1.18), p = 0.17

- **Survival at 30 days with favourable neurological outcome:**
  - Calcium: 3.6%
  - Saline: 7.6%
  - Risk ratio: 0.48 (95% CI: 0.20–1.12), p = 0.12

- **Survival at 90 days:**
  - Calcium: 5.2%
  - Saline: 9.1%
  - Risk ratio: 0.57 (95% CI: 0.27–1.18)

- **Survival at 90 days with good neurological outcome:**
  - Calcium: 3.6%
  - Saline: 9.1%
  - Risk ratio: 0.40 (95% CI: 0.17–0.91)

## Tertiary Outcomes
- First ionised calcium after ROSC was significantly higher in the calcium group (1.41 vs 1.17 mmol/l)

## Bayesian Analysis
- Probability that calcium has a positive effect (RR > 1) on the primary outcome is 4%

# Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, treatment with calcium compared with saline did not significantly improve sustained return of spontaneous circulation.

# Strengths
- Randomised controlled trial
- Blinded
- Balanced trial groups
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov
- No loss to follow-up

# Weaknesses
- Trial stopped early, not reaching pre-planned sample size
- Median time to drug administration was 17 minutes post-arrest, which may not be generalisable to in-hospital settings
- Regional trial with specific pre-hospital setup

# Bottom Line
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, calcium administration compared with saline did not significantly improve sustained return of spontaneous circulation. Point estimates suggest potential harm from calcium use in this population.
