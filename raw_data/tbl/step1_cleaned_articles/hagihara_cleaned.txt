# Title
Prehospital Epinephrine Use and Survival Among Patients with Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest  
**Hagihara.**  
JAMA 2012; 307(11): 1165-1168. doi:10.1001/jama.2012.294

# Clinical Question
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, is the use of adrenaline associated with improved mortality compared to not using adrenaline?

# Design
- Prospective, non-randomised cohort study
- Concurrent control group

# Setting
- Pre-hospital services in Japan
- Study period: 1 January 2005 to 31 December 2008

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients who experienced an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest before emergency services arrival

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >60 minutes from call to scene arrival
- >480 minutes from call to hospital arrival
- Missing data on adrenaline administration

**Enrollment:**
- 417,188 patients

# Intervention
- Patients treated with epinephrine
- From April 2006, certified paramedics were permitted to use adrenaline, forming the intervention and control groups

# Control
- Patients not treated with adrenaline

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcomes
- **Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC) before hospital arrival:**
  - Unadjusted OR: 3.75 (95% CI: 3.59–3.91)
  - Adjusted OR: 2.36 (95% CI: 2.22–2.50)

- **Survival at 1 month:**
  - Unadjusted OR: 1.15 (95% CI: 1.07–1.23)
  - Adjusted OR: 0.46 (95% CI: 0.42–0.51)

- **Good Neurological Outcome (CPC 1 or 2):**
  - Unadjusted OR: 0.61 (95% CI: 0.53–0.70)
  - Adjusted OR: 0.31 (95% CI: 0.26–0.36)

- Similar results were found in a propensity-matched subgroup (n=26,802)

# Authors’ Conclusions
The use of prehospital adrenaline was significantly associated with increased ROSC but decreased chances of survival and good functional outcomes.

# Strengths
- Large sample size
- Consistent results across different analytical models

# Weaknesses
- Non-randomised design
- Baseline characteristics generally favoured the adrenaline group (e.g., higher rates of bystander CPR, use of public AEDs, VF/VT)
- Data on in-hospital CPR not included
- Number of adrenaline doses not recorded
- Included only pre-hospital cardiac arrests
- One-month survival influenced by varying in-hospital care

# Bottom Line
This study found an association between adrenaline use in cardiac arrest and decreased survival and poor neurological outcomes. Despite being non-randomised, the large sample size warrants consideration. If adrenaline were a new drug, it might not be included in guidelines, but it remains due to historical use.
