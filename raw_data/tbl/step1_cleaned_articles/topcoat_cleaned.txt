# Title
Treatment of Submassive Pulmonary Embolism with Tenecteplase or Placebo: Cardiopulmonary Outcomes at 3 Months (TOPCOAT Trial)  
**Kline et al.**  
Journal of Thrombosis and Haemostasis 2014; 12: 459-468. doi:10.1111/jth.12521

# Clinical Question
In patients with submassive pulmonary embolism (PE), does the use of tenecteplase plus heparin, compared to heparin alone, improve patient-oriented outcomes?

# Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Double-blinded
- Placebo-controlled

# Setting
- 8 centres in the USA
- Study period: August 2008 to October 2012

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with submassive PE diagnosed on CT-PA
- Normal systolic blood pressure
- Right ventricular strain (e.g., hypokinesis on echocardiography, elevated Troponin I or T, BNP >90 pg/ml, or NT-proBNP >900 pg/ml)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Blood pressure <90 mmHg
- Contraindications to thrombolysis

**Enrollment:**
- 83 patients randomised

# Intervention

**Tenecteplase Group:**
- Tenecteplase (weight-tiered dose per TNKase insert) plus heparin

# Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Placebo plus heparin

**Both Groups:**
- Patients treated with 1 mg/kg enoxaparin or 200 units/kg dalteparin subcutaneously prior to tenecteplase or placebo

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Composite adverse outcome within 5 days (PE-related or treatment-related)
  - PE-related: death, hypotension requiring vasopressor infusion, intubation
  - Treatment-related: death from haemorrhage, major bleeding (e.g., intracranial haemorrhage, active bleeding with >2 g/dL drop in haemoglobin requiring transfusion, bleeding requiring surgery)

## Secondary Outcomes (within 90 days)
- Recurrent PE
- Poor functional capacity (e.g., right ventricular hypokinesis, exercise intolerance, severe dyspnoea)
- Poor physical health-related quality of life (e.g., SF36 PCS score <30, VEINES QOL survey <40)

**Results:**
- Significant improvement in tenecteplase group vs. placebo: 15% vs. 37% had at least 1 adverse outcome (Difference = 22%, 95% CI: 3.2–40, p = 0.017, NNT = 5)
- Secondary outcomes at 90-day follow-up:
  - NYHA class 3/4: 5.4% vs. 20.5%, p = 0.051
  - 6-minute walk distance: 407 vs. 403 m, p = 0.9
  - 6-minute walk distance <330 m: 16% vs. 28%, p = 0.19
  - Self-assessment of overall health: 3.3 vs. 2.4, p = 0.036 (only significant individual outcome)

# Authors’ Conclusions
Treatment with tenecteplase was associated with an increased probability of a favourable outcome.

# Strengths
- Randomised, double-blinded design
- Use of patient-oriented outcomes
- CONSORT diagram provided

# Weaknesses
- Underpowered; trial terminated early due to lead author relocation
- Composite outcome included many variables, complicating interpretation
- Majority of patients had lower-risk profiles (e.g., heart rates <110, SBP >100 mmHg)
- Financial support from Genentech, manufacturer of tenecteplase

# Bottom Line
In patients with submassive PE, tenecteplase reduced adverse outcomes based on a complex composite outcome. However, the only significant independent variable was self-assessed overall health at 90 days. The study's small size and incomplete nature limit its conclusions. I would not be persuaded to thrombolyse a patient with submassive PE based on this study.
