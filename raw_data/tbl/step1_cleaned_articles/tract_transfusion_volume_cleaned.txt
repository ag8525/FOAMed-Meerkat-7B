# Title
Transfusion Volume for Children with Severe Anaemia in Africa  
**K. Maitland.**  
New England Journal of Medicine 2019; 381: 420-431. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1900100

# Clinical Question
In African children presenting to hospital with severe anaemia, is a blood transfusion volume of 30 ml/kg superior to 20 ml/kg in reducing 28-day mortality?

# Background
- Severe anaemia is a major cause of death and morbidity in sub-Saharan African children.
- Blood products are scarce in most African countries.
- WHO guidelines recommend a 20 ml/kg blood transfusion for children with profound anaemia (<4 g/dL) or life-threatening severe anaemia (4 to 6 g/dL).
- This study investigates whether a 30 ml/kg transfusion is superior to 20 ml/kg.

# Design
- Open-label, multi-centre, factorial, randomised trial.
- Stratification by centre and illness severity.
- Intention-to-treat analysis.
- Power calculation aimed to detect a 30% relative difference in 28-day mortality with 80% power.

# Setting
- Three hospitals in Uganda and one in Malawi.
- Study period: September 2014 to May 2017.

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Children aged 2 months to 12 years with complicated or uncomplicated severe anaemia.
- Complicated severe anaemia: Hb < 4 g/dL or < 6 g/dL with severity features.
- Uncomplicated severe anaemia: Hb 4-6 g/dL without severity features.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Chronic disease (e.g., kidney or liver failure, malignancy).
- Children admitted with burns, trauma, or surgery.
- Prior transfusion during primary hospitalisation.
- Exclusively breastfed infants.

**Enrollment:**
- 3,199 children randomised.
- 2,418 (75.7%) had Hb < 4 g/dL.
- 1,137 (35.6%) had two or more severity features.
- 64.1% had malaria.

# Intervention
- 30 ml/kg of whole blood equivalent (15 ml/kg of packed red cells).

# Control
- 20 ml/kg of whole blood equivalent (10 ml/kg of packed red cells).

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- No difference in 28-day mortality:
  - 30 ml/kg: 55/1,592 (3.4%) died.
  - 20 ml/kg: 72/1,596 (4.5%) died.
  - Hazard ratio: 0.76 (95% CI 0.54–1.08); p = 0.12.

## Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in mortality at 48 hours, 90 days, or 180 days.
- Shorter time to hospital discharge in the intervention group.
- Higher correction of anaemia in the intervention group.
- Fewer repeat transfusions in the 30 ml/kg group.
- No significant difference in adverse events or hospital readmissions.

# Authors’ Conclusions
No overall evidence of mortality difference between 30 ml/kg and 20 ml/kg transfusion volumes in children with severe anaemia. The interaction with body temperature may warrant further research.

# Strengths
- Large, multi-centre RCT addressing an important clinical question.
- High follow-up rate despite resource limitations.
- Well-matched groups at baseline.
- Intention-to-treat analysis with appropriate power calculation.

# Weaknesses
- Overestimated expected mortality led to underpowered study.
- Open-label design may introduce bias.
- Exclusion of children with chronic conditions and those needing urgent transfusion.
- Limited external validity to developed world settings.

# Bottom Line
A transfusion volume one-third higher than WHO recommendations did not reduce 28-day mortality in sub-Saharan African children with anaemia. Current practice should not change based on this data.
