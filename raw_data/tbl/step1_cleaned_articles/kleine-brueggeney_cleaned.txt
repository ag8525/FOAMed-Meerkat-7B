# Title
Evaluation of Six Videolaryngoscopes in 720 Patients with a Simulated Difficult Airway: A Multicentre Randomised Controlled Trial  
**Kleine-Brueggeney et al.**  
BJA 2016; 116(5): 670-679. doi: 10.1093/bja/aew058

# Clinical Question
In patients undergoing elective surgery with a simulated difficult airway (using a cervical collar), does the choice of videolaryngoscope affect the first-attempt intubation success rate?

# Design
- Multicentre randomised controlled trial
- Computer-generated randomisation using sealed opaque envelopes
- Block randomisation of 10, separated for each anaesthetist
- Intention-to-treat analysis

# Setting
- 3 university hospitals in Switzerland
- Study period: December 2012 to January 2015

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients undergoing elective surgery requiring tracheal intubation
- ASA I-III

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Risk of aspiration
- Known or predicted difficult airway (BMI >35 kg/m², Mallampati >III, thyromental distance <6 cm, interincisor distance <3.5 cm, known difficult mask ventilation/laryngoscopy, planned or previous history of awake tracheal intubation)

**Enrollment:**
- 720 patients (120 in each group)
- Baseline characteristics (sex, age, BMI, Mallampati score, thyromental distance, mouth opening with and without collar) were similar across groups

# Intervention
- Six different videolaryngoscopes evaluated:
  - **Unchannelled:**
    - Reusable C-MAC™ D-blade and stylet
    - GlideScope™ blade 3 with stylet
    - McGrath™ with MAC blade 3 and stylet
  - **Channelled:**
    - Airtraq™ size 2 in women and 3 in men
    - A.P. Advance™ difficult airway blade
    - KingVision™ with blade 3
- Airway managed by one of twelve consultant anaesthetists
- Cervical collar used to simulate a difficult airway, adjusted for minimal mouth opening of 18 mm
- Successful intubation defined as tube placement in the trachea within 180 seconds, confirmed by end-tidal CO₂
- Two intubation attempts allowed with the randomised videolaryngoscope

# Control
- Each videolaryngoscope compared with the other five

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- **First-attempt intubation success rate:**
  - McGrath: 117 patients (98%), 95% CI 92-99
  - C-MAC: 114 patients (95%), 95% CI 89-98
  - KingVision: 104 patients (87%), 95% CI 79-92
  - GlideScope: 102 patients (85%), 95% CI 77-90
  - Airtraq: 102 patients (85%), 95% CI 77-90
  - A.P. Advance: 44 patients (37%), 95% CI 28-46

## Secondary Outcomes
- **Subjective grade of handling:**
  - Insertion: McGrath (94%) > C-MAC (91%) > GlideScope (79%) > Airtraq (73%) > KingVision (67%) > A.P. Advance (58%)
  - Quality of view: McGrath (93%) = GlideScope (93%) = Airtraq (93%) > C-MAC (92%) > KingVision (91%) > A.P. Advance (73%)
  - Ease of tube insertion: Airtraq (82%) > McGrath (78%) > KingVision (73%) > C-MAC (69%) > GlideScope (64%) > A.P. Advance (52%)
- **Median times (seconds):**
  - Time to view vocal cords: C-MAC (17) < McGrath (18) < GlideScope (19) < Airtraq (20) < KingVision (26) < A.P. Advance (30)
  - Time to advance tube: Airtraq (27) < KingVision (31) < McGrath (33) < C-MAC (36) < GlideScope (40) < A.P. Advance (50)
  - Intubation time of successful attempt: Airtraq (47) < McGrath (53) < C-MAC (56) < KingVision (59) < GlideScope (60) < A.P. Advance (93)
- **Adverse events:** No significant differences in side effects (hoarseness, sore throat, dysphagia, postoperative nausea and vomiting)

# Authors’ Conclusions
The trial revealed differences in the performance of six videolaryngoscopes in patients with restricted neck movement and limited mouth opening. First-attempt success rates were 85–98%, except for the A.P. Advance. The McGrath and C-MAC D-blade achieved the highest success and lowest tissue trauma rates, highlighting the importance of blade design.

# Strengths
- Registered at clinicaltrials.gov (NCT01692535)
- Block randomisation ensured each anaesthetist intubated 10 times with each videolaryngoscope
- Standardised induction and neuromuscular blocking agent (rocuronium 0.6 mg/kg)
- Adequate neuromuscular block controlled by loss of 1 Hz muscle twitching (TOF Watch™)
- Participating anaesthetists were airway management experts trained with all videolaryngoscopes

# Weaknesses
- Lack of a standard Macintosh laryngoscope as a control
- Performance ability based on self-assessment for competency
- Exclusion of patients with predicted difficult airways may limit generalisability
- Simulation with a collar does not represent all difficult airway scenarios

# Bottom Line
The primary hypothesis of a first-attempt intubation success rate >90% was accepted only for the McGrath. The A.P. Advance performed below other comparators. Despite limitations in internal validity due to subjectivity and unblinded operators, the trial provides a useful overview of performance and handling variations among videolaryngoscopes.
