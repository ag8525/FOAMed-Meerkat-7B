# Title
Effect of Vitamin C, Hydrocortisone, and Thiamine vs Hydrocortisone Alone on Time Alive and Free of Vasopressor Support Among Patients With Septic Shock (VITAMINS Trial)  
**Fujii et al.**  
JAMA 2020; Published online January 17, 2020. doi:10.1001/jama.2019.22176

# Clinical Question
Does treatment with vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine lead to a more rapid resolution of septic shock compared with hydrocortisone alone?

# Background
Sepsis is a life-threatening condition characterized by organ dysfunction due to a dysregulated host response to infection. High-dose intravenous vitamin C has been explored as an adjunctive therapy in sepsis for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Previous studies have suggested potential benefits of vitamin C and thiamine in sepsis management.

# Design
- Pilot, feasibility, multi-centre, randomised, open-label, phase IIb clinical trial
- Random allocation sequence with permuted block sizes stratified by site
- Sample size recalculated during the study based on pooled standard deviation

# Setting
- 10 intensive care units in Australia, New Zealand, and Brazil
- Study period: May 2018 to July 2019

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients admitted to ICU with a primary diagnosis of septic shock based on Sepsis-3 criteria:
  - Suspected or documented infection
  - Acute increase of ≥2 points in SOFA score
  - Lactate level >2 mmol/L
  - Vasopressor dependence for ≥2 hours at enrollment

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age <18 years
- Do-not-resuscitate order
- Imminent death
- Septic shock diagnosis >24 hours prior
- Contraindications for study drugs
- Other indications for hydrocortisone

**Enrollment:**
- 786 patients screened; 216 randomised; 211 included in final analysis

# Intervention
- **Intervention Group:** IV vitamin C (1.5 g every 6 hours), hydrocortisone (50 mg every 6 hours), and thiamine (200 mg every 12 hours)
- Continued until vasopressor cessation or other stopping criteria met

# Control
- **Control Group:** IV hydrocortisone (50 mg every 6 hours)
- Thiamine allowed at clinician's discretion, but not vitamin C

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in median time alive and free of vasopressors up to day 7:
  - Intervention: 122.1 hours [IQR, 76.3-145.4]
  - Control: 124.6 hours [IQR, 82.1-147.0]
  - Median difference: –0.6 hours [95% CI, –8.3 to 7.2]; P = .83

## Secondary Outcomes
- Of 10 pre-specified secondary outcomes, only change in SOFA score at day 3 showed a statistically significant difference

**Adverse Events:**
- Reported in 2 patients (fluid overload and hyperglycemia) in the intervention group and 1 patient (gastrointestinal bleeding) in the control group

# Authors’ Conclusions
Treatment with intravenous vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine did not significantly improve the duration of time alive and free of vasopressor administration over 7 days compared to hydrocortisone alone.

# Strengths
- Clinically important question with relevant outcomes
- A priori publication of trial protocol and statistical analysis plan
- Conducted across multiple sites in diverse economic settings

# Weaknesses
- Open-label design with potential for bias
- Effects of vitamin C and thiamine not assessed separately
- Thiamine levels not measured
- Underpowered for mortality or other patient-centered outcomes
- Adverse events not systematically examined

# Bottom Line
The VITAMINS trial does not support the use of the HAT protocol (hydrocortisone, ascorbic acid, and thiamine) for treating septic shock. Further research is needed to explore the timing and potential benefits of these interventions.
