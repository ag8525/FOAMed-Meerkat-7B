# Title
Immediate versus Delayed Fluid Resuscitation for Hypotensive Patients with Penetrating Torso Injuries  
**Bickell et al.**  
N Engl J Med 1994; 331: 1105-1109. doi:10.1056/NEJM199410273311701

# Clinical Question
In hypotensive patients with penetrating torso trauma, does a delayed fluid resuscitation strategy, compared to immediate fluid resuscitation, improve survival?

# Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Randomisation based on weekday presentation
- Non-blinded
- Intention-to-treat analysis

# Setting
- Pre-hospital setting serving a single trauma centre in the USA
- Study period: November 1989 to December 1992

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with penetrating torso trauma
- Blood pressure < 90 mmHg

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients for whom fluid resuscitation would not affect the outcome

**Enrollment:**
- 1069 patients randomised; 598 included in analysis

# Intervention

**Delayed Resuscitation Group:**
- Fluid resuscitation delayed until operative intervention
- Mean fluid given prior to surgery: 386 ml

# Control

**Immediate Resuscitation Group:**
- Fluid resuscitation given before operative intervention
- Mean fluid given prior to surgery: 2611 ml

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- **Survival to discharge:** 
  - Delayed resuscitation: 70%
  - Immediate resuscitation: 62%
  - p = 0.04

## Secondary Outcomes
- **Blood Pressure:**
  - On scene: 58 mmHg (immediate) vs. 59 mmHg (delayed)
  - At initial operative intervention: 112 mmHg (immediate) vs. 113 mmHg (delayed)
- **Intra-operative fluids per minute:** 
  - 117 ml (immediate) vs. 91 ml (delayed)

# Authors’ Conclusions
Aggressive administration of IV fluids to hypotensive patients with penetrating torso trauma should be delayed until operative intervention.

# Strengths
- Randomised trial with appropriate power calculation
- Intention-to-treat analysis

# Weaknesses
- Randomisation based on day of presentation; non-blinded with no allocation concealment
- Blood pressure improved in both groups by the time of surgery; unclear if vasopressors were used
- Practices have evolved with early use of blood products
- Single-centre study; results may not be generalisable to centres with different surgical timelines

# Bottom Line
Aggressive fluid resuscitation in hypotensive patients following penetrating torso trauma should be avoided. The priority in bleeding trauma patients is timely surgical intervention.
