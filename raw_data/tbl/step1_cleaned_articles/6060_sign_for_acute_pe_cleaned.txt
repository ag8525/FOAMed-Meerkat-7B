# Title
Disturbed Right Ventricular Ejection Pattern as a New Doppler Echocardiographic Sign of Acute Pulmonary Embolism  
**Kurzyna et al.**  
Am J Cardiology 2002; 90:507-511

# Clinical Question
In patients with suspected acute pulmonary embolism (APE), do echocardiographic features of disturbed right ventricular ejection allow accurate diagnosis?

# Background
- APE often causes right ventricular (RV) pressure overload, detectable by echocardiography.
- In patients with pre-existing cardiorespiratory disease, RV overload may be chronic.
- The 60/60 sign may aid in diagnosing APE in patients with pre-existing pulmonary hypertension.

# Design
- Prospective observational diagnostic cohort
- Consecutive patients
- Not stated if assessors were blinded to gold standard results
- Statistical analysis: Student’s t-test, Mann’s U test, Pearson’s chi-square test with Yates’ correction

# Setting
- Single tertiary pulmonary medicine referral centre, Poland
- Dates of data collection not documented

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Clinical suspicion of APE following history, examination, arterial blood gases, chest X-ray, and ECG

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Time from symptom onset/aggravation to study enrolment >14 days

**Enrollment:**
- 100 patients (57 male, 43 female)
- Mean age: 61 ± 15 years
- 54 patients with known cardiorespiratory disease (23 with COPD, 17 with CHF)
- 62 patients assessed in ICU

# Tests of Interest
- Trans-thoracic ECHO
- **60/60 Sign:** Pulmonary valve acceleration time ≤60ms and tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient ≤60mmHg
- **McConnell Sign:** Normokinesia/hyperkinesia of RV apical segment despite hypokinesia/akinesia of remaining RV free wall
- **RV Pressure Overload:** Defined by ≥1 of the following:
  - Right-sided cardiac thrombus
  - RV diastolic dimension >30mm or RV:LV ratio >1
  - Systolic flattening of interventricular septum
  - Pulmonary valve acceleration time <90ms or tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient >30mmHg (absent RV hypertrophy)

# Gold Standard Investigation
- APE diagnosed with:
  - Spiral CT (n=63)
  - High-probability lung perfusion scan (n=1)
  - Pulmonary angiography (n=1)
  - Surgery (n=1)
  - Autopsy (n=1)
- APE excluded with:
  - Negative CT and venous compression ultrasound (n=20)
  - Negative lung perfusion scan (n=10)
  - Autopsy (n=3)

# Outcomes

## Diagnostic Performance
- **APE Confirmed in 67 Patients**
  - **60/60 Sign:** Diagnosed 17/67 correctly; 2 false positives
  - **McConnell Sign:** Diagnosed 13/67 correctly; no false positives
  - **RV Pressure Overload:** Diagnosed 54/67 correctly; 18 false positives

- **Specificity in Patients Without Previous Cardiorespiratory Disease:**
  - 60/60 and McConnell’s signs were 100% specific for APE

- **Comparisons in APE Patients (60/60 Sign Positive vs Negative):**
  - Hypoxaemia: PaO₂ 51mmHg vs 60mmHg, p=0.01
  - IVC collapsibility: 19% vs 33%, p=0.02

- **ECHO Features in APE vs Non-APE:**
  - RV diameter: 31mm vs 28mm, p=0.01
  - RV:LV ratio: 0.72 vs 0.63, p=0.04
  - Septal flattening: 36% vs 12%, p=0.02
  - Tricuspid regurgitation gradient: 41mmHg vs 28mmHg, p=0.01
  - Pulmonary valve acceleration time: 73ms vs 95ms, p<0.0001

# Authors’ Conclusions
The 60/60 and McConnell signs are reliable but not sensitive indicators of APE.

# Strengths
- Consecutive patients
- Prospective design
- Appropriate gold standard

# Weaknesses
- Single-centre study with high APE prevalence limits external validity
- Blinding of assessors to gold standard not stated
- No sample size calculation

# Bottom Line
In a tertiary referral centre with high pre-test probability of APE, McConnell’s Sign has a high positive likelihood ratio. In patients without previous cardiorespiratory disease, the 60/60 sign also shows a high positive likelihood ratio. A negative result for either sign adds little diagnostic information. In high-probability APE cases where CT is not immediately available, bedside echo for 60/60 and McConnell’s signs can increase diagnostic confidence.
