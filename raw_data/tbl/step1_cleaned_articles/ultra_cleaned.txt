# Title
Ultra-early Tranexamic Acid after Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (ULTRA Trial)  
**Post, R. et al.**  
Lancet 2021; 397: 112-118. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(20)32518-6

# Clinical Question
In adults presenting with subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH), does ultra-early short-term treatment with tranexamic acid improve clinical outcomes at 6 months?

# Background
Tranexamic acid (TXA) has been considered effective in reducing bleeding, but recent studies suggest its use needs refinement. Patients with SAH are at risk of re-bleeding, often within 24 hours of the initial event. TXA may reduce re-bleeding rates, but its impact on overall clinical outcomes remains unclear.

# Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Computer-generated allocation
- Stratified by treatment centre
- Permuted blocks within each centre
- Non-blinded, except for assessors at 6-month follow-up
- Intention-to-treat analysis, with additional "as treated" and "per protocol" analyses

# Setting
- 24 sites in the Netherlands (16 referring hospitals and 8 SAH treatment centres)
- Data collection: July 2013 to July 2019

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years
- Admitted to participating centres
- Symptoms <24 hours
- Non-contrast CT confirming SAH

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Perimesencephalic bleeding pattern on CT with GCS 13-15 and no loss of consciousness or focal neurological deficit
- Traumatic SAH pattern on CT
- Ongoing treatment for DVT/PE
- Hypercoagulability disorder
- Pregnancy
- Creatinine >150 µmol/L
- Imminent death within 24 hours

**Enrollment:**
- 955 patients (480 in TXA group, 475 in control group)

# Intervention

**Tranexamic Acid Group:**
- 1g bolus followed by 1g infusion every 8 hours
- Continued until endovascular or surgical treatment, or a maximum of 24 hours

# Control
- Standard treatment

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Good clinical outcome at 6 months (mRS 0-3): No significant difference
  - TXA: 60%
  - Control: 64%
  - OR 0.87 (95% CI: 0.67–1.13); aOR 0.86 (95% CI: 0.66–1.12)

## Secondary Outcomes
- Excellent outcome (mRS 0-2) at 6 months: Lower in TXA group
  - TXA: 48%
  - Control: 56%
  - OR 0.74 (95% CI: 0.57–0.96); aOR 0.73 (95% CI: 0.57–0.95)
- No significant difference in:
  - Ordinal shift analysis of mRS at 6 months
  - All-cause mortality at 6 months (TXA: 27%, Control: 24%)
  - Serious adverse effects (TXA: 80%, Control: 78%)
  - Re-bleeding before aneurysm treatment (TXA: 10%, Control: 14%)
  - Delayed cerebral ischaemia (TXA: 23%, Control: 22%)
  - Thromboembolic complications during endovascular treatment (TXA: 11%, Control: 13%)

# Authors’ Conclusions
The use of tranexamic acid after CT confirmation of SAH does not improve clinical outcomes at 6 months as measured by mRS.

# Strengths
- Randomised and powered for clinical outcomes
- Adjusted OR used to account for potential confounders
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Low loss to follow-up (1%)

# Weaknesses
- Unblinded, potential for bias
- Single-country study, limiting external validity
- High number of patients with no aneurysms on angiography
- Median time from symptoms to TXA was 185 minutes
- mRS is subjective and scored by one assessor

# Bottom Line
This study does not show any clear clinical benefit from tranexamic acid use in SAH. While there may be potential future use, given the risk of delayed cerebral ischaemia, routine use is not recommended.
