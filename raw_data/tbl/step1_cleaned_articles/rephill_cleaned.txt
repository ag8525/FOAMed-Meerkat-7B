# Title
Resuscitation with Blood Products in Patients with Trauma-Related Haemorrhagic Shock Receiving Prehospital Care (RePHILL Trial)  
**Crombe N et al.**  
Lancet Haematology 2022; online March 7. doi:10.1016/S2352-3026(22)00040-0

# Clinical Question
In patients with trauma-related haemorrhagic shock, does prehospital administration of packed red cells and lyophilised plasma (LyoPlas) compared with normal saline reduce the composite outcome of mortality and failure to reach lactate clearance?

# Background
- Early transfusion in traumatic haemorrhagic shock is expanding from military to civilian settings.
- Evidence on prehospital transfusion benefits is limited and of low quality.
- Previous RCTs (PAMPER and COMBAT) showed discordant results on prehospital plasma transfusion.
- The PROPPR trial (2015) showed no significant mortality difference in hospital settings but reduced death from exsanguination with a 1:1:1 plasma, platelets, and RBCs ratio.

# Design
- Multicentre, open-label RCT with 1:1 allocation
- Conducted across 4 prehospital critical care services in the UK
- Randomisation: November 29, 2016, to January 2, 2021
- Temporary suspension during COVID-19 pandemic
- Allocation concealment with identical boxes for PRBC-LyoPlas and saline
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Blinded outcome assessors
- Ethics approval obtained; consent delayed due to urgency

# Setting
- Four prehospital critical care services in the UK

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (>16 years) with traumatic injury and hypotension (SBP < 90 mmHg) or absent palpable radial pulse
- Hypotension attributed to haemorrhage

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior transfusion of blood products
- Known refusal of blood products
- Pregnancy
- Isolated head injury without haemorrhage
- Prisoners

**Enrollment:**
- 432 participants: 209 to PRBC + LyoPlas group, 223 to saline group
- COVID-19 and funding issues prevented reaching the target of 490 participants

# Intervention

**PRBC + LyoPlas Group:**
- Up to 2 units of O negative, Rh negative, Kell negative, leukodepleted packed cells and 2 units of LyoPlas
- Alternating administration
- Further resuscitation with saline if SBP < 90 mmHg

# Control

**Normal Saline Group:**
- Up to 4 x 250 ml bags of 0.9% normal saline

**Common Management:**
- Fluids delivered through a fluid warmer
- TXA therapy in 96% of intervention and 98% of control group
- Interventions continued until hospital arrival or SBP > 90 mmHg

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Composite of mortality or failure to reach lactate clearance (<20% per hour in the first 2 hours)
- Intervention vs control: 64% vs 65% [ARR 1.01 (95% CI 0.88-1.17)]

## Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in mortality, lactate clearance, vital signs, or INR
- Higher haemoglobin on ED arrival in intervention group
- More blood products given in intervention group

# Authors’ Conclusions
The trial did not demonstrate a benefit for the composite outcome of mortality and lactate clearance in trauma patients with haemorrhagic shock receiving PRBC-LyoPlas compared with 0.9% saline.

# Strengths
- Allocation concealment and intention-to-treat analysis
- Blinded outcome assessors
- Addressed an important clinical question

# Weaknesses
- Patients were not anaemic on average, potentially underestimating treatment effect
- Composite outcomes with variable importance
- Short time from randomisation to hospitalisation
- Underpowered trial with wide confidence intervals
- Differences in trial design compared to PAMPER trial

# Bottom Line
The logistical challenges and costs of carrying packed cells and LyoPlas for prehospital administration in trauma-related haemorrhagic shock are substantial. In this trial, prehospital transfusion did not benefit patient outcomes. Further trials are needed to identify specific patient groups that may benefit.
