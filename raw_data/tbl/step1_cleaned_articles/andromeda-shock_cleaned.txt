# Title
Effect of a Resuscitation Strategy Targeting Peripheral Perfusion Status vs Serum Lactate Levels on 28-Day Mortality Among Patients With Septic Shock (ANDROMEDA-SHOCK)  
**Hernandez et al.**  
JAMA 2019; 321(7):654-664. doi:10.1001/jama.2019.0071

# Clinical Question
In adult patients with early septic shock, does peripheral perfusion-targeted resuscitation, compared to lactate level-targeted resuscitation, improve all-cause mortality at 28 days?

# Background
- The Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends lactate normalization during resuscitation.
- Issues with lactate as a target include non-hypoperfusion causes, delayed normalization, and limited availability.
- Capillary refill time (CRT) is a potential alternative, offering rapid response, ease of use, and independence from resources.

# Design
- Multicenter, randomized controlled, superiority trial
- Random allocation with central concealment
- Randomization by permutated blocks of 8 (1:1 allocation)
- Clinicians non-blinded
- Power calculation: 420 patients for 90% power to detect a 15% absolute risk reduction (alpha 0.05)
- Intention-to-treat analysis

# Setting
- 28 hospitals in 5 South and Central American countries (Argentina, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, Uruguay)

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- ≥18 years old with early (<4 hours) septic shock
- Septic shock defined by Sepsis-3: suspected/confirmed infection, lactate ≥2 mmol/L, vasopressors to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg after 20 ml/kg fluid over 60 minutes

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Bleeding, severe ARDS, dialysis, do-not-resuscitate status

**Enrollment:**
- 1,327 assessed, 424 randomized (212 CRT target, 212 lactate target)
- Baseline characteristics similar between groups (mean age 63 years, APACHE score 22, SOFA score 9.65)

# Intervention

**CRT Group:**
- CRT measured every 30 mins, then hourly after normalization for 8 hours
- CRT measured by applying a glass slide to the right index finger until blanching, maintained for 10 seconds
- Abnormal CRT defined as >3 seconds; goal to normalize CRT <3 seconds

# Control

**Lactate Group:**
- Lactate measured every 2 hours for 8 hours
- Goal to normalize or decrease lactate by 20% every 2 hours

# Management Common to Both Groups
- Protocol for unmet goals (CRT <3 seconds or lactate fall <20% every 2 hours):
  - Step 1: Fluid responsiveness assessment (e.g., pulse pressure variation)
  - Step 2: Vasopressor test for chronic hypertensive patients
  - Step 3: Inodilator test (dobutamine or milrinone)
- Adherence to Surviving Sepsis Guidelines

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- 28-day mortality: 92 (43.4%) in lactate group vs. 74 (34.9%) in CRT group
  - Hazard ratio: 0.75 (95% CI: 0.55–1.02); p = 0.06
  - Risk difference: −8.5% (95% CI: −18.2% to 1.2%)

## Secondary Outcomes
- CRT group received 408 ml less fluid in first 8 hours
- Less adrenaline use (9.9% vs. 16.5%, p=0.01)
- Lower lactate at 48 and 72 hours
- Lower SOFA score at 72 hours in CRT group: −1.00 (95% CI: −1.97 to −0.02); p = 0.045
- No significant differences in mechanical ventilation, RRT, vasopressor-free days, ICU/hospital length of stay

# Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with septic shock, a resuscitation strategy targeting CRT normalization did not significantly reduce 28-day mortality compared to lactate-targeted resuscitation.

# Strengths
- Multinational RCT with allocation concealment
- Early intervention (1.4 hours from eligibility to randomization)
- Standardized CRT assessment
- Reflects contemporary practices

# Weaknesses
- Underpowered to detect the pre-specified mortality difference
- Non-blinding of clinicians may introduce bias
- CRT group required additional staff education
- Complex intervention with parts lacking strong evidence base
- Day 28 mortality as a primary outcome is unstable

# Bottom Line
CRT is a potential alternative to lactate as a resuscitation target in sepsis, especially in resource-limited settings. Further studies are needed to validate CRT as an exclusive target.
