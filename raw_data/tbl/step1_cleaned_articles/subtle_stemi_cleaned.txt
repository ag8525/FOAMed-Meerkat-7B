# Title
Electrocardiographic Differentiation of Early Repolarization from Subtle ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction  
**Smith S.**  
Annals of Emergency Medicine 2012; 60(1): 45-56. doi:10.1016/j.annemergmed.2012.02.015

# Clinical Question
Are there specific ECG characteristics that can differentiate between anterior ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and early repolarization?

# Design
- Retrospective cohort study  
- Derivation and validation study  
- ECG measurements recorded manually  
- Readers not completely blinded to diagnosis  
- Multivariate logistic regression  
- Model concordance assessed with c-statistic  

# Setting
- Two urban hospitals in the USA  
- Data collected from 2003 to 2009  

# Population

**STEMI Group:**

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Acute LAD occlusion confirmed on coronary angiogram with TIMI 0/1 flow  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Bundle branch block or arrhythmia  
- Obvious STEMI ECG changes (e.g., ST elevation >5mm, nonconcave morphology)  
- Number of patients: 60 in derivation group, 83 in validation group  

**Early Repolarization Group:**

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with chest pain and ECGs coded as early repolarization  
- Final diagnosis of non-ischaemic chest pain with 3 serial negative cardiac troponin I markers  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- <1mm ST elevation in any leads V2-V4  
- Number of patients: 70 in derivation group, 101 in validation group  

# Tests of Interest
- ST-segment elevation in leads V2-V4 at the J point and 60 ms after  
- T-wave amplitude in leads V2-V4  
- R-wave amplitude in leads V2-V4  
- Computerised QTc  
- Degree of upward concavity  
- J-wave notching  
- T-wave height in V1 and V6  

# Gold Standard Investigation
- **STEMI patients:** Coronary angiogram  
- **Early repolarization patients:** ECGs coded by cardiologists, non-ischaemic chest pain, and 3 serial negative cardiac troponin I markers  

# Outcomes

## Individual Variables
- Greater ST elevation 60ms after J point in V3: OR per 1mm increase 2.5 (95% CI: 1.7-3.8)  
- Lower R wave amplitude in V4: OR per 5mm decrease 3.8 (95% CI: 2.4-6.2)  
- T wave in V1 > V6: OR 3.1 (95% CI: 1.4-6.9)  
- Longer QTc: OR per 10mm increase 1.5 (95% CI: 1.3-1.8)  

## Multiple Variables
- ECG criterion based on 3 measurements: ST elevation 60ms after J point, QTc, R wave amplitude in V4  
- Predictive equation: ([1.196 x ST-segment elevation 60ms after J point in V3 in mm] + [0.059 x QTc in ms] - [0.326 x R-wave amplitude in V4 in mm])  
- Sensitivity: 86% (95% CI: 79-91)  
- Specificity: 91% (95% CI: 85-95)  
- Accuracy: 88% (95% CI: 84-92)  
- Positive likelihood ratio: 9.2 (95% CI: 8.5-10)  
- Negative likelihood ratio: 0.1 (95% CI: 0.08-0.3)  
- Area under the curve: 0.96 (95% CI: 0.92-0.98)  

# Authors’ Conclusions
R-wave amplitude is lower, ST-segment elevation greater, and QTc longer for subtle anterior STEMI versus early repolarization. This derived and validated ECG equation could be an important adjunct in diagnosing anterior STEMI.

# Strengths
- ECG criteria derived and validated in separate groups  
- Positive likelihood ratio approaching 10 for combination of 3 variables  
- Appropriate gold standard investigations  
- Assessors included medical students and emergency medicine residents  

# Weaknesses
- Retrospective analysis  
- Control group limited to one hospital  
- Wide range of interrater reliability  
- QTc not corrected for sex  
- Limited to anterior STEMI or early repolarization  
- Complex calculation process for time-critical diagnosis  

# Bottom Line
This study derived and validated an ECG score to help differentiate anterior ST elevation from early repolarization. Online apps and calculators are available to assist but should be used by clinicians who understand the score in the context of the whole patient presentation.
