# Title
Short-term vs Conventional Glucocorticoid Therapy in Acute Exacerbations of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (REDUCE Trial)  
**JD Leuppi.**  
JAMA 2013; 309(21): 2223-2231. doi:10.1001/jama.2013.5023

# Clinical Question
Is short-term (5 days) systemic glucocorticoid treatment in patients with COPD exacerbation as effective as conventional (14 days) treatment in clinical outcomes, and does it decrease overall steroid exposure?

# Design
- Randomised, multi-centre, placebo-controlled, double-blind, non-inferiority trial

# Setting
- 5 teaching hospital Emergency Departments in Switzerland
- Study period: March 2006 to February 2011

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Exacerbation of COPD defined by 2 of the following:
  - Change in baseline dyspnoea
  - Cough
  - Change in sputum quantity or purulence
- Age over 40 years
- Smoking history of 20 pack-years or more

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- History of asthma
- FEV1:FVC ratio > 70% (evaluated by bedside post-bronchodilator spirometry)
- Radiological diagnosis of pneumonia
- Estimated survival < 6 months due to severe co-morbidities
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Inability to give written consent

**Enrollment:**
- 717 patients assessed, 314 randomised
- 15% absolute difference defined as threshold for non-inferiority

# Intervention
- 40 mg of intravenous methylprednisolone on day 1
- 40 mg of oral prednisolone from day 2 to day 5
- Placebo from day 6 to 14

# Control
- 40 mg of intravenous methylprednisolone on day 1
- 40 mg of oral prednisolone from day 2 to day 14

**All patients in both groups also received:**
- 7-day course of broad-spectrum antibiotics
- Standard nebulised and inhaled bronchodilators
- Inhaled glucocorticoids

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in exacerbation rates during the 6-month follow-up:
  - 5-day group: 35.9%
  - 14-day group: 36.8%

## Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - All-cause mortality
  - Clinical outcomes (dyspnoea questionnaire)
  - Change in FEV1
  - Need for mechanical ventilation (intubation or non-invasive ventilation)
  - Subjective outcomes (patient-reported overall and clinical performance)
  - Complications of steroids (glucocorticoid-associated adverse effects, hyperglycaemia, hypertension, newly diagnosed infection)

**Additional Findings:**
- Significant reduction in mean cumulative corticosteroid dose in the intervention group (379 mg vs 793 mg)

# Authors’ Conclusions
In patients presenting with acute exacerbations of COPD, a 5-day treatment with systemic glucocorticoids was non-inferior to a 14-day treatment regarding re-exacerbation within 6 months, while significantly reducing glucocorticoid exposure. These findings support the use of a 5-day glucocorticoid treatment in acute exacerbations of COPD.

# Strengths
- Study population reflective of typical COPD patients
- Randomised, blinded, placebo-controlled design
- Explanation for use of intravenous methylprednisolone provided (to facilitate administration to patients in distress)

# Weaknesses
- Non-inferiority trial not powered to detect differences between treatment groups
- Standard treatment (including antibiotics) applied to all COPD patients regardless of infective cause or COPD severity
- Only 6-month follow-up
- 40 mg methylprednisolone is equivalent to 50 mg prednisolone; further titration would have been difficult

# Bottom Line
This study reinforces the practice of prescribing short courses of steroids in COPD exacerbations, supporting a 5-day glucocorticoid treatment regimen.
