# Title
The Mulled Wine versus Normal Saline Evaluation Trial for Rehydration in the Critical Care Setting (MULLET Trial)  
**First published online:** NEJM (Northern Elf Journal of Musings), DOI: 25:12:2015.123

# Clinical Question
In critical care doctors, does mulled wine compared to normal saline improve the sense of well-being and ICU length of stay?

# Design
- Multi-centre, multi-country randomised controlled trial (RCT)
- Pseudo-randomisation: attendees of SMACC Dublin (#smaccDUB) allocated to mulled wine group
- Single-blinded: drink bottles blackened; taste not disguised
- Outcome assessor blinding: assessors also blinded
- Pilot trial with sample size adjustment post-SMACC ticket release
- Powered at 80% to detect a 10% absolute improvement in favourable characteristics

# Setting
- 55 centres across Australia, New Zealand, UK, Ireland, Scandinavia, USA, South Africa, and Canada
- Study period: October to December 2015

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- 4000 critical care doctors
  - 2000 allocated to mulled wine group
  - 2000 to normal saline group

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Doctors with pre-existing alcoholic liver disease

# Intervention

**Mulled Wine Group:**
- 5 bottles of 200 ml mulled wine (12% alcohol) once a week

# Control

**Normal Saline Group:**
- 5 bottles of 200 ml normal saline once a week

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Well-being quality of life score at 1 hour post-consumption
  - Statistically significant higher quality of life in mulled wine group
  - "How are you feeling now?" Good/Excellent: 90% in mulled wine vs 20% in saline (p=0.01)

## Secondary Outcomes
- Well-being quality of life score post-consumption:
  - At 4 hours: 80% vs 30% (p=0.04)
  - At 12 hours: 10% vs 40% (p=0.03)
- Length of stay as a critical care doctor:
  - 100% of mulled wine group intended to continue as critical care doctors vs 40% in saline group

# Authors’ Conclusions
In critical care doctors, rehydration with mulled wine once a week significantly improved quality of life and intention to continue working in the critical care environment.

# Strengths
- Trial protocol published a priori in Santa’s newsletter
- Participating centres had prior exposure to similar rehydration fluids
- Doctor-centred outcomes rather than surrogate endpoints
- Pragmatic design based on current practices
- Complete follow-up enhances result validity despite high fragility index at 12 hours

# Weaknesses
- Binomial distribution of results reduces statistical robustness compared to ordinal analysis, but clear margins suggest results are likely true
- Pseudo-randomisation may bias results due to natural positivity of SMACC attendees

# Bottom Line
In critical care doctors, consuming mulled wine in the pre-Christmas season was associated with significantly higher well-being scores and intention to remain in critical care.
