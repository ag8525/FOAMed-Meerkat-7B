# Title
A Systematic Review of Extravasation and Local Tissue Injury from Administration of Vasopressors through Peripheral Intravenous Catheters and Central Venous Catheters  
**Loubani.**  
Journal of Critical Care 2015; 30(3):654. doi:10.1016/j.jcrc.2015.01.014

# Clinical Question
In patients administered vasopressor medication intravenously, what are the published reports on local tissue injury and extravasation?

# Design
- Systematic, narrative review
- Two authors conducted the review
- Initial screening by one author, full review by both with consensus resolution

# Search Strategy
- Databases: Medline, Embase, Cochrane Library
- Search performed in January 2014, covering literature back to 1946
- No language restrictions
- Limited to human subjects
- Backward reference mapping performed

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age over 18 years

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Not cases of cardiac arrest

**Selection:**
- 78,671 abstracts screened
- 598 full-text articles reviewed
- 85 papers included

# Intervention
- Administration of IV vasopressors via peripheral or central venous line
- Drugs included: dopamine, epinephrine/adrenaline, norepinephrine/noradrenaline, vasopressin, terlipressin, phenylephrine, ephedrine

# Outcomes

## Complications
- Events categorized into:
  - **Extravasation:** Escape of drug into surrounding tissues, with or without injury
  - **Local Tissue Injury:** Evidence of tissue injury associated with vasopressor administration
- 64% of events caused local tissue injury
- 98% of these injuries were associated with peripheral venous catheters
- Tissue injuries included skin necrosis, tissue necrosis, or gangrene

## Drugs
- Data collected on specific drugs:
  - Norad/Norepi: 219 events (217 peripheral, 3 central)
  - Dopamine: 36 events (all peripheral)
  - Adren/Epineph: 18 events (17 peripheral, 1 central)
  - Vasopressin: 16 events (14 peripheral, 2 central)
  - Terlipressin: 4 events (3 peripheral, 1 central)
  - Phenylephrine: 6 events (all peripheral)
  - Ephedrine: 2 events (all peripheral)

## Location
- 62% of events associated with peripherally placed catheters
- Distal to antecubital fossa (ACF) or knee: 213 events
- At ACF or more proximal: 133 events

## Duration
- Described in 80% of cases
- Median duration: 24 hours for peripheral, 22 hours for central venous catheters
- Most injuries occurred after 6+ hours of infusion

## Gauge of Peripheral Venous Catheter
- Extravasation most commonly associated with 22-gauge lines
- Catheter gauge rarely reported

## Long-term Sequelae
- Simple extravasation infrequently associated with harm
- Tissue injury associated with long-term disability in 25% of events, death in 2%

# Authors’ Conclusions
Administration of vasopressors via peripheral IV lines for short term (<2 hours) and placed at or more proximal than the ACF is unlikely to lead to extravasation or local tissue injury. This should be a temporary measure during an emergency, replaced by a central venous line as soon as possible.

# Strengths
- Well-constructed literature search with clearly defined population, intervention, and outcomes
- Assessment of many articles for relevance
- Authors contacted for more recent articles to acquire specific patient-level data

# Weaknesses
- Narrative review, so exact numbers are less important
- Unable to define the denominator, so event rates or incidence of complications cannot be calculated
- Reporting bias may lead to overestimation of risk with peripheral lines and underestimation with central lines

# Bottom Line
This narrative review provides no firm conclusion. From the distribution of events described, it is reasonable to conclude that using proximal venous catheters for not more than 6 hours in emergencies, while a central venous catheter is inserted, is acceptable. This work should form a base for subsequent trials if the question remains clinically relevant.
