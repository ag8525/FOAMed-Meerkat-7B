# Title
Prehospital Plasma during Air Medical Transport in Trauma Patients at Risk for Hemorrhagic Shock (PAMPer Trial)  
**Sperry et al.**  
NEJM 2018; 379: 315-326. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1802345

# Clinical Question
In severely injured patients at risk for hemorrhagic shock, does prehospital plasma resuscitation, compared with standard-care resuscitation (excluding plasma), reduce 30-day mortality?

# Background
Traditionally, bleeding trauma patients are managed with prehospital crystalloids and component transfusion (RBCs, plasma, platelets) initiated upon hospital arrival. Recent evidence suggests early administration of blood components resembling whole blood may prevent trauma coagulopathy and improve outcomes. The PROPPR trial (2015) showed no significant mortality difference with different plasma, platelets, and RBC ratios, but reduced death from exsanguination. The COMBAT trial (2018) found no mortality difference with prehospital plasma versus saline, but had rapid transport times and was single-center.

# Design
- Pragmatic, phase 3, multicentre, cluster-randomized trial
- Randomisation of air medical services, not individual patients
- Delayed consent after community consultation
- No blinding of prehospital and hospital staff; trial assessors were blinded
- No commercial support

# Setting
- 27 prehospital air medical transport services to 9 level 1 trauma centers in the US
- Enrollment: 2014 to October 2017

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Blunt or penetrating trauma patients
- Transported from injury scene or outside ED to a trauma center
- At risk for hemorrhagic shock (hypotension and tachycardia or severe hypotension)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age >90 or <18
- Unable to establish IV/IO access
- Isolated fall, cervical cord injury, prisoner, pregnant
- Traumatic cardiac arrest >5 minutes, penetrating brain injury
- Isolated drowning/hanging, >20% TBSA burns
- Admitted to outside hospital
- Patient/family objected to trial participation

**Enrollment:**
- 501 patients enrolled (390 from scene, 111 from non-trauma ED)
- 72.7% male, median age 45, 82.4% blunt trauma
- High-risk trauma population (median ISS 22)

# Intervention
- Air medical bases randomized to intervention received 2 units of thawed plasma (AB or A with low anti-B titre)
- Plasma initiated prehospital before other fluids
- Goal-directed resuscitation continued post-plasma

# Control
- Goal-directed, crystalloid-based resuscitation targeting SBP ≥90 mmHg
- Local air ambulance protocols guided management

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- 30-day mortality lower in plasma group (23% vs. 33%; difference −9.8%, 95% CI −18.6 to −1.0; P=0.03)
- Number needed to treat: 10
- Adjusted analysis confirmed reduced 30-day mortality (adjusted OR 0.61; 95% CI 0.40–0.91; P=0.02)

## Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in 24-hour mortality, in-hospital mortality, RBC/crystalloid volume, vasopressor use, or multiorgan failure
- Lower initial INR in plasma group (P<0.001)
- Minor allergic/transfusion reactions higher in plasma group

# Authors’ Conclusions
Prehospital plasma administration in patients at risk for hemorrhagic shock is safe and reduces 30-day mortality compared to standard care.

# Strengths
- Multicentre RCT addressing a critical question
- Adjusted primary analysis for confounding factors
- Relevant high-risk population
- Simple, reproducible intervention

# Weaknesses
- Imbalances in prehospital crystalloid/RBC transfusion
- No blinding of clinicians
- Large mortality reduction from a single intervention is biologically questionable
- Generalisability to other systems unclear
- Majority blunt trauma; results not generalisable to penetrating trauma
- No economic analysis

# Bottom Line
Early prehospital plasma initiation significantly reduces mortality in high-risk bleeding patients without increasing harm. Further studies are needed to confirm benefits and applicability in different systems.
