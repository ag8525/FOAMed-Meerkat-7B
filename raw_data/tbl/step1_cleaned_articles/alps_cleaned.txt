# Title
Amiodarone, Lidocaine, or Placebo in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (ALPS)  
**Kudenchuk et al.**  
NEJM 2016; April 4: online. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1514204

# Clinical Question
In adult patients with nontraumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and shock-refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) or pulseless ventricular tachycardia (pVT), does amiodarone or lidocaine, along with standard care, improve survival and neurological outcomes compared to placebo?

# Design
- Double-blind, randomised controlled trial
- Multi-centre
- Randomisation in permuted blocks, stratified by site and EMS agency
- Sample size: 3026 (Amiodarone: 974, Lidocaine: 993, Placebo: 1059)
- Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses
- 90% power to detect a 6.3% difference in survival to discharge between amiodarone and placebo
- Interim reviews conducted biannually

# Setting
- 55 EMS agencies across 10 North American sites
- Study period: May 7, 2012 – October 25, 2015

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with non-traumatic cardiac arrest
- Shock-refractory VF or pVT with IV or IO access

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior open-label lidocaine or amiodarone during resuscitation
- Hypersensitivity to study drugs

**Enrollment:**
- 4653 in intention-to-treat analysis
- 3026 in per-protocol analysis

# Intervention

**Amiodarone Group:**
- 3 syringes of 3 ml each containing 150 mg amiodarone
- Initial dose: 2 syringes as bolus (1 syringe if <45 kg)
- Additional syringe if VF/pVT persisted

**Lidocaine Group:**
- 3 syringes of 3 ml each containing 60 mg lidocaine
- Same dosing protocol as amiodarone

# Control

**Placebo Group:**
- 3 syringes of 3 ml each containing 0.9% sodium chloride

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Survival to hospital discharge:
  - Amiodarone: 24.4%
  - Lidocaine: 23.7%
  - Placebo: 21%
  - Amiodarone vs placebo: 3.2% (95% CI: -0.4 to 7.0), p=0.08
  - Lidocaine vs placebo: 2.6% (95% CI: -1.0 to 6.3), p=0.16

## Secondary Outcome
- Survival with favourable neurological outcome at discharge:
  - Amiodarone: 18.8%
  - Lidocaine: 17.5%
  - Placebo: 16.6%

## Subgroup Analysis
- Witnessed arrest: higher survival in intervention groups
  - Bystander-witnessed: Amiodarone 27.7%, Lidocaine 27.8%, Placebo 22.7%
  - EMS-witnessed: Amiodarone 38.6%, Lidocaine 23.3%, Placebo 16.7%
  - Amiodarone vs placebo: 21.9% (95% CI: 5.8–38.0), p=0.01

## Adverse Events
- Temporary pacing required more in amiodarone group (4.9% vs lidocaine 3.2% vs placebo 2.7%), p=0.02

# Authors’ Conclusions
Neither amiodarone nor lidocaine significantly increased survival or improved neurological outcomes compared to placebo in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.

# Strengths
- RCT with multicentre design
- Clinically meaningful outcomes
- Allocation concealment and double-blinding
- Near-complete follow-up

# Weaknesses
- Potential underpowering to detect small differences
- Subgroup analysis caution
- More patients in amiodarone group required temporary pacing

# Bottom Line
Amiodarone did not show a statistically significant benefit over placebo in refractory VF/pVT in OHCA. However, the 3% difference in survival to discharge may be clinically important. Amiodarone and lidocaine were superior in witnessed OHCA, but caution is advised with subgroup analysis. Current arrest protocols should not change, and amiodarone should continue to be used for VF/pVT in OHCA.
