# Title
The HEART Pathway Randomised Trial: Identifying Emergency Department Patients With Acute Chest Pain for Early Discharge  
**Mahler et al.**  
Circulation Cardiovascular Outcomes 2015; 8:00-00. doi:10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.114.001384

# Clinical Question
In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with chest pain, does the HEART Pathway compared with usual care reduce objective cardiac testing?

# Design
- Randomised control trial
- Block randomisation stratified by known coronary artery disease
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Blinded assessment of subjective outcomes

# Setting
- Single-centre, tertiary academic Emergency Department, USA
- Study period: September 2012 to February 2014

# Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients >21 years old with symptoms suggestive of acute coronary syndrome (ACS)
- Both ECG and troponin ordered for ACS evaluation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- New ST elevation >1mm
- Hypotension
- Life expectancy <1 year
- Admission required for non-cardiac cause
- Previous enrolment
- Non-English speaking

**Enrollment:**
- 282 patients randomised

# Intervention

**HEART Pathway Group (n=141):**
- HEART score determined with serial troponin measurements at 0 and 3 hours
- HEART score components: history, ECG, age, risk factors, troponin (scored 0-2 each)
- Patients with HEART score ≤3 and negative troponins classified as low risk and suitable for early discharge
- 47% (n=66) classified as low risk; 32% (n=21) of these underwent objective cardiac testing within 30 days

# Control

**Usual Care Group (n=141):**
- Clinicians encouraged to follow American College of Cardiology guidelines
- Serial cardiac biomarkers and objective cardiac testing before discharge

# Outcomes

## Primary Outcome
- Rate of objective cardiac testing within 30 days:
  - HEART Pathway: 56.7%
  - Usual Care: 68.8%
  - Absolute risk reduction: 12.1%, p=0.048
  - NNT = 9

## Secondary Outcomes
- **Early discharge rate:** 
  - HEART Pathway: 39.7%
  - Usual Care: 18.4%, p<0.001
- **Length of stay:** 
  - HEART Pathway: median 9.9 hours
  - Usual Care: median 21.9 hours, p=0.013
- **Cardiac-related recurrent ED visits:** 
  - No significant difference (2.8% vs. 4.3%, p=0.75)
- **Cardiac-related non-index hospitalisation:** 
  - No significant difference (3.6% vs. 2.8%, p>0.999)
- **MACE (30 days):** 
  - No significant difference (5.7% vs. 6.4%, p=1)

**Test Characteristics for MACE:**
- Sensitivity: 100% (95% CI: 63.1-100)
- Specificity: 49.6% (95% CI: 40.8-58.4)
- +ve Likelihood Ratio: 1.99
- -ve Likelihood Ratio: 0

# Authors’ Conclusions
The HEART pathway significantly decreased objective cardiac testing, increased early discharge rates, and reduced length of stay without increasing the risk of missing significant cardiac events.

# Strengths
- Randomised control trial
- Clinically relevant endpoints
- Pragmatic study design
- Registered with clinicaltrials.gov

# Weaknesses
- Single-centre study with small sample size
- Convenience sample
- Not powered to detect differences in MACE
- Interobserver agreement for HEART score was moderate (k=0.63)
- Fragility index of 1

# Bottom Line
The HEART pathway may be a useful tool for risk stratification in the ED, reducing unnecessary testing and length of stay while maintaining safety. Further studies in different populations are needed before widespread adoption.
