# Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole versus Placebo for Uncomplicated Skin Abscess

## Clinical Question
In patients with drained cutaneous abscesses, does trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) compared with placebo increase clinical cure rates?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Web-based randomisation with randomly generated blocks
- Double-blind
- Superiority trial
- Primary outcome assessed as per-protocol analysis
- Two modified intention-to-treat analyses performed

## Setting
- 5 US Emergency Departments
- Data collection: April 2009 to April 2012

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age >12 years
- Cutaneous abscess >2 cm diameter, present for <1 week
- Purulent material on surgical exploration
- Clinician intended to treat as an outpatient

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Indwelling devices, suspected osteomyelitis/septic arthritis, diabetic foot
- Immunodeficiency, foreign bodies, intravenous drug use with fever
- Underlying skin conditions, long-term care/institutionalised
- Creatinine clearance <50 mL/min, allergy, pregnancy

**Enrollment:**
- 1265 patients randomised
- 1057 patients included in primary outcome analysis

## Intervention
- **TMP-SMX Group:** 80 mg/400 mg twice daily for 7 days

## Control
- **Placebo Group:** Twice daily for 7 days

**Common Management:**
- Standardised training on incision and drainage
- All abscesses were packed

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Clinical cure of abscess at day 14-21 (per-protocol analysis):**
  - TMP-SMX: 92.9%
  - Placebo: 85.7%
  - Absolute difference: 7.2% (95% CI: 3.2–11.7), p < 0.001
  - Number needed to treat (NNT): 14

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Modified intention-to-treat analysis 1 (n=1247):**
  - TMP-SMX: 80.5%
  - Placebo: 73.6%
  - Absolute difference: 6.9% (95% CI: 2.1–11.7), p = 0.005
  - NNT: 15

- **Modified intention-to-treat analysis 2 (n=1213):**
  - TMP-SMX: 92.7%
  - Placebo: 86.7%
  - Absolute difference: 6.1% (95% CI: 2.5–9.7), p < 0.001
  - NNT: 17

- **Additional surgical drainage (per-protocol):**
  - TMP-SMX: 3.4%
  - Placebo: 8.6%
  - Absolute difference: -5.2% (95% CI: -8.2% to -2.2%)

- **New skin infection at a different site:**
  - TMP-SMX: 3.1%
  - Placebo: 10.3%
  - Absolute difference: -7.2% (95% CI: -10.4% to -4.1%)

- **Similar infection in household member:**
  - TMP-SMX: 1.7%
  - Placebo: 4.1%
  - Absolute difference: -2.4% (95% CI: -4.6% to -0.2%)

- **Adverse Events:**
  - Gastrointestinal disorders significantly increased in TMP-SMX group: 42.7% vs. 36.1%

## Authors’ Conclusions
Use of TMP-SMX in addition to drainage for cutaneous abscess was more effective at achieving clinical cure and preventing recurrent drainage or infection compared to incision and drainage alone.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Relevant outcome for emergency medicine and primary care
  - Standardised incision and drainage technique
  - Effective randomisation and blinding
  - Multi-centre study
  - Registered on clinicaltrials.gov

- **Weaknesses**
  - Results may not apply to clinicians who do not irrigate and pack abscesses
  - High rate of MRSA; unclear applicability to populations with lower MRSA rates
  - Fragility index less than the number of patients lost to follow-up

## Bottom Line
In patients with drained cutaneous abscesses, TMP-SMX significantly improved clinical cure rates compared with placebo and had a benign safety profile. Clinicians should use clinical judgment, especially in abscesses with minimal surrounding erythema.
