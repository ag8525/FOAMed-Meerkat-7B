# Fentanyl versus Placebo with Ketamine and Rocuronium for Patients Undergoing Rapid Sequence Intubation in the Emergency Department: The FAKT Study

## Clinical Question
In patients requiring rapid sequence intubation (RSI) in the emergency department, does the addition of fentanyl to ketamine and rocuronium reduce the frequency of patients having a systolic blood pressure (SBP) outside of a target range of 100-150 mmHg?

## Background
- The INTUBE study showed high rates (42%) of cardiovascular instability in patients undergoing tracheal intubation in ICU, ED, and wards.
- Ketamine is commonly used for RSI due to its preferable haemodynamic profile.
- Fentanyl may prevent the pressor response to laryngoscopy and episodes of hypertension, but its effect on hypotension risk is unclear.

## Design
- Multi-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomised controlled trial
- Randomised 1:1 to treatment or placebo
- Active and placebo drugs prepared by Baxter Pharmaceutical, labelled by an unblinded pharmacist
- Blinding maintained until manuscript completion
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Observations recorded every 2 minutes for 10 minutes post-induction

## Setting
- 5 Emergency Departments in New South Wales, Australia (tertiary and secondary hospitals)
- Study period: August 2018 to December 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients requiring RSI in the emergency department

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Allergy to study drugs
- Paralysis only or no drug intubation
- Clinician preference for another regimen
- Overwhelmed emergency department
- No specialist trained in the protocol available

**Enrollment:**
- 476 patients assessed for eligibility
- 174 excluded (82 needed alternative regimen, 54 lacked trained staff)
- 302 randomised (149 fentanyl group, 153 placebo group)

## Intervention

**Fentanyl Group:**
- Fentanyl 10 mcg/ml in a 20 ml syringe, administered in volume equal to ketamine dose

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- 0.9% Saline in a 20 ml syringe, administered in volume equal to ketamine dose

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Standardised doses of ketamine and rocuronium
- Ketamine: 1-2 mg/kg (reduced dose: 0.5-1 mg/kg)
- Rocuronium: 1.5 mg/kg
- Drugs administered in order: study drug, ketamine, rocuronium
- Standardised preparation for intubation, including monitoring and resuscitation

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Proportion of patients with SBP outside 100-150 mmHg (or >10% change if outside this range) within 10 minutes of induction
  - Fentanyl: 66% vs Placebo: 65%
  - Difference: 1% (95% CI: -10 to 12), p = 0.86

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Hypotension (SBP < 100 mmHg):** Fentanyl 29% vs Placebo 16% (Difference: 13%, 95% CI: 3-23)
- **Hypertension (SBP > 150 mmHg):** Fentanyl 55% vs Placebo 69% (Difference: 14%, 95% CI: 3-24)
- **Tachycardia (HR ≥ 120):** Fentanyl 48% vs Placebo 61% (Difference: 13%, 95% CI: 2-25)
- No significant difference in hypoxia, cardiac arrest, intubation characteristics, or clinical outcomes

## Authors’ Conclusions
In a mixed population of adults requiring RSI in the ED, the addition of fentanyl to ketamine and rocuronium did not reduce the incidence of SBP outside the target range. However, there was an increased incidence of hypotension and reduced incidence of hypertension in the fentanyl group. Clinicians should consider post-induction haemodynamic targets before using fentanyl as a co-induction agent with ketamine.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed, double-blinded study in an emergency setting
  - Primary outcome data available for 96% of participants
  - Sensitivity analyses showed no change in primary outcome with imputed data
  - Included hospitals of varying sizes
  - Standardised pre-intubation optimisation
- **Weaknesses**
  - Non-patient centred primary outcome
  - Lack of consistent approach in literature for primary outcome
  - Unclear long-term clinical significance of transient haemodynamic changes
  - Fixed ratio of fentanyl to ketamine may not reflect clinical practice
  - Large number of exclusions due to clinician preference
  - Potential selection bias due to recruitment limitations
  - Limited generalisability due to regional focus

## Bottom Line
Fentanyl, when given in a fixed ratio with ketamine, may increase the risk of peri-intubation hypotension. Clinicians should individually select doses of induction agents based on patient characteristics and co-induction agents used.
