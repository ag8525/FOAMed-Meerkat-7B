# Lung Ultrasound Integrated with Clinical Assessment for the Diagnosis of Acute Decompensated Heart Failure in the Emergency Department: A Randomized Controlled Trial

## Clinical Question
In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with acute dyspnoea, does the addition of lung ultrasound improve diagnostic accuracy for acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) compared to the standard approach?

## Background
Point-of-care ultrasound, including lung ultrasound, is increasingly used in assessing critically unwell patients. Previous studies have focused on the accuracy of lung ultrasound compared to traditional imaging modalities like chest radiographs. However, no randomized controlled trial has compared lung ultrasound's use in diagnosing acute heart failure against the standard clinical approach.

## Design
- Randomised, multicentre, parallel-group trial
- Computer permuted block randomisation
- Patients were randomised after initial clinical evaluation and categorisation as ADHF or non-ADHF
- 1:1 allocation to lung ultrasound (LUS) or chest radiograph and NT-proBNP levels (CXR/NT-proBNP)
- All patients in the LUS group also underwent CXR and NT-proBNP
- CXR/NT-proBNP group could undergo LUS at the physician's request
- Diagnosis adjudicated by two experts blinded to LUS results, based on 2012 European Society of Cardiology guidelines
- 258 patients needed per arm to detect a 10% difference in sensitivity with 80% power and a 5% alpha error

## Setting
- Two Italian academic centres
- Enrolment: January 2014 to March 2015

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (>18 years) presenting with acute dyspnoea (sudden onset or increased severity of chronic dyspnoea within 48 hours)
- Presence of an emergency physician with expertise in lung ultrasonography

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Already on positive pressure ventilation (invasive or non-invasive)
- Acute dyspnoea due to trauma

**Enrollment:**
- 532 eligible, 518 enrolled (411 from a single centre)
- Median age: 79 years; 53% male
- 25.5% admitted to ICU after ED evaluation

## Intervention

**LUS Group:**
- Lung ultrasound performed by a certified practitioner using a curvilinear probe and standard 8-zone protocol
- Recorded presence of B-lines and pleural effusions

**CXR/NT-proBNP Group:**
- CXR and NT-proBNP performed after initial clinical evaluation
- LUS could be performed at the physician's request after CXR/NT-proBNP diagnosis was recorded

## Control
- Clinical evaluation common to both groups, including medical history, physical examination, ABG analysis, and ECG
- Therapeutic decisions were the physician's responsibility

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- LUS group showed greater accuracy in diagnosing ADHF compared to CXR/NT-proBNP group
  - 224 patients (43.2%) identified with ADHF by experts (Kappa agreement: 0.84; 95% CI: 0.79–0.88)
  - ADHF incidence: 48.1% in LUS vs. 38.5% in CXR/NT-proBNP
  - AUC: LUS vs. clinical evaluation alone: 94.5 vs. 88 (p<0.01)
  - AUC: CXR/NT-proBNP vs. clinical evaluation alone: 87.2 vs. 85 (p>0.05)
  - Sensitivity: LUS 93.5%, CXR/NT-proBNP 85%
  - Specificity: LUS 95.5%, CXR/NT-proBNP 89.4%
  - Positive predictive value: LUS 95.1%, CXR/NT-proBNP 83.3%
  - Negative predictive value: LUS 94.1%, CXR/NT-proBNP 90.5%

## Authors’ Conclusions
Integration of LUS with clinical assessment for diagnosing ADHF in the ED is more accurate than the current diagnostic approach based on CXR and NT-proBNP.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multicentre study
  - Appropriately powered
  - Sensitivity of LUS in diagnosing ADHF matched power calculation
  - Reflective of real-life clinical practice
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single centre recruited nearly 80% of subjects
  - High ICU admission rate may limit generalisability
  - Diagnosis based on expert judgement
  - Higher ADHF incidence in LUS group; unclear if clinically relevant or due to chance
  - Convenience sampling required physician presence for LUS
  - No echocardiography data included; zone 2 of LUS might view the heart

## Bottom Line
Adding LUS to clinical evaluation improves diagnostic accuracy for ADHF, whereas adding CXR and NT-proBNP does not. NT-proBNP is not routinely used in my practice, and this study supports that. I will continue using ultrasound for lung and heart evaluation in acute dyspnoea cases.
