# Effect of Noninvasive Airway Management of Comatose Patients with Acute Poisoning: A Randomized Clinical Trial

## Clinical Question
In adults with decreased consciousness from suspected acute poisoning, does withholding intubation versus routine practice reduce in-hospital death, ICU stay, and hospital stay length?

## Background
The decision to intubate poisoned patients with GCS < 9 is debated due to risks of aspiration versus intubation risks. No high-level evidence exists to determine the optimal strategy.

## Design
- Multi-centre, open-label, randomised controlled trial
- Investigator-initiated
- Unblinded due to intervention nature
- Randomisation: 1:1 ratio using sealed envelopes, stratified by hospital and block balanced
- Randomisation occurred in ambulance or ED
- Win Ratio (WR) used for primary outcome assessment

## Setting
- 20 Emergency Departments and 1 ICU in France
- Study period: May 2021 to April 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 18 years
- Clinical suspicion of acute poisoning (alcohol, drug, or medication)
- GCS ≤ 8

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Immediate need for intubation
- Respiratory failure (SpO₂ < 90% with oxygen ≤ 4 L/min)
- Shock (SBP < 90 mmHg despite 1L fluid resuscitation)
- Suspected brain injury (seizure or acute cerebral aggression)
- Suspected cardiotropic drug poisoning
- Sole intoxication with an antidote available

**Enrollment:**
- 237 patients randomised
- 225 included in primary analysis

## Intervention
- Intubation withheld unless emergency criteria evolved (e.g., regurgitation, seizure, shock, respiratory distress)
- GCS assessed every 30 minutes until > 8
- 18% intubated in intervention group

## Control
- Intubation at physician's discretion
- 60% intubated in control group
- Common management: French society guidelines for rapid sequence intubation

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Hierarchical composite endpoint (in-hospital death, ICU and hospital LOS) improved in restricted intubation group
- Win Ratio: 1.85 (95% CI 1.33–2.58, p < 0.001)
- No deaths in either group
- Lower median ICU stay: 0 hours vs 24 hours; RR: 0.39 (95% CI 0.24–0.66)
- Lower median hospital stay: 22 hours vs 37 hours; RR: 0.74 (95% CI 0.53–1.03)
- Absolute risk difference for intubation: -42.5% (95% CI -54.1 to -30.9)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No increased pneumonia rates: 7% vs 15%; OR 0.43 (95% CI 0.18–1.05)
- Lower ICU admission: 40% vs 66%; OR 0.23 (95% CI 0.12–0.44)
- No difference in intubation adverse effects or LOS for intubated patients

## Authors’ Conclusions
Withholding intubation in comatose patients with suspected acute poisoning is associated with greater clinical benefit for the composite endpoint of in-hospital death, ICU stay, and hospital stay.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Focused on patient-centered outcomes
  - Appropriate use of hierarchical composite endpoint
  - Pragmatic design with flexibility in standard management
  - Adequately powered for primary outcome
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded, potentially influencing intubation decisions
  - Primary outcome driven by ICU stay length
  - Wide exclusion criteria limit generalisability
  - No documentation on intubation personnel or devices used
  - First pass failure ~13% in control group

## Bottom Line
In patients with GCS < 8 due to simple acute poisoning without other adverse features, delaying intubation for up to 4 hours reduces ICU admissions and LOS.
