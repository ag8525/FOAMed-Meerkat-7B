# Intravascular Complications of Central Venous Catheterization by Insertion Site (3SITES Study)

## Clinical Question
In intensive care patients requiring a central venous catheter (CVC), does the choice of insertion site affect complication rates?

## Design
- Multi-centre, randomised-controlled trial
- Permuted-block randomisation
- Randomisation based on available venous sites: subclavian, jugular, or femoral
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 10 ICUs in France (4 university hospitals, 5 general hospitals)
- December 2011 – June 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients over 18 years
- Required non-tunnelled CVC during ICU admission
- Suitable for CVC insertion in at least 2 of 3 sites: subclavian, internal jugular, femoral veins

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Only one suitable site for CVC insertion
- Planned catheter removal outside ICU
- Moribund patients

**Enrollment:**
- 7,559 screened, 3,471 catheter insertions randomised
- 1,016 subclavian, 1,284 jugular, 1,171 femoral

## Intervention
- CVC insertion at subclavian (SC), internal jugular (IJ), or femoral (F) site
- Maximal sterile precautions using Seldinger technique
- Landmark or ultrasound-guided technique (ultrasound encouraged)
- CXR performed for subclavian or jugular lines

## Control
- No control group

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Composite of major CVC-related complications (CRBSI and DVT with clinical signs) from insertion to 48 hours post-removal
  - CRBSI: positive catheter-tip colonisation (≥10³ CFU/ml) with concordant peripheral blood culture
  - DVT: confirmed by ultrasound within 48 hours of catheter removal
- Event rates per 1,000 catheter days: SC 1.5, IJ 3.6, F 4.6
  - CRBSI: F vs SC (HR 3.4, CI 1.0–11.1, p=0.048); IJ vs SC (HR 2.3, CI 0.8–6.2, p=0.11)
  - DVT: F vs SC (HR 3.4, CI 1.2–9.3, p=0.02); IJ vs SC (HR 1.8, CI 0.6–4.9, p=0.29)

### Secondary Outcome
- Catheter-tip colonisation: lower in SC
  - F vs SC (HR 3.4, CI 2.4–5.0, p<0.001); J vs SC (HR 2.5, CI 1.7–3.5, p<0.001)
- Asymptomatic DVT: lower in SC
  - F vs SC (HR 3.0, CI 1.7–5.3, p<0.001); J vs SC (HR 3.1, CI 1.9–5.0, p<0.001)
- Mechanical complications: higher in SC (2.1%) vs IJ (1.4%) and F (0.7%), mainly pneumothorax

## Authors’ Conclusions
Subclavian vein catheterisation is associated with a lower risk of catheter-related bloodstream infection and symptomatic deep-vein thrombosis but a higher risk of mechanical complications, primarily pneumothorax.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre study
  - Relevant to ICU practice with pragmatic protocol
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded; clinician discretion on site suitability
  - Variable ultrasound use; considered "landmark" even with "mark and go"
  - Different antiseptic preparations and dressings across sites
  - High failure and crossover rate in subclavian arm (14.7%)

## Bottom Line
Subclavian vein catheterisation has a lower infection risk but higher pneumothorax risk. This study supports conventional wisdom but does not change my practice; I prefer ultrasound-guided internal jugular lines and would choose femoral over subclavian if needed.
