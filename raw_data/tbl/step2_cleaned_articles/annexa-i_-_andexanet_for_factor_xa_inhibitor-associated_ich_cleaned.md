# Andexanet for Factor Xa Inhibitor–Associated Acute Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ANNEXA-I)

## Clinical Question
In patients who have recently taken factor Xa inhibitors and have an intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), does the administration of Andexanet result in better hemostatic efficacy compared to standard care?

## Background
- Factor Xa inhibitors (e.g., apixaban, rivaroxaban) are widely used but can lead to ICH.
- Andexanet is a novel antidote hypothesized to rapidly reverse FXa inhibitors' effects, potentially reducing hematoma expansion and improving outcomes.
- Conventionally, prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) is used for reversal, as specific antidotes like Idarucizumab are unavailable for FXa inhibitors.

## Design
- International, multi-centre RCT
- Unblinded, with endpoints adjudicated by a blinded committee
- 1:1 randomisation, stratified by PCC use and time from symptom onset to baseline imaging (< or >180 minutes)
- Power calculation based on ANNEXA-4 study; 900 patients for 90% power to show a 10% improvement in hemostatic efficacy
- Trial stopped at interim analysis of 452 patients for efficacy

## Setting
- June 2019 – May 2023
- 131 sites in 23 countries

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Taken FXa inhibitor within 15 hours
- ICH within 12 hours of symptoms to baseline imaging
- Randomisation within 2 hours of baseline imaging
- Protocol amendment: only ICH (not SDH/SAH) with volume 0.5–60 ml and NIHSS ≤35

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- GCS <7
- NIHSS >35
- Surgery within 12 hours of enrollment
- Recent thrombotic event (within 2 weeks)

**Enrollment:**
- 550 patients randomised
- 530 included (263 Andexanet, 267 usual care)
- 452 included in primary efficacy analysis

## Intervention

**Andexanet Group:**
- High or low dose based on FXa inhibitor dose and timing
- Bolus over 15–30 minutes, followed by 2-hour infusion
- 58 high dose, 200 low dose

## Control

**Usual Care Group:**
- Determined by local physicians, could include PCC
- 230 received PCC (85.5%)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Hemostatic efficacy: 67% Andexanet vs 53% usual care
- Adjusted difference: 13.4% (95% CI 4.6–22.2, p = 0.003)

### Post-hoc Analysis
- mRS 0–3 at 30 days: 28.0% Andexanet vs 31.0% usual care

## Authors’ Conclusions
Andexanet rapidly reduced anti–factor Xa activity and resulted in better control of hematoma expansion compared to usual care but was associated with thrombotic events.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - International RCT with balanced baseline characteristics
  - Minimal loss to follow-up and cross-over
  - Robust safety analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded, though endpoints adjudicated by a blinded committee
  - Not powered for mortality or long-term functional outcomes
  - Primary outcome driven by hematoma volume change, which may not be clinically significant
  - Sponsored by Portola, with protocol amendments and eligibility changes

## Bottom Line
This trial shows a reduction in ICH hematoma expansion with Andexanet but no evidence of long-term patient-important benefits. Increased thrombotic events suggest caution in its use.
