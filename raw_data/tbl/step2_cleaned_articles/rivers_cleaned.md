# Early Goal-Directed Therapy in the Treatment of Severe Sepsis and Septic Shock

## Clinical Question
In adults with severe sepsis or septic shock, does the use of early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) reduce mortality?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Computer-generated blocks of 2 to 8
- Concealed allocation
- No blinding of clinicians, study personnel, or patients
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- Single academic tertiary hospital in Detroit, USA
- Study period: March 1997 to March 2000

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Severe sepsis or septic shock
- ≥ 2 SIRS features, and either:
  - Systolic BP ≤ 90 mmHg after crystalloid fluid of 20 to 30 ml/kg over 30 minutes
  - Blood lactate ≥ 4 mmol/l

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age under 18 years
- Pregnancy
- Other acute neurological, cardiac, gastrointestinal, or respiratory pathology
- Contraindications to central catheterisation
- Uncured cancer
- Immunosuppression
- Limitations defined for active care

**Enrollment:**
- 268 patients randomised, 236 completed the study

## Intervention

**EGDT Group:**
- Early goal-directed therapy for 6 hours
- Managed by an emergency physician, two residents, and three nurses
- Oxygen ± intubation and mechanical ventilation
- Central venous and arterial catheterisation
- Central venous catheter capable of measuring continuous ScvO₂
- 500 ml fluid boluses until CVP 8–12 mmHg
- Vasopressors or vasodilators until MAP 65–90 mmHg
- If ScvO₂ < 70%:
  - Transfused red cells until haematocrit ≥ 30%
  - Dobutamine 2.5–20 µg/kg/min until ScvO₂ > 70%
  - Dobutamine limited or reduced if MAP < 65 mmHg or pulse > 120/min

## Control

**Usual Care Group:**
- Usual care for 6 hours without protocol
- Treatment at clinician’s discretion
- Oxygen ± intubation and mechanical ventilation
- Central venous and arterial catheterisation
- Targets defined but without treatment algorithm:
  - CVP ≥ 8–12 mmHg
  - MAP ≥ 65 mmHg
  - Urine output ≥ 0.5 ml/kg/hr

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- In-hospital mortality was significantly lower in the EGDT group compared to the usual care group.
  - EGDT: 29.2%
  - Usual Care: 44.4%
  - Absolute risk reduction: 15.13% (95% CI: 3.62 to 26.64%), p = 0.015
  - Number needed to treat (NNT): 7

### Secondary Outcomes
- Subgroup analyses favoured EGDT for:
  - Severity of sepsis
  - 28-day and 60-day mortality
  - Cause of in-hospital death (sudden cardiovascular collapse and multi-organ failure)

**Tertiary Data Collection:**
- EGDT group received more fluid over the early 6-hour period
- More red-cell transfusions in the EGDT group
- Early inotropic use more prevalent in the EGDT group
- Vasopressor use more prevalent in the usual care group
- Intubation and ventilation more prevalent in the usual care group

## Authors’ Conclusions
"Goal-directed therapy provided at the earliest stages of severe sepsis and septic shock has significant short-term and long-term benefits."

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Adequate trial design for the clinical question
  - High completion rate (90%)
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre study
  - Intervention was a bundle of care; individual components not separately investigated
  - Usual care control group had higher than average mortality
  - In-hospital mortality as an outcome measure has limitations
  - Lead author has potential conflicts of interest

## Bottom Line
Until ProCESS, ARISE, and ProMISe, Rivers’ trial represented the best evidence for EGDT and demonstrated a strong benefit. Concerns and criticism have led to modified but widespread adoption of the 'bundle' of care in severe sepsis and septic shock. Until individual elements are tested, the specific goals are not unreasonable to target, although the algorithm is perhaps less robust.
