# Acupuncture vs Intravenous Morphine in the Management of Acute Pain in the ED

## Clinical Question
In patients with acute pain in the Emergency Department, is acupuncture more effective than intravenous morphine in reducing pain by ≥50%?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial  
- Computerised random number generation with sealed envelopes  
- Non-blinded  
- Convenience sample  
- Standardised data collection form  
- Sample size calculation: 300 patients needed to detect a 13% absolute difference with a 10% false negative rate and a 5% false positive rate  

## Setting
- Single Emergency Department in a University Hospital, Tunisia  
- Study period: April 2012 to March 2013  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with acute pain (<72 hours)  
- Moderate-severe pain (pain intensity ≥40 on a 0-100 scale)  
- Pain from musculoskeletal issues (without fracture/dislocation), abdominal pain (not requiring urgent surgery), or primary headache  
- Presented between 08:00 – 19:00, Monday to Friday  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Required resuscitation or specific procedures beyond pain treatment  
- Temperature >37.5°C  
- Conditions impairing acupuncture use (e.g., anticoagulant use, skin conditions)  
- Inability to participate as judged by the physician  
- Received analgesia in the previous 6 hours  
- ED visit in the previous 24 hours for the same complaint  
- Pregnancy  

**Enrollment:**
- 300 patients randomised  

## Intervention

**Acupuncture Group:**
- Performed by a single ED doctor with medical acupuncture qualification and 10 years of experience  
- Treatment protocol for each condition with pre-determined acupuncture points  
- Average needle placement time: 5 minutes  
- Duration: 20-30 minutes  

## Control

**Intravenous Morphine Group:**
- Initial dose: 0.1 mg/kg, with repeat dosing of 0.05 mg/kg every 5 minutes until objective reached  
- Maximum dose: 15 mg  
- Mean total dose administered: 0.17 mg/kg  
- Treatment suspended if VAS did not decrease by at least 50% within 30 minutes  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Reduction in pain score by at least 50% at 1 hour:  
  - Acupuncture: 92%  
  - Morphine: 78%  
  - p < 0.01  
  - Number needed to treat: 8  
  - Fragility index: 9 patients  
  - Mean difference: 14% (95% CI: 8.2–19.8)  
  - Adjusted mean difference: 13.2% (95% CI: 5.2–21.3)  

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Time to pain resolution (mean):**  
  - Acupuncture: 16 min  
  - Morphine: 28 min  
  - p < 0.01  
  - Mean difference: 12 min (95% CI: 9.2–14.9)  
  - Adjusted mean difference: 12.8 min (95% CI: 9.9–15.8)  

- **Pain score reduction (mean) from 0-60 minutes:**  
  - Acupuncture: 64  
  - Morphine: 56  
  - Mean difference: 7.7 (95% CI: 2.6–12.7)  
  - Adjusted mean difference: 9.6 (95% CI: 4.5–14.6)  

**Side Effects:**
- Minor side effects significantly higher in morphine group:  
  - Morphine: 29%  
  - Acupuncture: 3%  
  - p < 0.001  
- No major side effects in either group  

## Authors’ Conclusions
Acupuncture may have a role in treating acute pain in the Emergency Department, showing quicker and more effective pain reduction compared to intravenous morphine.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial with computerised random number generation  
  - Registered at clinicaltrials.gov  
- **Weaknesses**
  - Non-blinded  
  - Single centre with a single acupuncturist  
  - Cultural acceptance of acupuncture in Tunisia may augment placebo effect  
  - Change in primary outcome from registered protocol  
  - Unclear if acupuncture group allowed other treatments  
  - Primary outcome measured at 60 minutes, but morphine treatment suspended at 30 minutes if ineffective  

## Bottom Line
This non-blinded single-centre RCT found acupuncture to be quicker and more effective than intravenous morphine for acute pain in the ED. However, methodological weaknesses suggest further blinded, multi-centre international trials are needed before widespread adoption.
