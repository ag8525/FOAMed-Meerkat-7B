# Restriction of Intravenous Fluid in ICU Patients with Septic Shock (CLASSIC Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult ICU patients with septic shock, does a restrictive approach to fluid therapy compared to standard care result in fewer deaths at day 90?

## Background
Fluid therapy in critically ill patients has been extensively studied. Higher fluid volumes in sepsis and septic shock have been associated with harm, though evidence quality is low. The ANDROMEDA SHOCK trial indicated limited fluid responsiveness in septic shock patients after 2 hours. The CLASSIC pilot study showed feasibility of a restrictive fluid strategy, with reduced fluid use in the first 5 ICU days and a non-significant reduction in 90-day mortality.

## Design
- Stratified, parallel-group, open-label, international, randomised controlled trial
- Randomised 1:1 using a central web-based system
- Block sizes of 6 or 8
- Stratified by site and presence/absence of metastatic or haematological malignancy
- Treatment assignments not masked for clinicians, patients, or investigators
- Blinded data safety monitoring committee, statisticians, and management committee
- Intervention from randomisation until ICU discharge (max 90 days)
- Continued treatment assignment if re-admitted within 90 days
- Data from medical records and registries; patient/relative contact if needed
- Pre-specified subgroups: respiratory support, AKI, lactate > 4 mmol/L, weight > 76 kg, > 30 mL/kg fluids in 24 hours pre-randomisation
- Power calculation: 1554 patients for 7% absolute reduction in 90-day mortality from 45% baseline, with 5% Type I and 20% Type II error rates
- Registered with clinicaltrials.gov

## Setting
- 31 ICUs in Europe
- Study period: November 2018 to November 2021

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 18
- Septic shock per Sepsis-3
- Suspected/confirmed infection, plasma lactate > 2 mmol/L, vasopressor requirement
- 1L fluid administered in prior 24 hours
- Within 12 hours of screening

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Septic shock > 12 hours
- Life-threatening bleeding
- Burns > 10% TBSA
- Known pregnancy

**Enrollment:**
- 2223 screened; 1554 randomised
  - 770 to restrictive group
  - 784 to standard group

## Intervention

**Restrictive Group:**
- Fluids for severe hypoperfusion (lactate > 4 mmol/L, MAP < 50 mmHg, mottling, urine output < 0.1 mL/kg)
- 250-500 mL crystalloid bolus if criteria met, followed by re-evaluation
- Fluids for overt losses, electrolyte correction, and 1L/24hrs intake if oral/enteral contraindicated
- Fluids with medications/nutrition counted as input

## Control

**Standard Care:**
- No upper fluid limit
- IV fluids per SSC guidelines for circulatory impairment, dehydration, or electrolyte correction
- Maintenance fluids per protocol

**Common Management:**
- Isotonic crystalloids
- Albumin post-abdominal paracentesis
- Concomitant septic shock interventions per protocol
- Other treatments at clinician discretion

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death by day 90: Restrictive 42.3% vs Standard 42.1%
  - Adjusted absolute difference: 0.1 (95% CI: -4.7 to 4.9), p = 0.96

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Serious adverse events
  - Days alive without life support
  - Days alive and out of hospital

## Authors’ Conclusions
In adult ICU patients with septic shock, IV fluid restriction did not reduce 90-day mortality compared to standard IV fluid therapy.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, multi-centre trial across 31 ICUs in 8 European countries
  - Pre-published statistical analysis plan
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Minimal loss to follow-up (0.6%)
  - Balanced baseline characteristics
  - Achieved fluid separation via trial protocol
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded
  - GI infections may have required large fluid volumes
  - Powered for 7% mortality reduction
  - Large pre-randomisation fluid volumes
  - Small fluid balance difference by day 5 (~2L)
  - Potential bias towards null due to restrictive standard care
  - Protocol violations: 21.5% in restrictive vs 13.0% in standard group
  - No outcome difference in per-protocol analysis

## Bottom Line
This study did not show superiority of a restrictive fluid strategy over standard management for 90-day mortality in septic shock. Vigilant fluid management remains crucial in ICU care.
