# A Novel Diagnostic Protocol to Identify Patients Suitable for Discharge after a Single High-Sensitivity Troponin (TRUST)

## Clinical Question
In patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS), does a novel accelerated diagnostic protocol (ADP) identify low-risk patients suitable for discharge after a single high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-cTnT)?

## Design
- Prospective observational study
- Consecutive patients
- Blinding of medical staff to initial hs-cTnT result
- Emergency physician staff completed ADP

## Setting
- Single emergency department in the UK
- Study period: July 2012 to August 2013

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with ≥5 minutes of chest pain suggestive of ACS
- Attending physician determined inpatient evaluation was required

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ECG changes (STEMI, LBBB not known to be old, ST depression ≥1mm, T wave inversion consistent with ischaemia, arrhythmias)
- Age ≥80
- Atypical symptoms in absence of chest discomfort
- Clear non-ACS cause for chest pain found at presentation
- Renal failure requiring dialysis

**Enrollment:**
- 964 patients recruited
- 4 patients lost to follow-up

## Test of Interest
- TRUST (Triage Rule-out Using high-Sensitivity Troponin) Accelerated Diagnostic Protocol
- Considered low risk if all of the following:
  - Modified-Goldman score of 0 or 1
  - Non-ischaemic ECG
  - Hs-cTnT <14 ng/L at presentation
- Blood taken for hs-cTnT at a median of 2 hours 55 minutes after chest pain onset

## Gold Standard Investigation
- Risk assessment by ED staff using modified Goldman risk score
- Hs-cTnT at 6 hours after presentation
- Follow-up at least 6 months after attendance by independent review of hospital records, GP records, and national clinical records search
- 5th generation ELECSYS hs-cTnT assay used for both test of interest and gold standard

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Diagnostic accuracy of ADP for fatal or non-fatal MI within 30 days (including index visit)
  - ADP identified 39.8% of patients at low risk of primary outcome
  - 8.3% of all patients had MI within 30 days
  - ADP 98.8% (95% CI 92.5-99.9) sensitive for ruling out MI
  - Single false negative: 78-year-old female with initial hs-cTnT of 13 that rose to 20, managed medically with no further complications

### Secondary Outcomes
- Diagnostic accuracy of ADP for Major Adverse Cardiac Events (MACE) within 30 days
  - 10.1% of all patients had MACE within 30 days
  - ADP 99% (95% CI 93.7-99.9) sensitive for ruling out MACE

- Diagnostic accuracy using a single initial hs-cTnT of <5 ng/L (without risk assessment)
  - For MI within 30 days: Sensitivity 100% (95% CI 94.3-100)
  - For MACE: Sensitivity 96.8% (95% CI 90.6-99.2)
  - 3 false negatives, all aged in their 40s, required urgent revascularisation

## Authors’ Conclusions
The accelerated diagnostic protocol has the potential to allow early discharge in 40% of patients with suspected ACS.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Prospective study design
  - Appropriate inclusion and exclusion criteria
  - Blinding of medical staff to initial hs-cTnT result
  - All patients received gold standard test
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre study
  - Exclusion of patients >80 years limits external validity
  - Moderate patient numbers result in wider 95% confidence intervals for primary outcome
  - Initial hs-cTnT taken nearly 3 hours after chest pain onset; ADP may not apply to earlier presentations

## Bottom Line
The TRUST ADP is a promising tool that may enable clinicians to rapidly exclude ACS in low-risk patients. A multi-centre validation trial is required to confirm these findings, ideally comparing the TRUST ADP with the MACS rule.
