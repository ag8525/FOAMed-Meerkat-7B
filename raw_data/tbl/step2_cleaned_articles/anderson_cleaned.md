# Low-Dose versus Standard-Dose Intravenous Alteplase in Acute Ischemic Stroke

## Clinical Question
In patients with acute ischaemic stroke, is low-dose thrombolysis therapy with intravenous alteplase non-inferior to standard-dose therapy regarding death and disability at 90 days?

## Design
- Prospective, randomised, open-label trial with blinded outcome assessors
- Part of the ENCHANTED study, also examining early intensive BP lowering
- Non-inferiority design
- Follow-up via in-person or telephone by trained staff
- Sample size: 3,300 patients for 90% power to detect non-inferiority with a 14% margin

## Setting
- International, multicentre
- 111 centres across 13 countries (Asia, Australia/New Zealand, Europe, South America)
- March 2012 – August 2015

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- Clinical diagnosis of acute ischaemic stroke confirmed by imaging
- Eligible for thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of symptom onset
- Pre-stroke mRS ≤1
- Systolic BP ≤185 mmHg (or reduced to this level before alteplase)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pre-existing disability or high likelihood of death within 24 hours
- Medical conditions interfering with outcome assessments
- Inability to adhere to follow-up
- No consent
- Previous enrolment in ENCHANTED

**Enrollment:**
- 69,305 screened, 3,310 randomised

## Intervention

**Low-Dose Group:**
- 0.6 mg/kg IV alteplase (max 60 mg)
- 15% bolus (max 9 mg), remainder over 60 minutes

## Control

**Standard-Dose Group:**
- 0.9 mg/kg IV alteplase (max 90 mg)
- 10% bolus (max 9 mg), remainder over 60 minutes

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in mRS 2–6 (indicating poor outcome)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Reduced incidence of symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage in low-dose group
- Reduced mortality at 7 days in low-dose group: 3.6% vs 5.3% (OR 0.67; 95% CI 0.48–0.94; p=0.02)
- No significant difference in:
  - Death or major disability
  - Death within 90 days
  - Overall health utility score (EQ-5D)
  - Admission to residential care
  - Median hospitalisation duration
  - Death or neurological deterioration within 72 hours
  - Serious adverse events

## Authors’ Conclusions
Low-dose alteplase was not shown to be non-inferior to standard-dose in terms of death and disability in acute ischaemic stroke patients eligible for thrombolysis.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - International, multicentre study
  - Blinded outcome assessment
  - Thrombolysis protocol based on common practice
- **Weaknesses**
  - Over 60% of patients were Asian, limiting external validity
  - Not powered to detect differences between Asian and non-Asian populations
  - Main exclusion reason: inability to deliver thrombolysis within 4.5 hours
  - Approximately 10% excluded in per-protocol analysis
  - No consideration of occlusion site or interventional recanalisation strategies

## Bottom Line
Previous meta-analyses suggest increased risk of symptomatic and fatal intracranial haemorrhage with thrombolysis. This trial does not demonstrate non-inferiority (or superiority) of low-dose alteplase compared to standard-dose. Standard-dose alteplase should remain the practice in institutions capable of providing thrombolytic therapy for acute ischaemic stroke.
