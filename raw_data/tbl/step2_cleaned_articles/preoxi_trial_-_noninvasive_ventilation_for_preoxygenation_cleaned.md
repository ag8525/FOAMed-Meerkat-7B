# Noninvasive Ventilation for Preoxygenation during Emergency Intubation (PREOXI Trial)

## Clinical Question
In critically ill patients undergoing tracheal intubation, does preoxygenation with noninvasive ventilation (NIV) compared to an oxygen mask reduce the incidence of hypoxemia during intubation?

## Background
Emergency intubation is common in the ED and ICU, with high adverse event rates. Hypoxemia is a frequent complication, associated with cardiac arrest. Preoxygenation aims to denitrogenate the lungs, providing a buffer during apnoea. Concerns exist about NIV causing gastric insufflation and aspiration risk. Evidence on optimal preoxygenation technique is limited.

## Design
- Pragmatic, multi-centre, randomised controlled trial
- 1:1 randomisation with allocation concealment
- No blinding
- Delayed consent

## Setting
- 24 sites: 7 EDs and 17 ICUs across 15 hospitals in the USA
- Study period: March 2022 to October 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (>18 years) requiring urgent intubation with sedation and laryngoscopy

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Known pregnancy or prisoner status
- Already receiving positive-pressure ventilation
- Apnoea or hypopnoea
- Immediate need for intubation precluding randomisation
- Contraindication to NIV as determined by the operator

**Enrollment:**
- 1301 patients randomised from 4567 assessed for eligibility

## Intervention

**NIV Group:**
- 645 patients received BiPAP during preoxygenation for ≥3 minutes (FiO₂ 100%, EPAP >5 cm H₂O, IPAP >10 cm H₂O, RR >10/min)
- 616 received NIV, 22 received an oxygen mask, 7 another device
- 88.1% received positive pressure ventilation between induction and laryngoscopy

## Control

**Oxygen Mask Group:**
- 656 patients used a non-rebreather mask or bag mask without manual ventilation
- Techniques included non-rebreather mask (87.7%), HFNO (12.2%), bag mask without ventilation (13.7%), bag mask with ventilation (1.2%)
- 31.1% received positive pressure ventilation between induction and laryngoscopy

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Hypoxemia during intubation (SpO₂ <85% between induction and 2 minutes post-intubation)
  - NIV group: 57/624 (9.1%)
  - Oxygen mask group: 118/637 (18.5%)
  - Difference: -9.4% (95% CI: -13.2 to -5.6); p<0.001
  - NNT = 11

### Secondary Outcomes
- Hypoxemia with SpO₂ <80%: NIV 6.2% vs. mask 13.2% (difference: -6.8%, 95% CI: -10.2 to -3.7)
- Hypoxemia with SpO₂ <70%: NIV 2.4% vs. mask 5.7% (difference: -3.2%, 95% CI: -5.4 to -1.1)
- Cardiac arrest: NIV 0.2% vs. mask 1.1% (difference: -0.9%, 95% CI: -1.8 to -0.1)
- New/increased vasopressor use: NIV 17.2% vs. mask 17.8%
- SBP <65 mmHg: NIV 2.9% vs. mask 4.4%

**Subgroup Analysis:**
- All pre-specified subgroups favoured NIV

**Safety:**
- Aspiration: NIV 0.9% vs. mask 1.4%

## Authors’ Conclusions
Preoxygenation with NIV reduces hypoxemia incidence during emergency intubation compared to an oxygen mask.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Pragmatic design with allocation concealment
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Large sample size and multiple sites enhance generalisability
  - Data collection by independent assessors
- **Weaknesses**
  - 20% excluded due to existing NIV use
  - Hypoxemia is a surrogate outcome but correlates with clinical outcomes
  - Control arm (NRBM) may not reflect usual practice in some regions
  - Ventilation during apnoea not mandated, differing from some practices
  - Consideration of cost and waste with BiPAP use

## Bottom Line
This study suggests NIV is more effective than an oxygen mask for preoxygenation in reducing hypoxemia during emergency intubation. However, applicability may vary based on local practices.
