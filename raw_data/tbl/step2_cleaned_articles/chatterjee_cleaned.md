# Thrombolysis for Pulmonary Embolism and Risk of All-Cause Mortality, Major Bleeding, and Intracranial Hemorrhage – A Meta-analysis

## Clinical Question
In patients with pulmonary embolism (PE), does the addition of thrombolysis to anticoagulation, compared with anticoagulation alone, affect mortality and bleeding complications?

## Design
- Meta-analysis
- Followed PRISMA statement
- Systematic search using MeSH and keywords in databases: PubMed, Cochrane, EMBASE, EBSCO, Web of Science, CINAHL
- No language restriction

## Population

**Trial Inclusion:**
- Randomised trials comparing thrombolysis vs. anticoagulant therapy in patients with PE

**Trial Exclusion:**
- Comparisons of different thrombolytic agents or doses

**Trial Populations:**
- Adult patients stratified into high-, intermediate-, low-, and unknown-risk based on haemodynamic stability and RV dysfunction (by echocardiogram or biomarkers)

**Enrollment:**
- 16 trials comprising 2115 patients identified
- 4 trials accounted for 74% of total patients: PEITHO (1005), MAPPETT (256), UPETSG (180), MOPETT (121)
- 8 trials with 1775 patients for intermediate-risk PE subgroup analysis

## Intervention
- Thrombolysis and anticoagulation (various types and doses used)

## Control
- Anticoagulation alone (heparin, low molecular weight heparin, or fondaparinux)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **All-cause mortality:**
  - Thrombolytic: 2.17%
  - Anticoagulants: 3.89%
  - Odds Ratio (OR): 0.53 (95% CI: 0.32–0.88), p = 0.01
  - Absolute Rate Reduction (ARR): 1.72%
  - Number Needed to Treat (NNT): 59

- **Major bleeding:**
  - Thrombolytic: 9.24%
  - Anticoagulants: 3.42%
  - OR: 2.73 (95% CI: 1.91–3.91), p < 0.001
  - ARR: -5.82%
  - Number Needed to Harm (NNH): 18

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Recurrent PE:**
  - Thrombolytic: 1.17%
  - Anticoagulants: 3.04%
  - OR: 0.40 (95% CI: 0.22–0.74), p = 0.03
  - ARR: 1.87%
  - NNT: 53

- **Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH):**
  - Thrombolytic: 1.46%
  - Anticoagulants: 0.19%
  - OR: 4.63 (95% CI: 1.78–12.04), p = 0.002
  - ARR: -1.27%
  - NNH: 79

### Pre-specified Subgroup Analyses
- **Major bleeding (age >65 years):**
  - Thrombolytic: 12.93%
  - Anticoagulants: 4.1%
  - OR: 3.10 (95% CI: 2.10–4.56), p = 0.001
  - ARR: -8.83%
  - NNH: 11

- **Major bleeding (age ≤65 years):**
  - Thrombolytic: 2.84%
  - Anticoagulants: 2.27%
  - OR: 1.25 (95% CI: 0.50–3.14), p = 0.89
  - ARR: -0.57%
  - NNH: 175

- **All-cause mortality (intermediate-risk PE):**
  - Thrombolytic: 1.39%
  - Anticoagulants: 2.92%
  - OR: 0.48 (95% CI: 0.25–0.92), p = 0.03
  - ARR: 1.53%
  - NNT: 65

- **Major bleeding (intermediate-risk PE):**
  - Thrombolytic: 7.74%
  - Anticoagulants: 2.25%
  - OR: 3.19 (95% CI: 2.07–4.92), p = 0.001
  - ARR: -5.49%
  - NNH: 18

## Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with PE, including those who are haemodynamically stable, thrombolysis was associated with lower mortality but increased major bleeding.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Clear inclusion criteria for studies
  - Results of individual studies presented clearly
  - Trial registered with PROSPERO
- **Weaknesses**
  - Non-standardised definitions for haemodynamic instability, major/minor bleeding, and RV dysfunction
  - Varying doses and types of thrombolysis used
  - One study used catheter-directed thrombolysis, which may not be feasible in most centres
  - Risk of bias assessment not reported in results
  - MAPPETT-3 and PEITHO provided 1/3 of patients and have notable weaknesses

## Bottom Line
Thrombolysis for PE is associated with lower all-cause mortality compared to anticoagulation, especially in intermediate-risk PE, despite increased major bleeding risk. For patients ≤65 years, thrombolysis does not increase major bleeding risk. The meta-analysis supports thrombolysis use in submassive PE, considering individual patient risk factors.
