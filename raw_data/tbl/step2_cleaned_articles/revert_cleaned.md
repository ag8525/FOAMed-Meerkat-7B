# Postural Modification to the Standard Valsalva Manoeuvre for Emergency Treatment of Supraventricular Tachycardias (REVERT Trial)

## Clinical Question
Does a modified Valsalva technique, incorporating a leg lift procedure, improve success in converting supraventricular tachycardia to sinus rhythm in patients presenting to the Emergency Department?

## Design
- Multi-centre, randomised controlled trial
- Permuted block concealed allocation
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Treating clinicians non-blinded; investigators blinded to treatment allocation

## Setting
- 10 Emergency Departments in the UK (2 teaching and 8 district general hospitals)
- Study period: January 2013 to December 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients over 18 years with supraventricular tachycardia (regular, narrow complex tachycardia with QRS duration <0.12s on ECG)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Unstable patients (systolic BP <90 mm Hg) or requiring immediate cardioversion
- Atrial fibrillation or flutter
- Contraindications to Valsalva manoeuvre (e.g., aortic stenosis, recent MI)
- Inability to perform Valsalva manoeuvre, lie flat, or have legs lifted
- Third trimester pregnancy
- Previous inclusion in the study

**Enrollment:**
- 711 patients screened, 433 enrolled and randomised, 428 analysed (5 excluded due to repeat enrolment)

## Intervention

**Modified Valsalva Manoeuvre:**
- Lying down with leg lift Valsalva
- Standardised strain in semi-recumbent position, then laid flat with legs raised to 45° for 15 seconds
- Returned to semi-recumbent position for 45 seconds before reassessment

## Control

**Standard Valsalva Manoeuvre:**
- Stay sitting Valsalva
- Semi-recumbent position (45°) on a trolley, perform standardised strain, remain in position for 60 seconds before reassessment

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Presence of sinus rhythm 1 minute after Valsalva manoeuvre (confirmed by ECG)
  - Standard Valsalva: 37/217 (17%)
  - Modified Valsalva: 93/211 (43%)
  - Odds ratio: 3.7 (95% CI: 2.3–5.8), p < 0.0001, NNT = 4

### Secondary Outcomes
- Adenosine given: 148 (69%) vs 108 (50%), p = 0.0002, NNT = 5.3
- Any emergency anti-arrhythmic treatment: 171 (80%) vs 121 (57%), p < 0.0001
- Discharged home from ED: 146 (68%) vs 134 (63%), p = 0.28
- Any adverse effect: 8 (4%) vs 13 (6%), p = 0.32
- Median time in ED: 2.83 hours vs 2.82 hours, p = 0.31

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with supraventricular tachycardia, a modified Valsalva manoeuvre with leg elevation and supine positioning should be considered as a routine first treatment and can be taught to patients.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed, pragmatic study with no cost impact
  - Training video provided to all participating centres
  - Allocation concealment ensured
  - Adequacy of Valsalva technique assessed and similar in both groups
  - No crossover between patient groups
- **Weaknesses**
  - Emergency physician analysing treatment response was non-blinded
  - All trial ECGs retrospectively assessed by an independent cardiologist masked to treatment allocation

## Bottom Line
The modified Valsalva technique is a simple, effective, cost-free, and well-tolerated postural modification to the standard Valsalva technique. This study supports its use over the standard approach as an early treatment for haemodynamically stable patients with supraventricular tachycardia.
