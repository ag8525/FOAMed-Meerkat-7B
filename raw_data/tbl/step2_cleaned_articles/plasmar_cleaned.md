# A Randomized Trial of Convalescent Plasma in Covid-19 Severe Pneumonia

## Clinical Question
In hospitalized adult patients with severe Covid-19 pneumonia, does convalescent plasma compared with placebo improve patients’ clinical status at 30 days?

## Background
Convalescent plasma has been used for various infections, including SARS, MERS, and influenza. Its efficacy in Covid-19 has been frequently claimed but lacked conclusive data from randomized trials.

## Design
- Double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial
- Random assignment via REDCap randomization program in a 2:1 ratio (convalescent plasma vs placebo)
- Designed to enroll 333 patients to provide 80% power to detect a proportional odds ratio of 1.8 for plasma vs placebo on the clinical ordinal scale at the 0.05 level of significance
- 334 patients enrolled; 1 withdrew consent before intervention

## Setting
- Conducted at 12 clinical sites in Argentina
- Study period: May 28 to August 27, 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults over 18 years old
- Positive RT-PCR for SARS-CoV-2
- Radiologically confirmed pneumonia
- No directives rejecting advanced life support
- At least one severity criterion:
  - Oxygen saturation < 93% on ambient air
  - PaO₂/FiO₂ < 300 mm Hg
  - SOFA or mSOFA score ≥ 2 points above baseline

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant or lactating
- Not willing to use contraceptive measures for 30 days post-enrollment
- History of blood component allergies
- Infectious cause of pneumonia other than SARS-CoV-2
- Requirement for mechanical ventilation or multiorgan failure

**Enrollment:**
- 228 received convalescent plasma, 105 received placebo
- Median time from symptom onset to enrollment: 8 days

## Intervention

**Convalescent Plasma Group:**
- Single administration of Covid-19 convalescent plasma
- Plasma from 1-5 donors, antibody titer goal > 1:800
- Median volume: 500 ml

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Single administration of placebo (normal saline solution)

**Common Management:**
- Antiviral agents, glucocorticoids, or both allowed if part of standard care

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Clinical status at 30 days: no significant difference
  - Odds ratio: 0.83 (95% CI: 0.52–1.35); p = 0.46
  - Non-significant after adjustment for sex, COPD, tobacco use

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - 30-day mortality: 10.96% vs 11.43%
  - Invasive ventilatory support at 30 days: 8.3% vs 9.5%
  - Clinical status on ordinal scale at days 7 and 14
  - Time to hospital discharge

**Adverse Events:**
- No significant differences in overall or serious adverse events

**Sub-group Analysis:**
- Significant difference favoring placebo for patients < 65 years
  - Odds ratio: 0.18 (95% CI: 0.06–0.54)

## Authors’ Conclusions
The use of convalescent plasma did not result in significant clinical benefit compared with placebo in patients with severe Covid-19 pneumonia.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomized, controlled trial
  - Attempted subgroup analysis
  - No external funding
  - Blinded participants and research team
- **Weaknesses**
  - Median time from symptom onset to enrollment was 8 days
  - No difference in subgroup receiving intervention within 72 hours
  - Study focused on severe pneumonia; unclear efficacy in milder cases

## Bottom Line
PlasmAr data show no benefit of convalescent plasma in severe Covid-19 pneumonia for reducing mortality or improving clinical outcomes at 30 days. Efficacy in milder cases or with earlier administration remains unclear.
