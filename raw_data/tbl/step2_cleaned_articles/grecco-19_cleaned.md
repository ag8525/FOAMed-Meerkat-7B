# Effect of Colchicine vs Standard Care on Cardiac and Inflammatory Biomarkers and Clinical Outcomes in Patients Hospitalized With Coronavirus Disease 2019 (GRECCO-19 Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients hospitalized with symptomatic COVID-19, does the administration of colchicine improve clinical and laboratory outcomes?

## Background
COVID-19 has prompted numerous trials to explore potential treatments. Colchicine, an anti-inflammatory drug used in conditions like gout and pericarditis, was hypothesized to benefit COVID-19 patients due to its effects on inflammatory pathways.

## Design
- Open-label, randomised controlled trial
- Multi-centre
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Power calculation: 180 patients needed for 90% power to detect a 50% reduction in the primary clinical endpoint

## Setting
- 16 tertiary care hospitals in Greece
- Patient recruitment: 3 April 2020 to 27 April 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Hospitalized adults with confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection (rtPCR)
- Temperature ≥ 37.5°C and two or more symptoms (e.g., cough, sore throat, anosmia, fatigue, PaO₂ < 95 mm Hg)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy or lactation
- Known hypersensitivity to colchicine
- Hepatic failure, GFR < 20 mL/min/1.73 m²
- cQT ≥ 450 ms
- Imminent need for ventilatory support

**Enrollment:**
- 110 patients randomized; 105 included in final analysis

## Intervention

**Colchicine Group:**
- Loading dose: 1.5 mg + 0.5 mg after 1 hour if no GI effects
- Maintenance: 0.5 mg twice daily until discharge or 21 days
- Adjusted to once daily if weight < 60 kg

## Control
- Standard care

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcomes
- **Peak high sensitivity cardiac Troponin (hsTnT):** No significant difference
- **Time to CRP > 3x normal:** No significant difference
- **Clinical deterioration (WHO R&D Blueprint Ordinal Clinical Scale):** 
  - Intervention: 1.8% vs. Control: 14%, p = 0.02
  - Odds ratio: 0.11 (95% CI: 0.01–0.96)

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Cumulative event-free 10-day survival:** Intervention: 97% vs. Control: 83%, p = 0.03
- **All-cause mortality:** No significant difference
- **Need for mechanical ventilation:** No significant difference
- **Adverse events:** Diarrhoea more common in intervention group (46% vs. 18%, p = 0.003)
- **Median hospital stay:** No significant difference (12 vs. 13 days)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Colchicine improved time to clinical deterioration compared to standard care, but the clinical significance is narrow, suggesting these findings are hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Rapidly conducted in challenging circumstances
  - Thorough baseline characteristics comparison
  - Multi-centre study with no loss to follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - Small sample size and early termination due to slow enrollment
  - Fragility index of 1 for the primary clinical outcome
  - Heavily sponsored by pharmaceutical companies
  - Optimistic power calculation target
  - Two primary outcomes were not patient-centered

## Bottom Line
This trial suggests colchicine may reduce clinical deterioration in COVID-19 patients, but the evidence is not robust enough to change practice. Further research is needed before considering colchicine as a standard treatment.
