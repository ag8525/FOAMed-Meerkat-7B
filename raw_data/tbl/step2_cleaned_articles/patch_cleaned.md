# Prehospital Tranexamic Acid for Severe Trauma (PATCH Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with major trauma at risk for trauma-induced coagulopathy, does early administration of tranexamic acid (TXA) increase survival with a favourable functional outcome at 6 months compared to placebo?

## Background
- TXA is a competitive inhibitor of plasminogen activation, interfering with fibrinolysis.
- Trauma is a leading cause of death in young people, often due to haemorrhage exacerbated by coagulopathy.
- TXA use varies globally, with higher adoption in the UK compared to the USA and Australia.
- Previous trials (CRASH-2, CRASH-3, TAMPITI, STAAMP, ROC) have shown mixed results regarding TXA's efficacy in trauma.

## Design
- Pragmatic, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial
- 1:1 randomisation to TXA or placebo
- 90% power to detect an increase in favourable GOS-E (score 5-8) from 60% to 69% with TXA
- Consent sought as soon as practical
- Data and safety monitoring committee oversight
- Randomisation via phone app
- Blinding achieved with identical packaging

## Setting
- 36 trauma services in Australia, New Zealand, and Germany
- Enrollment: 28 July 2014 to 28 September 2021
- Data collection extended 6 months beyond final recruitment

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with major trauma at high risk for coagulopathy (COAST score >3)
- TXA administration possible within 3 hours of injury

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant patients or residents of aged care facilities

**Enrollment:**
- 1316 patients enrolled

## Intervention

**TXA Group:**
- 1g TXA slow push over 10 minutes at trauma scene or en route
- 1g TXA in 1L 0.9% saline over 8 hours

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Placebo slow push over 10 minutes at trauma scene or en route
- Placebo in 1L 0.9% saline over 8 hours

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in survival with favourable functional outcome at 6 months (GOS-E >5)
  - TXA: 53.7% (307/572)
  - Placebo: 53.5% (299/559)
  - ARR 0.2%; 95% CI, −5.6 to 6.0; RR 1.00; 95% CI, 0.90 to 1.12; p = 0.95

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Mortality:**
  - 24 hours: Reduced in TXA group (9.7% vs 14.1%; RR 0.69; 95% CI, 0.51-0.94)
  - 28 days: Reduced in TXA group (17.3% vs 21.8%; RR 0.79; 95% CI, 0.63-0.99)
  - 6 months: No significant difference (19% vs 22.9%; RR 0.83; 95% CI, 0.67-1.03)
- **Bleeding as cause of death:** 29.3% vs 36.1% (HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.43-1.01)
- **Vascular occlusion:** No significant difference

## Authors’ Conclusions
Prehospital TXA administration did not result in a greater number of patients surviving with a favourable functional outcome at 6 months compared to placebo.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses
  - Allocation concealment and blinding
  - Good baseline balance
  - High injury severity scores increase treatment effect detection
  - Focus on functional outcomes, important for patients and families
- **Weaknesses**
  - 13% loss to follow-up
  - Protocol deviations in 32.7% (TXA) and 37.0% (placebo)
  - Longer-term follow-up needed for functional outcomes
  - Inadequate power for subgroup analyses

## Bottom Line
The PATCH trial supports CRASH-2 findings that prehospital TXA reduces early death due to haemorrhage in major trauma. However, more survivors with poor neurological outcomes in the TXA group highlight the need for careful consideration of TXA use in trauma settings.
