# Expedited Transfer to a Cardiac Arrest Centre for Non-ST-Elevation Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (ARREST)

## Clinical Question
In patients with non-ST elevation out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does expedited delivery to a cardiac arrest centre, compared with delivery to the geographically closest hospital, improve all-cause mortality at 30 days?

## Background
Regional variations in survival following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest have been reported. Concentrating services within regionalised centres may improve survival by utilising the skills and infrastructure of these centres. However, previous studies have shown that expedited coronary angiography following OHCA without ST elevation does not improve survival and may worsen outcomes. It is unknown if direct transfer to a cardiac centre in this context is beneficial.

## Design
- Multicentre, open-label, randomised trial
- Randomisation by paramedics using an online system with permuted blocks of 4 or 6
- Non-blinded due to the nature of the intervention
- Randomisation paused twice due to COVID-19 pandemic
- Sample size: 430 patients per group for 80% power to detect a 10% absolute risk reduction in 30-day mortality
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Registered with the International Standard Randomised Controlled Trials Registry

## Setting
- London, UK: 35 hospitals and the London Ambulance Service
- Data collection: January 2018 – December 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged ≥18 years with return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) following OHCA

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Presumed non-cardiac cause
- Pregnancy
- ST-elevation myocardial infarction on post-resuscitation ECG
- Do-not-attempt-resuscitation order

**Enrollment:**
- 862 patients randomised (431 to each group)

## Intervention

**Cardiac Arrest Centre Group:**
- Direct transport to a cardiac arrest centre (CAC) with strategic delivery to the catheter laboratory
- Assessment by on-call team including cardiologist and intensivist

## Control

**Closest Emergency Department Group:**
- Transport to the geographically closest Emergency Department (ED)

## Management Common to Both Groups
- LAS resuscitation protocols following European and International guidelines
- In-hospital management at physician’s discretion
- Delayed neuroprognostication >72 hours

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- All-cause mortality at 30 days: No significant difference
  - CAC: 63%
  - ED: 63%
  - Relative risk (RR): 1.00 (95% CI: 0.90–1.11)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in 3-month mortality or modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores at discharge and 3 months
  - 3-month mortality: CAC 65% vs ED 64% (RR 1.02; 95% CI: 0.92–1.12)
  - Favourable mRS at discharge: CAC 32% vs ED 32% (RR 1.01; 95% CI: 0.92–1.11)
  - Favourable mRS at 3 months: CAC 30% vs ED 31% (RR 1.01; 95% CI: 0.92–1.11)

**Subgroup Analysis:**
- Age <57 years: Reduced risk of all-cause mortality by 30 days in CAC group (RR 0.76; 95% CI: 0.60–0.97)
- Age 57-71 years: Higher risk of all-cause mortality by 30 days in CAC group (RR 1.28; 95% CI: 1.05–1.56)

## Authors’ Conclusions
No difference in 30-day all-cause mortality for patients with resuscitated cardiac arrest without ST elevation delivered to a cardiac arrest centre compared to the closest emergency department.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre, randomised, controlled trial
  - Central randomisation ensured allocation concealment
  - Well-matched baseline demographics
  - Intention-to-treat analysis with minimal protocol deviations and attrition
  - Broad inclusion criteria enhance external validity
  - Patient-centered primary outcome with strategic planning implications
- **Weaknesses**
  - Open-label design could introduce bias
  - Hypothesis from observational data not tested for patients without ROSC before hospital admission or with ST-elevation myocardial infarction
  - Potential benefit for younger patients not fully explored
  - Limited external validity due to urban setting

## Bottom Line
This study does not support prehospital transfer of all patients to a cardiac arrest centre following resuscitated cardiac arrest without ST elevation.
