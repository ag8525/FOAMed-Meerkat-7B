# Microaxial Flow Pump or Standard Care in Infarct-Related Cardiogenic Shock

## Clinical Question
In adults presenting with STEMI and cardiogenic shock, does the use of a microaxial flow pump (Impella CP) compared to standard care reduce death from any cause at day 180?

## Background
Cardiogenic shock is a frequent complication of STEMI, associated with significant morbidity and mortality. Microaxial flow pumps (MAFP) are percutaneously inserted devices that pump blood from the left ventricle into the ascending aorta. Previous RCTs have been small, showing no benefit and some harm (e.g., bleeding) in registry studies.

## Design
- International, multi-center RCT
- Open-label
- Randomised via web-based system, stratified by STEMI location and timing relative to revascularisation
- Randomisation occurred in the catheter laboratory, either before or after revascularisation (up to 12 hours post-leaving), depending on when cardiogenic shock was recognised
- Sample size: 360 patients to detect a reduction in 180-day mortality from 60% to 42% with 80% power at α = 0.05
- Pre-defined subgroups
- Registered with clinicaltrials.gov

## Setting
- 14 centers in Denmark (4), Germany (9), and the UK (1)
- Study period: January 2013 to July 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥ 18 years old
- STEMI or STEMI equivalent
- Cardiogenic shock (SBP < 100 mmHg ± vasopressor support, lactate > 2.5 mmol/L, LV ejection fraction < 45%)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Resuscitated from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest and comatose (GCS < 8)
- Severe right ventricular failure
- Shock duration > 24 hours
- Other shock causes or mechanical MI complications
- Severe aortic regurgitation/stenosis
- Severe arterial disease precluding placement

**Enrollment:**
- 1211 screened, 360 enrolled
- 851 excluded; 39 eligible but not randomised
- 5 excluded post-randomisation due to consent issues

## Intervention

**MAFP Group:**
- Insertion of MAFP immediately post-randomisation
- Operated at highest performance level for 48 hours unless complications occurred
- Median MAFP support duration: 59 hours
- 8 did not receive MAFP

## Control

**Standard Care Group:**
- No MAFP use
- 3 crossed over to MAFP

**Common Management:**
- All underwent revascularisation as needed
- Escalation for haemodynamic instability: Impella 5.0/RP or ECMO in MAFP group; ECMO recommended in control group (Impella 5.0 allowed as protocol violation)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death from any cause at 180 days:
  - MAFP: 45.8%
  - Standard care: 58.5%
  - HR 0.74 (95% CI: 0.55–0.99), NNT = 8

### Secondary Outcomes
- Composite cardiac endpoint (mechanical support escalation, heart transplantation, or death): 53% vs 64%; HR 0.72 (95% CI: 0.55–0.95)
- Days alive and out of hospital at day 180: 82 vs 73; Mean difference 8 (95% CI: -8 to 25)

**Safety Outcomes:**
- Composite safety endpoint (severe bleeding, limb ischaemia, haemolysis, worsening aortic regurgitation, device failure): 24% vs 6%; RR 4.74 (95% CI: 2.36–9.55)
- RRT: 42% vs 27%; RR 1.98 (95% CI: 1.27–3.09)

**Subgroups:**
- Favourable 95% CI for MAFP in males, MAP < 64 mmHg, and ≥2 disease vessels

## Authors’ Conclusions
Routine use of a microaxial flow pump in STEMI-related cardiogenic shock reduces 180-day mortality but increases adverse events.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - High internal validity
  - Rapid device placement post-randomisation
  - Well-balanced baseline characteristics
  - Minimal protocol violations and loss to follow-up
  - Clear safety outcome definitions
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded
  - Confidence intervals lose significance in as-treated analysis
  - Single UK center may limit external validity
  - Slow enrollment over 10 years
  - Safety outcomes do not account for competing risks
  - Industry involvement (Abiomed)

## Bottom Line
This trial shows a mortality benefit with MAFP use in STEMI-related cardiogenic shock, despite increased adverse events. It should prompt discussions on MAFP use in local healthcare settings.
