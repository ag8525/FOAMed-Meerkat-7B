# A Randomised Trial of Hydroxychloroquine as Postexposure Prophylaxis for COVID-19

## Clinical Question
In individuals with high-risk or moderate-risk exposure to confirmed COVID-19 cases, does hydroxychloroquine post-exposure prophylaxis reduce the incidence of new illness compatible with COVID-19 compared to placebo?

## Background
- COVID-19, caused by SARS-CoV-2, emerged in Wuhan, China, in December 2019 and was declared a pandemic by WHO in March 2020.
- Healthcare workers (HCWs) are particularly vulnerable due to occupational exposure.
- Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) has been considered for COVID-19 prophylaxis due to its potential to inhibit viral entry and endocytosis.

## Design
- Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- 1:1 assignment to HCQ or placebo
- Participants enrolled online and received interventions via courier
- Outcome assessments via email surveys on Days 1, 5, 10, 14, and 4-6 weeks

## Setting
- Participants from the USA and three Canadian provinces (Quebec, Alberta, Manitoba)
- Study period: March 17 to May 6, 2020
- Trial halted after third interim analysis due to futility

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Self-selected participants with household or occupational exposure to confirmed COVID-19 cases
- Exposure within 6 feet for >10 minutes without mask/goggles (high-risk) or with mask/no goggles (moderate-risk)
- Enrollment within 3 days of exposure

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age <18 years
- Current use of hydroxychloroquine, chloroquine, or certain cardiac medications
- Hydroxychloroquine allergy, retinal disease, G6PD deficiency, CKD 4/5, porphyria, weight <40 kg, chemotherapy
- Additional exclusions in Canada: pregnancy, severe GI symptoms, cirrhosis, prolonged QT interval, ventricular arrhythmia

**Enrollment:**
- 821 participants: 245 household exposure, 545 HCW exposure, 31 other occupational exposure

## Intervention

**Hydroxychloroquine Group:**
- 414 participants received 19 x 200 mg HCQ tablets
- Dosing: 4 tablets on Day 1, then 3 tablets 8 hours later, followed by 3 tablets daily for 4 days

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- 407 participants received 19 placebo tablets
- Dosing matched HCQ group

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Symptomatic illness confirmed by positive assay or US Epidemiological criteria
- No significant difference: HCQ 11.8% vs. Placebo 14.3% (95% CI: -7 to 2.2; p=0.35)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Hospitalisation: 0.2% vs. 0.2%
- Death: 0% vs. 0%
- PCR-confirmed SARS-CoV-2: 2.7% vs. 2.2% (p=0.82)
- COVID-19 symptoms: 11.6% vs. 13.5% (p=0.46)
- Adherence: 75.4% vs. 82.6% (p=0.01)
- Side effects: 40.1% vs. 16.8% (p<0.001), mainly nausea and GI symptoms

## Authors’ Conclusions
This trial did not demonstrate a significant benefit of hydroxychloroquine as post-exposure prophylaxis for COVID-19.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Allocation concealment and blinding of outcome assessors
  - Clinically meaningful outcomes
  - Geographically diverse participant pool
  - Timely implementation during the pandemic
- **Weaknesses**
  - Trial stopped early, underpowered for primary outcome
  - Delayed intervention in many participants
  - Limited PCR testing availability

## Bottom Line
Based on this trial, hydroxychloroquine is not recommended for post-exposure prophylaxis against COVID-19 due to lack of efficacy and high incidence of adverse effects.
