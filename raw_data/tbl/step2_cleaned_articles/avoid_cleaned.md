# Air Versus Oxygen in ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction (AVOID Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) without hypoxia, does receiving supplemental oxygen compared with no supplemental oxygen reduce early myocardial injury?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled clinical trial  
- Computer-generated randomisation sequence  
- Sealed opaque envelopes for allocation concealment  
- Block randomisation  
- Single-blinded (outcome assessors and statisticians)  

## Setting
- Pre-hospital Ambulance Victoria and 9 metropolitan hospitals with 24-hour PCI in Melbourne, Australia  
- Study period: October 2011 to July 2014  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with acute STEMI  
- Age >18 years  
- Chest pain <12 hours  
- Prehospital ECG evidence of STEMI (ST-segment elevation >0.1mV in 2 contiguous limb leads or >0.2mV in 2 contiguous chest leads, or new LBBB pattern)  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- SaO₂ < 94%  
- Bronchospasm requiring nebulised salbutamol with oxygen  
- Altered conscious state or planned transport to a nonparticipating hospital  

**Enrollment:**
- 638 patients screened and randomised  
- 470 patients (74%) included in analysis  
- All had emergent angiography  

## Intervention

**Oxygen Group:**
- Supplemental oxygen via face mask at 8 L/min administered by paramedics  
- Continued until transfer from cardiac catheter lab to cardiac ward  
- 1 patient did not receive oxygen as assigned  

## Control

**No Oxygen Group:**
- No oxygen unless SaO₂ < 94%  
- If required, oxygen via nasal prongs (4 L/min) or mask (8 L/min) to achieve SaO₂ > 94%  
- 17 patients required oxygen  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Myocardial Injury:**  
  - No difference in mean peak troponin level: 57.4 vs 48 mcg/L (95% CI: 0.92–1.56; p = 0.18)  
  - Significant increase in mean peak creatinine kinase (CK) in oxygen group: 1948 vs 1543 U/L (95% CI: 1.04–1.52; p = 0.01)  

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Major Cardiac Arrhythmia at Discharge:**  
  - No difference: 40.4% vs 31.4% (p = 0.05)  
- **Recurrent Myocardial Infarction at Discharge:**  
  - Increased in oxygen group: 5.5% vs 0.9% (p = 0.006)  
- **Recurrent Myocardial Infarction at 6 Months:**  
  - No significant difference: 7.6% vs 3.6% (p = 0.07)  
- **Median Infarct Size at 6 Months (CMR):**  
  - Increased in oxygen group: 20.3 vs 13.1 (p = 0.04)  
  - Only 32% received CMR  
- **Adverse Events at 6 Months:**  
  - No difference in major cardiac events (all-cause mortality, recurrent MI, repeat revascularisation, stroke): 46 (21.9%) vs 34 (15.4%) (p = 0.08)  

## Authors’ Conclusions
This trial does not provide evidence that routine oxygen therapy for STEMI is beneficial and suggests it may increase myocardial injury.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Relevant clinical question challenging existing dogma  
  - Allocation concealment  
  - Intention-to-treat analysis of primary endpoints  
  - Only 3% lost to follow-up  
  - Outcomes assessed by blinded assessors  

- **Weaknesses**
  - Not generalisable to all AMI, only STEMI  
  - Treatment not blinded  
  - Primary outcome measures are surrogate endpoints  
  - Median saturation difference may not be clinically significant  
  - No information on occlusion location  
  - Secondary outcomes not analysed on intention-to-treat basis  
  - Not powered for clinically meaningful outcomes  
  - Only 32% had follow-up CMR imaging  

## Bottom Line
This trial fails to show any benefit in giving oxygen to normoxic patients with STEMI undergoing PCI and may cause harm. Results should be interpreted cautiously as endpoints are surrogate outcomes only. Await results of DETO2X-AMI for more clinically meaningful outcomes.
