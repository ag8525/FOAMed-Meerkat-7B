# Early Use of Norepinephrine in Septic Shock Resuscitation (CENSER Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients presenting to the emergency department with septic shock, does early low-dose norepinephrine compared with standard care increase shock control at six hours?

## Background
The optimal timing for vasopressor therapy in septic shock is unclear. Traditionally, fluids are administered first, followed by vasopressors based on fluid response. The 2018 Surviving Sepsis Campaign Bundle suggests rapid initial resuscitation, including vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation to maintain MAP >65 mmHg. However, the evidence supporting these guidelines is weak, and no large prospective studies have defined the optimal timing for vasopressor initiation.

## Design
- Single-centre, double-blind, randomised controlled trial
- 1:1 assignment to groups
- Allocation concealment via computer-generated randomisation
- Blinding with identical packaging for norepinephrine and placebo
- Outcome assessors were unaware of group allocation
- Sample size of 300 for 80% power to detect shock resolution rate differences
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- Conducted at Siriraj Hospital, a large university-based tertiary referral hospital in Thailand
- Recruitment: October 2013 to March 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients >18 years with MAP <65 mmHg and suspected infection

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Septic shock >1 hour
- Acute CVA, ACS, APO, status asthmaticus, active cardiac arrhythmia, acute GIH, pregnancy, burns, advanced cancer, refusal of medical therapies

**Enrollment:**
- 456 patients screened; 320 met inclusion criteria
- 310 patients randomised (155 per group)

## Intervention
- 4 mg norepinephrine in 250 ml 5% Dextrose, administered at 0.05 µg/kg/min
- Infusion for 24 hours without titration

## Control
- 250 ml 5% Dextrose at identical rate without titration

## Common Management
- SSC 2012 guidelines: crystalloid bolus, antibiotics, source control, organ support
- Open-label vasopressors if MAP >65 mmHg not achieved after 30 ml/kg crystalloid

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Shock control by 6 hours: sustained MAP >65 mmHg and urine output >0.5 ml/kg/hr for 2 hours or lactate decrease >10%
  - Early norepinephrine: 76.1% vs. Control: 48.4% (OR 3.4, 95% CI 2.09–5.53, p<0.001)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Mortality at Day 28: 15.5% vs. 21.9% (p=0.15)
- Shock resolution time: 4:45 hrs vs. 6:02 hrs (p<0.001)
- Intravenous fluid before open-label norepinephrine: 2080 ml vs. 1900 ml (p=0.32)
- Need for open-label norepinephrine: 67.7% vs. 80% (p=0.014)
- Time to norepinephrine: 1:10 hrs vs. 2:47 hrs (p<0.001)
- ICU admission: 54.8% vs. 51.6% (p=0.57)
- Mechanical ventilation: 37.4% vs. 38.1% (p=0.91)
- Renal replacement therapy: 12.3% vs. 14.8% (p=0.51)
- Pulmonary oedema: 14.4% vs. 27.7% (p=0.004)
- New-onset arrhythmia: 11% vs. 20% (p=0.03)
- Skin necrosis: 0.6% vs. 0.6%

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early norepinephrine was associated with increased shock control at 6 hours.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses an important clinical question on vasopressor timing in septic shock
  - First prospective trial on this component of the sepsis bundle
  - Robust methodology with allocation concealment, blinding, and intention-to-treat analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre trial in a developing nation with limited ICU resources, affecting external validity
  - Potential unblinding due to blood pressure changes
  - Primary outcome not clinically meaningful; secondary outcomes were hypothesis-generating

## Bottom Line
The results are encouraging and support conducting a larger, multi-centre trial on vasopressor timing in septic shock. Low-dose norepinephrine was safely administered peripherally, reassuring for resource-limited settings.
