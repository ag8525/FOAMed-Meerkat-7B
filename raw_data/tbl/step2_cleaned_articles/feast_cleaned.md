# Mortality after Fluid Bolus in African Children with Severe Infection (FEAST Trial)

## Clinical Question
In paediatric patients with severe febrile illnesses in a resource-limited setting, do fluid boluses with albumin versus saline versus no fluid boluses affect mortality?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Use of opaque, sealed envelopes
- Permuted blocks of random sizes
- Stratified by clinical centre
- Non-blinded
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Sample size calculation: 3600 patients for 80% power to detect a 33% relative reduction in mortality with saline vs. control and a 40% reduction with albumin vs. saline
- Study stopped early on safety monitoring committee's recommendation

## Setting
- 6 centres in Africa
- Study period: January 2009 to January 2011

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Children aged 60 days to 12 years
- Severe febrile illness
- Impaired consciousness (prostration or coma) and/or respiratory distress (increased work of breathing)
- Impaired perfusion, evidenced by:
  - Capillary refill time ≥ 3 seconds
  - Lower-limb temperature gradient
  - Weak radial-pulse volume
  - Heart rate > 180 if < 1 year, > 160 if 1-5 years, or > 140 if > 5 years

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Severe malnutrition
- Gastroenteritis
- Non-infectious cause of shock (e.g., trauma, surgery, burn)
- Contraindication to volume expansion

**Enrollment:**
- 3170 patients randomised

## Intervention

**In patients without severe hypotension (n=3141):**
- 20 ml/kg normal saline
- 20 ml/kg 5% albumin
- No bolus

**In children with severe hypotension (n=29):**
- 40 ml/kg normal saline
- 40 ml/kg 5% albumin

**Protocol amendment (June 2010):**
- Initial bolus increased to 40 ml/kg in patients without severe hypotension and 60 ml/kg in patients with severe hypotension

**Additional Treatment:**
- Patients in bolus groups received an additional 20 ml/kg at 1 hour if impaired perfusion persisted
- Severe hypotension treated with 40 ml/kg fluid (saline in control group)
- All children treated on general paediatric wards; assisted ventilation limited to short-term bag-and-mask support
- Transfusion if Hb < 5 g/dl

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Mortality at 48 hours in patients without severe hypotension was significantly worse in bolus groups vs. control group. No significant difference between albumin vs. saline:
  - Albumin bolus: 10.6%
  - Saline bolus: 10.5%
  - Control: 7.3%
  - RR of death with saline vs. control: 1.44 (95% CI: 1.09–1.90, p = 0.01)
  - RR of death with albumin vs. saline: 1.00 (95% CI: 0.78–1.29, p = 0.96)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Pulmonary oedema: 1.3% (albumin) vs. 0.6% (saline) vs. 0.6% (control)
- Mortality at 4 weeks significantly worse in bolus groups: 12.2% (albumin) vs. 12.0% (saline) vs. 8.7% (control)
- In patients with severe hypotension, no significant difference in mortality between albumin vs. saline bolus: 69% vs. 56% (RR 1.23, 95% CI: 0.70–2.16, p = 0.45)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In a resource-limited setting in Africa, children with severe febrile illnesses and impaired perfusion treated with fluid boluses had significantly increased mortality compared with controls.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Allocation concealment maintained
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Multi-centre study
- **Weaknesses**
  - Inclusion criteria non-specific for shock diagnosis
  - Protocol change mid-study
  - Concerns over external validity
  - Low transfusion threshold may have led to haemodilution
  - High incidence of malaria, which may affect fluid bolus efficacy

## Bottom Line
In an African setting, febrile children with impaired perfusion had increased mortality if treated with a fluid bolus compared to no fluid bolus. The study population had high malaria and severe anaemia incidence, managed with a low transfusion threshold and without critical care facilities, differing from other settings.
