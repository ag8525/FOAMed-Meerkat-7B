# Early Noninvasive Ventilation in General Wards for Acute Respiratory Failure (NAVIGATE Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with mild acute respiratory failure (ARF), does a regime of 2-hour cycles of noninvasive ventilation (NIV), delivered on general wards every 8 hours, reduce the progression to severe acute respiratory failure?

## Background
- ARF is a significant cause of ICU referrals and admissions, with high morbidity and mortality.
- The UK has fewer critical care beds per capita, making prevention of severe ARF crucial.
- NIV can maintain alveolar recruitment and reduce work of breathing but may also worsen shunt and secretion clearance.
- Continuous NIV is standard for COPD and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema but not recommended for community-acquired pneumonia and asthma.

## Design
- Multicentre, international, open-label, randomised controlled trial
- Pre-published trial protocol and statistical analysis plan
- 1:1 randomisation, stratified by centre
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Sample size: 520 patients for 90% power to detect a reduction in primary outcome from 22% to 11%

## Setting
- 11 hospitals in Italy, Greece, and Kazakhstan
- Recruitment from May 2012 to July 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients ≥18 years old with mild ARF admitted to non-ICU wards
- Mild ARF defined by at least one of:
  - New pulmonary consolidation or atelectasis
  - Hypoxaemia (SpO₂ <92% on room air or PaO₂/FiO₂ <300)
  - Mild decompensated hypercapnoea (PaCO₂ >6 with pH <7.35)
  - Clinical signs of respiratory distress

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ARF due to acute COPD exacerbation
- Severe hypoxaemia (PaO₂/FiO₂ <200)
- Severe hypercapnoeic acidaemia (PaCO₂ >6 with pH <7.30)
- Immediate need for mechanical ventilation or ICU admission
- Prior mechanical ventilation during hospital admission
- Poor prognosis, refusal of consent, or contraindications to NIV

**Enrollment:**
- 1317 screened; 524 randomised; 523 in primary analysis

## Intervention

**Early NIV Group:**
- 2-hour cycles of NIV every 8 hours
- CPAP (5–8 cmH₂O) or BIPAP for hypercapnic patients
- Target SpO₂ >92% and PaCO₂ <6
- Continued for 24 hours to 12 days

## Control

**Usual Care Group:**
- Standard care at physician's discretion
- No NIV unless primary endpoint reached

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Progression to severe respiratory failure during hospitalisation
  - Early NIV: 18.5%
  - Usual care: 28.3%
  - Relative risk: 0.65 (95% CI: 0.48–0.90), p = 0.08, NNT = 11

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Length of hospital stay
  - All-cause 28-day mortality
  - 90-day mortality
  - ICU admission
  - Invasive mechanical ventilation

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early NIV in non-ICU wards reduces progression to severe ARF in patients with mild ARF.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multicentre, investigator-initiated trial
  - Pragmatic inclusion of diverse ARF subgroups
  - Excellent follow-up and low crossover rate
- **Weaknesses**
  - Primary outcome driven by subjective clinical signs
  - No difference in intubation rates, length of stay, or mortality
  - Trial predates widespread use of high-flow nasal oxygen
  - Non-standardised patient monitoring

## Bottom Line
Early NIV may reduce progression to severe ARF in mild cases but lacks impact on more patient-centred outcomes. Cost-effectiveness remains uncertain.
