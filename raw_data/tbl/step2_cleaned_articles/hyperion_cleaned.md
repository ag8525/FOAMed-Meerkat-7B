# Therapeutic Hypothermia After Cardiac Arrest With Non-Shockable Rhythm (HYPERION)

## Clinical Question
In patients with coma following cardiac arrest with a non-shockable rhythm, does moderate hypothermia versus normothermia improve neurological outcomes?

## Background
- The HACA trial (2000) showed therapeutic hypothermia improved neurological outcomes after VF arrest, but control group fever confounded results.
- The TTM study found no significant difference between 33°C and 36°C in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause.
- A TTM sub-study (n=178) showed no benefit of 33°C vs. 36°C in non-shockable rhythm arrests.
- ILCOR guidelines recommend targeted temperature management (32-36°C) for all comatose post-cardiac arrest patients.
- Unclear if hypothermia benefits non-shockable rhythm arrests, including in-hospital cases.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Randomised via web-based system (1:1 ratio, permuted blocks)
- Stratified by centre and cardiac arrest cause
- Open-label, blinded-outcome-assessor
- 90-day follow-up via semi-structured telephone interview by a psychologist
- 584 patients needed for 80% power to detect primary outcome improvement from 14% to 23% (5% significance level)
- Missing data assumed as death (1 intervention, 2 control)

## Setting
- 25 ICUs in France (11 university, 14 community hospitals)
- Data collection: 2014–2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- Resuscitated from out-of-hospital or in-hospital cardiac arrest with non-shockable rhythm
- GCS ≤8 at ICU admission or pre-sedation GCS

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- No-flow time >10 minutes
- Low-flow time >60 minutes
- Epinephrine/norepinephrine >1 mcg/kg/min
- Time from arrest to screening >300 minutes
- Moribund, Child-Pugh class C cirrhosis, pregnancy/breastfeeding

**Enrollment:**
- 584 patients randomised from 2723 assessed

## Intervention

**Hypothermia Group:**
- Target temperature: 33°C (±0.5°C) for 24 hours
- Cooling methods: intravascular catheter (15.1%), closed-loop surface device (47.9%), basic external device (37.0%)
- Median cooling start: 16 minutes post-randomisation
- Rewarming: 0.25-0.5°C/hour to 36.5-37.5°C, maintained for 24 hours
- Sedation: RASS -5, tapered when >36°C
- 3 patients withdrew consent, excluded from analysis

## Control

**Normothermia Group:**
- Temperature: 36.5-37.5°C for 48 hours
- Cooling methods: intravascular catheter (14.8%), closed-loop surface device (34.0%), basic external device (50.8%)
- >5% had temp >38°C at each 2-hour interval (first 48 hours)
- Sedation: first 12 hours, RASS 0

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Favourable neurological outcome (CPC 1 or 2 at 90 days):**
  - Hypothermia: 10.2%
  - Normothermia: 5.7%
  - Difference: 4.5% (95% CI: 0.1–8.9), p=0.04
  - Fragility index: 1

### Secondary Outcomes
- **CPC score at 90 days:**
  - CPC 1: 5.6% vs. 3.7%
  - CPC 2: 4.6% vs. 2.0%
  - CPC 3: 7.7% vs. 10.4%
  - CPC 4: 0.4% vs. 0%
  - CPC 5: 81.3% vs. 83.2%
- **No significant difference in:**
  - Death by day 90: 81.3% vs. 83.2%, difference -1.9 (95% CI: -8 to 4.4)
  - Mechanical ventilation duration (median): 4.5 vs. 4.0 days
  - ICU stay length (median): 4 vs. 4 days
  - Survival to ICU discharge: 21.8% vs. 20.5%, HR 1.07 (0.75–1.52)
  - Survival to hospital discharge: 19.7% vs. 16.8%, HR 0.81–1.74
  - Cause of death: withdrawal of life support (61.9% vs. 65.2%)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Moderate hypothermia improved favourable neurological outcomes at 90 days in comatose patients post-non-shockable cardiac arrest compared to normothermia.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Blinded outcome assessor
  - Multi-centre
  - Life-sustaining treatment withdrawal decisions followed guidelines
  - Registered on clinicaltrials.gov
- **Weaknesses**
  - Differences in intervention vs. control group treatment duration and sedation
  - Longer time to life-sustaining treatment decisions in intervention group
  - More follow-up from next of kin in intervention group
  - >5% normothermia group had temp >38°C
  - Single primary outcome assessor
  - Conducted in one country, limiting generalisability
  - Low fragility index

## Bottom Line
This RCT reported improved neurological outcomes with moderate hypothermia in non-shockable cardiac arrest coma patients. Further evidence is needed due to treatment differences, low fragility index, and control group fever incidence.
