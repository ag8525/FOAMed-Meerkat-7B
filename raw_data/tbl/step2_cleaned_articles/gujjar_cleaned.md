# Intravenous Levetiracetam vs Phenytoin for Status Epilepticus and Cluster Seizures: A Prospective, Randomized Study

## Clinical Question
In patients with status epilepticus (SE) and cluster seizures (CS), does intravenous levetiracetam compared to intravenous phenytoin improve the rate of seizure control?

## Background
Status epilepticus is a medical emergency with high morbidity and mortality. Current guidelines recommend benzodiazepines followed by phenytoin. Levetiracetam, a newer agent, has shown promise in small studies but lacks extensive evaluation.

## Design
- Prospective, randomized controlled trial
- Non-blinded
- Randomization issues: 28% of patients did not follow randomization due to clinical considerations
- Per-protocol analysis

## Setting
- Single university teaching hospital in Oman
- Dates of data collection not stated

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients >15 years with SE or CS
  - SE: >5 minutes or recurrent seizures without regaining consciousness
  - CS: ≥2 seizures in 24 hours with consciousness return between episodes

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- BP <100, HR <50, heart block, pregnancy, cardiac failure, pre-terminal, requiring immediate neurosurgery, pseudo-seizures, subtle SE

**Enrollment:**
- 52 patients with SE
- 63 patients with CS

## Intervention

**Levetiracetam Group:**
- 30 mg/kg IV over 30 minutes

## Control

**Phenytoin Group:**
- 20 mg/kg IV over 30 minutes

## Common Management
- Initial IV benzodiazepine for ongoing seizures
- Additional doses or alternative AEDs if seizures persisted

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Control of seizures with first AED (cessation, mental state improvement, no recurrence in 24 hours)
  - SE: Levetiracetam 82% vs. Phenytoin 73.3%, p=0.33
  - CS: Levetiracetam 81.6% vs. Phenytoin 80%, p=0.87

### Secondary Outcomes
- Good functional outcome at discharge (modified Rankin scale 0-3)
  - SE: No significant difference
  - CS: Levetiracetam 79% vs. Phenytoin 56%, p=0.049
- Mortality: No significant difference
  - SE: Levetiracetam 9% vs. Phenytoin 10%
  - CS: Levetiracetam 0% vs. Phenytoin 4%
- Adverse events: No significant difference

## Authors’ Conclusions
Levetiracetam is comparable to phenytoin for treating SE or CS.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Consecutive patient enrollment
  - Assessment of adverse events
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre, non-blinded
  - Randomization not followed in 28% of patients
  - Underpowered to detect differences
  - Protocol deviations from UK standard practice

## Bottom Line
This trial found no significant difference in seizure control rates between levetiracetam and phenytoin. However, due to significant methodological concerns and underpowering, the results should not change clinical practice.
