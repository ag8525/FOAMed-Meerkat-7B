# Effect of a Low vs Intermediate Tidal Volume Strategy on Ventilator-Free Days in ICU Patients Without ARDS (PReVENT Trial)

## Clinical Question
In mechanically ventilated patients without acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), does a low tidal volume ventilation strategy (6 mL/kg) compared to an intermediate tidal volume strategy (10 mL/kg) reduce the number of ventilator-free and alive days at day 28?

## Background
- Current guidelines recommend low tidal volumes (<6-8 mL/kg) for ARDS patients.
- Uncertainty exists regarding benefits for non-ARDS patients.
- Non-ARDS patients are a larger group and have been excluded from ARDS trials.
- Previous studies suggest lower tidal volumes may reduce ventilation time and ICU stay but could increase patient-ventilator asynchrony and sedation needs.

## Design
- Phase 3, randomised, clinical trial
- Randomisation: 1:1 ratio via automated web-based system, concealed allocation
- Stratified by centre and intubation location
- Sample size: 952 patients for 80% power to detect a 1-day difference in ventilator-free days

## Setting
- 6 university and non-university ICUs in the Netherlands
- Study period: September 2014 to August 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult ICU patients (>18 years) receiving invasive ventilation, expected not to be extubated within 24 hours

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ARDS (Berlin definition), age <18, pregnancy, ventilation >12 hours before ICU, uncontrollable intracranial pressure, history of pulmonary disease, new pulmonary thromboembolism, previous trial participation

**Enrollment:**
- 961 patients randomised

## Intervention

**Low Tidal Volume Strategy:**
- Initiated at 6 mL/kg predicted body weight (PBW)
- Volume-controlled or pressure support modes
- Adjustments allowed for severe dyspnea or patient-ventilator asynchrony

**Intermediate Tidal Volume Strategy:**
- Initiated at 10 mL/kg PBW
- Adjustments for plateau pressure >25 cm H₂O

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistical difference in ventilator-free days at day 28: 21 days in both groups (IQR, 0-26); mean difference, –0.27 (95% CI –1.74 to 1.19); p = 0.71

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in ICU/hospital stay, mortality rates, or safety outcomes

## Authors’ Conclusions
A low tidal volume strategy was not more effective than an intermediate strategy in terms of ventilator-free days and survival at day 28 in ICU patients without ARDS.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large, multicentre study with randomised design
  - Well-defined protocol and pre-published statistical plan
  - Intermediate tidal volume reflects current practice
- **Weaknesses**
  - Inadequate separation between strategies after day 1
  - Selection bias due to missed eligible patients and consent refusal
  - Lack of blinding may have influenced management
  - Primary outcome's clinical significance is questionable

## Bottom Line
No clear difference was found between lower and higher tidal volume strategies. Clinicians are likely to continue targeting lower tidal volumes (6-8 mL/kg) in non-ARDS patients until further evidence suggests otherwise.
