# High-Flow Nasal Oxygen vs Noninvasive Ventilation in Patients with Acute Respiratory Failure (RENOVATE Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with acute respiratory failure, does high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) compared to noninvasive ventilation (NIV) reduce the rate of endotracheal intubation or death at 7 days?

## Background
- HFNO and NIV are used for acute respiratory failure (ARF) management.
- HFNO offers better patient comfort and ease of use compared to NIV.
- Previous trials (FLORALI, HERNANDEZ et al) showed mixed results regarding intubation rates and patient comfort.

## Design
- Prospective, randomised controlled trial
- Multi-centre
- Bayesian adaptive trial design
- Non-inferiority trial

## Setting
- 33 Brazilian hospitals
- Study period: November 2019 to November 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years with ARF admitted to ICU/ED or hospital wards
- SpO₂ <90% or PaO₂ <60 mmHg on room air with increased respiratory effort or tachypnoea (>25 bpm)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Urgent need for intubation
- Prolonged respiratory pauses
- Cardiorespiratory arrest
- GCS ≤ 12
- Heart rate < 50 bpm with decreased consciousness
- Arterial blood pH < 7.15
- Haemodynamic instability
- Contraindication to NIV
- Do not intubate order
- NIV use in ACPO group prior to randomisation

**Enrollment:**
- 2731 patients assessed, 1800 randomised (883 HFNO, 883 NIV)

## Intervention

**HFNO Group:**
- Continuous delivery via Airvo-2, Fisher & Paykel Healthcare
- Flow rate: 30 L/min for COPD, 45 L/min for others, titrated up to 60 L/min
- FiO₂: Started at 50%, titrated for SpO₂ targets

## Control

**NIV Group:**
- NIV through a face mask
- Pressure settings: IPAP 12-16 cmH₂O, EPAP 4-8 cmH₂O
- Tidal volume: 6-9 ml/kg of ideal body weight
- FiO₂ targets same as HFNO group

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Endotracheal intubation or death within 7 days
  - HFNO: 39% (344/883)
  - NIV: 38.1% (336/883)
  - Non-inferiority reached in nonimmunocompromised, COPD, ACPO, and COVID-19 groups

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in 28-day mortality, 90-day mortality, mechanical ventilation-free days, ICU-free days

### Tertiary Outcomes
- Comfort was superior with HFNO

## Authors’ Conclusions
HFNO met non-inferiority criteria for preventing intubation or death within 7 days in 4 of 5 patient groups with ARF. Further studies are needed for COPD, immunocompromised patients, and ACPO.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large multicentre trial with well-matched baseline characteristics
  - Pragmatic design allowing subgroup analyses
  - Adaptive Bayesian design for dynamic borrowing of information
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded to patients and clinicians
  - High intubation rates in COPD groups
  - Small sample sizes in COPD and immunocompromised groups
  - Single-country study

## Bottom Line
HFNO is non-inferior to NIV for preventing intubation and mortality at 7 days in certain ARF subgroups. HFNO's simpler setup and improved tolerability make it a compelling first-line option in selected scenarios.
