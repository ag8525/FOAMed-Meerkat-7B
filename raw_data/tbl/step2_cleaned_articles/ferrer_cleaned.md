# Empiric Antibiotic Treatment Reduces Mortality in Severe Sepsis and Septic Shock From the First Hour

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe sepsis or septic shock, is the timing of the first administration of antibiotics associated with the in-hospital mortality rate?

## Design
- Retrospective observational cohort study
- Utilised data from the Surviving Sepsis Campaign database
- Generalized Estimating Equation (GEE) used for population-level average

## Setting
- 165 intensive care units across Europe, United States, and South America
- Study period: January 2005 to February 2010

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients admitted to ICU with suspected infection
- Two or more SIRS criteria
- One or more organ dysfunction criteria (severe sepsis or septic shock)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Antibiotics not given (1.6%)
- Antibiotics already given (32%)
- Unclear timing of antibiotic administration (3.0%)

**Enrollment:**
- 28,150 screened; 17,990 analysed after exclusions

## Intervention
- Timing of antibiotics stratified into intervals: 0–1 hr, 1–2 hrs, 2–3 hrs, 3–4 hrs, 4–5 hrs, 5–6 hrs, >6 hrs
- Appropriateness of antibiotics, specific drug, and dosage not analysed

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Hospital mortality was lowest in the cohort given antibiotics within 1 hour
- Mortality increased significantly with each hour of delay beyond this time frame

### Adjusted Outcomes
- Adjusted hospital mortality (Odds Ratio, p-value, Mortality, 95% CI, NNH):
  - <1 hr: OR 1.00, Mortality 24.6% (23.2–26.0)
  - 1–2 hrs: OR 1.07, p = 0.165, Mortality 25.9% (24.5–27.2), NNH 73
  - 2–3 hrs: OR 1.14, p = 0.021, Mortality 27.0% (25.3–28.7), NNH 35
  - 3–4 hrs: OR 1.19, p = 0.009, Mortality 27.9% (25.6–30.1), NNH 25
  - 4–5 hrs: OR 1.24, p = 0.006, Mortality 28.8% (25.9–31.7), NNH 19
  - 5–6 hrs: OR 1.47, p < 0.001, Mortality 32.3% (28.5–36.2), NNH 8
  - >6 hrs: OR 1.52, p < 0.001, Mortality 33.1% (30.9–35.3), NNH 7

## Authors’ Conclusions
This study demonstrates a significant association between delay in antibiotic administration over the first 6 hours after identification of severe sepsis and septic shock and increasing mortality.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large dataset from three continents over 5 years
  - Validated statistical method to control for covariates
  - Inclusion of both severe sepsis and septic shock patients
- **Weaknesses**
  - Authors affiliated with Surviving Sepsis Campaign, potential cognitive biases
  - Quality, accuracy, and completeness of dataset not verified
  - Zero time subjectivity due to potential delay in diagnosis

## Bottom Line
This observational study provides strong evidence that the time between diagnosis of severe sepsis or septic shock and the first dose of antibiotics is critical. Delays beyond 2 hours are associated with higher hospital mortality.
