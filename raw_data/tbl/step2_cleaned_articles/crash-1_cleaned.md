# Effect of Intravenous Corticosteroids on Death within 14 Days in Adults with Clinically Significant Head Injury (CRASH-1 Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adults with head injury, do early corticosteroids compared to placebo reduce death and disability?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial
- Centralised (21%) and non-centralised (79%) randomised concealed allocation
- Blinding of clinicians, patients, and data analysts
- Annual interim analyses with unmasking only if:
  - "Proof beyond reasonable doubt of a difference" was found
  - New published data removed equipoise and uncertainty regarding corticosteroid use
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Powered at 90% to detect a 2% difference in mortality from a baseline of 15%, with a type I error of 0.01, if 20,000 patients were recruited

## Setting
- 239 hospitals across 49 countries
- Europe 38%, Asia 27%, South America 16%, Africa 14%, North America 4%, Oceania 1%
- Study period: April 1999 to May 2004

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults over 16 years with head injuries
- Presented within 8 hours of injury with GCS ≤ 14

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Clear indication or contraindication for steroids

**Enrollment:**
- 10,008 patients randomised

## Intervention
- Administration of methylprednisolone for 48 hours
  - Loading dose: 2 g over 1 hour in 100 ml 0.9% NaCl
  - Maintenance: 0.4 g per hour for 48 hours in 20 ml per hour 0.9% NaCl

## Control
- Identical regime of placebo

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Unmasking of randomisation after 10,008 patients due to a clear difference favouring placebo for mortality at 2 weeks
  - Corticosteroid group: 21% mortality
  - Placebo group: 18% mortality
  - Relative risk: 1.18 (95% CI: 1.09–1.27; p = 0.0001)
  - Absolute risk increase: 3.15% (95% CI: 1.60%–4.70%; NNH = 32)

### Secondary Outcomes
- 6-month follow-up favoured placebo for mortality and severe disability
  - 6-month mortality: 
    - Corticosteroids: 25.7%
    - Placebo: 22.3%
    - Relative risk: 1.15 (95% CI: 1.07–1.24; p = 0.0001)
    - Absolute risk increase: 3.40% (95% CI: 1.70%–5.10%; NNH = 29)
  - 6-month mortality or severe disability:
    - Corticosteroids: 38.1%
    - Placebo: 36.3%
    - Relative risk: 1.05 (95% CI: 0.99–1.10; p = 0.079)
    - Absolute risk increase: 1.80% (95% CI: -0.12%–3.72%; NNH = 55)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Corticosteroids should not be used routinely to treat head injury, regardless of severity.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Excellent external validity due to worldwide recruitment and minimal exclusion criteria
  - Robust statistical thresholds in planning phase (α < 0.01, β = 90%)
  - Primary outcome data available for over 99% of patients
  - Rapid publication of important outcomes with secondary outcomes published later
- **Weaknesses**
  - Halting at interim stage may produce results due to an extreme play of chance
  - Hyperglycaemia from corticosteroid administration may have unblinded clinicians or led to unbalanced management
  - Cause of death not investigated, limiting further theories and explanations for results

## Bottom Line
Corticosteroids should not be given to patients with head injuries unless other specific indications exist that outweigh the increased risk of death demonstrated by this trial.
