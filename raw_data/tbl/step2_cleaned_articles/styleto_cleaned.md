# Effect of the Use of an Endotracheal Tube and Stylet versus an Endotracheal Tube Alone on First-Attempt Intubation Success: A Multicentre, Randomised Clinical Trial

## Clinical Question
In critically ill adults, does the use of an intubating stylet compared to a tracheal tube alone increase first-attempt successful intubation?

## Background
- Acute respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation is a leading cause of ICU admission.
- Intubation in the ICU has a higher complication rate compared to the operating theatre.
- Various devices and strategies have been developed to improve intubation success rates.
- A preformed stylet has been proposed to increase first-attempt success rates.

## Design
- Unblinded, parallel-group, pragmatic randomised controlled trial
- Central randomisation in a 1:1 ratio with variable block sizes
- Treatment assignments concealed from patients, research staff, and the statistician

## Setting
- 32 ICUs in France
- Study period: October 2019 to March 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults over 18 years old
- Covered by public health insurance
- Required emergency mechanical ventilation via tracheal tube in the ICU

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Intubation following cardiac arrest
- Previous intubation in the ICU during the same admission with prior inclusion in the study
- Pregnant or breastfeeding women

**Enrollment:**
- 1626 screened, 1040 randomised
- 501 in the stylet group, 498 in the control group included in final analysis

## Intervention

**Stylet Group:**
- First intubation attempt with a tracheal tube and a stylet bent at a 25 to 35-degree angle at the distal tip

## Control

**Tracheal Tube Alone Group:**
- First intubation attempt with a tracheal tube alone

## Management Common to Both Groups
- All other aspects of patient care during and after intubation were at the discretion of attending physicians
- Montpellier intubation protocol was recommended

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Higher incidence of successful first-attempt intubation in the stylet group compared to control (78.2% vs 71.5%, p=0.01)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in complication rates (38.7% vs 40.2%, p=0.64)
- No significant difference in serious adverse events (4.0% vs 3.6%, p=0.76)
- No significant difference in safety outcomes, traumatic injuries, clinical outcomes including length of stay, and mortality

## Authors’ Conclusions
In this multicentre, randomised trial involving critically ill adults undergoing tracheal intubation, the use of a stylet for tracheal intubation was safe and resulted in significantly higher first-attempt intubation success than the use of a tracheal tube alone.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Prepublished trial protocol
  - Well-conducted trial addressing a relevant question in airway management
  - Inclusion criteria reflective of ICU practice, likely generalisable
  - Good balance of patients from all 32 sites involved in randomisation
  - Results consistent with previous studies regarding first-pass success in ICU intubations
- **Weaknesses**
  - Did not achieve recruitment target
  - No blinding of the intervention
  - First pass success primarily defined by capnography trace, yet only ~85% used capnography
  - Some adverse events were operator-reported, potentially leading to underreporting
  - Management of other aspects of care was at the discretion of treating teams
  - High proportion of intubations performed by anaesthetists, not reflective of all ICU practices

## Bottom Line
Successful intubation is the result of complex interactions between operator and devices. The use of a stylet increases first-attempt success rates, but the overall success rate remains low. Operator experience did not affect the primary outcome. Current practice may include using a bougie or stylet depending on the situation.
