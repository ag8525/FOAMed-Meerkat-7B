# Conservative versus Interventional Treatment for Spontaneous Pneumothorax

## Clinical Question
In patients with primary moderate-large spontaneous pneumothorax, does conservative management compared to interventional treatment result in acceptable rates of lung re-expansion at 8 weeks?

## Background
- Annual hospitalization rate for spontaneous pneumothorax: ~140 per million (Halifax, JAMA 2018)
- Interventional drainage is common but carries risks (pain, infection, organ injury)
- Mean hospital stay for primary spontaneous pneumothorax: 4 days (Thelle, ERS 2017)
- Lack of robust trial data on conservative management; historical support from a 1966 cohort study

## Design
- Prospective, randomised control, non-inferiority trial
- Protocol and statistical analysis plan published prior
- Randomisation: 1:1 conservative to intervention groups
- Open-label due to nature of intervention
- Target recruitment: 342 patients (accounting for 20% dropout, 95% power to detect 9% non-inferiority margin)

## Setting
- 39 hospitals across Australasia (metropolitan and rural)
- Recruitment period: July 2011 to March 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 14-50 years with unilateral primary spontaneous pneumothorax
- Moderate to large pneumothorax (Collins method: intrapleural distances >6 cm, volume loss ≥32%)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous spontaneous pneumothorax on the same side
- Secondary pneumothorax, bilateral pneumothorax, co-existing haemothorax
- Instability suggesting tension, unlikely follow-up, pregnancy

**Enrollment:**
- 316 patients randomised (154 intervention, 162 conservative)
- 25 (15.4%) in conservative group required intervention; 10 (6.5%) in intervention group declined treatment

## Intervention

**Interventional Group:**
- Small bore (<12Fr) Seldinger drain with underwater seal (no suction)
- Repeat CXR after 1 hour; if re-expanded and not bubbling, clamped with a three-way valve
- Further CXR after 4 hours; if stable, drain removed and patient discharged
- If pneumothorax recurred, valve reopened and patient admitted

## Control

**Conservative Management Group:**
- Observation for minimum 4 hours, repeat CXR
- Discharge if walking comfortably, no supplemental oxygen needed
- Interventions allowed for chest pain, dyspnoea, physiological instability, or patient preference

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Complete radiological resolution at 8 weeks:
  - Intervention: 129/131 (98.5%)
  - Conservative: 118/125 (94.4%)
  - Risk difference: -4.1% (95% CI: -8.5 to 0.5), p = 0.02
  - Within 9% non-inferiority margin

### Secondary Outcomes
- Time to symptom resolution similar between groups
- Conservative management resulted in fewer hospital days, less surgery, fewer adverse events

## Authors’ Conclusions
Conservative management is non-inferior to interventional management for radiographic resolution at 8 weeks, challenging the need for immediate drainage in all large primary spontaneous pneumothorax cases.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses a clinically relevant question with a lack of robust data
  - Conducted across diverse settings (rural and urban)
  - Broad applicability to Western populations
- **Weaknesses**
  - Open-label design
  - Potential bias in pneumothorax resolution reporting
  - Original analysis plan did not specify handling of missing data, affecting result robustness

## Bottom Line
Conservative management is a viable option for moderate-large primary spontaneous pneumothorax, reducing hospital days and adverse events without chest tube insertion. Discuss treatment options with patients, considering non-intervention as reasonable.
