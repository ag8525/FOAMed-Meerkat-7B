# Balanced Crystalloids versus Saline in Non-Critically Ill Adults (SALT-ED Study)

## Clinical Question
In non-critically ill adult patients admitted to the hospital from the Emergency Department, does the administration of a balanced salt solution compared with normal saline reduce hospital-free days to day 28?

## Background
- Over one million patients receive intravenous (IV) crystalloid solutions daily in hospitals.
- Previous studies have shown synthetic colloids are harmful, highlighting the need to establish the safety of IV crystalloids.
- 0.9% sodium chloride can cause hyperchloraemic acidosis and potentially reduce renal blood flow, leading to acute kidney injury.
- Balanced salt solutions may mitigate some risks associated with 0.9% saline.

## Design
- Single-centre, randomised, open-label, multiple crossover study
- Pragmatic design with intention-to-treat analysis
- Consecutive patient enrollment
- Multivariable proportional odds model for primary outcome
- Multivariable logistic regression for secondary outcomes
- Crystalloid type assigned by month, alternating between 0.9% saline and balanced salt solution

## Setting
- Single-centre study at Vanderbilt University Hospital, USA
- Study period: 1 January 2016 to 30 April 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients over 18 years old
- Received at least 500 mL of IV isotonic crystalloid in the ED
- Admitted to a non-ICU setting

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Received less than 500 mL IV fluid
- Admitted to ICU (recruited to the SMART study)

**Enrollment:**
- 19,949 patients received IV fluids; 3,689 excluded for receiving less than 500 mL
- 2,913 admitted to ICU
- Final sample size: 13,347 patients

## Intervention

**Balanced Crystalloid Group:**
- Predominantly lactated Ringer’s (95.3%), remainder Plasmalyte (4.7%)
- 83.8% received only the assigned crystalloid in the ED
- Median total crystalloid received: 1089 mL (IQR 1000–2000 mL)

## Control

**Saline Group:**
- 0.9% saline for IV fluid therapy
- 92.8% received only the assigned crystalloid in the ED
- Mean total crystalloid received: 1597 mL

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in hospital-free days between groups (median 25 days each)
- Adjusted odds ratio with balanced crystalloids: 0.98 (95% CI: 0.92–1.04); p = 0.41

### Secondary Outcomes
- Lower incidence of Major Adverse Kidney Events within 30 days (MAKE 30) in balanced-crystalloids group (4.7% vs. 5.6%)
  - Adjusted odds ratio: 0.82 (95% CI: 0.70–0.95); p = 0.01
  - Number needed to treat: 111
- No significant difference in in-hospital death (1.4% vs. 1.6%; p = 0.36)
- Sub-group analysis: lower MAKE 30 in balanced crystalloid group for patients with acute kidney injury

## Authors’ Conclusions
The number of hospital-free days did not differ between patients assigned to balanced crystalloids and those assigned to saline.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses a common intervention with global relevance
  - Pragmatic design with minimal exclusions
  - High adherence to allocated intervention
  - Patient-centred primary outcome
  - Clear differences in electrolyte concentrations between groups
  - Robust data analysis supports hypothesis generation for future studies
- **Weaknesses**
  - Secondary endpoint differences driven by surrogate outcome (serum creatinine)
  - Retrospective outcome data from medical records
  - Single-centre limits external validity
  - Unblinded fluid administration could introduce bias
  - No evaluation of post-admission fluid administration
  - Fewer patients in balanced salt solution arm received allocated fluid

## Bottom Line
Results need replication in a multi-centre RCT before concluding on the effects of 0.9% saline. Until then, balanced salt solutions or saline can be used based on clinical context.
