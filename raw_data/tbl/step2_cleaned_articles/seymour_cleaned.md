# Time to Treatment and Mortality During Mandated Emergency Care for Sepsis

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe sepsis or septic shock, does a faster time to completion of a 3-hour bundle of care, compared with a slower time, affect in-hospital mortality?

## Background
Sepsis is a life-threatening condition with high mortality. Sepsis "bundles" combining high-impact interventions have been popularized since the 2000s, notably the Early Goal Directed Therapy (EGDT) approach, part of the Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC). These bundles typically include early antibiotics, source control, and rapid hemodynamic resuscitation. New York City mandated their sepsis bundle in 2013.

## Design
- Retrospective observational study
- Utilized the New York Department of Health Database
- Risk adjustment model for in-hospital mortality using confounding variables:
  - Age, ethnicity, source of funding, co-morbidities, site of infection, admission source
  - Measures of illness severity (shock, lactate, platelet count, mechanical ventilation)
- 90% of cohort used to develop model; accuracy tested on remaining 10% (AUROC 0.77)
- Additional models created excluding certain patient groups

## Setting
- 185 hospitals in New York State Department of Health database
- Study period: April 2014 to June 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age >17 years
- Severe sepsis or septic shock as defined by 2001 International Sepsis Definitions Conference (Sepsis-2)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Completion of 3-hour bundle >12 hours after initiating protocol
- Bundle contraindicated
- Advanced directives limiting treatment
- Patient declined intervention
- Patient already in another clinical trial
- 36 hospitals excluded for seeing <50 cases of sepsis

**Enrollment:**
- 111,816 patients screened, 49,337 eligible

## Intervention

**3-hour Bundle:**
- Blood cultures before antibiotics
- Measurement of lactate
- Administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics

**6-hour Bundle:**
- 30 ml/kg fluid bolus in patients with hypotension or lactate >4 meq/L
- Vasopressors for refractory hypotension
- Re-measurement of lactate within 6 hours of starting protocol

## Control
- Did not have 3-hour bundle completed within the time set

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- In-hospital mortality
  - Odds ratio of dying increased by 1.04 per hour delay in completing bundle (95% CI: 1.03–1.06; p < 0.001)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Antibiotics administered between 3-12 hours: Odds ratio of dying 1.14 compared to within 3 hours (95% CI: 1.06–1.22; p < 0.001)
- Delaying fluid bolus: Odds ratio per hour of delay 1.01 (95% CI: 0.99–1.02; p = 0.21)
- Delaying blood cultures: Odds ratio per hour of delay 1.04 (95% CI: 1.02–1.06; p < 0.001)

## Authors’ Conclusions
More rapid completion of a 3-hour bundle of sepsis care and rapid administration of antibiotics, but not rapid completion of an initial bolus of intravenous fluids, were associated with lower risk-adjusted in-hospital mortality.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Important clinical question
  - Large data set
  - Multiple centers
  - Real-world "pragmatic" data
  - Multiple statistical analyses performed to minimize confounders
- **Weaknesses**
  - Observational data can only describe associations
  - Large confounders remain, particularly the "Hawthorne effect"—units with better compliance may function better overall
  - Early blood cultures associated with reduced mortality, though not a treatment

## Bottom Line
This study demonstrates an association between a delay in administering a 3-hour bundle of care and increased mortality in a large cohort of patients in New York. This does not change my current practice of delivering high-impact interventions in sepsis as early as possible.
