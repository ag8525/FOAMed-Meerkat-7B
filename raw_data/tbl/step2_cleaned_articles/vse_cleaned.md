# Vasopressin, Steroids, and Epinephrine and Neurologically Favourable Survival After In-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Randomized Clinical Trial (VSE Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients experiencing in-hospital cardiac arrest, does the combination of vasopressin and epinephrine during CPR, along with steroid supplementation during and after CPR, improve survival to discharge with a good neurological outcome compared to epinephrine alone?

## Design
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial
- Blinding of staff assessing neurological outcomes
- Block randomization stratified by center
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 3 tertiary centers in Greece
- Study period: 1 September 2008 to 1 October 2010

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- In-hospital cardiac arrest requiring vasopressors

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age < 18 years
- Terminal illness/DNAR orders
- Cardiac arrest due to exsanguination
- Pre-hospital cardiac arrest
- Treatment with IV steroids prior to arrest

**Enrollment:**
- 300 patients randomized out of 364 assessed for eligibility
- 32 patients excluded post-randomization due to ROSC prior to vasopressor need

## Intervention
- Combination of:
  - Vasopressin 20 IU per CPR cycle for 5 cycles
  - Adrenaline 1 mg per CPR cycle
  - Methylprednisolone 40 mg during the first CPR cycle
  - Surviving patients received hydrocortisone 300 mg/day for <7 days with gradual taper if post-resuscitation shock occurred

## Control
- Adrenaline 1 mg per CPR cycle + placebos
- Average CPR cycle duration ~3 minutes in both groups

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital discharge with favourable neurological outcome (Glasgow-Pittsburgh Cerebral Performance Category 1/2):**
  - Significant improvement in the intervention group: 13.9% vs. 5.1%
  - Odds ratio (OR) 3.28 (95% CI: 1.17–9.2), p = 0.02, NNT = 12

### Secondary Outcomes
- **ROSC for >20 minutes:**
  - Significant improvement in the intervention group: 83.9% vs. 65.9%
  - OR 2.98 (95% CI: 1.39–6.4), p = 0.005, NNT = 6
- Patients with post-resuscitation shock showed significant improvement in survival to discharge with a good neurological outcome in the intervention group: 21.1% vs. 8.2%, OR 3.74 (95% CI: 1.2–11.62), p = 0.02
- Higher insulin treatment within the first 10 days in the intervention group: 50.4% vs. 36%, p < 0.001
- No significant difference in infection or gastrointestinal bleeding rates between groups

## Authors’ Conclusions
Patients in cardiac arrest requiring vasopressors have improved survival with a good neurological outcome when treated with combined vasopressin, epinephrine, and steroids compared to epinephrine alone.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
  - Blinding of neurological outcome assessors
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single geographical area
  - Small number of patients with good neurological outcomes (25 total), limiting definitive conclusions
  - Therapeutic hypothermia used in only 24.6% of patients
  - Use of atropine and bicarbonate not consistent with current practice
  - Differences in baseline characteristics between groups
  - Large number of arrests in critical care settings, limiting generalizability
  - Bundle approach prevents determination of which component(s) contributed to outcomes

## Bottom Line
This study shows a significant improvement in survival to discharge with a good neurological outcome when using a combination of steroids, vasopressin, and epinephrine compared to epinephrine alone. However, further validation in different settings is needed before routine practice changes.
