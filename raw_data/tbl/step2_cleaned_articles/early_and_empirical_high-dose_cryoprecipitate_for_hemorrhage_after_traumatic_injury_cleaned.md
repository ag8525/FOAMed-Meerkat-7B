# Early and Empirical High-Dose Cryoprecipitate for Hemorrhage After Traumatic Injury (CRYOSTAT-2)

## Clinical Question
In trauma patients with critical bleeding requiring activation of a major haemorrhage protocol, does the empiric administration of 3 pools of cryoprecipitate (6g fibrinogen) within 90 minutes of randomisation improve survival compared to standard care?

## Background
- Trauma is a leading cause of death globally, with haemorrhage being a primary mechanism.
- Fibrinogen stabilises clot formation, but levels drop rapidly post-trauma.
- Previous studies, like the PAMPer trial, showed benefits of early plasma administration.
- The CRYOSTAT pilot indicated feasibility and potential benefits of early cryoprecipitate administration.

## Design
- Multi-centre, phase 3, interventional RCT
- Open-label, parallel-groups
- Allocation concealment with opaque sealed envelopes
- Computer-generated randomisation sequence
- Waiver of consent allowed for randomisation
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 26 major trauma centres in the UK and USA
- Study period: August 2017 to November 2021

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Trauma with active haemorrhage requiring Major Haemorrhage Protocol (MHP) activation
- MHP activation included SBP < 90 mmHg
- Received at least 1 unit of any blood component

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Transferred from another hospital
- Injuries deemed incompatible with life
- >3 hours since injury

**Enrollment:**
- 1604 eligible patients: 799 in cryoprecipitate group, 805 in standard care group
- 1531 patients included in intention-to-treat analysis

## Intervention
- 3 pools of cryoprecipitate (6g fibrinogen) administered early (ideally within 90 minutes)
- 85% of patients in the intervention group received cryoprecipitate
- Median time to administration: 68 minutes

## Control
- Standard MHP care: RBC:FFP:Platelets in 1:1:1 ratio, 2 pools cryoprecipitate with subsequent packs
- 32% of control group received cryoprecipitate
- Median time to administration: 120 minutes

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in all-cause mortality at 28 days
  - Cryoprecipitate: 25.3% (192/760)
  - Standard Care: 26.1% (201/771)
  - OR 0.96 [95% CI 0.75-1.23], p=0.74

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in 6-hour, 24-hour, or 6- or 12-month mortality
- No difference in transfusion requirements, ventilator days, ICU LOS, hospital LOS, or GOS score at day 28
- Median time to death: 191 minutes (cryoprecipitate) vs 86 minutes (control)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early empiric high-dose cryoprecipitate in trauma patients with haemorrhage did not reduce 28-day mortality.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Strong internal validity with allocation concealment and intention-to-treat analysis
  - Well-balanced baseline characteristics
  - Clinically meaningful outcomes
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded design
  - Some crossover between groups
  - Intervention not tailored to individual fibrinogen levels
  - Potential pro-inflammatory effects in patients without hypofibrinogenemia
  - Inadequate power to interpret subgroup findings

## Bottom Line
The trial does not support empiric cryoprecipitate therapy in trauma patients with major haemorrhage. Further exploration of the timing of administration is warranted.
