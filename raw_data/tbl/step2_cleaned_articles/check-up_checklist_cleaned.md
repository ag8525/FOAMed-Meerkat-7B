# A Multicenter Randomized Trial of a Checklist for Endotracheal Intubation of Critically Ill Adults

## Clinical Question
In critically ill patients undergoing intubation, does the use of a pre-intubation checklist compared to no checklist prevent oxygen desaturation or systolic hypotension?

## Background
Complications occur in up to one-third of critically ill patients undergoing airway intubation, with hypoxaemia and hypotension being the most common events. Operator inexperience and inadequate preparation are associated with increased incidence of complications. A checklist is a cost-effective intervention that can improve non-technical “human” factors during times of stress and high-intensity mental activity.

## Design
- 2 x 2 factorial design  
- Randomised, multicentre pragmatic trial  
- Patients randomised in a 1:1 ratio using a computer and permuted blocks  
- Data collected by an independent observer, with quality/accuracy assurance on 11% of cases  
- 248 patients required to power the study at 80% to detect a 5% difference in lowest recorded saturation  
- Initial power calculations required only 170 patients; revised due to larger than anticipated SD  
- Group assignments remained concealed  

## Setting
- Five tertiary ICUs in the United States  
- Study period: July 2015 to July 2016  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients over 18 undergoing endotracheal intubation by critical care fellows, pulmonary fellows, or anaesthetic residents in the ICU  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Planned awake intubation  
- Urgent intubation precluding randomisation  
- Specific checklist required based on clinical need  

**Enrollment:**
- 318 patients eligible, 51 excluded, 267 randomised, 262 analysed  
- 5 excluded post-randomisation  

## Intervention

**Checklist Group:**
- Pre-intubation checklist read aloud by a team member with verbal acknowledgment by the intubator  
- Checklist contained 10 items focusing on setup and non-technical skills  
- Checklist performed before procedural medications  
- Operators could abort the checklist if clinically necessary  

## Control
- Usual care without a structured, verbalised checklist  
- All aspects of intubation at the discretion of the clinical team  
- Measurement of primary outcomes from procedural medication administration until two minutes post-intubation  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcomes
- **Lowest median saturation:**  
  - Checklist: 92% [IQR 79-98]  
  - Usual Care: 93% [IQR 84-100]  
  - p = 0.34  

- **Lowest SBP:**  
  - Checklist: 112 mmHg [IQR 94-133]  
  - Usual Care: 108 mmHg [IQR 90-132]  
  - p = 0.61  

### Secondary Outcomes
- No decrease in number of attempts, time to intubation, or improvement in Cormack-Lehane view  
- No significant difference in life-threatening complications such as cardiac arrest or severe hypotension  

### Tertiary Outcomes
- No significant difference in ventilator-free days, ICU days, or in-hospital mortality  

## Authors’ Conclusions
The use of a verbally performed pre-intubation checklist was not associated with a reduction in complications or an improvement in clinical outcomes.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Prospective, multicentre, randomised study with large numbers  
  - Focused on trainees likely to benefit from a checklist  
  - Pragmatic and transparent checklist design  
  - Independent data collection to limit bias  

- **Weaknesses**
  - Factorial design complexity may have reduced power  
  - 19% of checklist arm did not complete the checklist  
  - Study was unblinded, potentially influencing clinician behaviour  
  - Checklist items often performed without the checklist, reducing detectable differences  

## Bottom Line
This study suggests that the use of a pre-intubation checklist does not reduce peri-procedural complications and may delay intubation. The use of checklists should not be discouraged based on this trial alone, as there may be other unmeasured benefits.
