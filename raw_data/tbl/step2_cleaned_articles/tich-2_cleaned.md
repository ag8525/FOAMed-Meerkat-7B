# Tranexamic Acid for Hyperacute Primary IntraCerebral Haemorrhage (TICH-2)

## Clinical Question
In adults with acute, spontaneous intra-cerebral haemorrhage (ICH), does the administration of tranexamic acid (TXA) compared to placebo improve functional status at 90 days?

## Background
Spontaneous ICH is associated with high mortality and morbidity, often due to haematoma expansion, which occurs frequently within the first three hours but can occur later. Previous interventions like early surgery and intensive blood pressure lowering have not reduced death or severe disability. Recombinant Factor VII has been studied but showed no significant reduction in death or disability. TXA had not been widely studied in this context before TICH-2.

## Design
- Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase III trial
- Registered in the UK by the NIHR
- Randomised 1:1 centrally in real-time
- Stratified by country
- Minimisation for key prognostic factors
- Total sample size: 2000 participants
- Significance: 0.05, power: 90% to detect an ordinal OR of 0.79 by shift analysis of modified Rankin Scale (mRS)
- Pre-specified secondary outcomes, subgroups, and safety outcomes

## Setting
- 124 hospital sites in 12 countries
- Study period: March 2013 to September 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with ICH admitted within 8 hours of stroke onset

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ICH secondary to anticoagulation, trauma, thrombolysis, or known structural abnormality
- Pre-morbid mRS > 4
- Life expectancy < 3 months
- GCS < 5
- Contraindication to TXA
- Females of child-bearing potential, pregnant, or breastfeeding
- Geographical factors preventing follow-up at 90 days

**Enrollment:**
- 2325 randomised: 1161 assigned TXA, 1164 assigned placebo
- 9 excluded from analysis in both arms

## Intervention

**TXA Group:**
- 1g IV TXA in 100ml 0.9% NaCl over 10 minutes
- Followed by 1g in 250ml 0.9% NaCl over 8 hours

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Normal saline used in place of TXA

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in functional status at day 90
  - Ordinal OR: 0.88 (95% CI: 0.76 to 1.03; p = 0.11)

### Pre-specified Subgroups
- TXA beneficial if systolic BP < 170 mmHg
  - OR: 0.79 (95% CI: 0.59 to 0.90; p = 0.0188)
- TXA beneficial if baseline haematoma volume 30–60 ml
  - OR: 0.66 (95% CI: 0.44 to 0.98)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in other pre-specified secondary outcomes

### Safety Outcomes
- Fewer serious events in TXA group at day 2 and day 90
  - VTE events: TXA 3.4% vs Placebo 3.2% (P = 0.98)
  - Seizures: TXA 6.6% vs Placebo 7.3% (P = 0.44)

## Authors’ Conclusions
TXA does not affect functional status at day 90, although potential benefits were noted in reducing haematoma expansion, early death, and serious adverse events.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre, randomised, controlled study
  - Pragmatic design addressing an important clinical question
  - Rigorous methodology with good internal validity
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Clinically relevant primary outcome
- **Weaknesses**
  - Subjective primary outcome (mRS)
  - Short follow-up period (90 days)
  - Broad inclusion criteria may bias results
  - Small sample size compared to other TXA trials

## Bottom Line
The trial did not show a statistically significant benefit of TXA in improving functional status at 90 days. However, given the safety profile and potential benefits in certain subgroups, further research is warranted. Early administration of TXA in ICH patients may be reasonable.
