# Remdesivir for the Treatment of Covid-19: Preliminary Report of the Adaptive COVID-19 Treatment Trial (ACTT-1)

## Clinical Question
In hospitalised adult patients with evidence of lower respiratory tract infection due to Covid-19, is remdesivir superior to placebo in shortening the time to recovery?

## Background
- SARS-CoV-2 identified in December 2019, responsible for a global pandemic.
- No effective interventions other than supportive care for hospitalised Covid-19 patients.
- Remdesivir, an antiviral, inhibits viral RNA polymerase, reducing viral RNA production.
- Shown activity against SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV in animal models.
- Previous RCT found no difference in time to clinical improvement but was stopped early.

## Design
- Double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled trial using an adaptive platform.
- Patients randomised 1:1 to receive remdesivir or placebo for up to 10 days.
- Designed for 85% power to detect a recovery rate ratio of 1.35.
- Randomisation stratified by study site and disease severity.
- Primary outcome: time to recovery within 28 days, defined by an eight-category ordinal scale.
- Primary outcome changed from clinical status difference to time to recovery after 72 patients enrolled.
- DSMB monitored interim data for early closure or study arm changes.
- Results made public on April 27, 2020, by NIAID recommendation.

## Setting
- 60 trial sites and 13 sub-sites across USA, Denmark, UK, Greece, Germany, Korea, Mexico, Spain, Japan, and Singapore.
- Data collected from February 21 to April 19, 2020.

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years hospitalised with symptoms suggestive of COVID-19.
- Laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection.
- Radiographic infiltrates, SpO₂ ≤94% on room air, or requiring supplemental oxygen/mechanical ventilation/ECMO.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ALT/AST >5 times normal, impaired renal function, allergy to study product, pregnancy/breastfeeding, anticipated discharge/transfer within 72 hours.

**Enrollment:**
- 1059 patients (538 intervention, 521 control) included in final analysis.
- 79.8% enrolled at North American sites.

## Intervention

**Remdesivir Group:**
- 200 mg IV loading dose on day 1, then 100 mg daily for up to 10 days or until discharge/death.

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Matched placebo administered on the same schedule.

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Supportive care per trial site hospital standard.

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Remdesivir group had a significantly shorter time to recovery: median 11 days vs. 15 days; rate ratio 1.32; 95% CI 1.12–1.55; p<0.001.
- Recovery most pronounced in patients requiring supplemental oxygen (ordinal score 5).

### Secondary Outcomes
- Odds of improvement in ordinal scale score at day 15 higher in remdesivir group: OR 1.50; 95% CI 1.18–1.91; p=0.001.
- No significant difference in mortality: 32 deaths in remdesivir vs. 54 in placebo; HR for death 0.70; 95% CI 0.47–1.04.

## Authors’ Conclusions
Preliminary findings support remdesivir use in hospitalised Covid-19 patients requiring supplemental oxygen. High mortality despite remdesivir indicates antiviral alone may not suffice.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Matched placebo used in most patients.
  - Intention-to-treat analysis.
  - Daily patient review and home visits post-discharge.
  - Randomisation stratified by site and severity.
  - Multinational participation enhances generalisability.
  - Safety evaluation conducted.
- **Weaknesses**
  - Main effect in patients requiring oxygen therapy; no protocolised oxygen therapy.
  - Higher mechanical ventilation/ECMO in control at randomisation.
  - Primary outcome change after study start.
  - DSMB review before full follow-up.
  - No matched placebo at European sites; potential blinding compromise.
  - Time to recovery differs from previous studies.
  - Exclusion of renal failure without GFR definition.
  - No long-term outcome data.
  - Missing baseline ordinal scores in 42 patients.

## Bottom Line
Remdesivir may shorten recovery time in hospitalised Covid-19 patients, especially those needing oxygen. Clinical relevance uncertain due to study limitations and lack of long-term data.
