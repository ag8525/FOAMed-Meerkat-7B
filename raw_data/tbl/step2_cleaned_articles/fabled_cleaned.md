# Blood Culture Results Before and After Antimicrobial Administration in Patients with Severe Manifestations of Sepsis (FABLED Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe sepsis, what is the sensitivity of blood cultures taken after the initiation of antimicrobial therapy?

## Background
Early empiric antibiotic therapy is crucial in managing septic shock, with delays in administration linked to increased mortality. Inappropriate antibiotic selection also raises mortality risk. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends obtaining blood cultures before starting antimicrobial therapy, but concerns exist about potential delays in treatment.

## Design
- Patient-level, single-group diagnostic cohort study
- Prospective and observational
- Based on previous data, 335 patients were needed, assuming 35% bacteremia on initial cultures and a 10% maximum difference in sensitivity between pre- and post-antimicrobial blood cultures would be clinically acceptable. Revised to 328 patients for 90% power.

## Setting
- Seven urban emergency departments across Canada and the USA
- Study period: November 2013 to September 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients ≥18 years presenting to the ED with severe sepsis
- Evidence of severe sepsis: two of four SIRS criteria, suspected/confirmed infection, and either initial serum lactate ≥4 mmol/L or systolic BP <90 mmHg

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Severe coagulopathy, platelet count <20,000 x 10⁹ cells/L, or INR >6.0

**Patient Characteristics:**
- Mean age: 66 years
- Male: 63%
- Lactate >4 mmol/L: 62%
- BP <90 mmHg: 57%
- Source of infection: Respiratory (33%), Genitourinary (18%)

**Enrollment:**
- 330 participants

## Intervention

**Gold Standard Test:**
- Blood cultures taken before antimicrobial administration (2 sets, each with 1 aerobic and 1 anaerobic bottle, from separate venepunctures)

**Test of Interest:**
- Blood cultures taken after antimicrobial administration (protocol: 30-120 minutes post-treatment initiation, amended to include up to 240 minutes)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Sensitivity of blood cultures obtained 30-120 minutes post-antimicrobial therapy:
  - Per protocol: 56.3% (95% CI: 44.7%–67.3%)
  - Entire study population: 52.9% (95% CI: 42.8%–62.9%)

- Rate of positive blood cultures pre- vs. post-antimicrobial:
  - Per protocol: 30.3% vs. 19.7%, Absolute difference: 10.6% (95% CI: 3.3%–17.9%)
  - Entire study population: 31.4% vs. 19.4%, Absolute difference: 12% (95% CI: 5.4%–18.6%)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Post-antimicrobial culture positivity: 51.7% if organism susceptible, 66.7% if not
- Pre-therapy cultures became positive a median of 4 hours sooner than post-therapy

## Authors’ Conclusions
Drawing blood cultures after antimicrobial therapy in severe sepsis significantly reduces culture sensitivity.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Best practice in pre-antimicrobial blood culture collection (2 sets from separate sites)
  - Supports recommendation to sample pre-therapy
  - Acceptable contamination rate (4.9%)
- **Weaknesses**
  - No comparator control group; time to antibiotic therapy not measured
  - Limited generalisability due to selective recruitment
  - Variability in repeat culture methods across sites

## Bottom Line
Positive blood cultures are more likely if drawn before antibiotic therapy, aiding in tailoring and de-escalating treatment. Efforts should be made to obtain cultures pre-antibiotics without delaying treatment.
