# Duration of Antibiotic Treatment in Community-Acquired Pneumonia: A Multicentre Randomised Clinical Trial

## Clinical Question
In hospitalised patients with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), is five days of antibiotic therapy non-inferior to standard practice regarding clinical improvement?

## Design
- Multicentre, noninferiority randomised controlled trial
- Patients randomised at day 5
- 80% power calculation

## Setting
- Four teaching hospitals in Spain
- Study period: 1 January to 31 August 2012

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Hospitalised patients over 18 years with a diagnosis of CAP (consolidation on CXR with at least one symptom like cough, fever, dyspnoea, or chest pain)
- Temperature below 37.8°C for 48 hours and no more than one CAP sign of clinical instability (e.g., systolic BP < 90 mmHg, pulse > 100, RR > 24 BPM, O₂ saturations < 90%, or PaO₂ < 60 mmHg in room air)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- HIV infection, chronic immunosuppression, nursing home residents
- Recent hospital discharge within 14 days
- Antibiotic therapy in the previous 30 days
- Pathogens requiring longer antibiotic courses
- Chest drain insertion, extra-pulmonary infection complications
- ICU admission or death before randomisation

**Enrollment:**
- 539 patients screened; 312 randomised (150 control, 162 intervention)

## Intervention
- Antibiotic therapy discontinued at 5 days

## Control
- Antibiotic duration determined by usual clinician practice

## Common Management
- Antibiotic type selected according to local guidelines

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistical difference in primary outcomes
- Clinical success rate at day 10:
  - Control: 48.6% (71/150) vs. Intervention: 56.3% (90/162), p = 0.18 (intention-to-treat)
  - Control: 50.4% (67/137) vs. Intervention: 59.7% (86/146), p = 0.12 (per-protocol)
- Clinical success rate at day 30:
  - Control: 88.6% (132/150) vs. Intervention: 91.9% (147/162), p = 0.33 (intention-to-treat)
  - Control: 92.7% (126/137) vs. Intervention: 94.4% (136/146), p = 0.54 (per-protocol)
- CAP symptom questionnaire scores at day 10:
  - Control: 18.6 (SD 9.0) vs. Intervention: 17.9 (SD 7.6), p = 0.69 (intention-to-treat)
  - Control: 18.1 (SD 8.5) vs. Intervention: 17.6 (SD 7.3), p = 0.81 (per-protocol)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Median antibiotic duration: Control 10 days vs. Intervention 5 days; p < 0.001
- Hospital readmission by day 30: Control 9 (6.6%) vs. Intervention 2 (1.4%); p = 0.02
- No significant difference in other secondary outcomes (e.g., hospital mortality, time to clinical improvement)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Shorter antibiotic therapy duration, as recommended by IDSA/ATS, can be safely implemented in hospitalised CAP patients, significantly reducing treatment duration.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multicentre RCT addressing an important clinical question
  - Pragmatic design allowing physician-selected antibiotics
  - Well-matched baseline characteristics
  - Significant difference in antibiotic duration between groups
  - Both intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses conducted

- **Weaknesses**
  - Initial primary outcome measure changed due to infrequency
  - High use of quinolones, limiting generalisability to regions where beta-lactams are first-line
  - Lack of blinding may have influenced control group decisions
  - Clinical criteria for stopping antibiotics applied to only 70.1% in the experimental arm

## Bottom Line
Stopping antibiotics at day five is a safe strategy for stable hospitalised patients with mild to moderate CAP in Northern Spain. Results may not generalise to the UK due to different prescribing practices. Further studies could enhance antibiotic stewardship.
