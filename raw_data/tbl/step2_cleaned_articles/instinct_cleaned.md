# Immunoglobulin G for Patients with Necrotising Soft Tissue Infection: A Randomised, Blinded, Placebo-Controlled Trial

## Clinical Question
In patients with necrotising soft tissue infection (NSTI), does the use of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) compared to placebo improve patient-reported physical outcomes?

## Background
- IVIG consists of pooled plasma from 2000-10000 donors and has anti-infective and immunomodulatory properties.
- NSTIs are characterised by fulminant tissue destruction, systemic signs of toxicity, and high mortality.
- Prior to INSTINCT, the effects of IVIG vs placebo were assessed in only one small RCT, which was stopped early due to slow recruitment.

## Design
- Randomised, blinded, placebo-controlled trial
- 1:1 randomisation
- Stratified by NSTI location (head/neck/extremities)
- Variable block sizes (2, 4, 6)
- Blinded administration and analysis

## Setting
- Single specialist centre for NSTI management in Denmark
- Study period: April 2014 to March 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with confirmed NSTI at surgery
- Admitted or planned admission to ICU

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- More than one dose of IVIG before randomisation
- >48 hours since NSTI diagnosis
- Pregnant or breastfeeding
- Hypersensitivity to IVIG

**Enrollment:**
- 129 patients screened, 100 enrolled

## Intervention

**IVIG Group:**
- IVIG 25g/day for three consecutive days

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- 0.9% sodium chloride, equivalent volume for three consecutive days

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Mean physical component summary (PCS) score at day 180: higher in IVIG group but not statistically significant
  - IVIG: 36 (IQR 0-43)
  - Placebo: 31 (IQR 0-47)
  - 95% CI: -7 to 10, p = 0.81

### Secondary Outcomes
- No statistical difference in:
  - Mortality at 28, 90, and 180 days
  - Time to resolution of shock
  - Severe bleeding
  - Total volume of blood products used
  - SOFA scores at days 1-7
  - Use of RRT, ventilation, and vasopressors
  - Serious adverse events
  - Days alive off life support in 90 days
  - Days alive and out of hospital in 180 days
  - Any amputation within 180 days

## Authors’ Conclusions
In ICU patients with NSTI, there was no apparent effect of adjuvant IVIG on self-reported physical functioning at 6 months.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised and blinded
  - Patient-reported outcomes
  - Early intervention upon confirmed diagnosis
  - Protocolised management
  - Registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02111161)
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single prior dose of IVIG allowed
  - Lower dose of IVIG compared to UK practice
  - Uneven distribution of infections between groups
  - Centralised management in Denmark affects external validity
  - Hyperbaric oxygen and antimicrobial use not typical in UK practice

## Bottom Line
This study does not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in patient-centred outcomes when IVIG is used with surgery, antimicrobials, and hyperbaric oxygen. Despite weaknesses, it provides valuable data for future NSTI studies, particularly in identifying and treating subgroups with streptococcal and staphylococcal infections.
