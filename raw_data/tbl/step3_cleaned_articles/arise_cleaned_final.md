# Australasian Resuscitation In Sepsis Evaluation Randomised Controlled Trial (ARISE)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with septic shock, does early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) compared with standard therapy reduce mortality at 90 days?

## Design
- Multi-centre, unblinded, randomised controlled trial
- Permuted block randomisation stratified by site
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Sample size: 1600 patients, 85-90% power to detect a 7.6% absolute risk reduction

## Setting
- 51 hospitals (45 in Australia/New Zealand, 6 in Finland, Hong Kong, Ireland)
- October 2008 – April 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults presenting to ED with suspected/confirmed infection and ≥2 SIRS criteria
- Evidence of refractory hypotension or hypoperfusion:
  - Refractory hypotension: SBP < 90 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg after 1000 ml IV fluid challenge
  - Hypoperfusion: blood lactate ≥ 4.0 mmol/L
- First dose of IV antimicrobial therapy commenced before randomisation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Contraindication to CVC insertion or blood products
- Inability to commence EGDT within 1 hour or complete 6 hours of EGDT
- Haemodynamic instability due to active bleeding
- Pregnancy
- Transfer from another acute facility
- Life expectancy < 90 days or imminent death

**Enrollment:**
- 1600 patients

## Intervention

**EGDT Group:**
- Arterial line and CVC for continuous ScvO2 measurement
- Treatment algorithm based on Rivers’ EGDT:
  - Supplemental oxygen to achieve SpO2 > 93%
  - Fluid boluses to achieve CVP > 8 mmHg (self-ventilating) or > 12 mmHg (NIV/invasive ventilation)
  - Vasopressors to achieve MAP 65–90 mmHg
  - ScvO2 > 70% once CVP and MAP targets achieved
  - PRBC transfusion if ScvO2 < 70% and HCT < 30%
  - Dobutamine if ScvO2 < 70% despite HCT > 30%
  - Increase oxygen, NIV, or mechanical ventilation if needed
- Intervention continued for 6 hours

## Control

**Usual Care Group:**
- Standard resuscitation care
- Arterial line and CVC at clinician’s discretion
- No ScvO2 measurement during 6-hour intervention period

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- All-cause mortality at 90 days: 18.6% EGDT vs. 18.8% usual care (RR 0.98, p = 0.90)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Median ED length of stay: 1.4 hours EGDT vs. 2.0 hours usual care
- No difference in hospital/ICU length of stay
- Vasopressor requirement: 76.3% EGDT vs. 65.8% usual care (p < 0.001)
- 28-day mortality: 14.8% EGDT vs. 15.9% usual care (RR 0.93, p = 0.53)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In critically ill patients with early septic shock, EGDT did not reduce all-cause mortality at 90 days compared to usual care.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large, multi-centre study
  - Use of original EGDT algorithm
  - Pragmatic design allowing clinician discretion
  - Subgroup analyses for variation in mortality between countries
  - Pre-published statistical analysis plan
- **Weaknesses**
  - Lower APACHE scores than ProCESS and Rivers study
  - Low recruitment rate per month across centres
  - High adherence to EGDT protocol and established sepsis management in usual care

## Bottom Line
EGDT, including continuous central venous co-oximetry and liberal blood transfusion, does not reduce mortality in septic shock. High standards of sepsis care, including early recognition, source control, antimicrobial therapy, fluid resuscitation, and escalation, remain essential.
