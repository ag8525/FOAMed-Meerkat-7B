# A Randomized Trial of Epinephrine in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (PARAMEDIC2)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA), does the administration of epinephrine compared to placebo improve survival?

## Background
Epinephrine is used in cardiac arrest to increase coronary blood flow by raising aortic diastolic pressure. However, it may also increase myocardial oxygen demand and platelet activation, potentially worsening cerebral ischemia. Previous studies have shown mixed results regarding its efficacy, prompting the need for a large-scale RCT.

## Design
- Randomized, double-blinded trial
- Computer-generated treatment assignments
- 1:1 allocation ratio
- Identical pre-prepared packs containing either 1 mg epinephrine or 0.9% saline
- Conducted by 5 NHS ambulance services in the UK
- Study period: December 2014 to October 2017
- Funded by the Health Technology Assessment Programme of the National Institute of Health Research

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults >16 years with OOHCA receiving ALS by trial-trained paramedics

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy, asthma or anaphylaxis as cause of arrest, prior epinephrine administration
- Traumatic cardiac arrest excluded in London Ambulance Service

**Enrollment:**
- 10,623 screened; 8,103 eligible and trial packs opened
- 8,014 randomized: 4,015 in epinephrine group, 3,999 in control

## Intervention

**Epinephrine Group:**
- 1 mg boluses every 3-5 minutes as per ALS guidelines

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- 0.9% saline in place of epinephrine

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **30-day survival**: 
  - Epinephrine: 3.2% 
  - Placebo: 2.4% 
  - Absolute Risk Increase: 0.89% (95% CI: 0.17 to 1.61%)
  - Adjusted Odds Ratio: 1.47 (95% CI: 1.09 to 1.97; p = 0.02)
  - Number Needed to Treat (NNT): 112

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Survival until hospital admission**: 
  - Epinephrine: 23.8% 
  - Placebo: 8.0% 
  - Adjusted Odds Ratio: 3.83 (95% CI: 3.30–4.43)
- **Neurological outcomes**: 
  - No significant difference in favorable outcomes
  - Worse neurological impairment in epinephrine group

## Authors’ Conclusions
The use of epinephrine in OOHCA increases 30-day survival but may lead to worse neurological outcomes. The trial raises important clinical and ethical questions about the balance between survival and quality of life.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large, pragmatic trial with robust methodology
  - Good internal and external validity
  - Balanced baseline characteristics
  - Rigorous blinding and minimal attrition bias
- **Weaknesses**
  - Exclusion of early ROSC limits generalizability
  - Primary focus on mortality, not neurological outcomes
  - Short follow-up period for secondary outcomes
  - Potential attrition bias due to loss to follow-up

## Bottom Line
Epinephrine improves 30-day survival in OOHCA but may worsen neurological outcomes. This trial highlights the need for further research and better access to early CPR and defibrillation.
