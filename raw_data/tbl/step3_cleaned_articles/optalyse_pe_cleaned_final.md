# A Randomized Trial of the Optimum Duration of Acoustic Pulse Thrombolysis Procedure in Acute Intermediate-Risk Pulmonary Embolism (OPTALYSE PE)

## Clinical Question
In patients with intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism (PE), do low doses of tPA and short durations of ultrasound-facilitated catheter-directed thrombolysis (USCDT) result in a reduction in RV:LV diameter at 48 hours?

## Background
Systemic thrombolysis in intermediate-risk PE has not shown long-term mortality benefits and is associated with increased bleeding. USCDT, a form of pharmaco-mechanical thrombolysis, uses ultrasound waves to enhance thrombolysis. Previous trials have shown improvements in RV:LV diameter ratio with USCDT compared to anticoagulation alone.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Block randomisation (initial block sizes of 4, changed to 3 after arm 4 closed)
- Multi-centre, parallel group study
- Imaging studies read in a central laboratory (blinded)
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Modified per protocol (mPP) population analyses also reported
- Statistical significance assessed using Student’s t-test

## Setting
- 17 centres in the USA and Europe
- Dates of data collection not stated

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 18-75
- Symptomatic, intermediate-risk, acute PE
- PE symptoms for <14 days
- Systolic BP >90 mmHg
- RV:LV diameter ratio ≥0.9 on CT angiography
- Proximal PE in at least one main or proximal lobar pulmonary artery

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Stroke or TIA; head trauma; other active intracranial or intraspinal disease within 1 year
- Recent active bleeding from major organ within 1 month; major surgery within 7 days of screening
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg
- Use of vasopressors
- Haematocrit <30%; platelet count <100; INR >3
- High risk for catastrophic bleeding; life expectancy <1 year; active cancer (except non-melanoma skin cancer)

## Intervention
- **Group 1 (n=28):** USCDT for 2 hours with tPA at 2 mg/h per catheter (4-8 mg total)
- **Group 2 (n=27):** USCDT for 4 hours with tPA at 1 mg/h per catheter (4-8 mg total)
- **Group 3 (n=28):** USCDT for 6 hours with tPA at 1 mg/h per catheter (6-12 mg total)
- **Group 4 (n=18):** USCDT for 6 hours with tPA at 2 mg/h per catheter (12-24 mg total)
  - Enrollment to this group stopped early due to an intracranial hemorrhage

## Control
- No control group
- All patients received therapeutic anticoagulation; heparin dose adjusted during thrombolysis

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Change in RV:LV diameter ratio from baseline to 48±6 hours after USCDT
  - Significantly reduced in all groups (p ≤ 0.0011)
  - % change from baseline: -24% (Group 1), -23% (Group 2), -26% (Group 3), -26% (Group 4)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Change in embolic burden (modified Miller score) significantly reduced in all groups (p ≤ 0.0043)
- Major bleeding within 72 hours: 0% (Group 1), 4% (Group 2), 4% (Group 3), 11% (Group 4)
- All-cause mortality at 30 days: 0% (Groups 1-3), 6% (Group 4)
- Recurrent PE within 1 year: 0% (Groups 1 and 4), 4% (Groups 2 and 3)

## Authors’ Conclusions
USCDT with lower doses and shorter infusions of thrombolytic therapy in acute intermediate-risk PE is associated with an improvement in RV:LV diameter ratio.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Multi-centre
  - Blinded assessors for imaging studies
- **Weaknesses**
  - Small sample size in each arm
  - No control group for comparison
  - Non-blinded participants and clinicians
  - Sponsors involved in study design
  - Primary outcome not patient-centred
  - Baseline characteristic differences

## Bottom Line
In intermediate-risk PE, USCDT with low-dose, short-duration thrombolysis significantly reduces RV:LV diameter ratio at 48 hours. Further trials with patient-centred outcomes and a control group are needed to determine the benefit of USCDT.
