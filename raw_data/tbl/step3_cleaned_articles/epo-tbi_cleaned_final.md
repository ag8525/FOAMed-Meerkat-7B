# Erythropoietin in Traumatic Brain Injury: A Double-Blind Randomised Controlled Trial

## Clinical Question
In patients with moderate or severe traumatic brain injury, does the administration of erythropoietin compared with placebo improve neurological outcomes at 6 months post-injury?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial  
- Double-blinded  
- Permuted blocks with computer-generated concealed allocation  
- Stratification by site and severity of injury  
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis  

## Setting
- Multicentre, multinational: 29 hospitals in 7 countries (Australia, New Zealand, France, Germany, Finland, Ireland, Saudi Arabia)  
- Study period: 3 May 2010 to 1 November 2014, with follow-up finalised on 6 May 2015  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 15-65 years with non-penetrating moderate (GCS 9-12) or severe (GCS 3-8) TBI  
- <24 hours since injury  
- Anticipated ICU stay >48 hours  
- Haemoglobin not exceeding the upper limit of normal  
- Ability to provide consent  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- GCS 3 and fixed dilated pupils  
- History of DVT, PE, or other thromboembolic event  
- Treatment with EPO in the past 30 days  

**Enrollment:**
- 3384 patients assessed, 606 randomised (308 to EPO, 298 to placebo)  
- 2778 excluded (2645 did not meet criteria, 133 declined participation)  

## Intervention

**EPO Group:**
- 40,000 IU erythropoietin subcutaneously  
- First dose within 24 hours of TBI, then weekly for up to 3 doses if in ICU, haemoglobin <120 g/L, and no withholding criteria met  

## Control
- Identical placebo (normal saline) subcutaneously  

## Both Groups
- Screening ultrasound of lower extremities within 48 hours after first dose, then weekly until ICU discharge  
- Structured telephone interview at 6 months to assess Extended Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS-E)  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in 6-month neurological status (GOS-E 1-4 vs. 5-8)  
  - EPO: 134/302 (44%)  
  - Placebo: 132/294 (45%)  
  - 95% CI: 0.81–1.18, p = 0.9  

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in proportional odds model of neurological outcome & mortality at 6 months (OR 1.04, 95% CI: 0.78–1.28, p = 0.79)  
- No difference in proximal DVT (EPO: 16%, Placebo: 18%, RR 0.87, 95% CI: 0.61–1.24, p = 0.44)  
- No difference in composite thrombotic outcomes (EPO: 22%, Placebo: 20%)  
- No difference in mortality at 6 months (EPO: 11%, Placebo: 16%, RR 0.68, 95% CI: 0.61–1.03, p = 0.07)  

## Authors’ Conclusions
Erythropoietin did not reduce severe neurological dysfunction or increase DVT or thrombotic outcomes in moderate/severe TBI. A pre-planned sensitivity analysis showed lower 6-month mortality with EPO (adjusted OR 0.58, 95% CI: 0.34–0.99, p = 0.04).

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed with allocation concealment and double-blinding  
  - Near-complete follow-up  
  - Multicentre, multinational study increases external validity  
  - Examined clinically meaningful outcomes and EPO safety  
- **Weaknesses**
  - Powered to detect a 24% RRR in GOS-E ≤4; smaller risk reductions cannot be excluded  

## Bottom Line
This trial does not support erythropoietin use in moderate/severe TBI. It highlights a high incidence of proximal DVT in this group, though clinical significance is unexplored.
