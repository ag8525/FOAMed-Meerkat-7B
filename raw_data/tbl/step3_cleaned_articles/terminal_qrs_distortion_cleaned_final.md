# Terminal QRS Distortion in Anterior Myocardial Infarction vs. Early Repolarization

## Clinical Question
In patients with benign early repolarization (BER), is there an absence of terminal QRS distortion on the ECG?

## Background
Differentiating ECG findings of benign early repolarization from left anterior descending artery occlusion (LADO) can be challenging in patients presenting with chest pain. Terminal QRS distortion is often present in LADO cases. This study investigates its presence in BER patients.

## Design
- Retrospective observational cohort study
- Secondary analysis
- Terminal QRS distortion defined as the absence of both:
  - S wave: Any deflection at the end of the R wave dipping below the PQ junction
  - J wave: Any positive deflection (notching/slurring) above the ST segment at the J point
- Only the first ECG analyzed if multiple were available
- Two reviewers assessed ECGs, not blinded to study objectives

## Setting
- Single center, USA
- Data collected from 2003 to 2005

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients presenting to ED with chest pain
- Admitted and ruled out for MI with serial negative Troponin I
- Cardiologist-confirmed interpretation of ER on ECG

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- <1mm ST elevation in any of leads V2–V4

**Enrollment:**
- 242 patients identified with early repolarization and non-ischemic chest pain
- 71 patients excluded due to <1mm ST elevation
- Mean age: 38 years
- 86% male

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Terminal QRS distortion was absent in 100% of patients with benign early repolarization (95% CI: 97.8–100%).

### Secondary Outcomes
- In patients with BER:
  - S wave absent in 0.6% in lead V2 and 9% in lead V3
  - J wave absent in 95% in lead V2 and 83% in lead V3
  - When a J wave was present, the S wave had significantly smaller amplitude:
    - V2: 4 mm vs. 14.5 mm, p = 0.004
    - V3: 0 mm vs. 9 mm, p < 0.0001

## Authors’ Conclusions
The presence of any terminal QRS distortion is 100% specific to acute myocardial infarction.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Use of two reviewers
  - Inclusion of patients with serial troponin testing
  - Clearly defined criteria for terminal QRS distortion assessment
- **Weaknesses**
  - Retrospective, single-center study
  - Did not include patients with other causes of ST elevation (e.g., pericarditis, left ventricular hypertrophy)
  - ECG readers were not blinded to study objectives
  - Limited data set with potential bias due to demographics (mean age 38, 86% male)

## Bottom Line
In patients with benign early repolarization, terminal QRS distortion is absent. The presence of both an S wave and a J wave in V2/V3 is typical in BER. Absence of both suggests a diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction should be considered.
