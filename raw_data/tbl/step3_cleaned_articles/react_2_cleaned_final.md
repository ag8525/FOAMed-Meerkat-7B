# Immediate Total-Body CT Scanning versus Conventional Imaging and Selective CT Scanning in Patients with Severe Trauma (REACT 2 Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe trauma, does immediate total-body CT scanning compared with standard workup with selective CT scanning reduce hospital mortality?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Computer randomisation
- Stratified by centre
- Non-blinded
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 4 hospitals in the Netherlands and 1 hospital in Switzerland
- All level 1 trauma centres and academic teaching hospitals
- Data collection: April 2011 – January 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult trauma patients with potentially severe injuries, defined by:
  - Abnormal physiology (e.g., RR ≥30 or ≤10, HR ≥120, BP ≤100, estimated blood loss ≥500 ml, GCS ≤13, abnormal pupillary reaction)
  - Clinical suspicion of severe injuries (e.g., fractures from ≥2 long bones, flail chest, severe abdominal injury, pelvic fracture)
  - Severe mechanism of injury (e.g., fall from height >3 meters, ejection from vehicle)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Patients referred from another hospital
- Clearly low-energy blunt trauma
- Stab wound in 1 body region
- Too unstable for CT (requiring CPR or immediate operation)

**Enrollment:**
- 1403 patients randomised; 320 excluded post-randomisation (e.g., did not meet criteria, declined participation)

## Intervention

**Immediate Total-Body CT Group:**
- CT scanning from vertex to pubic symphysis without prior conventional imaging
- Life-saving interventions performed prior to imaging (e.g., intubation, chest tube insertion)

## Control

**Selective CT Scanning Group:**
- Chest and pelvic X-ray and FAST scan during primary survey
- Selective CT scanning based on predefined local protocols
- 46% underwent sequential CT scans of all body regions

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **In-hospital mortality:** No significant difference
  - Intervention: 15.9%
  - Control: 15.7%
  - p = 0.923

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Radiation exposure:** Increased in intervention group
  - ED radiation (median): 20.9 mSv vs. 20.6 mSv, p < 0.0001
  - Hospital admission radiation (median): 21 mSv vs. 20.6 mSv, p < 0.0001
- **Time to imaging completion in ED:** Reduced in intervention group
  - 30 min vs. 37 min, p < 0.0001
- **Time to diagnosis:** Reduced in intervention group
  - 50 min vs. 58 min, p < 0.001
- **Time in trauma room, direct medical costs, missed injuries, life-threatening events during scanning:** No significant differences

## Authors’ Conclusions
Immediate whole-body CT scanning did not reduce mortality compared with standard radiological workup.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Multi-centre
  - Clear criteria for CT scans in control group
  - Allocation concealment with computer randomisation
- **Weaknesses**
  - Large number of post-randomisation exclusions, potentially introducing bias
  - Subjective inclusion/exclusion criteria
  - Differences in baseline characteristics (e.g., more patients with ISS ≥16 in intervention group)

## Bottom Line
For patients with suspected major trauma in high-functioning level 1 trauma centres, immediate whole-body CT scanning does not change mortality but increases radiation exposure. Focused CT scanning often results in a total-body CT scan due to segmental scans.
