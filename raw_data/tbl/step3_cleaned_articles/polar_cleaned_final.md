# Prophylactic Hypothermia for Severe Traumatic Brain Injury (POLAR RCT)

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe blunt traumatic brain injury (TBI), does early and sustained cooling compared with standard care improve neurological outcomes at 6 months?

## Background
- TBI is a leading cause of death and disability, with 37% of injury-related mortality in Europe associated with TBI.
- Severe TBI (GCS < 8) often results in unfavourable outcomes.
- Hypothermia is considered neuroprotective, potentially reducing secondary injury.
- Previous studies have shown mixed results; rapid induction of hypothermia may be key.

## Design
- Multicentre, partially-blinded, randomised-controlled trial
- Block randomisation with allocation concealment
- 1:1 assignment to control vs. cooling arm
- Stratification by randomisation site and geographical location
- Outcome assessors blinded; bedside clinical staff not blinded
- 82% power to detect a 15% Absolute Risk Increase in favourable outcomes
- Target sample size: 510 patients

## Setting
- 14 institutes across 6 countries (Australia, France, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Switzerland, New Zealand)
- Recruitment: 2010 to November 2017
- Final follow-up: May 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Blunt trauma with severe TBI (GCS < 9)
- Age 18-60
- Intubated or imminent intubation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >3 hours since injury
- >2.5 hours transport to hospital
- SBP < 90 mmHg, HR > 120/min
- Cardiac arrest at scene
- GCS 3 with fixed pupils
- Significant bleeding, pregnancy

**Enrollment:**
- 510 patients

## Intervention

**Cooling Arm:**
- 260 patients (47% pre-hospital)
- IV infusion of 4°C saline to target 35°C
- Maintenance with temperature control devices to achieve 33°C
- Temperature maintained for 3-7 days
- Rewarming based on ICP

## Control

**Normothermia Arm:**
- 240 patients (42% pre-hospital)
- Target temperature: 36.5-37.5°C
- Paracetamol or cooling vests if temperature >38°C

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Favourable neurological outcomes (GOSE 5-8) at 6 months:
  - Hypothermia: 48.8%
  - Normothermia: 49.1%

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in GOSE at 6 months (proportional odds model and sliding dichotomy)
- Mortality at discharge and 6 months: no difference
- Adverse events: increased pneumonia in hypothermia group
- No significant difference in ICP or fluid balance

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early and sustained prophylactic hypothermia does not improve neurological outcomes in severe TBI and increases pneumonia, ventilation days, bradycardia, and noradrenaline use.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Largest and best-conducted trial on this topic
  - Rapid induction of hypothermia
  - Blinded outcome assessors
  - Low loss to follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - Not blinded at the bedside
  - High proportion did not reach target temperature

## Bottom Line
Normothermia should be targeted in severe TBI patients. Hypothermia did not improve outcomes and increased complications.
