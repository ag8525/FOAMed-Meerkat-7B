# Effect of Thiamine Administration on Lactate Clearance and Mortality in Patients with Septic Shock

## Clinical Question
In patients with septic shock, does the administration of thiamine lead to more rapid lactate clearance and improved clinical outcomes?

## Background
Septic shock is characterized by a hypermetabolic state similar to thiamine deficiency. Thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency is common in critically ill patients. Previous pilot RCTs have shown lower lactate levels and improved mortality in thiamine-deficient septic shock patients.

## Design
- Retrospective, matched cohort study
- Data from medical records
- Cohorts matched using Mahalanobis distance matching based on:
  - ICU service (medical vs surgical)
  - Presence of liver disease
  - Peak lactate
  - SOFA score on ICU admission
  - Elixhauser comorbidity index
  - Age, gender, race
- Regression analysis with mortality as a competing event

## Setting
- Single academic center in the USA
- Study period: January 2013 to December 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients coded as septic shock in the electronic medical database
- Aged 18 years and older
- Admission to medical or surgical services

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Septic shock not present at admission (lactate < 2 mmol/L or no vasopressor therapy)
- Missing baseline data

**Enrollment:**
- 1049 patients included after validation with Sepsis-3 criteria
- Thiamine group (n=123) vs No Thiamine group (n=926)

## Intervention
- Intravenous thiamine administered within 24 hours of hospital admission

## Control
- No thiamine
- Patients matched in a 1:2 ratio with those who did not receive thiamine

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Lactate Clearance:**
  - Time from hospital admission to lactate ≤ 2 mmol/L, or
  - Time to lowest lactate plus 24 hours (if no lactate < 2 mmol/L and patient survived)
  - Thiamine group showed more rapid lactate clearance
  - Subdistribution hazard ratios: 1.292 to 1.339 across models
  - Greater effect in female patients

### Secondary Outcome
- **28-day Mortality:**
  - Thiamine significantly reduced 28-day mortality
  - Hazard ratio for death: 0.666 (95% CI: 0.490–0.905)
  - Greater benefit in female patients
- No significant differences in:
  - SOFA scores on day 5
  - Vasopressor-free days
  - Ventilator-free days
  - ICU-free days
  - Incidence of AKI
  - Requirement for renal replacement therapy

## Authors’ Conclusions
Intravenous thiamine administered within 24 hours of ICU admission in septic shock patients resulted in more rapid lactate clearance and significantly reduced 28-day mortality.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large study evaluating thiamine's effect on lactate clearance and mortality
  - Matching attempted to remove confounding variables
  - Appropriate regression analysis with mortality as a competing event
- **Weaknesses**
  - Retrospective observational study
  - Baseline differences suggest matching may not remove all confounding variables
  - Variable thiamine dosing (100–500 mg)
  - Thiamine levels not measured
  - High cohort mortality limits generalizability

## Bottom Line
This study suggests intravenous thiamine may improve outcomes in septic shock, but due to inherent biases, it should be considered hypothesis-generating. Given low risk, thiamine may be considered in critically ill sepsis patients.
