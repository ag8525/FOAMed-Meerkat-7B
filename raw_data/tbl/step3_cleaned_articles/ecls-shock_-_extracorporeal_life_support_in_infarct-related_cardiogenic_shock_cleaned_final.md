# Extracorporeal Life Support in Infarct-Related Cardiogenic Shock (ECLS-Shock)

## Clinical Question
In patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock undergoing revascularisation, does early unselected application of extracorporeal life support (ECLS) reduce 30-day all-cause mortality?

## Background
- VA ECMO is an advanced technology providing temporary cardiac and respiratory support.
- Observational studies suggest VA ECMO reduces mortality in cardiogenic shock.
- VA ECMO is associated with high complication risks and may increase left ventricular afterload.
- A recent RCT showed no benefit with early ECMO application in cardiogenic shock.

## Design
- Investigator-initiated, multi-centre, parallel-group, unblinded RCT
- Randomisation: 1:1 ratio via web-based system, stratified by trial site
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Powered to detect a 14% reduction in all-cause mortality

## Setting
- 44 institutions in Germany and Slovenia
- Study period: June 2019 to November 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults >18 years
- Acute myocardial infarction with planned revascularisation (PCI or CABG)
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg for >30 mins or requiring catecholamine infusion
- Signs of impaired organ perfusion
- Arterial lactate >3 mmol/L

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >45 mins of CPR prior to enrolment
- Mechanical or non-cardiac cause of shock
- Onset of shock >12 hours
- Severe peripheral vascular disease preventing ECMO insertion
- Age >80
- Life-limiting illness (survival <6 months)

**Enrollment:**
- 877 screened, 420 enrolled
- 3 withdrew consent, 417 included in intention-to-treat analysis

## Intervention
- Immediate commencement of VA ECMO in angiography suite
- ECMO ideally before PCI, maximal flows targeted
- Distal perfusion catheter insertion encouraged
- Pre-defined criteria for LV unloading and weaning
- 17 patients did not receive ECLS due to technical or clinical reasons

## Control
- Medical management per current guidelines
- Pre-defined criteria for escalation to additional mechanical support
- Crossover to VA ECMO for refractory cardiac arrest allowed but discouraged (28 crossed over)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- 30-day all-cause mortality: no difference (48% vs 49%)
  - Relative risk: 0.98 (95% CI: 0.80–1.19), p = 0.81
  - Early separation of Kaplan-Meier curve over first 5 days, disappears by day 15
  - No difference in pre-defined subgroups

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Poor neurological outcome
  - Renal replacement therapy
  - Repeat revascularisation
  - Time to stabilisation
  - Duration of ICU or hospitalisation
- Complications higher in intervention group:
  - Limb ischaemia requiring intervention: 11% vs 4%
  - Moderate/severe bleeding: 23% vs 10%
  - Many bleeding complications at ECMO decannulation

## Authors’ Conclusions
Early unselective use of VA ECMO in acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock does not reduce 30-day mortality and may increase complications.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed, investigator-initiated trial in a complex population
  - Clinically relevant question
  - Rapid patient recruitment reducing bias
  - Large patient numbers with clinically justifiable power calculation
  - Web-based randomisation, allocation concealment, and follow-up
  - Well-balanced groups at baseline
- **Weaknesses**
  - Liberal inclusion criteria limit generalisability
  - High pre-ECMO cardiac arrest rates limit findings to this population
  - No standardised neuroprognostication process
  - 12% crossover to ECMO in control group reduces group separation
  - Low LV unloading rates and high initial ECMO flows may limit cardiac recovery
  - Lack of blinding may influence clinician practice
  - Large number of sites with low recruitment numbers
  - Short-term outcomes reported

## Bottom Line
This trial suggests early unselected VA ECMO in acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock does not reduce mortality and may increase complications. VA ECMO should be considered for patients refractory to medical therapy with potential for myocardial recovery or destination therapy.
