# Cefepime vs Piperacillin-Tazobactam in Adults Hospitalized with Acute Infection (ACORN Trial)

## Clinical Question
In acutely ill adults in the ED or ICU, does the administration of cefepime or piperacillin-tazobactam result in an increased incidence of acute kidney injury (AKI) or death by day 14?

## Background
- Sepsis is a common reason for ICU admission, necessitating frequent antibiotic use.
- Anti-pseudomonal antibiotics are often recommended, but concerns exist about pip-taz and renal dysfunction, and cefepime and neurotoxicity.
- Limited randomized trials evaluate antibiotic-associated harm.

## Design
- Pragmatic, open-label, parallel-group, randomized trial
- Investigator-initiated
- Randomized 1:1 without stratification
- Clinical decision tool in EMR used for patient identification and randomization
- Primary outcome: 5-stage ordinal scale of AKI or death
- Initially required 2050 patients, increased to 2500 for power
- Ethical waiver of consent granted
- Independent DSMB

## Setting
- Single center, USA
- November 2021 – October 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- Located in ED or ICU
- <12 hours from presentation
- Initiating anti-pseudomonal cephalosporin or penicillin

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >1 dose of anti-pseudomonal antibiotic in last 7 days
- Allergy to cephalosporins or penicillin
- Prisoner
- Lack of equipoise for anti-pseudomonal antibiotic

**Enrollment:**
- 3806 assessed, 2634 randomized (1277 cefepime, 1357 pip-taz)
- 123 excluded (119 did not receive allocated drug, 4 prisoners)

## Intervention

**Cefepime Group:**
- 2-g IV push over 5 minutes every 8 hours
- Median duration: 3 days

## Control

**Piperacillin-Tazobactam Group:**
- 3.375-g bolus over 30 minutes, then extended infusion every 8 hours over 4 hours
- Median duration: 3 days

## Management Common to Both Groups
- No other anti-pseudomonal antibiotics allowed
- Duration and addition of other antibiotics at clinician discretion
- Dedicated clinical pharmacists in ED and ICU

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- AKI (any stage) or death by day 14
  - OR 0.95 (95% CI 0.80–1.13)
  - Death: 7.6% vs 6.0% (Risk Difference 1.6%, 95% CI -0.5 to 3.6)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in Major Adverse Kidney Events (MAKE) at day 14
- Delirium and coma-free days at day 14: Median 14 vs 14
  - OR 0.79 (95% CI 0.65–0.95)
  - Delirium or coma incidence: 20.8% vs 17.3% (Risk Difference 3.4%, 95% CI 0.3–6.6)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In hospitalized adults, treatment with pip-taz did not increase the incidence of AKI or death compared to cefepime. Cefepime use resulted in more neurological dysfunction.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large study addressing a common intervention with limited randomized evidence
  - Strong internal validity with randomization and allocation concealment
  - Use of EMR for recruitment, randomization, and data collection
- **Weaknesses**
  - Primary outcome may not be patient-centric
  - Single center, potentially limiting generalizability
  - Unblinded study
  - Short median duration of antibiotic exposure

## Bottom Line
In practice, pip-taz is preferred unless specific indications for cefepime exist. When using cefepime, monitor for potential neurotoxicity, especially in critically ill patients.
