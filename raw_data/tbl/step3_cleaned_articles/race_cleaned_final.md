# Effect of Levocarnitine vs Placebo as an Adjunctive Treatment for Septic Shock (RACE Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with septic shock, does levocarnitine, compared with placebo, reduce organ failure at 48 hours?

## Background
Despite extensive research, specific pharmacologic therapies targeting sepsis pathophysiology remain elusive. Levocarnitine may mitigate metabolic effects of sepsis by enhancing fatty acid entry into mitochondria and reducing toxic effects.

## Design
- Prospective, randomised, blinded, placebo-controlled clinical trial
- Bayesian response-adaptive randomisation
- Centralised web-based portal for treatment allocation
- Intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) analyses
- Interim analyses every 12 patients
- Monte Carlo simulation for trial power estimation

## Setting
- Emergency Departments and ICUs in 16 large urban medical centers in the United States
- Study period: 5 March 2013 to 5 February 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients ≥18 years with confirmed or presumed infection
- ≥2 systemic inflammatory response criteria
- Enrolment within 24 hours of septic shock recognition
- High-dose vasopressors for ≥4 hours at enrolment
- Cumulative SOFA score ≥6
- Blood lactate >18 mg/dL or 2 mmol/L

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Primary diagnosis other than sepsis
- Do-not-resuscitate status or advance directive
- History of seizures or inborn error of metabolism
- Anticipated surgery interfering with infusion
- Active participation in another trial
- CPR before enrolment
- Known allergy to levocarnitine
- Active warfarin treatment
- Severe immunocompromised state

**Enrollment:**
- 2694 individuals screened, 250 patients enrolled
- 247 patients included in modified ITT analysis
- 230 patients included in per-protocol analysis

## Intervention

**Active Treatment Arms:**
- Low dose: 6 g levocarnitine IV over 12 hours (n=35)
- Medium dose: 12 g levocarnitine IV over 12 hours (n=34)
- High dose: 18 g levocarnitine IV over 12 hours (n=106)

## Control
- Placebo: 0.9% saline IV over 12 hours (n=75)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Change in SOFA scores from enrolment to 48 hours
  - High dose (18 g) showed 78% and 77% probabilities of superiority over placebo in ITT and PP analyses, respectively
  - Posterior probability of superiority did not meet the 0.90 threshold

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Median ICU length of stay
  - Additional hospital days post-ICU discharge
  - Proportion of patients with care withdrawn

## Authors’ Conclusions
Levocarnitine did not significantly reduce organ failure at 48 hours in patients with septic shock.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Pharmacists masked to treatment with no study-related contact
  - Identical infusion bags for levocarnitine and placebo
  - Bayesian adaptive randomisation design
- **Weaknesses**
  - SOFA score may not be the best surrogate endpoint
  - Small treatment effects may not have been detected
  - APACHE II scores lower in high-dose arm

## Bottom Line
Levocarnitine at low, medium, or high doses did not significantly reduce organ failure in septic shock patients compared to placebo. This trial highlights the potential of metabolic modulation in sepsis but shows no benefit with the doses used.
