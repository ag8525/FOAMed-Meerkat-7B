# Etomidate versus Ketamine for Rapid Sequence Intubation in Acutely Ill Patients: A Multicentre Randomised Controlled Trial (KETASED)

## Clinical Question
In critically ill adults, does a single induction dose of etomidate compared to ketamine affect organ failure scores during the subsequent first 3 days?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled, prospective trial
- Randomisation by computer in blocks of 4
- Single-blinded: recruiting emergency physician aware, but nurses and intensive care physicians blinded
- Modified Intention-to-treat analysis (mITT) without post-randomisation exclusions
- 28-day follow-up period

## Setting
- 12 French emergency medical services (EMS) or emergency departments (ED)
- 65 French intensive care units (ICU)
- Study period: April 2007 to February 2008

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults aged 18 years or older
- Required sedation for emergency intubation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Cardiac arrest
- Contraindications to suxamethonium, ketamine, or etomidate
- Pregnancy

**Post-randomisation Exclusions:**
- Discharged from ICU within 3 days
- Died before reaching hospital

**Enrollment:**
- 689 patients screened, 655 randomised, 469 included in mITT analysis

## Intervention

**Ketamine Group:**
- Ketamine (Ketalar, Panpharma) 2 mg/kg intravenous bolus

## Control

**Etomidate Group:**
- Etomidate (Lipuro, B Braun Medical) 0.3 mg/kg intravenous bolus

**Both Groups:**
- Suxamethonium 1 mg/kg
- Continuous infusions of midazolam (0.1 mg/kg/hr) with either fentanyl (2–5 mcg/kg/hr) or sufentanil (0.2–0.5 mcg/kg/hr)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in maximum Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scores between groups
  - Mean SOFA scores: Etomidate: 10.3 (SD 3.7), Ketamine: 9.6 (SD 3.9)
  - Absolute difference: 0.7 (95% CI: 0.0 to 1.4), p = 0.056

### Secondary Outcomes
- No differences in secondary outcomes between groups
- Sub-group analysis showed no differences in sepsis, trauma, or combined sepsis and trauma groups for primary outcome

**Tertiary Data:**
- Lower basal cortisol levels and higher adrenal insufficiency in the etomidate group
- No difference in mortality between ACTH test non-responders and responders

## Authors’ Conclusions
Ketamine is a safe and valuable alternative to etomidate for intubation in critically ill patients, particularly in septic patients.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - State-funded study with no influence from the funding body
  - A priori publication of study methods and statistical plan
  - Maintained allocation concealment
  - Both modified and full intention-to-treat analyses presented
- **Weaknesses**
  - Findings not applicable in countries where etomidate is unavailable
  - Unclear control vs intervention objectives; non-inferiority design might have been better
  - Advanced statistical analysis complicates interpretation; p-value = 0.056 is borderline non-significant
  - Sub-group data discrepancies in figure presentation

## Bottom Line
This study addresses an important clinical question and finds no significant difference in organ failure scores between ketamine and etomidate for induction. There is a trend suggesting potential benefit from ketamine in septic patients, but the likelihood of a type II error is high.
