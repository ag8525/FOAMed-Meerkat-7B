# Effect of Recombinant Human Soluble Thrombomodulin on Mortality in Patients with Sepsis-Associated Coagulopathy (SCARLET Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with sepsis-associated coagulopathy, does the administration of human recombinant thrombomodulin (rhsTM) compared to placebo reduce 28-day mortality?

## Background
Sepsis-associated coagulopathy is linked to increased hospital mortality. Previous trials with Activated Protein C (Drotrecogin Alfa) have been controversial. ART-123, a recombinant human soluble thrombomodulin, acts as a co-factor with thrombin in activating Protein C. A Phase 2b study suggested safety and potential efficacy of rhsTM.

## Design
- Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase III trial
- Randomisation stratified by site with a block size of 4
- Allocation in a 1:1 ratio
- Consent sought prospectively
- Study supported by Asahi-Kasei Pharma America Corporation

## Setting
- 319 sites in 27 countries
- Patients enrolled from October 2012 to March 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults >18 years old
- Clinical evidence of bacterial infection with a known site
- SIRS response (WBC < 4000/mm³ or > 12000/mm³, Temperature < 36ºC or > 38ºC)
- Concurrent cardiovascular and/or respiratory dysfunction
- Vasopressors to maintain MAP > 65 mmHg after fluid resuscitation
- Mechanical ventilation with P/F ratio < 250 (or < 200 if respiratory infection)
- Coagulopathy (INR > 1.4, Platelets 30–150 x 10⁹/L)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Platelets < 30 x 10⁹/L
- Recent use of anticoagulants/antiplatelets (except specified exceptions)
- Recent CVA, trauma, GI bleed, or surgery with bleeding risk
- Renal dysfunction requiring RRT or acute oliguria > 48 hours

**Enrollment:**
- 800 patients (405 placebo, 395 rhsTM)

## Intervention
- ART-123 (rhsTM): 0.06 mg/kg/day (max 6 mg/day) for 6 days
- Administered as IV bolus or diluted in 50 ml 0.9% saline for 15-minute infusion

## Control
- Matching placebo in identical ampoules
- Management common to both groups, guided by clinical state

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- 28-Day Mortality: 26.8% rhsTM vs 29.4% placebo
  - Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR): 2.55% (95% CI: -3.68% to 8.77%)

### Subgroup Analyses
- No significant mortality difference in higher severity score (APACHE II > 25) or concomitant heparin use
- Suggestion of reduced mortality in baseline INR > 1.4 group

### Coagulation Markers
- Lower levels of D-dimer, Prothrombin fragment F1.2, and thrombin-antithrombin complex at day 6 in rhsTM group

### Safety Outcome
- Serious Adverse Events: 52% rhsTM vs 50% placebo
- Serious Major Bleeding: 5.8% rhsTM vs 4% placebo

## Authors’ Conclusions
The administration of rhsTM did not significantly reduce 28-day mortality in patients with sepsis-associated coagulopathy.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre, international, randomised, double-blind trial
  - Well-balanced baseline characteristics
  - Inclusion of vasopressors/mechanical ventilation as criteria
  - Collection of coagulation markers
- **Weaknesses**
  - Extended time window led to ~20% without coagulopathy at drug administration
  - Sample size based on 8% difference; Phase 2b showed 3.8% difference
  - Heterogeneous sepsis population
  - Some outcomes were post-hoc analyses

## Bottom Line
This study does not support the use of rhsTM in sepsis-associated coagulopathy. Continued research is needed due to the association with hospital mortality.
