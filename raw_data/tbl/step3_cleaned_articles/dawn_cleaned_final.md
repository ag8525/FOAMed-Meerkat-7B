# Thrombectomy 6 to 24 Hours after Stroke with a Mismatch Between Deficit and Infarct (DAWN Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients 6-24 hours post-acute stroke with a mismatch between clinical deficit and infarct, does thrombectomy plus standard medical care improve functional outcomes at 90 days compared to standard medical care alone?

## Background
Previous trials showed benefit of thrombectomy within 6 hours of stroke onset. Limited data exists on thrombectomy after 6 hours. Patients with ischaemic brain tissue not yet infarcted may benefit from reperfusion, identified by a clinical deficit disproportionate to infarct volume on imaging.

## Design
- Randomised clinical trial
- Web-based randomisation (1:1)
- Stratified by age, mismatch criteria, time from onset, occlusion site
- Un-blinded/open-label
- Allocation concealment during randomisation
- Primary outcome assessed by blinded structured interviews
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Adapted trial design with sample size 150-500 patients
- Trial stopped after first interim analysis
- Funded by Stryker Neurosciences

## Setting
- 26 centres in USA, Canada, Europe, and Australia
- Study period: September 2014 to February 2017
- 206 patients enrolled (107 intervention, 99 control)

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 18
- Acute ischaemic stroke
- Failed IV t-PA or contraindicated
- Last known normal 6-24 hours prior
- Baseline mRS of 0 or 1
- Occlusion of intracranial ICA and/or M1 MCA
- Mismatch between clinical deficit and infarct volume

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy, septic emboli, recent severe head injury or major haemorrhage
- Seizures at stroke onset precluding accurate diagnosis
- Sustained hypertension not reduced with medication
- Laboratory abnormalities (e.g., blood glucose <50 mg/dL or >400 mg/dL)
- Imaging findings (e.g., intracranial haemorrhage, infarct >1/3 MCA territory)

## Intervention
- Mechanical thrombectomy with Trevo device plus standard medical therapy
- No rescue devices or stenting of cervical ICA allowed
- Thrombectomy performed in 105 of 107 patients

## Control
- Standard medical therapy only

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcomes
- **Utility-weighted mRS for disability at 90 days**: 5.5 vs. 3.4 (thrombectomy vs. control)
  - Absolute difference: 2.1 (95% CI: 1.2-3.1)
  - Adjusted difference: 2.0 (95% CI: 1.1-3.0)
  - Posterior probability of superiority >0.999

- **Functional independence at 90 days (mRS 0-2)**: 49% vs. 13%
  - Absolute difference: 36% (95% CI: 24-47)
  - Adjusted difference: 33% (95% CI: 24-44)
  - NNT: 3

### Secondary Outcomes
- Improved early therapeutic response, vessel recanalisation, and change in infarct volume in thrombectomy group

### Safety Outcomes
- No significant differences in stroke-related mortality, death from any cause, or symptomatic ICH at 24 hours
- Neurologic deterioration reduced in thrombectomy group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Thrombectomy is superior for functional independence and disability at 90 days in patients with intracranial ICA or proximal MCA strokes with a mismatch of clinical symptoms vs. infarct volumes, last seen normal 6-24 hours prior.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Effective randomisation and allocation
  - Clinically relevant co-primary endpoints
  - Blinded assessors of primary outcome
  - Multi-centre study
- **Weaknesses**
  - Industry-sponsored using a single device
  - Imaging modality and resources may not be available to all clinicians
  - Trial closed early, subgroups underpowered
  - Control group had worse outcomes than prior studies

## Bottom Line
Thrombectomy with the Trevo device improves functional outcomes at 90 days in patients with stroke last seen normal 6-24 hours prior. The study's advanced imaging techniques and comprehensive stroke care may not be available to smaller centres. Further studies are needed to validate these findings across broader stroke populations.
