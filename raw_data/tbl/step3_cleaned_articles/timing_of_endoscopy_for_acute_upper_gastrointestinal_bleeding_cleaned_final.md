# Timing of Endoscopy for Acute Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding

## Clinical Question
In patients with acute upper GI bleeding and a Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS) ≥ 12, does endoscopy performed within 6 hours of gastroenterological consultation, compared with endoscopy performed within 6–24 hours, reduce 30-day mortality?

## Background
- Endoscopy for acute UGI bleed is recommended within 24 hours by an International Consensus Group.
- The optimal timing within the first 24 hours is unclear.
- Previous studies showed no differences in mortality or re-bleeding when comparing immediate vs. delayed endoscopy.

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial
- Patients randomly assigned (1:1) using a computer-generated sequence
- Screened by gastroenterology team in ED or on admission
- Written informed consent obtained
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Power calculations: 258 patients per arm for 80% power to detect an 8% absolute difference in 30-day mortality
- Local ethics approval and registered with clinicaltrials.gov

## Setting
- Hong Kong
- Study period: July 2012 to October 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults >18 years old
- Overt signs of UGI bleeding (haematemesis, melaena, or both)
- GBS ≥ 12

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- <18 years old
- Unable to provide written consent
- Pregnant
- Moribund from terminal illness
- In shock or unstable after initial resuscitation

**Enrollment:**
- 4715 screened → 598 had GBS ≥ 12 → 516 enrolled (258 in each arm)

## Intervention

**Urgent Endoscopy Group:**
- Endoscopy within 6 hours of gastroenterology consultation
- Mean time: 2.5 hours post-consultation, 9.9 hours after initial presentation

## Control

**Early Endoscopy Group:**
- Endoscopy between 6–24 hours of gastroenterology consultation
- Mean time: 16.8 hours post-consultation, 24.7 hours after initial presentation

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Pre-endoscopy: IV PPI infusion (80 mg bolus + 8 mg/hr)
- Suspected variceal bleeding: vasoactive drug and antibiotics
- During endoscopy: treatment of ulcers and varices
- Post-endoscopy: 72 hours of PPI infusion

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- All-cause mortality at 30 days: no significant difference
  - Urgent group: 8.9%
  - Early group: 6.6%
  - Difference: 2.3% (95% CI: -2.3 to 6.9)
  - Hazard ratio: 1.35 (95% CI: 0.72–2.54), p = 0.34

### Secondary Outcomes
- Endoscopic treatment during initial endoscopy: significantly greater in urgent group (60.1% vs. 48.4%)
- No significant difference in further bleeding, hospitalisation duration, or red cell transfusion

## Authors’ Conclusions
Endoscopy within 6 hours of gastroenterology consultation does not reduce 30-day mortality compared to endoscopy within 6–24 hours.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses a common clinical question with limited evidence
  - Objective outcome measure (all-cause mortality)
  - Randomised trial with well-balanced baseline characteristics
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Timings based on gastroenterology consultation, not presentation
  - Not applicable to critically unwell patients with UGI bleeding
  - Study conducted in Hong Kong, limiting external validity

## Bottom Line
In patients with high-risk UGI bleeding treated with pre-endoscopy PPI and not in persistent shock, endoscopy at a median of 10 hours vs. 25 hours post-presentation does not reduce 30-day mortality. The study's findings should not be used to delay endoscopy in critically unwell patients.
