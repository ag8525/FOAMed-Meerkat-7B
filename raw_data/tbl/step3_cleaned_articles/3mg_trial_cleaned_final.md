summary: |
  The 3Mg Trial investigated the efficacy of intravenous (IV) and nebulised magnesium sulphate (MgSO₄) in adults with severe acute asthma, comparing these interventions to standard therapy. Conducted as a double-blinded, placebo-controlled, multi-centre trial across 34 UK emergency departments, the study included 1084 patients but was underpowered due to early termination. The primary outcomes showed no significant reduction in hospital admissions or improvement in breathlessness with either form of MgSO₄ compared to placebo. Secondary outcomes, including mortality and need for respiratory support, also showed no significant differences. The trial concluded that nebulised MgSO₄ is ineffective in this context, and while IV MgSO₄ may have a limited role, its clinical benefit is minimal. The study's strengths include its robust design and adherence to established guidelines, but its early termination and protocol changes are noted weaknesses.

# Intravenous or Nebulised Magnesium Sulphate versus Standard Therapy for Severe Acute Asthma (3Mg Trial)

## Clinical Question
Does intravenous or nebulised MgSO₄ improve symptoms of breathlessness and reduce the need for hospital admission in adults with severe acute asthma?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial  
- Double-blinded  
- Placebo-controlled  
- Multi-centre  
- Intention-to-treat analysis  

## Setting
- 34 Emergency Departments in the UK  
- Over one-third of recruitments were from 3 EDs: Edinburgh, Sheffield, and Bath  
- Study period: 30 July 2008 to 30 June 2012  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults over 16 years old  
- Attendees to the ED with severe acute asthma per BTS Guidelines  
  - PEFR < 50% of best or predicted  
  - Respiratory rate > 25/min  
  - Heart rate > 110/min  
  - Inability to complete sentences in 1 breath  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Life-threatening asthma:
  - O₂ sats < 92%
  - Silent chest, cyanosis, poor respiratory effort
  - Bradycardia, arrhythmia, hypotension, exhaustion, coma, confusion  
- Contraindication to MgSO₄ (IV or nebulised)  
- Inability to give verbal or written consent  
- Previous participation in the 3Mg trial  

**Enrollment:**
- 1084 patients randomised and analysed by intention-to-treat  
- Target was 1200 patients; recruitment was stopped early due to expired funding  

## Intervention

**IV Group:**
- Intravenous MgSO₄ (2g over 20 minutes)  
- Standard BTS/SIGN therapy  

**Nebulised Group:**
- Nebulised MgSO₄ (500 mg × 3 at 20-minute intervals)  
- Standard BTS/SIGN therapy  

## Control
- Placebo (IV and nebulised)  
- Standard BTS/SIGN therapy, including:  
  - Oxygen  
  - Nebulised salbutamol (5 mg)  
  - Nebulised ipratropium (500 mcg)  
  - Oral prednisolone  
  - Other treatments at clinician’s discretion  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcomes
- **Hospital admission within 7 days**:
  - IV MgSO₄: 285/394 (72%)  
  - Nebulised MgSO₄: 261/332 (79%)  
  - Placebo: 281/358 (78%)  
  - Odds ratio (IV vs placebo): 0.84 (95% CI: 0.61–1.15); p = 0.276  

- **Patient-reported breathlessness at 2 hours (VAS):**  
  - No difference: 0 mm (95% CI: –1.9 to 1.9); p = 0.999  

### Secondary Outcomes
No significant differences across groups for:
- Mortality (1 death each in intervention groups, none in control)  
- Use of ventilation/respiratory support  
- Length of hospital stay  
- Admission to HDU/ICU  
- Change in PEFR or physiological variables  

**Side Effects:**
- Increased incidence of side effects in both intervention groups compared to placebo (up to 30 days post-treatment)

**Still to be reported:**
- Change in quality of life at 1 month  
- Number of unscheduled healthcare visits over 1 month  
- Satisfaction with care  

## Authors’ Conclusions
Nebulised MgSO₄ has no role in managing severe acute asthma in adults. IV MgSO₄ may have a limited role but is unlikely to offer significant clinical benefit in most cases.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Double-blinded, placebo-controlled RCT  
  - Treatment packs included both IV infusion and nebulised solutions for all groups  
  - Multi-centre study with wide recruitment  
  - Control group followed well-established BTS/SIGN recommendations  
  - Study registered: ISRCTN04417063  

- **Weaknesses**
  - Recruitment terminated early due to expired funding  
  - Underpowered: original target was 1200 patients to detect a 10% absolute difference with 90% power; actual recruitment provided only 84–87% power  
  - Protocol changed mid-trial to include patients who had received MgSO₄ in the prior 24 hours  

## Bottom Line
This is the largest trial to date on MgSO₄ in severe adult asthma. The results do not support nebulised magnesium use, and while IV MgSO₄ might still be considered in life-threatening asthma, its expected clinical impact is low.
