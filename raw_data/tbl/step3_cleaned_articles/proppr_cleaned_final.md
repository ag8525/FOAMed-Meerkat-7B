# Transfusion of Plasma, Platelets, and Red Blood Cells in a 1:1:1 vs 1:1:2 Ratio and Mortality in Patients With Severe Trauma (PROPPR Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe trauma predicted to require massive transfusion, does a transfusion protocol using a 1:1:1 ratio of plasma to platelets to red blood cells (RBCs) compared to 1:1:2 improve mortality?

## Design
- Randomised-controlled trial
- Non-blinded treating clinicians
- Allocation concealment with sealed containers of blood products
- Blinded assessors of primary outcome
- Sample size increased from 580 to 680 due to lower actual mortality than predicted

## Setting
- 12 North American Level 1 trauma centers
- Study period: 3 August 2012 to 2 December 2013

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Highest trauma level activation
- Estimated age ≥15 years or weight ≥50 kg if age unknown
- Directly from the scene of injury
- Received ≥1 unit of blood product pre-hospital or within 1 hour of hospital arrival
- Predicted to require massive transfusion (Assessment of Blood Consumption Score ≥2 or attending trauma surgeon’s judgment)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Life-saving intervention from another hospital
- Non-survivable injuries
- Prisoners directly from jail
- Required emergency thoracotomy before randomisation
- Obvious pregnancy
- Severely burned
- ≥5 minutes of CPR with chest compressions before admission
- Known “Do Not Resuscitate” orders before randomisation
- Enrolled in another interventional, randomised clinical trial
- Wearing “opt-out” bracelet
- >3 units RBCs given before randomisation
- PROPPR products not given within 2 hours
- Patient improved, no further transfusion needed

**Enrollment:**
- 11,185 screened, 680 randomised (338 to intervention, 342 to control)

## Intervention

**1:1:1 Ratio Group:**
- Blood product packs: 6 units plasma, 1 dose platelets (pool of 6 units), 6 units RBCs

## Control

**1:1:2 Ratio Group:**
- Alternating packs: 3 units plasma, 0 dose platelets, 6 units RBCs with 3 units plasma, 1 dose platelets, 6 units RBCs

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcomes
- **24-hour mortality:** No significant difference
- **30-day mortality:** No significant difference

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Time to haemostasis
  - Any of 23 pre-defined complications
  - Hospital-, ventilator-, and ICU-free days
  - Incidence of surgical procedures
  - Functional status at discharge
  - Blood product volumes transfused until haemostasis

**Post-Hoc Analysis:**
- Death by exsanguination in the first 24 hours significantly decreased in the intervention group
- Achieved haemostasis significantly greater in the intervention group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with severe trauma and major bleeding, early administration of plasma, platelets, and RBCs in a 1:1:1 ratio compared with a 1:1:2 ratio did not result in significant differences in mortality at 24 hours or 30 days.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, multi-centre, pragmatic trial
  - Targeted severely injured trauma patients at high risk of haemorrhage
  - Pre-specified transfusion order to maintain target ratios
  - Sample size increased to maintain statistical power
  - Minimal loss during follow-up
  - Allocation concealment maintained
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded
  - 30-day maximum follow-up time
  - Powered to detect a 10% absolute difference in mortality; unable to detect smaller effects
  - 2,968 patients needed to detect the observed 4.2% difference in 24-hour mortality with 90% power

## Bottom Line
Among patients with severe trauma and major bleeding, there was no significant difference in 24-hour or 30-day mortality between those receiving a 1:1:1 ratio of blood products compared to a 1:1:2 ratio. However, the study was underpowered for the observed mortality difference. A 1:1:1 ratio reduced mortality from exsanguination within the first 24 hours, with similar complication rates to a lower ratio protocol. Therefore, aiming for a 1:1:1 ratio in this population is reasonable.
