# A Multicenter, Randomized Trial of Ramped Position vs Sniffing Position During Endotracheal Intubation of Critically Ill Adults

## Clinical Question
In critically ill patients undergoing emergency intubation, does the ramped position compared to the sniffing position reduce the incidence of oxygen desaturation?

## Background
Hypoxaemia occurs in nearly 50% of critical care intubations. Patient positioning may improve variables such as functional residual capacity and laryngeal anatomy. Observational data suggest the ramped position (torso and head elevated) may be beneficial compared to the sniffing position (patient supine, neck flexed, and head extended).

## Design
- 2 x 2 factorial design
- Randomised, multicentre pragmatic trial
- Patients randomised in a 1:1 ratio using a computer and permuted blocks
- Data collected by an independent observer with quality assurance on 10% of cases
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- Four tertiary ICUs in the United States
- Study period: July 2015 to July 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients over 18 undergoing endotracheal intubation by critical care or pulmonary fellows in the medical ICU

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Urgent intubation where randomisation envelope could not be obtained in time
- Specific position clinically indicated by treating clinicians

**Enrollment:**
- 309 eligible, 49 excluded (42 due to urgency, 6 due to specific position), 260 randomised

## Intervention

**Ramped Position:**
- Electronic bed used to elevate head of the bed to 25 degrees
- Patient’s head positioned at the top of the mattress, face parallel to the ceiling

## Control

**Sniffing Position:**
- Entire bed remained horizontal
- Folded blankets/towels placed beneath the patient’s head with neck in flexion and head in extension

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistically significant difference in median lowest oxygen saturation:
  - Sniffing: 92% [IQR 79-98%]
  - Ramped: 93% [IQR 84-99%]
  - Adjusted for confounders including saturation at induction, BMI, operator experience, and use of video laryngoscopy

### Secondary Outcomes
- Worse view and difficulty of intubation in the ramped group:
  - Cormack-Lehane view Grade III or IV: Sniffing 11.6% vs Ramped 25.4% (p=0.01)
  - Operator-reported “difficult” intubation: Sniffing 4.6% vs Ramped 12.3% (p=0.04)
  - First attempt success: Sniffing 85.4% vs Ramped 76.2% (p=0.02)
  - More than 3 attempts at laryngoscopy: Sniffing 2.3% vs Ramped 7.7% (p=0.02)
- No difference in other oxygenation or haemodynamic outcomes

## Authors’ Conclusions
The ramped position was not associated with an increase in the lowest arterial saturation during intubation of critically ill patients in the ICU, but it may increase the difficulty of the intubation and the likelihood of repeated attempts.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Relevant question for daily ICU practice
  - Prospective, multicentre study with larger numbers than similar trials
  - First study of its kind in the ICU
  - Baseline characteristics well balanced between groups
  - Minimal deviation from protocol
  - Quality assurance improved data accuracy
- **Weaknesses**
  - Factorial design with potential interaction between position and checklist use
  - Incomplete data on clinician’s past experience with ramped position
  - Only included fellow/trainee clinicians
  - Cases where senior clinicians intubated may have been more challenging

## Bottom Line
Among adult ICU patients, the ramped position did not improve the lowest recorded saturations but did increase the difficulty of intubation and the likelihood of repeated attempts. Each intubation should be assessed on a case-by-case basis, considering previous experience and comfort level when choosing patient position.
