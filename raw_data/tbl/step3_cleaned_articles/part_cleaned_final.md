# Effect of a Strategy of Initial Laryngeal Tube Insertion vs Endotracheal Intubation on 72-Hour Survival in Adults With Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (PART Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adults suffering out-of-hospital non-traumatic cardiac arrest, does a strategy of airway management initially with a laryngeal tube (LT) compared to endotracheal intubation (ETI) improve survival at 72 hours?

## Design
- Pragmatic, cluster-randomised, multiple cross-over design
- Paramedic/EMS units were the unit of randomisation
- Open-label intervention and control groups

## Setting
- 27 EMS agencies in 13 randomisation clusters
- Study period: December 2015 to November 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults over 18 years
- Non-traumatic cardiac arrest
- Requiring advanced airway management or anticipated ventilatory support

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Initial care by non-participating EMS agency
- Major facial trauma, major bleeding, asphyxia cardiac arrest
- LVAD or total artificial heart
- Do not attempt resuscitation orders
- Pregnancy, prisoners

**Enrollment:**
- 3,840 patients screened, 3,004 included

## Intervention

**Laryngeal Tube Group:**
- Initial airway management with a laryngeal tube
- EMS instructed to use LT during cardiac arrests
- Combitubes, LMAs, iGels, or other supraglottic devices not allowed

## Control

**Endotracheal Intubation Group:**
- Initial airway management with endotracheal intubation
- Conventional orotracheal intubation preferred
- Videolaryngoscopy allowed, but not nasotracheal or digital intubation

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- 72-hour survival rate:
  - LT group: 18.3%
  - ETI group: 15.4%
  - Adjusted Absolute Risk Reduction (ARR): 2.9% (95% CI: 0.2% to 5.6%; p = 0.04)
  - Number Needed to Treat (NNT): 35

### Secondary Outcomes
- Return of spontaneous circulation: LT 27.9% vs ETI 24.3% (ARR 3.6%; p = 0.03)
- Hospital survival: LT 10.8% vs ETI 8.1% (ARR 2.7%; p = 0.01)
- Favourable neurological status at discharge: LT 7.1% vs ETI 5.0% (ARR 2.1%; p = 0.02)

## Authors’ Conclusions
A strategy of initial airway management with a laryngeal tube was associated with modest but significantly improved survival at 72 hours compared to endotracheal intubation.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Important clinical question with pragmatic methodology
  - Complex randomisation strategy to minimise between-group differences
  - Thorough statistical analysis
- **Weaknesses**
  - Cluster-randomised cross-over design may introduce systematic differences
  - Lack of blinding and concealment of allocation
  - Not generalisable beyond laryngeal tubes to other supraglottic devices

## Bottom Line
This trial suggests that a strategy involving laryngeal tubes as the initial airway management device may be superior to endotracheal intubation in pre-hospital, non-traumatic, adult cardiac arrest settings. However, potential systematic biases may affect the observed difference.
