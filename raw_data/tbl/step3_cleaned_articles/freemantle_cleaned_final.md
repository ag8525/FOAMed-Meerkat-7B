The study included over 14 million cases and found that patients admitted on weekends had a higher risk of 30-day mortality, with hazard ratios of 1.11 for Saturday and 1.16 for Sunday admissions compared to Wednesday. # Weekend Hospitalization and Additional Risk of Death: An Analysis of Inpatient Data

## Clinical Question
Do patients admitted to English NHS hospitals on weekends have an increased 30-day mortality compared to those admitted on weekdays?

## Design
- Retrospective cohort study
- Mortality data from Office of National Statistics
- Cox models used to adjust for risk differences

## Setting
- All NHS hospitals in England
- Study period: April 2009 to March 2010

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- All admissions to NHS hospitals

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- None

**Enrollment:**
- 14,217,640 cases analyzed from 15,061,472 reported admissions
- 5.6% of patients had incomplete data and were excluded

## Analysis Groups

**Weekend Days Cohort:**
- Day of admission analysis: Patients admitted on Saturday or Sunday
- Day of in-patient stay analysis: Patients already in hospital on Saturday or Sunday

**Weekdays Cohort:**
- Day of admission analysis: Patients admitted on Monday to Friday
- Day of in-patient stay analysis: Patients already in hospital on Monday to Friday

## Outcomes

### Descriptive Statistics
- Total 30-day mortality: 284,852 (2.00% of analyzed admissions)
- In-hospital deaths within 30 days: 187,337 (1.32%)
- Weekend day mortality: 46,591 (25% of in-hospital deaths)
- Weekday mortality: 140,746 (75% of in-hospital deaths)

### Day of Admission Analysis
- **Weekend Days:**
  - Increased risk of death at 30 days compared to weekdays
  - Saturday vs. Wednesday: HR 1.11 (95% CI: 1.14–1.18), p < 0.0001
  - Sunday vs. Wednesday: HR 1.16 (95% CI: 1.09–1.13), p < 0.0001

- **Weekdays:**
  - Monday vs. Wednesday: HR 1.02 (95% CI: 1.01–1.04)
  - No significant difference for Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday vs. Wednesday

### Day of In-patient Stay Analysis
- **Weekend Days:**
  - Reduced risk of death while in hospital
  - Saturday vs. Wednesday: HR 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.96), p < 0.0001
  - Sunday vs. Wednesday: HR 0.92 (95% CI: 0.91–0.94), p < 0.0001

- **Weekdays:**
  - Thursday vs. Wednesday: HR 0.96 (95% CI: 0.95–0.98), p < 0.0001
  - Friday vs. Wednesday: HR 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.96), p < 0.0001

## Subgroup Analyses
- **Elective vs. Emergency Admissions:**
  - Increased mortality for weekend admissions, more pronounced in elective patients
  - Sunday vs. Wednesday: Emergency HR 1.14, Elective HR 1.62

- **Diagnostic Categories:**
  - Increased weekend risk for stroke, heart failure, heart attack, COPD, lung cancer, acute kidney injury
  - No increased risk for pneumonia, sepsis, UTI, fractured hips

- **University vs. Non-university Hospitals:**
  - Similar patterns of increased weekend mortality risk

## Authors’ Conclusions
Being admitted on a weekend is associated with increased 30-day mortality, but being an in-patient on a weekend is associated with reduced in-hospital mortality.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre study including all NHS England trusts
  - Large patient cohort
  - Comprehensive statistical analysis with covariate adjustments
  - Follow-up includes out-of-hospital deaths

- **Weaknesses**
  - Association does not imply causation
  - Lack of absolute numbers for subgroups limits understanding
  - Simplified covariate adjustments may miss important factors
  - No adjustment for severity of illness or time of day of admission

## Bottom Line
This study shows an association between weekend admissions and increased 30-day mortality, but many confounding variables limit definitive conclusions. It should be considered hypothesis-generating only.
