# Single-Dose Etomidate Does Not Increase Mortality in Patients with Sepsis: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with sepsis, severe sepsis, or septic shock, is a single dose of etomidate for induction of anaesthesia associated with a difference in mortality compared to other induction agents?

## Design
- Systematic review and meta-analysis
- Conducted in adherence to PRISMA guidelines
- Two reviewers performed systematic review and data extraction
- Databases: PubMed, Embase, and CENTRAL up to July 2014

## Setting of Trials
- 10 trials in North America, 6 in Europe, 2 in Asia
- Published between 2002 and 2014

## Population of Trials

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult populations
- Single-dose etomidate compared against any other sedative or no agent
- Mortality as an outcome
- Randomised controlled trials or observational studies

**Enrollment:**
- 424 trials identified, 20 full-text assessed, 18 analysed in full
- Total population: 2801 etomidate group vs 2751 other sedatives group

## Methodology of Trials
- 2 RCTs
- 3 post-hoc analyses of RCTs
- 10 retrospective cohort studies
- 3 prospective cohort studies

## Intervention
- Single-dose etomidate
- Specific details such as dose or indication not provided

## Control
- Any other sedative agent
  - RCT: ketamine (n=76), midazolam (n=120)
  - Cohort: midazolam (n=65)
  - Others: not specified

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistically significant difference in mortality was shown by meta-analysis of the RCTs or cohort studies (performed separately)
  - RCTs: risk ratio 1.20 (95% CI 0.84 to 1.72), favouring control but overlapping no effect
  - Cohorts: risk ratio 1.05 (95% CI 0.97 to 1.13), favouring control but overlapping no effect

### Secondary Outcome
- Insufficient data prevented meta-analysis of resource use outcomes, including length of stay and duration of mechanical ventilation
- Meta-analysis of available data from 8 cohort studies found a statistically significant increase in adrenal insufficiency associated with etomidate compared to control
  - Risk ratio 1.42 (95% CI 1.22 to 1.64)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with sepsis, a single dose of etomidate was not associated with increased mortality. However, given the data come from two small RCTs and other observational studies with potentially significant biases, this conclusion is not certain, and further trials should be performed.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Systematically designed and conducted review following established PRISMA guidelines
  - Identified studies are recent and global
  - Sensitivity analysis performed
  - Did not overemphasise the strength of data from post-hoc analysis of RCTs
- **Weaknesses**
  - Various drugs used as control for comparison
  - Secondary outcomes of resource use could not be analysed due to the paucity of available data
  - No attempt was made to contact the authors of the original trials for additional data
  - Individual patient meta-analysis may provide stronger certainty about the conclusion

## Bottom Line
This meta-analysis did not find evidence of harm from single-dose etomidate, but the trials on which it is based are small and the comparators are not clearly defined. Therefore, while there are alternative agents for cardio-stable induction, this trial does not provide evidence to support etomidate use over and above these other agents.
