# Moderate Pulmonary Embolism Treated with Thrombolysis (MOPETT)

## Clinical Question
In patients with moderate pulmonary embolism (PE), does low-dose thrombolysis combined with anticoagulation reduce pulmonary artery pressure at 28 months compared to anticoagulation alone?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Non-blinded patients and clinicians
- Blinded cardiologists for primary outcome assessment
- Powered at 90% to detect a 10% reduction in pulmonary artery pressure with a 5% alpha error

## Setting
- Single centre in the USA
- Study period: 2008 to 2010

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Signs and symptoms suggestive of PE, confirmed by imaging (CT or V/Q scan)
- Moderate PE defined by:
  - CT-PA showing >70% thrombus involvement in ≥2 lobar or main pulmonary arteries
  - High-probability V/Q scan showing mismatch in ≥2 lobes

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Symptom onset >10 days
- >8 hours since start of parenteral anticoagulation
- Blood pressure <95 or ≥200/100 mmHg
- Contraindication to thrombolysis

**Enrollment:**
- 121 patients randomised

## Intervention

**Thrombolysis Group:**
- tPA and enoxaparin
  - tPA: ≥50 kg: 10 mg bolus, 40 mg over 2 hours; <50 kg: 0.5 mg/kg total dose
  - Enoxaparin: 1 mg/kg SC BID (max 80 mg) or unfractionated heparin

## Control

**Anticoagulation Group:**
- Enoxaparin alone: 1 mg/kg SC BID or unfractionated heparin

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Development of pulmonary hypertension (PASP ≥40 mmHg) at 28 months
  - Thrombolysis: 16%
  - Control: 57%
  - p < 0.001, NNT = 2.5
  - Mean PASP: 28 mmHg vs. 43 mmHg, p < 0.001

### Secondary Outcomes
- Composite of pulmonary hypertension and recurrent PE at 28 months
  - Thrombolysis: 16%
  - Control: 63%
  - p < 0.001
- Recurrent PE: 0% vs. 5%, p = 0.08
- Mortality: 1.6% vs. 5%, p = 0.3
- Total mortality + recurrent PE: 1.6% vs. 10%, p = 0.049
- Bleeding: 0% in both groups

## Authors’ Conclusions
Low-dose thrombolysis is safe and effective in treating moderate PE, significantly reducing pulmonary artery pressure.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised trial
  - Blinded cardiologists for ECHO assessments
  - Standard method for determining pulmonary artery pressure
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre study
  - Non-blinded patients and clinicians
  - Small sample size, underpowered for mortality differences
  - Older definition of 'sub-massive' PE
  - Trial not registered on clinicaltrials.gov
  - Potential under-treatment in control group

## Bottom Line
Low-dose thrombolysis in moderate PE significantly reduces pulmonary artery systolic pressure at 28 months. Further studies are needed to assess clinical relevance and potential risks.
