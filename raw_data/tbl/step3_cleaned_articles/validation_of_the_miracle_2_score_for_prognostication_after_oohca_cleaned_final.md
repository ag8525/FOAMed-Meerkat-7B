# Validation of the MIRACLE2 Score for Prognostication After Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest

## Clinical Question
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA), how does the MIRACLE2 score compare with previously reported scoring systems for predicting poor neurological outcomes at hospital discharge?

## Background
- OOHCA has an ~8% survival rate to hospital discharge.
- Among those admitted to intensive care post-OOHCA, ~50% survive, with hypoxic brain injury being the leading cause of death.
- Current guidelines recommend prognostication after 72 hours following cardiac arrest.
- The MIRACLE2 score was developed for early prediction of neurological outcomes.
- This study aimed to externally validate the MIRACLE2 score and compare it with other scoring systems.

## Design
- Retrospective cohort study
- Single-centre
- All admissions screened for cardiac arrest ICD-10 codes; records manually reviewed
- No sample size calculation

## Setting
- Single tertiary cardiac centre, UK
- 24-hour access to coronary angiography, cardiac surgery, and specialist intensive care services
- Data collection: January 2019 – July 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with OOHCA of presumed cardiac origin who had return of spontaneous circulation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Died before hospital arrival
- Evidence of non-cardiac cause of arrest (e.g., respiratory arrest, suicide, trauma, drowning, substance overdose)
- Confirmed intracerebral haemorrhage
- Baseline neurological disability (CPC 3 or 4)
- Co-morbidity with life expectancy <6 months

**Enrollment:**
- Data from 682 patients analyzed
- Exclusions: 321 unrelated to new OHCA, 40 non-cardiac cause, 2 dead on arrival, 33 pediatric, 11 life expectancy <6 months or significant prior neurological disability, 28 not initially presented to study centre, 35 no medical information available
- 219 patients included in validation cohort
- Complete data for MIRACLE2 score available for 77% of patients; missing data for pupillary response in 18% and initial pH in 6%

## Test of Interest
- **MIRACLE2 Score:** 7 variables, maximum score of 10 points
  - Unwitnessed cardiac arrest: 1 point
  - Non-shockable initial rhythm: 1 point
  - Changing rhythm (any 2 of VF, PEA, or asystole): 1 point
  - Any adrenaline dose: 2 points
  - No pupil reactivity at ROSC: 1 point
  - Initial blood pH <7.2: 1 point
  - Age: ≤60 years: 0 points, 61-80 years: 1 point, >80 years: 2 points (note: original derivation study gave 3 points for age >80)

## Control Tests
- Cardiac Arrest Hospital Prognosis (CAHP) score
- Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (OHCA) score
- Target Temperature Management (TTM) score

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Poor neurological recovery (CPC 3-5) at hospital discharge
- Area under receiver operating curve for predicting poor neurological outcome at discharge:
  - MIRACLE2: 0.93
  - CAHP: 0.92
  - OHCA: 0.83
  - TTM: 0.94

- **Good vs Poor Neurological Outcome:**
  - Low MIRACLE2 score (≤2): 94% good vs 6% poor
  - Intermediate MIRACLE2 score (3-4): 53% good vs 47% poor
  - High MIRACLE2 score (≥5): 5% good vs 95% poor

- **MIRACLE2 score ≥5:**
  - Specificity: 97.5% (95% CI 93-99.5%)
  - Sensitivity: 62.3%
  - +ve Likelihood Ratio: 25.6 (95% CI 8.3-79)
  - -ve Likelihood Ratio: 0.38

- **MIRACLE2 score ≥3:**
  - Specificity: 68.9%
  - Sensitivity: 94.9% (95% CI 88.4-98.3%)
  - +ve Likelihood Ratio: 3.05
  - -ve Likelihood Ratio: 0.07 (95% CI 0.03-0.18)

### Secondary Outcomes
- Area under curve for predicting poor neurological outcome at 6 months:
  - MIRACLE2: 0.93
  - CAHP: 0.92
  - OHCA: 0.86
  - TTM: 0.94

## Authors’ Conclusions
The MIRACLE2 score demonstrates good prognostic performance and is easily applicable to cardiac-origin OHCA presentation at the hospital front door. Prognostic scoring may assist decision-making regarding early angiographic assessment.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Screening of all admissions
  - Compared with 3 other scoring systems
- **Weaknesses**
  - Only 64 patients included with MIRACLE2 score ≥5
  - Single-centre
  - Retrospective
  - Number of patients had missing data

## Bottom Line
The MIRACLE2 score reported high accuracy for determining neurological prognostication at admission. However, due to this being a single-centre retrospective validation cohort with only small numbers of patients that had a high MIRACLE2 score, and >20% of patients having missing data, decisions regarding treatment or escalation should not currently be made solely on the MIRACLE2 score at admission.
