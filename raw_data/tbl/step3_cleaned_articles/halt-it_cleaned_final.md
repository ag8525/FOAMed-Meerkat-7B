# Effects of a High-Dose 24-Hour Infusion of Tranexamic Acid on Death and Thromboembolic Events in Patients with Acute Gastrointestinal Bleeding (HALT-IT Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with gastrointestinal (GI) bleeding, does high-dose tranexamic acid (TXA) compared to placebo reduce mortality from GI bleeding?

## Background
- GI bleeding mortality can be as high as 10%, with a fourfold increase in risk of death if re-bleeding occurs.
- TXA, an anti-fibrinolytic, has shown benefits in early administration for traumatic and postpartum hemorrhage.
- A 2012 Cochrane Review suggested a potential mortality reduction in upper GI bleeding with TXA, but recommended further trials due to validity concerns.

## Design
- Randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial
- Randomisation via blocks, not stratified
- Outcome data collected at death, discharge, or 28 days
- Sample size recalculated mid-trial for death due to bleeding within 5 days
- Subgroup analyses planned for time to treatment, site of bleeding, variceal bleeding, and Rockall score
- Funded by UK National Institute for Health Research Health Technology Assessment Programme

## Setting
- 164 hospitals across 15 countries
- Data collection: July 2013 – June 2019

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults (age 16–18+ depending on country)
- Clinical equipoise regarding TXA benefit
- Significant bleeding risk, signs of shock, likely transfusion or urgent intervention

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- None specified

**Enrollment:**
- 12,009 patients randomised
  - TXA: 5,956
  - Placebo: 5,981
- Baseline characteristics similar across groups

## Intervention

**TXA Group:**
- 1g TXA in 100ml 0.9% saline over 1 hour
- Followed by 3g TXA in 1L isotonic solution at 125mg/hour over 24 hours

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Placebo solution with identical infusion regimen

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death due to bleeding within 5 days: no significant difference
  - TXA: 222 (3.7%)
  - Placebo: 226 (3.8%)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Death within 24 hours or 28 days
  - All-cause mortality at 28 days
  - Re-bleeding rates at 24 hours, 5 and 28 days
  - Thromboembolic events
  - Interventions or transfusions

## Authors’ Conclusions
TXA does not reduce death from GI bleeding and should not be used as a standard treatment approach for GI bleeding.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Pragmatic design for rapid trial drug access
  - High internal and external validity
  - Large international study with balanced baseline characteristics
  - Minimal loss to follow-up and protocol violations
  - Sensible subgroup analyses, especially regarding early treatment
- **Weaknesses**
  - Amendment to primary outcome late in trial
  - Heterogeneity of trial population
  - Potential bias due to clinical equipoise requirement
  - Small number of VTEs detected; study not powered for this outcome

## Bottom Line
This large, well-designed trial indicates no benefit of TXA in GI bleeding management. Future trials (e.g., PATCH) will further explore TXA's side effect profile.
