This rule was derived and validated through prospective observational diagnostic cohort studies, incorporating eight key variables such as high sensitivity troponin T and ECG ischaemia. The studies demonstrated high sensitivity (99.4% in derivation and 98% in validation) and a low negative likelihood ratio, indicating the rule's effectiveness in identifying low-risk patients who could be safely discharged. # The Manchester Acute Coronary Syndromes (MACS) Decision Rule for Suspected Cardiac Chest Pain: Derivation and External Validation

## Clinical Question
Can a clinical decision rule be derived and validated for use in the Emergency Department that allows immediate exclusion of acute coronary syndrome (ACS)?

## Design
- Prospective observational diagnostic cohort studies
- Both derivation and validation phases
- Blinded assessment of gold standard and test of interest

## Setting
- Derivation study: Single Emergency Department in the UK (January 2006 – February 2007)
- Validation study: Different single Emergency Department in the UK (April – July 2010)

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients >25 years presenting to ED within 24 hours of experiencing chest pain suspected to be cardiac in origin

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Required admission due to another medical condition
- Dialysis patients
- Pregnant individuals
- Chest trauma

**Enrollment:**
- 698 patients in derivation study
- 463 patients in validation study

## Test of Interest
- 66 variables tested; 8 included in final decision rule:
  - High sensitivity troponin T
  - Heart-type fatty acid binding protein
  - ECG ischaemia
  - Sweating observed
  - Vomiting
  - Systolic BP <100 mmHg
  - Worsening angina
  - Pain radiating to right arm/shoulder

## Gold Standard Investigation
- Acute MI diagnosed by two independent investigators based on troponin rise/fall and clinical context
- MACE (Major Adverse Cardiac Events) diagnosed by follow-up after 30 days

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- MACE within 30 days

### Test Performance
- Area under curve (AUC) for predicting MACE:
  - Derivation study: 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.97)
  - Validation study: 0.92 (95% CI: 0.89–0.95)

- Very low-risk group:
  - Zero missed MIs in both studies
  - 1 (0.4%) missed MACE in derivation study
  - 2 (1.6%) missed MACEs in validation study

- Overall sensitivity:
  - Derivation study: 99.4% (95% CI: 96.5–100)
  - Validation study: 98% (95% CI: 93–99.8)

- Negative likelihood ratio in validation study: 0.05
- Post-test probability following a negative test: 1.3%

- High-risk group:
  - 100% MACE rate in derivation study
  - 95.7% MACE rate in validation study

## Authors’ Conclusions
The proposed decision rule could identify 25% of patients for immediate discharge and identify high-risk patients effectively.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Validated in a separate centre
  - Blinding of investigators
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Appropriate gold standard investigation

- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre studies
  - Wide confidence intervals for inter-observer reliability
  - Moderate sample size in validation study leading to wider confidence intervals for sensitivity
  - Inclusion of coronary stenosis without intervention as MACE is debatable

## Bottom Line
The MACS decision rule shows promise in identifying patients with suspected cardiac chest pain who can be safely discharged. A randomised trial comparing the MACS rule with standard treatment is planned, and results are eagerly awaited.
