# Targeted Temperature Management at 33°C versus 36°C after Cardiac Arrest (TTM Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adults who suffered out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause, does induced therapeutic hypothermia targeting 36°C compared to 33°C reduce mortality or neurological deficit?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled clinical trial
- Single-blinded
- Pragmatic methodology
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 36 intensive care units in Europe and Australia
- Study period: November 2010 to January 2013

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients who were unconscious (GCS < 8) on admission after OOH cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >6 hours from ROSC to enrolment screening
- Unwitnessed arrest with systole as initial rhythm
- Hypothermia < 30°C
- Suspected or known intracranial haemorrhage

**Enrollment:**
- 950 patients enrolled

## Intervention

**TTM at 36°C Group:**
- 28 hours of temperature management targeting 36°C (using invasive or surface cooling)
- Gradual warming to 37°C at 0.5°C/hour
- Avoidance of pyrexia for 72 hours from randomisation
- 4 patients did not receive this intervention as assigned

## Control

**TTM at 33°C Group:**
- 28 hours of temperature management targeting 33°C (using invasive or surface cooling)
- Gradual warming to 37°C at 0.5°C/hour
- Avoidance of pyrexia for 72 hours from randomisation
- 3 patients did not receive this control as assigned

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in all-cause mortality through to the end of the trial (mean follow-up 256 days)
  - 36°C group: 48% died
  - 33°C group: 50% died
  - Hazard ratio for death (33°C vs 36°C): 1.06 (95% CI: 0.89–1.28), p = 0.51

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in neurological status (modified Rankin Scale or Cerebral Performance Category)

## Authors’ Conclusions
This trial does not provide evidence that targeting a body temperature of 33°C offers any benefit for unconscious patients admitted after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest compared to targeting 36°C.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Highly relevant clinical question
  - Well-designed, pragmatic methodology
  - Objective outcomes assessed by blinded external physicians
  - Intention-to-treat analysis with minimal drop-out/loss-to-follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - Not generalisable to arrests with long or unknown 'down-time'
  - Unwitnessed arrests with systole as initial rhythm were excluded
  - 90% had bystander witness; 73% had bystander CPR
  - Very short median time to basic life support (1 min) and advanced life support (9–10 min)
  - Median time to ROSC: 25 minutes
  - Short-term follow-up; unclear if 36°C confers long-term neurological benefit or harm
  - Wide confidence intervals: true hazard ratio for 33°C could range from strong benefit (0.89) to strong harm (1.28)

## Bottom Line
This trial has not shown any benefit of therapeutic hypothermia at 33°C over 36°C after OOH cardiac arrest. If cooling to 33°C causes unwanted effects, targeting 36°C instead is reasonable and probably will not affect outcomes.
