# Transfusion Strategies for Acute Upper Gastrointestinal Bleeding

## Clinical Question
In patients presenting with an upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleed, does a restrictive transfusion strategy compared to a liberal transfusion strategy affect mortality?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Computer-generated, block randomisation
- Stratified by presence of cirrhosis
- Non-blinded
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Follow-up: 45 days

## Setting
- Single hospital in Spain
- Study period: June 2003 to December 2009

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with haematemesis or malaena

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Minor or massive bleeding
- History of stroke/TIA/acute coronary syndrome/symptomatic peripheral vasculopathy within last 90 days
- Recent trauma/surgery
- Rockall score of 0 with Hb > 12 g/dL

**Enrollment:**
- 921 patients randomised from 2372 admitted with GI bleeding

## Intervention

**Restrictive Strategy Group (n=461):**
- Transfusion threshold of 7 g/dL
- Target Hb of 7-9 g/dL

## Control

**Liberal Strategy Group (n=460):**
- Transfusion threshold of 9 g/dL
- Target Hb of 9-11 g/dL

- All patients underwent emergent gastroscopy within 6 hours of presentation, alongside other appropriate interventions (e.g., somatostatin, prophylactic antibiotics, definitive management of bleeding point).

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **All-cause mortality at 45 days:**
  - Restrictive group: 5%
  - Liberal group: 9%
  - p = 0.02

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Further bleeding with haemodynamic instability or Hb drop ≥2 within 6 hours:**
  - Restrictive group: 10%
  - Liberal group: 16%
  - p = 0.01

- **Transfusion and fluid use:**
  - RBC transfusion: 49% (restrictive) vs. 86% (liberal), p < 0.0001
  - Mean units transfused: 1.5 ± 2.3 (restrictive) vs. 3.7 ± 3.8 (liberal)
  - FFP: 6% vs. 9%, p = 0.13
  - Platelets: 3% vs. 4%, p = 0.27
  - Crystalloids in first 72 hours: 5.5L vs. 5.8L, p = 0.19
  - Colloids: 19% vs. 21%, p = 0.62

- **Subgroup Analysis:**
  - All-cause mortality at 45 days:
    - Child-Pugh A or B: 4% vs. 12%, p = 0.02
    - Child-Pugh C: 38% vs. 41%, p = 0.91
    - Variceal bleeding: 11% vs. 18%, p = 0.18
    - Peptic ulcer disease: 3% vs. 5%, p = 0.26

- **Probability of Survival:**
  - Higher in restrictive strategy for PUD (HR 0.7; 95% CI 0.26-1.25)
  - Significantly higher in cirrhosis Child-Pugh A or B (HR 0.3; 95% CI 0.11-0.85)
  - No difference in cirrhosis Child-Pugh C (HR 1.04; 95% CI 0.45-2.37)

- **Portal Pressure Gradient:**
  - Increased significantly in liberal group within first 5 days, p = 0.03

- **Overall Complications:**
  - Lower in restrictive group: 40% vs. 48%, p = 0.02
  - More transfusion reactions and cardiac events in liberal group

## Authors’ Conclusions
A restrictive transfusion strategy can be standard treatment for upper GI bleeding, provided early endoscopy is feasible. The study underscores the importance of 24-hour emergency endoscopy facilities in managing upper GI bleeding.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, computer-generated allocation
  - Appropriate inclusion/exclusion criteria
  - Adequate power calculation
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-centre, non-blinded
  - >700 patients with GI bleeding not screened
  - Results may not generalise to centres without rapid endoscopy
  - Transfusion protocol violations: 9% in restrictive vs. 3% in liberal, p < 0.001
  - Use of packed RBCs alone may have caused coagulopathy in liberal group

## Bottom Line
A restrictive transfusion strategy is effective for upper GI bleeding when early endoscopy is available, highlighting the need for 24-hour emergency endoscopy services.
