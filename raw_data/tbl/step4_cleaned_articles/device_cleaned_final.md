  In a pragmatic, multicenter, unblinded randomized trial conducted across 17 sites in the USA, the use of videolaryngoscopy (VL) was compared to direct laryngoscopy (DL) for tracheal intubation in critically ill adults. The study found that VL significantly improved the first-pass success rate of intubation (85.1% for VL vs. 70.8% for DL, with an absolute risk difference of 14.3%, p < 0.001). # Video versus Direct Laryngoscopy for Tracheal Intubation of Critically Ill Adults

## Clinical Question
In critically ill adults undergoing tracheal intubation, does the use of a videolaryngoscope (VL) compared to a direct laryngoscope (DL) improve the first-pass success rate?

## Design
- Pragmatic, multicentre, unblinded, randomised, parallel-group trial
- Randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to VL or DL
- Permuted blocks of variable size, stratified by trial site
- Sequentially numbered opaque envelopes for allocation concealment
- Blinding of intubators and outcome assessors not possible
- Data collected by trained observer

## Setting
- 17 sites (10 ICUs and 7 EDs) in the USA
- Study period: March to November 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patient located in a participating unit
- Planned orotracheal intubation using a laryngoscope
- Planned operator routinely performs tracheal intubation in the unit

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age < 18 years
- Pregnancy
- Prisoner status
- Immediate need for intubation precluding study procedures
- Requirement or contraindication for either VL or DL

**Enrollment:**
- 1947 patients assessed, 1908 met inclusion criteria
- 1420 patients randomised: 707 to VL, 713 to DL
- 3 patients excluded post-randomisation due to prisoner status

## Intervention

**VL Group:**
- Videolaryngoscope at first attempt
- VL defined as a laryngoscope with a camera and video screen
- Operators instructed to view the video screen during the procedure

## Control

**DL Group:**
- Direct laryngoscope at first attempt
- DL defined as a laryngoscope without a camera or video screen

**Common Management:**
- Stylet or bougie routinely used during first attempt
- Waveform capnography or colorimetric end-tidal CO₂ detection for confirmation
- Subsequent attempts at clinician’s discretion

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Successful intubation on the first attempt:**
  - VL: 85.1%
  - DL: 70.8%
  - Absolute risk difference: 14.3% (95% CI: 9.9 to 18.7; p < 0.001)

### Secondary Outcome
- **Severe complications between induction and 2 minutes post-intubation:**
  - VL: 21.4%
  - DL: 20.9%
  - Absolute risk difference: 0.5% (95% CI: -3.9 to 4.9)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In critically ill adults undergoing tracheal intubation in an ED or ICU, videolaryngoscopy resulted in a higher first-attempt success rate compared to direct laryngoscopy.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Robust design and execution
  - Large sample size with minimal loss to follow-up
  - Minimal protocol deviations and cross-over
  - Intention-to-treat primary analysis
  - Pragmatic design enhances generalisability
- **Weaknesses**
  - Choice of blade left to intubating clinician introduces heterogeneity
  - Open-label design; blinding of outcome assessors could have been attempted
  - First-pass success is not strictly a patient-oriented outcome
  - Lack of generalisability due to skill mix and prior experience of intubators

## Bottom Line
Videolaryngoscopy improves first-pass success in critically ill patients undergoing tracheal intubation by relatively inexperienced intubators. It supports existing evidence on VL use, though it may not significantly alter practice for those already using VL.
