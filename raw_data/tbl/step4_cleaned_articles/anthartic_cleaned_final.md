  The ANTHARTIC trial investigated whether empirical antibiotics could prevent early ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) in post-out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OOHCA) patients undergoing targeted temperature management. Conducted across 16 French ICUs, this double-blind, placebo-controlled study randomized 198 patients to receive either a 2-day course of amoxicillin-clavulanate or placebo. Results indicated a significant reduction in early VAP incidence in the antibiotic group (19% vs 34%), though no differences were observed in late VAP, mortality, or ICU stay length. The study highlights the potential benefit of short-term antibiotic prophylaxis in reducing early VAP without impacting other clinical outcomes, suggesting a possible need for practice reassessment in this patient population.

# Prevention of Early Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia after Cardiac Arrest (ANTHARTIC)

## Clinical Question
In post out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients treated with targeted temperature management, does the use of empirical antibiotics prevent early ventilator-associated pneumonia?

## Background
- Survival to hospital discharge and good neurological outcomes post-OOHCA remain poor.
- Targeted temperature management aims to improve neurological outcomes but may increase secondary infection risk.

## Design
- Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- Randomisation within 6 hours after return of spontaneous circulation
- Secure, computer-generated, web-response system (1:1 ratio)
- Stratified by centre; fixed block size of four units
- Blinded patients, care providers, and adjudication committee
- Sample size: 192 patients (based on VAP incidence, mortality, and power calculations)

## Setting
- 16 ICUs in France
- August 2014 to September 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Aged >18 years
- ICU admission post-OOHCA due to shockable rhythm
- Targeted temperature management 32–34°C

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- OOHCA with non-shockable rhythm
- In-hospital cardiac arrest
- Ongoing pneumonia or gross aspiration
- Pregnancy
- Previous lung disease affecting radiograph interpretation
- Use of extracorporeal life support
- Recent antibiotic therapy
- Known chronic colonisation with multidrug-resistant bacteria
- Allergy to beta-lactam antibiotics
- Contraindication to amoxicillin or clavulanate
- Predictable decision of early limitation
- Participation in another trial within 30 days

**Enrollment:**
- 1116 screened, 198 randomised, 194 included in final analysis

## Intervention

**Antibiotic Group:**
- 2-day course of amoxicillin-clavulanate (1g/200mg IV, three times daily)

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Saline injections IV, three times daily

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Sedation protocol and neuromuscular blocking agents at clinician discretion
- Targeted temperature management 32–34°C for 24–36 hours
- Antibiotic therapy for secondary infections per local guidelines
- Routine VAP prevention bundles and daily oral care recommended
- VAP confirmed using 2010 FDA guidance (clinical, radiological, microbiological criteria)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Early VAP (within 7 days):**  
  - Lower incidence in antibiotic group (19% vs 34%; HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.31–0.92, p=0.03)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in:
  - Late VAP
  - Other nosocomial infections
  - 28-day mortality
  - Intestinal acquisition of multidrug-resistant bacteria
  - Antibiotic use outside trial intervention
  - ICU length of stay
  - Ventilator-free days until day 28

### Cost Analysis
- Lower cost in antibiotic group (data in Supplementary Material)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients treated with targeted temperature management after OOHCA with shockable rhythm, a 2-day course of amoxicillin-clavulanate reduces early VAP incidence compared to placebo.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Relevant clinical question
  - Multicentre, randomised, and blinded
  - Treatment strategies aligned with European ICU pathogen data
- **Weaknesses**
  - Key exclusions (non-shockable rhythm, suspected aspiration, lung disease)
  - Lower than expected early VAP incidence
  - Challenges in VAP diagnosis
  - 25% of VAP cases initially reported were not confirmed during adjudication
  - 78% agreement between adjudicators, requiring referee decision

## Bottom Line
This trial shows that a short course of antibiotics in OOHCA patients with shockable rhythm reduces VAP rates without affecting mortality or ICU stay length. Current practice may need review in light of these findings.
