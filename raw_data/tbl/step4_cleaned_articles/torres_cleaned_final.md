The primary outcome showed a reduction in treatment failure in the corticosteroid group, particularly in late treatment failures marked by increased pulmonary infiltrates. # Effect of Corticosteroids on Treatment Failure Among Hospitalized Patients with Severe Community-Acquired Pneumonia and High Inflammatory Response

## Clinical Question
In patients with severe community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and high inflammatory response, does the use of corticosteroids compared to placebo improve outcomes?

## Design
- Multi-centre
- Double-blind, randomised-controlled trial
- Sample size calculation: 80% power to detect a 20% absolute risk reduction in treatment failure (assuming a 35% failure rate in the placebo group)

## Setting
- 3 Spanish teaching hospitals
- Study period: June 2004 to February 2012

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age > 18 years
- Clinical symptoms suggestive of CAP
- New chest radiographic infiltrates
- Severe CAP as defined by:
  - Modified American Thoracic Society criteria for severe CAP
  - Pneumonia Severity Index risk class 4
  - C-reactive Protein (CRP) > 150 mg/L

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Prior treatment with systemic corticosteroids
- Nosocomial pneumonia
- Severe immunosuppression
- Pre-existing medical condition with life expectancy < 3 months
- Uncontrolled diabetes mellitus
- Major gastrointestinal bleed within 3 months
- Condition requiring acute treatment with > 1 mg/kg/day of methylprednisolone equivalent
- H1N1 influenza A pneumonia

**Enrollment:**
- 519 screened, 120 patients randomised (61 to intervention, 59 to control)

## Intervention

**Methylprednisolone Group:**
- 0.5 mg/kg every 12 hours for 5 days
- Commenced within 36 hours of hospital admission

## Control
- Identical placebo injection every 12 hours for 5 days
- Commenced within 36 hours of hospital admission
- Antibiotic therapy according to international guidelines

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Less overall (early + late) treatment failure in the methylprednisolone group, mainly due to fewer late treatment failures, specifically increased pulmonary infiltrates on radiographs

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in time to stability, length of stay, or mortality

## Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with severe CAP and high initial inflammatory response, the acute use of methylprednisolone compared to placebo decreased treatment failure.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, blinded, multi-centre design
  - Blinding of patients, investigators, and data assessors
  - Targeted severe CAP using recognised definitions
  - Adequate power calculation with observed baseline rate in control group approximating assumptions
- **Weaknesses**
  - Long trial duration with potential changes in practice
  - Treatment discontinued in 10% of intervention group
  - Choice and duration of antibiotics not specified
  - Unusual composite primary outcome limits clinical applicability
  - Fragility index of 2: small changes in outcomes could alter significance

## Bottom Line
This study suggests corticosteroids may reduce treatment failure in severe CAP with high inflammatory response, but small sample size and composite outcomes limit generalisability. Further research is needed before changing clinical practice.
