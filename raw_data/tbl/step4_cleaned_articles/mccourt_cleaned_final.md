  In a randomized controlled trial comparing rocuronium and suxamethonium for rapid sequence induction (RSI) of anesthesia, both agents at 1 mg/kg provided similar clinically acceptable intubating conditions, though suxamethonium was more likely to produce 'excellent' conditions. The study involved 310 patients across two centers, with exclusions for pregnancy, significant weight deviation, and anticipated difficult airways. Rocuronium at 1 mg/kg was found to be as effective as suxamethonium, with the latter showing a slight edge in achieving excellent intubating conditions. Despite the study's strengths, such as randomization and blinding, limitations included sponsorship bias, lack of patient-oriented outcomes, and unreported side effects. The findings suggest that while suxamethonium may be preferred for its superior intubating conditions, rocuronium's longer duration could be advantageous for ongoing airway management.

# Comparison of Rocuronium and Suxamethonium for Use During Rapid Sequence Induction of Anaesthesia

## Clinical Question
In patients undergoing rapid sequence induction, does the use of suxamethonium or rocuronium (at a standard or higher dose) produce better intubating conditions?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial  
- Blinding of those performing laryngoscopy (not present in room until ~45 seconds after neuromuscular blocking agent given)  
- Power calculation: 260 patients required to detect a 10% difference  
- Probability of false negative result: 20%  
- Probability of false positive result: 5%  

## Setting
- Two centres: UK and Finland  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 18-75  
- Undergoing elective or emergency surgery requiring tracheal intubation  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy  
- Weight deviated >35% from ideal weight  
- Predicted/known difficult airway  

**Enrollment:**
- 310 patients included in analysis out of 348 enrolled  

## Intervention

**Rocuronium Group:**
- Rocuronium at either 0.6 mg/kg or 1.0 mg/kg  
- After 50 patients in both groups, study continued with the dose providing better intubating conditions  

## Control

**Suxamethonium Group:**
- Suxamethonium 1 mg/kg  
- All patients received pre-oxygenation, 1-2 mcg/kg fentanyl, 3-5 mg/kg thiopentone, and cricoid pressure  
- Laryngoscopy performed 50 seconds after neuromuscular agent injection  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Clinically acceptable intubating conditions (excellent or good)  
- No significant difference between suxamethonium 1 mg/kg and rocuronium 1 mg/kg  

### Secondary Outcomes
- Frequency of good/excellent intubating conditions higher in 1 mg/kg rocuronium group vs. 0.6 mg/kg rocuronium group  
- Excellent intubating conditions more common with suxamethonium vs. rocuronium 1 mg/kg  

## Authors’ Conclusions
1 mg/kg rocuronium provides acceptable intubating conditions similar to suxamethonium.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised  
  - Blinding of outcome assessors  
  - Assessed for unmasking (fasciculations from suxamethonium observed in 5 patients)  
  - Standard induction technique  
- **Weaknesses**
  - 38 (11%) excluded due to major protocol violations or unanticipated difficult intubations  
  - Sponsored by manufacturers of rocuronium  
  - Primary outcome not patient-oriented  
  - Did not report episodes of desaturation or side effects  
  - Experience of doctor performing laryngoscopy not reported  

## Bottom Line
In patients undergoing RSI without anticipated difficult airway, 1 mg/kg suxamethonium and 1 mg/kg rocuronium produced similar clinically acceptable intubating conditions. Suxamethonium was more likely to produce 'excellent' conditions, which may influence clinician preference. Rocuronium is increasingly favored for its longer duration, potentially aiding ongoing airway management.
