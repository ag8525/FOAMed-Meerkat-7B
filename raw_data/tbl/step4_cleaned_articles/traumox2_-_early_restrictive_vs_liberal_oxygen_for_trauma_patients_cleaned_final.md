# Early Restrictive vs Liberal Oxygen for Trauma Patients (TRAUMOX2 Trial)

## Clinical Question
In adult patients experiencing blunt or penetrating trauma, does an early restrictive oxygen strategy compared to a liberal oxygen strategy reduce death and/or major respiratory complications within 30 days?

## Background
ATLS guidelines recommend supplemental oxygen for severely injured patients initially. Hyperoxaemia is common in trauma patients, but evidence supporting this practice is limited, especially regarding optimal oxygen targets in trauma.

## Design
- Investigator-initiated, international, multi-centre, parallel-group, superiority trial
- Open-label design with blinded outcome assessors
- Randomised 1:1 using sealed envelopes
- Variable block sizes (4, 6, 8)
- Stratification by site (hospital vs pre-hospital) and intubation status
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 15 pre-hospital bases and 5 major trauma centres in Denmark, Netherlands, and Switzerland
- Study period: December 2021 to September 2023

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults ≥18 years old
- Blunt or penetrating trauma
- Direct transfer to participating MTC with full trauma team activation
- Expected hospital stay >24 hours

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Traumatic cardiac arrest
- Carbon monoxide poisoning
- Discharge expected <24 hours due to no/minor injuries

**Enrollment:**
- 5441 screened, 1979 randomised
- 733 in restrictive group, 724 in liberal group included in primary analysis

## Intervention

**Restrictive Oxygen Strategy:**
- Lowest oxygen dose to maintain SpO₂ of 94%
- PaO₂ at 1 hour: 85 mmHg
- PaO₂ at 6 hours: 86 mmHg

## Control

**Liberal Oxygen Strategy:**
- Pre-hospital, trauma room, and intra-hospital: 15L/min or FiO₂ 1.0 if intubated
- In OT, ICU, or ward: ≥12L/min or FiO₂ 0.6 or higher
- PaO₂ at 1 hour: 280 mmHg
- PaO₂ at 6 hours: 230 mmHg

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death and/or major respiratory complications: no significant difference
  - Restrictive: 118/733 (16.1%)
  - Liberal: 121/724 (16.7%)
  - Risk difference: 0.56% (95% CI: -2.7 to 3.82)
  - OR: 1.01 (95% CI: 0.75–1.37)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in death or major respiratory complications
- Exploratory outcomes showed increased hypoxaemic episodes and decreased surgical site infections in the restrictive group

## Authors’ Conclusions
A restrictive oxygen strategy compared to a liberal one for 8 hours did not reduce death and/or major respiratory complications within 30 days.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised trial with allocation concealment
  - Large pre-hospital patient cohort
  - Clear separation between groups
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Low protocol violation rates
- **Weaknesses**
  - Post-randomisation exclusions may introduce bias
  - Open-label design
  - Lower observed event rates than expected
  - Short intervention duration (8 hours) may not show effects
  - Potential contamination bias due to prior oxygen exposure

## Bottom Line
Targeting normoxia (SpO₂ 94-98%) in the early phase for patients with moderate to severe trauma seems reasonable based on this trial.
