# Supraclavicular versus Infraclavicular Approach for Ultrasound-Guided Right Subclavian Venous Catheterisation: A Randomised Controlled Non-Inferiority Trial

## Clinical Question
In patients undergoing elective neurosurgery, is a supraclavicular in-plane ultrasound-guided approach non-inferior to an infraclavicular out-of-plane ultrasound-guided approach for catheterisation-related complications?

## Background
- Central venous lines are commonly placed in the internal jugular, femoral, and subclavian veins.
- Subclavian lines have a lower infection risk but a higher pneumothorax risk.
- Previous RCTs suggest the supraclavicular approach is quicker with similar success and fewer complications than the infraclavicular approach.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Non-inferiority trial
- Computer-generated random allocation sequence (1:1 ratio)
- Allocation concealment with sealed opaque envelopes
- Sample size: 416 patients (accounting for a 5% drop-out rate)

## Setting
- Single centre: Seoul National University Hospital
- Study period: September 2018 to May 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 20-79 years
- Required central venous catheterisation
- Undergoing elective general anaesthesia for neurosurgery

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Infection or medical device at puncture site
- Right subclavian venous thrombosis
- Haemostatic disorders or anticoagulant use
- Previous surgery affecting right subclavian vein anatomy

**Enrollment:**
- 416 patients randomised
- 401 patients included in analysis after exclusions

## Intervention

**Supraclavicular Approach:**
- Long-axis in-plane ultrasound

## Control

**Infraclavicular Approach:**
- Short-axis out-of-plane ultrasound

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Central line insertion post-induction of general anaesthesia
- Bed in flat position, pillow removed, head, neck, and arms in neutral position
- Vscan™ portable ultrasound machine used
- Performed by one of four anaesthetists experienced in both techniques
- Mechanical ventilation paused during venous puncture

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Catheterisation-related complications (e.g., misplacement, arterial puncture, haematoma, pneumothorax, haemothorax)
  - Supraclavicular: 3%
  - Infraclavicular: 13.4%
  - Mean difference: -10.4% (95% CI: -15.7 to -5.1%), OR 0.20 (95% CI: 0.08-0.49), p < 0.001

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Overall success: 99.5% vs 99.5%
  - Success at first attempt: 87% vs 88%
  - Time for catheterisation: 79 vs 78 seconds
  - Mechanical complications: 2% vs 3%
  - Arterial puncture: 2% vs 1.5%
  - Pneumothorax: 0% vs 1%
  - Rescue ventilation: 14.5% vs 12.4%
- Significantly fewer catheter misplacements in supraclavicular group: 1% vs 10.4%
  - Mean difference: -9.4% (95% CI: -13.9 to -5%), OR 0.09 (95% CI: 0.02-0.37), p < 0.001
- Time for venous puncture: 9 vs 13 seconds

## Authors’ Conclusions
The supraclavicular approach is superior for ultrasound-guided right subclavian venous catheterisation, with fewer complications, particularly catheter misplacement.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Multi-operator with experienced practitioners
  - Maintained allocation concealment
- **Weaknesses**
  - Different ultrasound techniques used (in-plane vs out-of-plane) may confound results
  - Non-inferiority study not designed to assess superiority
  - Single-centre study in South Korea
  - Limited to mechanically ventilated neurosurgery patients; not generalisable
  - Blinding not possible for line insertion
  - 3.6% of randomised patients not included in analysis

## Bottom Line
In elective neurosurgery patients, the supraclavicular approach with in-plane ultrasound is non-inferior to the infraclavicular approach with out-of-plane ultrasound, with significantly fewer catheter-related complications, mainly due to reduced misplacement.
