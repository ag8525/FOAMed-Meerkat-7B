# Chlorhexidine-Impregnated Dressing for Prevention of Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection: A Meta-Analysis

## Clinical Question
Does chlorhexidine-impregnated dressing compared to conventional site care prevent catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI) and catheter colonisation?

## Design
- Meta-analysis of prospective randomised controlled trials
- Search strategy included PubMed, Medline, Embase, and Web of Science
- Keywords: "chlorhexidine, dressing, sponge, central venous catheter, arterial catheter, bacteremia, bloodstream infection"
- Data extraction by three independent investigators
- Study quality assessed per Cochrane Handbook of Systematic Reviews

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Prospective randomised controlled trials
- Microbiologically based definition for CRBSI
- Systematic reporting of CRBSI prevalence in both intervention and control groups

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Case-control, retrospective, non-randomised studies

**Enrollment:**
- Primary search yielded 505 citations; 9 studies included
- Study populations: 4 adult ICU, 1 paediatric ICU, 1 neonatal ICU, 2 haematology/oncology, 1 unspecified adult

## Intervention
- Chlorhexidine-impregnated dressing for intravascular catheters

## Control
- Conventional site care for intravascular catheters
- Occlusive dressing in 8 studies, no dressing in 1 study

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Catheter-related bloodstream infections:** Significantly less in chlorhexidine group (1.1%) vs. control (2.1%)

### Secondary Outcome
- **Catheter colonisation:** Significantly less in chlorhexidine group
- Sub-group analysis showed significant reduction in haematological malignancy and adult ICU populations, but not in paediatric populations or long-term catheters (>2 weeks)

**Other Outcomes:**
- No data on chlorhexidine resistance
- Contact dermatitis was the most common adverse event

## Authors’ Conclusions
Chlorhexidine-impregnated dressing significantly reduces catheter colonisation and CRBSI. It may not be beneficial for long-term catheters (>2 weeks).

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Authors have established expertise in the field
  - Appropriate primary and secondary outcome measures
- **Weaknesses**
  - Lack of robust search strategy
  - Unclear risk of bias assessment; none of the studies were double-blinded
  - Significant clinical and statistical heterogeneity among included studies
  - Limited generalisability to centres with low baseline CRBSI rates

## Bottom Line
This meta-analysis suggests chlorhexidine-impregnated dressings may reduce CRBSI, but the findings are limited by significant heterogeneity. For adult ICU patients, the results are less applicable due to variability in baseline CRBSI rates and skin preparation methods.
