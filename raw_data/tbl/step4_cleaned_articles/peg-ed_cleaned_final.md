# Diagnosis of Pulmonary Embolism with d-Dimer Adjusted to Clinical Probability (PEG-eD Study)

## Clinical Question
In low and moderate-risk patients presenting to the Emergency Department with symptoms suggestive of pulmonary embolism (PE), can a higher d-dimer threshold be used to safely exclude PE without the need for imaging?

## Background
- Diagnostic testing for PE in the ED typically involves clinical assessment, biomarker levels, and imaging.
- A d-dimer cut-off of <500 ng/mL is commonly used to exclude PE in low-risk patients.
- Moderate-risk patients often undergo imaging rather than relying on d-dimer.
- Imaging, such as CTPA, exposes patients to radiation and potential contrast allergies, and can increase ED length of stay.
- Previous studies (ADJUST-PE, YEARS) suggested adjusted d-dimer thresholds based on age or pre-test probability could safely exclude PE.
- The PEG-eD study aimed to evaluate if a d-dimer cut-off twice the usual threshold could safely exclude PE, including in moderate-risk patients.

## Design
- Prospective, multi-centre cohort study
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Sample size calculated to provide 90% power to exclude a 2.0% VTE rate at 90 days if ruled out by the PEG-eD algorithm.

## Setting
- University-based clinical centres in Canada
- Study period: December 2015 to May 2018

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Outpatients with symptoms or signs suggestive of PE

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Age <18 years
- Full-dose anticoagulant therapy for >24 hours
- Major surgery in the past 21 days
- Known d-dimer level before C-PTP assessment
- Chest imaging contrary to protocol
- Contrast-enhanced CT for other reasons
- Ongoing need for anticoagulant therapy
- Life expectancy <3 months
- Pregnant or geographically inaccessible for follow-up

**Enrollment:**
- 2017 patients included in the final analysis
- Baseline demographics: mean age 52 years, 66.2% female
- C-PTP distribution: 86.9% low, 10.8% moderate, 2.3% high

## Intervention
- Use of the PEG-eD algorithm for all patients
- Patients with low C-PTP and d-dimer <1000 ng/mL, and moderate C-PTP and d-dimer <500 ng/mL, underwent no further testing.
- All other patients, including high C-PTP, underwent chest imaging (mainly CTPA).

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No symptomatic, objectively verified VTE at 90-day follow-up in patients with low or moderate C-PTP and d-dimer below the threshold.
- 149/2017 (7.4%) diagnosed with PE.
- Of 1970 patients with low/moderate C-PTP, 1325 (67.3%) had negative d-dimer and did not receive anticoagulant therapy; none had VTE at follow-up (95% CI: 0.00 to 0.29%).

### Secondary Outcomes
- 1 patient (0.05%; 95% CI: 0.01 to 0.30) with low C-PTP, positive d-dimer, and negative CTPA findings had VTE at follow-up.
- Chest imaging reduced from 51.9% to 34.3% with PEG-eD algorithm (difference -17.6%; 95% CI: -19.2 to -15.9).
- 7 major bleeding episodes, 23 minor bleeding episodes, and 34 deaths during follow-up; none attributed to PE.

## Authors’ Conclusions
Ruling out PE in patients with low C-PTP and d-dimer <1000 ng/mL is low-risk. This strategy is also appropriate for excluding PE in moderate C-PTP patients with d-dimer <500 ng/mL.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Addresses a common ED presentation and clinical dilemma.
  - Power calculation for precision of estimate.
  - Used internationally recognized Wells scoring system.
  - Consistent with prior studies.
  - 90-day follow-up ensured capture of most VTEs.
  - Intention-to-treat analysis.
  - Likely reasonable external validity in USA, Europe, and Australia.
  - Outcome events evaluated by blinded central adjudication committee.
- **Weaknesses**
  - Overall PE rate lower than expected in ED setting.
  - Only 13% of patients were moderate/high-risk for PE.
  - Observational study, potential for selection bias.
  - Discrepancy in numbers between figure and table.
  - No direct comparison group of usual practice.
  - Only 315 patients directly relevant to higher d-dimer threshold question.

## Bottom Line
Using a d-dimer cut-off adjusted for clinical probability appears safe to reduce diagnostic imaging in low-risk PE patients. This approach should be adopted by EDs for suspected PE work-up.
