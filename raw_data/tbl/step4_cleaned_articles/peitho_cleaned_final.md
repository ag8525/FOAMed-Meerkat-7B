# Fibrinolysis for Patients with Intermediate-Risk Pulmonary Embolism (PEITHO Trial)

## Clinical Question
In patients with intermediate-risk/sub-massive pulmonary embolism (PE), does the addition of tenecteplase, compared with heparin alone, improve the composite outcome of death and haemodynamic decompensation?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Double-blinded
- Stratified by centre

## Setting
- 76 sites in 13 countries
- Study period: November 2007 to July 2012

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with PE confirmed by V/Q scan, CT, or pulmonary angiogram
- Right Ventricular (RV) dysfunction confirmed by ECHO or CT
- Raised Troponin I/T

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Onset of symptoms >15 days prior
- Contraindication to thrombolysis
- Haemodynamic decompensation requiring CPR
- BP <90 mmHg for >15 min or drop of systolic BP >40 mmHg for >15 min with signs of end-organ hypoperfusion
- Need for catecholamine infusion

**Enrollment:**
- 1006 patients randomised

## Intervention

**Thrombolysis Group:**
- Tenecteplase (30-50 mg bolus according to body weight) + heparin

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Placebo + heparin

**Heparin Administration:**
- Unfractionated heparin given as a bolus followed by infusion
- Adjustments made if patient had prior heparin, LMWH, or fondaparinux

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death or haemodynamic decompensation within 7 days:
  - Thrombolysis: 2.6%
  - Placebo: 5.6%
  - Odds ratio: 0.44 (95% CI: 0.23–0.87), p = 0.02, NNT = 34

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Mortality at 7 days and 30 days:**
  - 7 days: 1.2% vs. 1.8% (OR 0.65, 95% CI: 0.23–1.85, p = 0.42)
  - 30 days: 2.4% vs. 3.2% (OR 0.73, 95% CI: 0.34–1.57, p = 0.42)

- **Haemodynamic decompensation:**
  - Thrombolysis: 1.6%
  - Placebo: 5%
  - Odds ratio: 0.3 (95% CI: 0.14–0.68), p = 0.002, NNT = 30

- **Major extracranial bleeding:**
  - Thrombolysis: 6.3%
  - Placebo: 1.2%
  - Odds ratio: 5.55 (95% CI: 2.3–13.39), p < 0.001, NNH = 19

- **Haemorrhagic stroke:**
  - Thrombolysis: 2%
  - Placebo: 0.2%
  - p = 0.003, NNH = 55

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with intermediate-risk PE, thrombolysis prevented haemodynamic decompensation but increased the risk of major bleeding and stroke.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, double-blinded, multi-centre trial
  - Large sample size with appropriate power calculation
- **Weaknesses**
  - Composite outcome of death and haemodynamic decompensation may not be clinically meaningful
  - Low incidence of haemodynamic decompensation questions its clinical relevance
  - Not powered to determine mortality benefit
  - Long inclusion period (up to 15 days) may affect risk-benefit balance
  - Higher pre-randomisation use of LMWH/fondaparinux in the tenecteplase group
  - Funded by Boehringer, but no influence on trial conduct

## Bottom Line
In patients with sub-massive PE, tenecteplase offers modest haemodynamic benefits but no mortality reduction at 30 days. Its use is associated with increased risks of major bleeding and intracranial haemorrhage, questioning its routine use in this setting.
