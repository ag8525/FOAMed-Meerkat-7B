  In the management of acute intermediate high-risk pulmonary embolism (PE) with right heart strain, a study compared the effects of volume expansion versus diuretic therapy on troponin levels and other cardiac biomarkers. The randomized controlled trial found no significant difference in the time to normalisation of troponin levels between the two groups. However, diuretic therapy, specifically intravenous furosemide, was associated with a faster reduction in BNP levels and a greater decrease in systolic pulmonary artery pressure and inferior vena cava diameter, suggesting potential benefits in right ventricular function. The study highlights the complexity of managing PE with right heart strain and suggests a tailored approach that may include diuretics, fluids, and other supportive therapies.

# Diuretics Versus Volume Expansion in the Initial Management of Acute Intermediate High-Risk Pulmonary Embolism

## Clinical Question
In adult patients with acute pulmonary embolism and right heart strain, does a strategy of volume expansion or early diuretic therapy result in a more rapid resolution of troponin levels?

## Background
- Acute pulmonary embolism (PE) is a common cause for hospital presentation.
- Right heart strain is associated with increased mortality in patients with PE.
- Current ESC 2019 guidelines recommend consideration of ‘cautious fluid loading’ if CVP is low.
- Right ventricular (RV) dilatation may compress the left ventricle, compromising cardiac output, potentially ameliorated by diuretic therapy.

## Design
- Multi-centre, unblinded parallel group randomised controlled trial
- 1:1 randomisation
- Written informed consent obtained
- Study powered to 90% to demonstrate ‘superiority of diuretics’ in normalising troponin at 72 hours
- 44 patients required, 60 recruited to offset study exits
- TTE performed at admission, 4 hours post-treatment, and daily until normalisation or post-ICU discharge
- Troponin and BNP measured on admission, then every 12 hours until normalisation
- Maximum time delay from definitive diagnosis to randomisation: 2 hours; from randomisation to treatment: 1 hour
- All patients followed for at least 30 days
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov (NCT02531581)

## Setting
- Multi-centre study (number of centres not reported)
- Authors from two hospitals in Cannes and Nice
- Study period: January 2016 to December 2019

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with acute pulmonary embolism confirmed on CT and all of:
  - RV Dilatation (RV:LV ratio > 0.9 in apical view or >0.7 in parasternal long axis)
  - RV dysfunction (TAPSE < 16mm and RV S’ < 10mm/s)
  - Troponin (Tn) ≥ 70 ng/L and BNP > 100 pg/mL

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Cardiogenic shock
- Need for thrombolysis or catecholamine infusion
- Cardiopulmonary resuscitation on admission
- Severe chronic renal disease (GFR < 30 ml/min)
- IVC diameter < 21 mm
- Prior fluid or diuretic therapy within 24 hours
- Chronic diuretic use

**Enrollment:**
- 181 patients assessed for eligibility
- 121 excluded (120 not meeting criteria, 1 declined consent)
- 60 randomised (30 volume expansion, 30 diuretic therapy)

## Intervention

**Volume Expansion Group:**
- 500 ml saline infusion over 4 hours
- Followed by 1000 ml over 24 hours

**Diuretic Therapy Group:**
- IV Furosemide bolus 40 mg
- Additional 40 mg bolus if urine output < 500 ml in 4 hours

**Common Management:**
- Immediate therapeutic anticoagulation
- Thrombolysis if indicated by hypotension or cardiogenic shock

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in time to normalisation of troponin concentrations (< 70 ng/L): 72 vs 76 hours (p = 0.74)

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Biomarkers:**
  - Longer time to BNP normalisation in volume expansion group: 108 vs 56 hours (p = 0.05)
  - Shorter time to 50% BNP reduction in diuretic group; greater proportion with reduction at 12 hours (47% vs 13%)

- **ECHO Features:**
  - Greater reduction in systolic pulmonary artery pressure at 4 hours in diuretic group: 0 vs -7 mmHg (p = 0.006)
  - Greater reduction in IVC diameter at 4 hours: 0 vs -3 mm (p = 0.008)
  - No significant difference in TAPSE or RV S’ at 4, 24, or 48 hours

## Authors’ Conclusions
A single intravenous bolus of 40 mg furosemide was well-tolerated. Compared with volume expansion, intravenous diuretic therapy does not modify troponin kinetics or RV echocardiographic parameters but accelerates BNP normalisation and reduces sPAP and IVC diameter significantly. These findings may translate to rapid improvement in RV function with diuretics, pending confirmation in trials with clinical endpoints.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-designed phase 2 trial addressing a challenging clinical question
  - Detailed ECHO and biomarker data obtained
  - No loss to follow-up
  - Good separation between groups regarding urine output
- **Weaknesses**
  - Limited methodology details hinder assessment of internal validity
  - Phase 2 trial; further studies needed before clinical implementation
  - Normalisation of biomarkers may not equate to improved prognosis
  - Conclusion on RV function improvement not directly supported by trial data
  - No cardiac output measurement, which could have been informative

## Bottom Line
Routine ECHO in PE patients shows high prevalence of RV dysfunction (33% in this study). Diuretics likely reduce right atrial and pulmonary artery pressures but unclear if this changes outcomes. A tailored approach to maintain cardiac output in PE patients is recommended, potentially involving fluid or diuretics, inodilators, vasodilators, and anticoagulants.
