  The PREOXYFLOW study, a multicenter randomized controlled trial, evaluated the efficacy of high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) oxygen therapy compared to high fraction-inspired oxygen facial mask (HFFM) for preoxygenation in hypoxemic patients requiring intubation in the ICU. # High-flow Nasal Cannula Oxygen during Endotracheal Intubation in Hypoxemic Patients: A Randomised Controlled Clinical Trial (PREOXYFLOW)

## Clinical Question
Does high-flow therapy by nasal cannula (HFNC) improve the efficiency of preoxygenation before intubation compared with a high fraction-inspired oxygen facial mask (HFFM)?

## Design
- Multicenter, randomised controlled trial
- Open-labelled and parallel
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Randomisation: computer-generated using fixed blocks of four patients (1:1 ratio), stratified by centre

## Setting
- 6 French ICUs (3 medical, 2 medical-surgical, 1 surgical)
- Study period: December 2012 to August 2013

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18 years or older
- Acute hypoxaemic respiratory failure requiring intubation in ICU
  - Respiratory rate > 30/min
  - FiO₂ requirement ≥ 50% to maintain SpO₂ ≥ 90%
  - Estimated PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio ≤ 300 mmHg within 4 hours before inclusion

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Contraindications to orotracheal intubation
- Intubation without anaesthetic rapid sequence induction
- Requirement for intubation due to cardiac arrest or asphyxia
- Nasopharyngeal difficulties
- Grade 4 Cormack-Lehane view
- Pregnancy

**Enrollment:**
- 119 patients

## Intervention

**HFNC Group:**
- Preoxygenation with high-flow nasal cannula (Optiflow™) for 4 minutes
- 60 L/min of humidified oxygen flow (FiO₂ 100%)
- Nasal cannula maintained throughout endotracheal intubation

## Control

**HFFM Group:**
- Preoxygenation with high FiO₂ facial mask for 4 minutes
- 15 L/min oxygen flow
- Facemask removed after rapid sequence induction for laryngoscopy

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Lowest SpO₂ during endotracheal intubation (ETI) procedure
  - HFNC: 91.5% [80-96]
  - HFFM: 89.5% [81-95]
  - p = 0.44

### Secondary Outcomes
- No statistical difference in secondary outcomes, except for duration of mechanical ventilation
- Categories: preoxygenation, ETI procedure, respiratory outcome, morbidity in ICU
- Adverse events during intubation and the following hour: no statistical difference
  - Severe complications: HFNC 58.1% vs HFFM 66.6%
  - Desaturation < 80%: HFNC 25.8% vs HFFM 22.8%
  - Cardiovascular collapse: HFNC 38.7% vs HFFM 52.6%
  - One cardiac arrest in HFFM group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Compared to HFFM, HFNC as a preoxygenation device did not reduce the lowest level of desaturation.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - External observer and nurse concurrently assessed the period from laryngoscopy to mechanical ventilator connection
- **Weaknesses**
  - Study may be underpowered; based on a previous single study comparing NIV to HFFM
  - Small sample size (119 patients)
  - Unclear statistical analysis choice; Van Elteren analysis used for stratification by medical centres
  - Unspecified high FiO₂ facial mask type
  - High NIV use prior to randomisation could affect results
  - Secondary outcomes dependent on operator variability
  - Large difference in urgency of intubation between groups
  - Lack of arterial oxygen measurement; reliance on SpO₂
  - No muscle relaxant doses recorded

## Bottom Line
In this study, HFNC was not more effective than HFFM at preventing desaturation during RSI. Methodological and statistical concerns suggest a larger RCT is needed for conclusive results.
