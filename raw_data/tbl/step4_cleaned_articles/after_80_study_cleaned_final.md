summary: |
  The After Eighty Study investigates the efficacy of an early invasive strategy versus a conservative approach in patients aged 80 years or older with non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or unstable angina. Conducted as a randomized controlled trial across 16 hospitals in Norway, the study found that an invasive strategy significantly reduced the composite outcome of myocardial infarction, urgent revascularization, stroke, and death compared to conservative management, with a hazard ratio of 0.53 and a number needed to treat of 5. The invasive approach involved early coronary angiography and potential percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), while the conservative strategy relied on optimal medical treatment with angiography reserved for specific complications. The study highlights the benefit of invasive management in reducing myocardial infarction and urgent revascularization, though no significant advantage was observed in patients over 90 years. The trial's strengths include its randomized design and intention-to-treat analysis, while limitations involve the exclusion of many patients due to logistical issues and the use of a composite outcome measure.

# Invasive versus Conservative Strategy in Patients Aged 80 Years or Older with Non-ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction or Unstable Angina Pectoris (After Eighty Study)

## Clinical Question
In patients aged 80 years or older with unstable angina or non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), does an early invasive strategy compared with a conservative strategy reduce the composite outcome of myocardial infarction, need for urgent revascularisation, stroke, and death?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Permuted block randomisation
- Stratified by hospital
- Use of sealed opaque envelopes
- Non-blinded
- Consecutive patients
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Median follow-up: 1.53 years
- Power calculation: 412 patients required for 80% power to detect a 10% absolute risk reduction from a baseline risk of 21%, with a 5% false positive rate

## Setting
- 16 academic and teaching hospitals without percutaneous coronary intervention facilities in Norway
- December 2010 – February 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Aged 80 years or older
- NSTEMI/unstable angina
- With or without ST depression
- Normal or raised Troponin T/I
- Assessed for inclusion within 2 days of hospital admission

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Unstable with continuing chest pain, ischaemic symptoms/signs, cardiogenic shock, bleeding problems
- Life expectancy <12 months due to serious comorbidity
- Substantial mental disorder, including severe dementia

**Enrollment:**
- 457 randomised out of 4187 patients assessed
- 2214 met exclusion criteria
- 1516 candidates who met inclusion not included (402 refused, 1011 logistical issues)

## Intervention

**Early Invasive Strategy:**
- Transport to Oslo University Hospital 1 day after inclusion
- Early coronary angiography with immediate assessment for PCI, CABG, or optimal medical treatment
- Return to local hospitals after:
  - 6-18 hours if PCI performed
  - 4-6 hours if angiography alone
- Angiography not performed in 9 patients (4%)
- PCI performed in 107 patients (47%)
- CABG performed in 6 patients (3%)

## Control

**Conservative Strategy:**
- Optimal medical treatment
- Remained in community hospital
- Considered for urgent coronary angiography if reinfarction, refractory angina, malignant arrhythmias, or worsening heart failure
- All patients treated according to existing guidelines

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Composite of myocardial infarction, need for urgent revascularisation, stroke, and death significantly reduced in invasive group
  - 40.6% vs. 61.4%, HR 0.53 (95% CI 0.41–0.69), p=0.0001, NNT = 5
  - Fragility index: 26 patients

### Secondary Outcomes
- Myocardial infarction: 17% vs. 30%, HR 0.52 (95% CI 0.35–0.76), p=0.001
- Need for urgent revascularisation: 2% vs. 11%, HR 0.19 (95% CI 0.07–0.52), p=0.001
- Stroke: 3% vs. 6%, HR 0.60 (95% CI 0.25–1.46), p=0.27
- Death from any cause: 25% vs. 27%, HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.62–1.28), p=0.53
- Complications: Major bleeding 2% vs. 2%, Minor bleeding 10% vs. 7%
- Length of hospital stay: 6 days vs. 5 days

### Subgroup Analysis
- Age <90 years: Significantly reduced in invasive group, HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.35–0.62; p=0.0001)
- Age >90 years (n=34): No significant difference, HR 1.21 (95% CI 0.53–2.7; p=0.64)

## Authors’ Conclusions
The study supports an invasive strategy in clinically stable patients aged 80 years or older with NSTEMI/unstable angina. In patients older than 90 years, the evidence is unclear.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Listed on clinicaltrials.gov
  - Patients quickly returned to community hospitals post-intervention, reducing bias from differences in medical management
  - Multi-centre
- **Weaknesses**
  - Large numbers of patients excluded for logistical reasons
  - Use of composite outcome; need for urgent revascularisation included, but risk of death or severe morbidity may be more relevant

## Bottom Line
In patients over 80 years with NSTEMI/unstable angina, an invasive approach reduces the composite outcome of death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or need for urgent revascularisation, mainly due to reduced myocardial infarction and urgent revascularisation. In patients over 90 years, no benefit was found, but caution is needed due to low numbers.
