# Effect of Treatment With Low Doses of Hydrocortisone and Fludrocortisone on Mortality in Patients With Septic Shock

## Clinical Question
In patients with septic shock and relative adrenal deficiency, do low-dose corticosteroids improve 28-day mortality?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Double-blind
- Parallel group
- Block randomisation stratified by centre
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 19 ICUs in France
- November 1995 to February 1999

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with acute septic shock, defined by:
  - Documented or suspected infection
  - Temperature >38.3°C or <35.6°C
  - Heart rate >90 bpm
  - Blood pressure <90 mmHg for >1 hour despite fluid resuscitation and vasopressors
  - Urinary output <0.5 ml/kg/hr or PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio <280 mmHg
  - Arterial lactate >2 mmol/L
  - Need for mechanical ventilation within 8 hours of shock onset

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy, acute myocardial infarction, pulmonary embolism, advanced cancer, AIDS, contraindication to steroids
- Recent etomidate use (within 6 hours prior to randomisation)

**Enrollment:**
- 300 patients randomised
- All underwent short corticotropin test
- Relative adrenal insufficiency defined as a response of ≤9 μg/dL

## Intervention

**Steroid Group:**
- Hydrocortisone 50 mg IV every 6 hours for 1 week
- Fludrocortisone 50 μg once daily for 1 week

## Control

**Placebo Group:**
- Placebo treatment

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- 28-day survival distribution in non-responders to corticotropin test:
  - Significant improvement in steroid group
  - Median time to death: 12 days (placebo) vs 24 days (steroid)
  - Hazard ratio: 0.67 (95% CI: 0.47–0.95), p=0.02
  - NNT: 7 (95% CI: 4–49)

### Secondary Outcomes
- 28-day mortality in non-responders: 63% (placebo) vs 53% (steroid), p=0.04
- No significant difference in responders or overall population
- Median time to vasopressor withdrawal in non-responders: 10 days (placebo) vs 7 days (steroid), p=0.001
- No significant difference in infection or gastrointestinal bleeding rates

## Authors’ Conclusions
Low-dose hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone reduce the risk of death in patients with septic shock and relative adrenal insufficiency without increasing adverse events.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, double-blind, multi-centre trial
  - High rate of appropriate antibiotic use
- **Weaknesses**
  - Primary outcome (survival distribution) not ideal; 28-day mortality would be more informative
  - 28-day mortality significance only after adjustment for baseline characteristics
  - 24% received etomidate, affecting adrenal function
  - Delayed antibiotic administration compared to current standards
  - Limited external validity due to small sample size

## Differences Between Annane and CORTICUS Trials
- Annane found improved mortality with steroids in septic shock with adrenal deficiency, unlike CORTICUS
- CORTICUS had lower power and included less severe cases

## Bottom Line
Conflicting results from major studies on steroids in septic shock. Routine use not recommended, but may be considered in high-risk, non-responding patients. Awaiting results from the ADRENAL study for further guidance.
