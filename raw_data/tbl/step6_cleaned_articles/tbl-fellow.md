  The FELLOW trial investigated whether apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy could improve the lowest arterial oxygen saturation in critically ill ICU patients undergoing intubation. Conducted as a single-centre, randomized controlled trial at Vanderbilt University Medical Center, the study included 150 patients and compared high-flow nasal cannula oxygenation to usual care. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in the lowest SpO₂ between the two groups, with secondary outcomes also indicating no significant differences in hypoxemia incidence, desaturation, or changes in saturation from baseline. The study concluded that apneic oxygenation did not enhance oxygen saturation during intubation, though it was a pragmatic trial reflecting real-world conditions. Despite its strengths, the trial's small size and single-centre design limit its external validity.

# Randomized Trial of Apneic Oxygenation during Endotracheal Intubation of the Critically Ill (FELLOW Trial)

## Clinical Point
In ICU patients undergoing intubation, does apneic oxygenation during laryngoscopy increase the lowest arterial oxygen saturation experienced by patients?

## Design
- Single-centre, randomised controlled trial  
- Permuted block randomisation  
- Allocation concealment with sequentially-numbered opaque envelopes  
- Treating clinicians non-blinded; data collection by independent observers  
- Pragmatic design  
- Intention-to-treat analysis  

## Setting
- Medical ICU at Vanderbilt University Medical Center  
- Study period: February 2014 to February 2015  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged 18 years or older  
- Undergoing intubation by a pulmonary and critical care medicine fellow in the ICU  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Awake intubation  
- Urgent intubation precluding randomisation  
- Specific intra-procedural oxygenation or laryngoscopy device mandated  

**Enrollment:**
- 196 patients met inclusion criteria  
- 150 patients randomised (73 to usual care, 77 to apneic oxygenation)  
- 46 excluded (23 urgent intubation, 18 required specific intubation method, 5 other reasons)  

## Intervention

**Apneic Oxygenation Group:**
- High-flow nasal cannula at 15 L/min of 100% O₂ placed before induction and maintained until intubation completion  
- Additional oxygen delivery methods allowed until laryngoscopy  

## Control
- Usual care with oxygen delivery methods at clinician's discretion until laryngoscopy  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in lowest arterial oxygen saturation (SpO₂) between induction and two minutes post-intubation  
  - Median lowest SpO₂: 92% (IQR 84-99%) [usual care] vs 90% (IQR 80-96%) [apneic O₂]; p = 0.16  

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:  
  - Incidence of hypoxemia (SpO₂ <90%): 47.2% [usual] vs 44.7% [apneic O₂]; p = 0.87  
  - Incidence of severe hypoxemia (SpO₂ <80%): 25% [usual] vs 15.8% [apneic O₂]; p = 0.22  
  - Incidence of desaturation (SpO₂ decrease >3%): 55.6% [usual] vs 53.9% [apneic O₂]; p = 0.87  
  - Change in saturation from baseline: 4.5% [usual] vs 4% [apneic O₂]; p = 0.6  

### Tertiary Outcomes
- Duration of mechanical ventilation: 3 days [usual] vs 3 days [apneic O₂]; p = 0.73  
- ICU length of stay: 7 days [usual] vs 4 days [apneic O₂]; p = 0.24  
- In-hospital mortality: 49% [usual] vs 35% [apneic O₂]; p = 0.1  
- Time from induction to secured airway: 150 seconds [usual] vs 132 seconds [apneic O₂]; p = 0.31  

## Authors’ Conclusions
Apneic oxygenation does not increase the lowest arterial oxygen saturation during endotracheal intubation of critically ill patients compared to usual care.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Pragmatic study design reflecting real-world critical care intubations  
  - Focus on a low-cost, potentially effective intervention  
  - Intention-to-treat analysis with consistent results in multivariate analysis  
  - Data collectors blinded to study hypothesis  

- **Weaknesses**
  - Small trial with surrogate primary outcome  
  - Single-centre study limiting external validity  
  - Exclusion of patients requiring specific intubation methods may have omitted sicker patients  
  - Majority received supplemental oxygen, limiting applicability to those contraindicated  

## Bottom Line
In this medical ICU setting, apneic oxygenation did not improve lowest arterial oxygen saturation compared to usual care during intubation by experienced fellows. No harm was demonstrated.
