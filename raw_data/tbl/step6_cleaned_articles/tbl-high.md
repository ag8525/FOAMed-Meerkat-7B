# High-Sensitivity Troponin in the Evaluation of Patients with Suspected Acute Coronary Syndrome: A Stepped-Wedge, Cluster-Randomised Controlled Trial

## Clinical Point
In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS), does the use of a high-sensitivity troponin assay reduce subsequent myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death at 12 months compared to a standard troponin assay?

## Background
Despite the use of biomarkers for myocardial injury, some patients with significant ACS are discharged from the ED. High-sensitivity troponin assays may identify more patients for treatment, potentially preventing future coronary events. However, increased sensitivity might reduce specificity, leading to more testing without patient-centered benefits.

## Design
- Stepped-wedge, cluster-randomised controlled trial
- Two phases: validation (6 months) and implementation (6-24 months)
- Sites randomly allocated to early or late implementation of high-sensitivity troponin assay
- Sex-specific thresholds used
- Independent review of clinical information by masked physicians
- Complete follow-up ensured through regional and national registries

## Setting
- Ten secondary or tertiary care hospitals in Scotland
- Enrollment: 10 June 2013 to 3 March 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients with suspected ACS and paired cardiac troponin measurements

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previous admission during trial period
- Non-residents of Scotland

**Enrollment:**
- 48,282 patients included
- Average age: 61 years; 47% women

## Intervention

**High-Sensitivity Troponin Assay (hs-TnI):**
- ARCHITECT stat; Abbott Laboratories, USA
- 99th centile upper reference limit: 34 ng/L (men), 16 ng/L (women)

## Control

**Contemporary Cardiac Troponin I (c-TnI) Assay:**
- Abbott Laboratories, USA
- Thresholds: 40 ng/L or 50 ng/L depending on site

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death within one year
  - Validation phase: 1,106/18,978 (6%)
  - Implementation phase: 1,480/29,304 (5%)
  - Adjusted OR: 1.05 (95% CI: 0.92–1.19); p = 0.48

### Reclassified Cohort
- Primary outcome in reclassified patients:
  - Validation phase: 105/720 (15%)
  - Implementation phase: 131/1,051 (12%)
  - OR: 1.10 (95% CI: 0.75–1.61); p = 0.620
- 1,771/10,360 (17%) reclassified by hs-TnI assay
- Reclassified patients more likely to undergo coronary angiography and start antiplatelet therapy

### Secondary Outcomes
- No differences in secondary outcomes or safety measures
- Reduced hospital stay in patients with negative hs-TnI result

## Authors’ Conclusions
The high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I assay reclassified 17% of patients with myocardial injury, but only one-third had type 1 myocardial infarction. The assay did not affect the incidence of myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death within one year.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Adequately powered, pragmatic study
  - Appropriate cluster-randomised design
  - Robust definitions and blinded assessment
  - Large sample size with 100% follow-up
- **Weaknesses**
  - One-year follow-up may be too short for some outcomes
  - Limited generalisability outside Scotland
  - Smaller than anticipated reclassification proportion
  - Potential confounding from practice changes over time

## Bottom Line
High-sensitivity troponin assays lower the diagnostic threshold for myocardial infarction but do not improve patient-centered outcomes at one year. Longer-term data in broader settings are needed to assess downstream benefits.
