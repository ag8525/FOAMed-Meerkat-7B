# Tenecteplase versus Alteplase before Thrombectomy for Ischemic Stroke (EXTEND-IA TNK)

## Clinical Point
In patients with anterior circulation strokes undergoing endovascular thrombectomy, is IV thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of stroke onset with tenecteplase non-inferior to alteplase at establishing reperfusion?

## Background
- Management of acute stroke has evolved, incorporating endovascular thrombectomy with alteplase for large-vessel occlusions.
- Tenecteplase, a variant of alteplase, offers superior fibrin specificity, longer half-life, and rapid bolus administration.
- Previous studies suggested tenecteplase may improve cerebral reperfusion and clinical outcomes.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Centralised web server
- Stratified by vessel site
- Open-label with blinded outcome assessment
- Non-inferiority design with a boundary of 4.6%
- Superiority assessment planned if non-inferiority established
- Logistic regression analysis with vessel occlusion adjustment
- Blinded adaptive sample size re-estimation after 100 patients
- Intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses

## Setting
- 12 centres in Australia and 1 in New Zealand
- Study period: March 2015 to October 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Ischaemic stroke within 4.5 hours of onset
- Large vessel occlusion on CT angiography
- Eligible for IV thrombolysis and thrombectomy

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Severe pre-existing disability (mRS > 3)

**Enrollment:**
- 202 patients included

## Intervention

**Tenecteplase Group:**
- IV tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg)

## Control

**Alteplase Group:**
- IV alteplase 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Reperfusion >50% or absence of thrombus at initial angiography
  - Tenecteplase: 22%
  - Alteplase: 10%
  - Incidence difference: 12% (95% CI: 2-21), p=0.002 for non-inferiority
  - Adjusted OR for superiority: 2.6 (95% CI: 1.1-5.9), p=0.02
  - NNT = 9

### Secondary Outcomes
- Better median mRS at 90 days for tenecteplase
  - mRS: 2 (IQR 0-3) vs. 3 (IQR 1-5)
  - OR: 1.7 (95% CI: 1.0-2.8), p=0.04
- No significant difference in:
  - Early neurological improvement at 24 hours
  - Recovery to independent function at 90 days
  - Symptomatic/asymptomatic cerebral haemorrhage
  - Mortality
  - Time to thrombectomy post-thrombolysis

## Authors’ Conclusions
Tenecteplase was non-inferior to alteplase in restoring perfusion in proximal cerebral-artery occlusion. Functional outcomes were better with tenecteplase, with no significant difference in cerebral haemorrhage incidence.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Adequately powered non-inferiority design
  - Planned superiority analysis
  - Stratification by clot location
  - Blinded outcome assessors
  - Safe administration in sicker patients
- **Weaknesses**
  - Open-label due to drug administration differences
  - Non-inferiority design limits conclusions on superiority
  - Primary outcome was a surrogate endpoint
  - Select patient group, predominantly urban centres
  - Revised inclusion criteria during study

## Bottom Line
Tenecteplase was non-inferior to alteplase for achieving >50% reperfusion in acute anterior circulatory strokes. Its pharmacodynamic properties favor its use over alteplase, though conclusions on superiority await further studies.
