# A Crossover Trial of Hospital-Wide Lactated Ringer’s Solution versus Normal Saline

## Clinical Point
In hospitalised patients, does the use of Lactated Ringer’s (LR) versus Normal Saline (NS) lower the incidence of death or readmission to the hospital within 90 days after the index admission?

## Background
Fluid choice between balanced solutions and NS has been widely studied, with mixed results. Concerns exist that the chloride load with NS may impair renal perfusion, leading to adverse outcomes. Previous studies like SMART showed decreased rates of major adverse kidney events with balanced solutions, but little work has assessed fluid choice outside the ICU and ED.

## Design
- Investigator-initiated, open-label trial
- Cluster, crossover design
- All clinical data obtained from health administrative sources
- Trial consisted of two 15-week periods with a run-in and washout period
- Hospitals randomised to starting arm, notified 1 month before commencement
- Power calculation: 12 sites for 80% power to detect a 1% difference in primary outcome
- Trial stopped after 7 sites due to COVID-19

## Setting
- 7 hospitals in Ontario, Canada
- Study period: August 2016 to March 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- All patients with an index admission during the study period
- Hospitals admitting >1500 patients during trial period with a level II/III ICU

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Missing birth date
- <1 month old
- Admission during run-in or washout period

**Enrollment:**
- 43,626 patients included (22,017 in LR group, 21,609 in NS group)

## Intervention

**LR Group:**
- Use of LR for all fluid administration (>80% of stocked fluids were LR)
- 78.2% compliance based on fluid inventory

## Control

**NS Group:**
- Use of NS for all fluid administration (>80% of stocked fluids were NS)
- 93.6% compliance

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death or readmission within 90 days after index admission
  - LR: 20.3% (SD 3.5) vs NS: 21.4% (SD 3.3)
  - Adjusted mean difference: -0.53 (95% CI: -1.85 to 0.79)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Death within 90 days: 6.9% (SD 1.2) vs 7.6% (SD 1.7)
  - Readmission within 90 days: 15.1% (SD 2.9) vs 15.4% (SD 2.1)
  - Initiation of dialysis: 0.5% (SD 0.3) vs 0.6% (SD 0.5)
  - Hospital LOS: 164.7 hours (SD 34.7) vs 172.3 hours (SD 34.2)
  - Discharged to a facility other than home: 15.4% (SD 4.8) vs 16.3% (SD 4.6)

## Authors’ Conclusions
In this cluster-randomized, crossover trial, the use of lactated Ringer’s solution compared with normal saline did not result in a significant difference in the incidence of death or readmission within 90 days.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - No missing data for primary outcome
  - Large trial population
  - Assessed fluid use across all hospital phases
  - Pragmatic trial with a heterogeneous patient population
- **Weaknesses**
  - Limited to hospitals in Ontario, reducing external generalisability
  - Inclusion of all hospital admissions means the population was not severely ill
  - Lower adherence to LR
  - No patient-level fluid use data
  - Early cessation with fewer hospital clusters than planned
  - No economic evaluation

## Bottom Line
While the use of LR did not significantly reduce mortality or readmissions within 90 days, point estimates favored LR. The trial does not provide compelling evidence to change current practice. Further work on strategies to reduce overall hospital fluid usage is recommended.
