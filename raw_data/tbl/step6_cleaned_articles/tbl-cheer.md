  The CHEER trial investigates a protocolized approach for managing refractory cardiac arrest using mechanical CPR, hypothermia, ECMO, and early reperfusion, conducted at The Alfred Hospital in Melbourne. This prospective pilot observational study included 26 patients with out-of-hospital and in-hospital cardiac arrests, primarily due to cardiac etiology, and demonstrated a 54% survival rate with good neurological recovery. # Refractory Cardiac Arrest Treated with Mechanical CPR, Hypothermia, ECMO, and Early Reperfusion (CHEER Trial)

## Clinical Point
In patients experiencing refractory cardiac arrest, does a protocolised management approach involving mechanical CPR, hypothermia, ECMO, and early reperfusion improve survival rates?

## Design
- Prospective pilot observational study

## Setting
- Single center: The Alfred Hospital, Melbourne, Australia
- 45 ICU beds and a referral center for ECMO
- Data collected over a 32-month period (specific dates not provided)

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) with refractory cardiac arrest (>30 minutes)
- Aged 18–65 years
- Cardiac arrest due to suspected cardiac etiology
- Chest compressions commenced within 10 minutes by bystander or EMS
- Initial cardiac arrest rhythm of ventricular fibrillation (VF)
- Mechanical CPR machines available

**In-Hospital Cardiac Arrest (IHCA):**
- At the discretion of the attending physician, provided the cause of cardiac arrest was considered reversible and ECMO was immediately available

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- OHCA: None specifically mentioned
- IHCA: Known significant pre-existing neurological disability, non-cardiac comorbidities limiting daily activities (e.g., COPD, liver cirrhosis, renal failure, terminal illness)

**Enrollment:**
- 26 patients (11 OHCA, 15 IHCA)
- Median age: 52 years, predominantly male
- Initial rhythm VF in 73%

## Intervention
- **CHEER Protocol:**
  - **Mechanical Chest Compressions:** Using the Autopulse™ device
  - **Hypothermia:** Rapid IV administration of 30 mL/kg ice-cold saline to induce hypothermia, target temperature of 33°C maintained for 24 hours, followed by slow rewarming (0.24°C/hr)
  - **ECMO:** Percutaneous cannulation of the femoral artery and vein by two critical care physicians, commencement of veno-arterial ECMO
  - **Early Reperfusion:** Urgent percutaneous coronary intervention

## Control
- No control group; this was a pilot observational study

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Survival with good neurological recovery (CPC 1-2): 14/26 (54%)

### Secondary Outcomes
- ROSC achieved in 25/26 (92%) of patients
- Survival to hospital discharge: 14/26 (54%)

**Complications:**
- Blood loss: 2 patients died from major bleeding, 2 had cerebral hemorrhage, 16/26 (69%) required blood transfusion (median 3.5 units, IQR 0-6)
- Vascular injury: Surgical intervention required in 10/24 (42%) patients who underwent cannulation, 9 required femoral artery repair, 1 required fasciotomy

**Survivors:**
- Median time from collapse to ECMO initiation: 40 minutes (IQR 27-57)
- Median time on ECMO: 3 days (IQR 1.8-5)
- Median ICU stay: 230 hours (9.6 days; IQR 118-320)
- Median hospital stay: 20 days (IQR 12-26)
- 2 patients achieved ROSC during cannulation and did not receive ECMO

## Authors’ Conclusions
A protocol involving ECMO-CPR for refractory cardiac arrest, including mechanical CPR, peri-arrest therapeutic hypothermia, and ECMO, is feasible and associated with a relatively high survival rate.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Impressive survival rates with CPC 1-2 of 54% in patients with prolonged cardiac arrest
  - Protocolised resuscitation therapy driven by clinical teams with pre-hospital EMS engagement
  - Collaboration between clinical research and industry support (Autopulse device donated without study influence)
- **Weaknesses**
  - Pilot observational study with small, selected patient numbers in a single specialist ECMO center
  - OHCA group had defined inclusion criteria; IHCA group was selective
  - Unclear if outcomes would be equivalent with conventional treatment
  - Comparison with historical studies favors the CHEER protocol in small numbers

## Bottom Line
The CHEER protocol improves survival with favorable neurological outcomes following cardiac arrest compared to historical data. It is effective in an experienced ECMO center with pre-hospital service engagement. A multi-center RCT is needed to determine if these results can be replicated.
