summary: |
  The ATACH-2 trial investigated whether intensive systolic blood pressure (BP) lowering in patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) improves outcomes compared to standard BP management. Conducted across multiple international sites, the trial included patients with systolic BP ≥180 mmHg and aimed to reduce BP to 110–139 mmHg in the intervention group versus 140–179 mmHg in the control group. The primary outcome, significant disability or death at 3 months, showed no difference between groups, with a higher incidence of serious adverse events in the intensive treatment group. The trial concluded that aggressive BP reduction does not confer additional benefits and may increase adverse events, aligning with findings from the INTERACT2 trial.

# Intensive Blood-Pressure Lowering in Patients with Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage (ATACH-2 Trial)

## Clinical Point
In patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage who are hypertensive, does rapid lowering of systolic blood pressure compared to standard therapy improve patient outcomes?

## Design
- Randomised, multi-centre, open-label trial
- Central randomisation using a trial website with a minimisation algorithm and biased-coin method
- Follow-up period: 3 months
- Power calculation: 90% power to detect a 10% reduction in death or disability in the intervention group (expected control event rate: 60%)
- Alpha error: 0.05
- Sample size: 1280 (accounting for non-adherence)
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 110 sites across the USA, Japan, China, Taiwan, South Korea, and Germany
- Study period: May 2011 to September 2015

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥18 years
- GCS > 4
- Intraparenchymal hematoma <60 cm³ on initial CT
- Within 3 hours (extended to 4.5 hours) of symptom onset
- At least one systolic BP reading ≥180 mmHg between symptom onset and initiation of IV antihypertensive treatment

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Unreliable symptom onset time
- Known AVM, neoplasm, or aneurysm
- Hematoma related to trauma
- ICH in infratentorial regions (pons or cerebellum)
- IVH with blood filling one lateral ventricle or >50% of both ventricles
- Candidate for immediate surgical intervention
- Pregnancy, recent parturition, or active lactation
- History of bleeding diathesis or coagulopathy
- Recent warfarin use
- Platelet count <50,000/mm³
- Known sensitivity to nicardipine
- Pre-morbid mRS ≥4
- Living will precluding aggressive ICU management

**Enrollment:**
- 8532 patients screened, 1000 randomised

## Intervention

**Intensive BP Lowering Group:**
- Target systolic BP: 110–139 mmHg for 24 hours post-randomisation
- Treatment could begin before randomisation to lower systolic BP <180 mmHg, but excluded if BP <140 mmHg before randomisation

## Control

**Standard BP Lowering Group:**
- Target systolic BP: 140–179 mmHg for 24 hours post-randomisation

## Management Common to Both Groups
- First-line: IV nicardipine infusion (5 mg/hr, increased by 2.5 mg/hr every 15 minutes, max 15 mg/hr)
- Second-line (if target not achieved after 30 mins): IV labetalol (or IV diltiazem/urapidil if labetalol unavailable)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Proportion with mRS 4–6 at 3 months (significant disability or death)
  - No difference: 38.7% (intervention) vs. 37.7% (control)
  - Unadjusted RR: 1.02 (95% CI: 0.83–1.25, p=0.84)
  - Adjusted RR: 1.04 (95% CI: 0.85–1.27, p=0.72)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in EQ-5D scores, VAS at 3 months, or hematoma expansion ≥33% on 24-hour scan
- Higher incidence of serious adverse events within 3 months in intervention group (25.6% vs. 20%, p=0.05)
- Primary treatment failure (not achieving target within 2 hours): 12.2% (intervention) vs. 0.8% (control), p<0.001
- Secondary treatment failure (hourly systolic BP above target for 2 consecutive hours): 15.6% (intervention) vs. 1.4% (control), p<0.001

## Authors’ Conclusions
The trial results do not support reducing systolic BP to 110–139 mmHg in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multi-centre trial
  - Clinically meaningful and relevant outcome measures
- **Weaknesses**
  - Recruitment criteria changed during the trial
  - Reasons for failure to randomise screened population not included
  - Underpowered: actual event rate was 38% (vs. expected 60%), increasing type 2 error risk
  - Over 50% of patients recruited from Asia, may not reflect European population
  - ICH treated as a single entity
  - Significant treatment failure differences between groups

## Bottom Line
The ATACH-2 and INTERACT2 trials do not support early, intensive systolic BP control in acute intracranial hemorrhage.
