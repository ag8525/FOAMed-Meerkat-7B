# Effect of Out-of-Hospital Sodium Nitrite on Survival to Hospital Admission After Cardiac Arrest: A Randomized Clinical Trial

## Clinical Point
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does sodium nitrite, compared with placebo, improve survival to hospital admission?

## Background
Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest has a high risk of morbidity and mortality. Nitrate therapy may mitigate cellular injury and death following reperfusion after ischemia, showing cytoprotective effects in animal models. Previous phase 1 trials indicated potential benefits of sodium nitrite at specific serum levels during early reperfusion.

## Design
- Double-blind, randomized phase 2 clinical trial
- Randomization: computer-generated, permuted block sizes (3, 6, 9)
- Stratification across 4 regions in Washington State
- 1:1:1 randomization: 45 mg sodium nitrite, 60 mg sodium nitrite, or placebo
- Blinding: paramedics, nurses, physicians, study abstractors
- Sample size: 1500 patients for 80% power to detect an 8% absolute increase in primary outcome

## Setting
- Conducted in Seattle and King County, Washington State
- Study period: 8 February 2018 to 19 August 2019
- Follow-up and data abstraction completed by 31 December 2019

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Received advanced life support by paramedics
- Intravenous or intraosseous access
- Age >18 years
- Comatose at enrollment

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Trauma as cause of cardiac arrest
- Age <18 years
- DNR order, rigor, or lividity
- Not comatose
- Incarcerated
- Pregnant

**Enrollment:**
- 1502 out of 2264 patients with cardiac arrest enrolled
- Mean age: 64 years (SD 17 years)
- 990 men, 502 women
- 1497 patients randomized: 500 (45 mg sodium nitrite), 498 (60 mg sodium nitrite), 499 (placebo)

## Intervention

**Sodium Nitrite Groups:**
- 45 mg or 60 mg sodium nitrite administered intravenously or intraosseously

## Control
- Placebo administered intravenously or intraosseously
- Standard out-of-hospital resuscitation protocols applied to all groups

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital admission**: no significant difference
  - 45 mg: 41%
  - 60 mg: 43%
  - Placebo: 44%
  - Mean difference (45 mg vs placebo): −2.9% (95% CI: −8.0 to ∞; p = .82)
  - Mean difference (60 mg vs placebo): −1.3% (95% CI: −6.5 to ∞; p = .66)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No statistical differences in:
  - Rate of rearrest
  - Rate of return of spontaneous circulation
  - Number of days in ICU
  - Cumulative survival to 24 hours
  - Survival to hospital discharge
  - Cerebral Performance Category at discharge

**Additional Analyses:**
- No significant differences in time to death, blood pressure at hospital arrival, sustained hypotension, withdrawal of life support, cardiac catheterization, or targeted temperature management

## Authors’ Conclusions
Sodium nitrite administration during resuscitation from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest does not significantly improve survival to hospital admission compared to placebo.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Double-blinded, randomized design reduces bias
  - Clinically relevant Point
  - Phase 2 study extends beyond preliminary animal data
  - Negative results published transparently
  - Clinically relevant secondary and post-hoc outcomes
- **Weaknesses**
  - Longer time to sodium nitrite administration than in animal studies
  - Underpowered to detect smaller differences; effect size may be overestimated
  - Potential enrollment bias
  - Not specifically powered for VF population
  - Some patients not considered for enrollment by EMS, unclear impact

## Bottom Line
This well-designed study provides convincing data that sodium nitrite is not beneficial during resuscitation from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest.
