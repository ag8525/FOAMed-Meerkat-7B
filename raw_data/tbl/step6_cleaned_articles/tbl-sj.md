# Pre-oxygenation Using High-Flow Nasal Oxygen vs. Tight Facemask During Rapid Sequence Induction

## Clinical Point
In adult patients undergoing rapid sequence intubation (RSI) for emergency surgery, does pre-oxygenation with high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) compared with standard face-mask oxygen reduce the incidence of oxygen desaturation?

## Background
Rapid sequence induction aims to minimize the apnoeic period between pre-oxygenation and intubation. Hypoxia is a recognized complication of RSI. HFNO may offer continuous oxygen delivery during apnoea, potentially reducing desaturation.

## Design
- Unblinded international prospective randomised controlled trial
- Power calculation: 326 patients needed for 80% power to detect a 7.5% absolute risk reduction in desaturation with HFNO (type-1 error of 5%)

## Setting
- Six centres in Europe (five in Sweden, one in Switzerland)
- Study period: March 2018 to February 2020

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients undergoing elective surgery with planned RSI

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- BMI > 35 kg/m²
- Pregnancy
- Need for non-invasive ventilation prior to anaesthesia
- Inability to achieve oxygen saturations >93% during pre-oxygenation

**Enrollment:**
- 350 patients randomly allocated to HFNO or facemask
- 349 patients included in final analysis (1 excluded due to protocol violation)

## Intervention

**HFNO Group:**
- High-flow nasal oxygen via Optiflow™ (30-50 L/min, increased to 70 L/min during apnoea)
- Continuous oxygen delivery until tracheal tube placement

## Control

**Facemask Group:**
- 100% oxygen via tight-fitting facemask (10 L/min fresh gas flow)
- Delivered via a circle system

## Common Management
- Standard monitoring
- RSI per local protocol
- Pre-oxygenation for ≥3 minutes
- Supine reverse Trendelenburg position
- Chin lift and jaw thrust during apnoea

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No difference in oxygen desaturation incidence (HFNO: 2.9% vs. Facemask: 3.4%; p=0.77)
- No difference in lowest mean oxygen saturations (HFNO: 99.1% vs. Facemask: 99.0%)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in desaturation incidence between centres
- Higher desaturation rates in Switzerland (7%) vs. Sweden (1.6%; p=0.009)
- Longer apnoea and intubation times in Switzerland
- Higher ETO₂ levels in facemask group (84.9 kPa vs. 76.7 kPa; p<0.001)
- No significant difference in desaturation between office hours and on-call hours
- One regurgitation incident in HFNO group

## Authors’ Conclusions
HFNO is a suitable alternative to facemask pre-oxygenation for maintaining oxygen levels during RSI.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-conducted with appropriate power calculation and randomisation
  - Representative sample with few exclusion criteria
  - Balanced baseline characteristics
  - Inclusion of patients during both office and on-call hours
- **Weaknesses**
  - Underpowered due to lower than anticipated desaturation incidence
  - Primary outcome not patient-centered; clinical importance of desaturation <93% unclear
  - No blinding, potential bias
  - Not applicable to pregnant or obese patients

## Bottom Line
This study shows no significant benefit of HFNO over facemask in reducing desaturation during RSI. Clinicians should use the technique they are most comfortable with until further evidence suggests a change in practice.
