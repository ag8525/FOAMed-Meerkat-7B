summary: |
  This document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the impact of intravenous drug administration during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest on patient outcomes. The study, conducted in Oslo, Norway, compared standard Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) with intravenous drugs to ACLS without intravenous drugs. The primary outcome, survival to hospital discharge, showed no significant difference between the groups. However, the intravenous group had a significantly higher rate of Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC), particularly in patients with pulseless electrical activity (PEA) or asystole. Despite these findings, there was no improvement in survival with good neurological outcomes. The study suggests that while intravenous drugs may enhance ROSC, they do not improve overall survival or neurological outcomes, highlighting the need for further research to assess the efficacy and safety of these interventions in cardiac arrest management.

# Intravenous Drug Administration during Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest

## Clinical Point
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does removing the use of intravenous medications improve survival?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Use of sealed envelopes for allocation concealment
- Non-blinded
- Intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- Pre-hospital setting in Oslo, Norway
- Study period: 1 May 2003 to 28 August 2008

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Traumatic cardiac arrest
- Arrest secondary to asthma/anaphylaxis
- Arrest witnessed by ambulance staff

**Enrollment:**
- 851 patients included out of 1183 for whom resuscitation was attempted

## Intervention
- Use of intravenous drug administration with Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) according to standard guidelines (adrenaline, atropine, amiodarone)

## Control
- No attempt at intravenous drug administration with ACLS

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital discharge:** No significant difference
  - IV group: 10.5%
  - No IV group: 9.2%
  - Odds ratio: 1.16 (95% CI: 0.74–1.82), p = 0.61

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Return of Spontaneous Circulation (ROSC):** Significant improvement in IV group
  - Overall: 40% vs. 25% (OR: 1.99, 95% CI: 1.48–2.67, p < 0.001)
  - VF/VT: No significant difference (59% vs. 53%, p = 0.35)
  - PEA/asystole: Significant improvement in IV group (29% vs. 11%, p < 0.001)
- **Survival with good neurological outcome:** No significant difference
  - IV group: 9.8%
  - No IV group: 8.1%
  - Odds ratio: 1.24 (95% CI: 0.77–1.98), p = 0.45

## Authors’ Conclusions
Patients in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest had increased rates of ROSC with intravenous drug administration, but no statistically significant improvement in survival to discharge or neurological outcome.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised design
  - Quality of chest compressions assessed
  - All patients treated with post-resuscitation protocols, including therapeutic hypothermia
- **Weaknesses**
  - Potentially underpowered; power calculation based on doubling survival rate
  - Only 79% of the IV group received adrenaline

## Bottom Line
Intravenous resuscitation medications increased ROSC rates in patients with PEA/asystole but did not improve survival to discharge or neurological outcomes. A larger trial is needed to determine the benefit of this standard treatment, especially given concerns about adrenaline's association with poor neurological outcomes.
