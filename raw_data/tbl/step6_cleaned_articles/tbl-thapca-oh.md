# Therapeutic Hypothermia after Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest in Children (THAPCA-OH)

## Clinical Point
In children suffering an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does hypothermia compared to normothermia increase survival with a good neurobehavioural outcome?

## Design
- Randomised, controlled trial
- 1:1 allocation by permuted blocks stratified by clinical centre and age group
- Randomisation via telephone-based system with internet and sealed envelope backup
- Pre-specified modified intention-to-treat analysis
- Powered at 85% to detect a 20% absolute treatment effect

## Setting
- 38 sites across the USA and Canada
- Study period: September 2009 to December 2012

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Children aged 48 hours to 18 years
- Cardiac arrest requiring ≥2 minutes of CPR
- Dependent on mechanical ventilation post-ROSC

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Inability to randomise within 6 hours
- Motor score of 5 or 6 on GCS
- Decision to withhold aggressive treatment
- Major trauma associated with cardiac arrest

**Enrollment:**
- 1355 met inclusion criteria, 475 eligible, 295 randomised
- 260 included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Intervention

**Hypothermia Group:**
- Core temperature targeted at 33.0ºC (range 32.0 to 34.0) for 48 hours
- Rewarmed over ≥16 hours to 36.8ºC (range 36.0 to 37.5)
- Normothermia maintained until 120-hour intervention completion

## Control

**Normothermia Group:**
- Core temperature targeted at 36.8ºC (range 36.0 to 37.5) for 120 hours

**Common Management:**
- Pharmacological sedation (midazolam, fentanyl) and paralysis (vecuronium if needed)
- Surface cooling applied
- Dual central temperature monitoring (oesophageal primary, bladder/rectal secondary)

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in survival to 12 months with good neurobehavioural outcome (VABS-II score ≥70)
  - Hypothermia: 20%
  - Normothermia: 12%
  - Absolute risk difference: 7.3% (95% CI: -1.5 to 16.1)
  - Relative likelihood: 1.54 (95% CI: 0.86 to 2.76), p = 0.14

### Secondary Outcomes
- No difference in survival to 12 months
- Longer survival over time with hypothermia (p = 0.04, log-rank test)
  - Hypothermia: mean survival 149±14 days
  - Normothermia: mean survival 119±14 days
- No difference in change in neurobehavioural function

**Tertiary Safety Data:**
- Similar adverse events (infection, bleeding, dysrhythmias) between groups
- More hypokalaemia and thrombocytopenia in hypothermia group
- More renal-replacement therapy in normothermia group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Therapeutic hypothermia in children post-out-of-hospital cardiac arrest does not improve survival with good function at 1 year compared to normothermia.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Well-constructed trial with pre-published pilot data and methodology
  - Inclusion of all pediatric age groups with block randomisation
  - Included both shockable and non-shockable rhythms
- **Weaknesses**
  - Potential under-powering leading to a false negative conclusion
  - Modified intention-to-treat analysis included only 88% of randomised patients
  - Loss of 12% due to poor baseline function, unknown status, or incomplete data
  - Sensitivity analyses did not alter outcomes

## Bottom Line
Therapeutic hypothermia after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in children cannot be recommended based on this trial. Although no difference was found between normothermia and hypothermia, a false negative is possible, and confidence intervals are wide. Longer survival with hypothermia may suggest delayed risk of death.
