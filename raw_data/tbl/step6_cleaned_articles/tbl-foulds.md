# A Randomised Cross-Over Trial Comparing the McGrath® Series 5 Videolaryngoscope with the Macintosh Laryngoscope in Patients with Cervical Spine Immobilisation

## Clinical Point
In patients with cervical immobilisation, does the McGrath® videolaryngoscope compared to the Macintosh laryngoscope provide a better view of the larynx?

## Design
- Prospective, randomised controlled cross-over study
- Randomisation by random number sequence
- Powered for equivalence: 50 patients required to determine that the primary outcome would be the same or better in 90% of patients (with 2-sided 95% CI of ±8%)

## Setting
- Operating theatres, single centre in Scotland
- Dates of data collection not stated

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients presenting for elective operative procedures requiring tracheal intubation
- Without suspected cervical spine pathology

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Hiatus hernia, symptomatic reflux, pharyngeal pathology
- Requiring alternative laryngoscopic procedure or raised intracranial pressure

**Enrollment:**
- 50 patients randomised; 1 procedure cancelled post-randomisation

## Intervention
- Laryngoscopy with Macintosh blade, followed by laryngoscopy and intubation with McGrath videolaryngoscope

## Control
- Laryngoscopy with McGrath videolaryngoscope, followed by laryngoscopy and intubation with Macintosh blade

**Procedure:**
- Patients paralysed with muscle relaxant and immobilised with cervical spine collar
- Initial laryngoscopy to assess view only
- Macintosh laryngoscopy with size 4 blade
- McGrath laryngoscopy with series 5 videolaryngoscope, blade set at middle length, stylet pre-formed with 60-degree bend 4 cm from the tip
- Re-oxygenation between each laryngoscopy

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **View obtained (Cormack and Lehane grade):**
  - McGrath® group: 92% had a Grade 1 view, 0% had a Grade 3/4 view
  - Macintosh group: 6% had a Grade 1 view, 57% had a Grade 3/4 view
  - Significantly better view with McGrath® (p < 0.01)

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Time to intubation:** No significant difference (45 seconds vs. 60 seconds, p = 0.125)
- **Rate of successful intubation:** Higher in McGrath group (100% vs. 72%, p = 0.017)
- **Complications:** No significant difference
  - Minor trauma to lips/pharynx: 10% vs. 20%
  - Desaturation <92%: 0% vs. 4%

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with simulated difficult airway via cervical immobilisation, the McGrath® videolaryngoscope provided significantly improved laryngeal views and increased tracheal intubation success rates compared to the Macintosh laryngoscope.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Clinically important Point
  - Appropriate design to compare techniques
  - Randomisation via random number sequence
  - Powered prospectively
  - Similar baseline characteristics
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single centre
  - Only two anaesthetists, both experienced in McGrath® videolaryngoscopy
  - Did not reach target recruitment for power calculation
  - Immobilised patients often anaesthetised as an emergency using manual in-line stabilisation rather than electively with a cervical collar
  - Non-blinded
  - Lack of standard anaesthesia/muscle relaxant
  - Subjective primary outcome

## Bottom Line
In this study, in patients immobilised with a semi-rigid cervical collar, the grade of laryngoscopic view and rate of successful intubations was greater when using a McGrath® series 5 videolaryngoscope compared to a Macintosh blade, in the hands of two anaesthetists experienced in both techniques.
