summary: |
  A randomized controlled trial investigated the impact of targeted temperature management (TTM) at 33°C versus 36°C on cognitive function in survivors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. The study included adult patients who were unconscious upon admission and excluded those with prolonged time to enrollment, unwitnessed arrests with asystole, or suspected intracranial hemorrhage. The primary outcome, measured by the Rivermead Behavioural Memory Test, showed no significant difference in cognitive outcomes between the two temperature groups at six months. Secondary outcomes, including executive dysfunction and mental speed, also showed no significant differences. The study concluded that TTM at 33°C versus 36°C does not affect cognitive function, highlighting the need for further research to explore other potential benefits of TTM in this patient population.

# Cognitive Function in Survivors of Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest After Target Temperature Management at 33°C Versus 36°C

## Clinical Point
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does target temperature management at 33°C versus 36°C improve cognitive function at 6 months?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial  
- Treating clinicians non-blinded  
- Face-to-face follow-up at ~180 days  
- Examiners blinded to TTM allocation but not to control status  
- >80% power to detect a 10% difference in Rivermead Behavioural Memory Test profile score  

## Setting
- 5 European countries  
- 20 out of 36 sites from the TTM Trial completed this follow-up study  
- Follow-up period: June 2011 to September 2013  

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients unconscious (GCS < 8) on admission after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause  

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >6 hours from return of spontaneous circulation to enrolment  
- Unwitnessed arrest with systole as initial rhythm  
- Hypothermia < 30°C  
- Suspected or known intracranial haemorrhage  

**Control Group:**
- Patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction treated with percutaneous coronary intervention  
- Matched for age, gender, country, and time-point of hospitalisation  

**Enrollment:**
- 652 patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia  
- 119 control patients  
- 342 patients alive at 180 days, 287 completed follow-up  

## Intervention

**TTM at 33°C Group:**
- 28 hours of temperature management targeting 33°C  
- Gradual warming to 37°C at 0.5°C/hour  
- Avoidance of pyrexia for 72 hours from randomisation  

**TTM at 36°C Group:**
- 28 hours of temperature management targeting 36°C  
- Gradual warming to 37°C at 0.5°C/hour  
- Avoidance of pyrexia for 72 hours from randomisation  

## Control
- Recruited from cohort of ST-elevation myocardial infarction patients treated with percutaneous coronary intervention  
- Matched for age, gender, country, and time-point of hospitalisation  

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Rivermead Behavioural Memory Test (RBMT): No significant difference between 33°C and 36°C, p = 0.56  

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference between 33°C and 36°C in:
  - Executive dysfunction (Frontal Assessment Battery), p = 0.43  
  - Mental speed and agitation (Symbol Digit Modalities Test), p = 0.31  

- STEMI controls had significantly better SDMT scores than both treatment groups (unadjusted p = 0.0001, covariate-adjusted p = 0.002)  
- No significant difference in:
  - RBMT (unadjusted p = 0.46, covariate-adjusted p = 0.06)  
  - FAB (unadjusted p = 0.22, covariate-adjusted p = 0.008)  
  - Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) for depression (p = 0.99) and anxiety (p = 0.89)  

## Authors’ Conclusions
Survivors of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest treated with targeted temperature management at 33°C versus 36°C showed no difference in cognitive outcomes.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised controlled trial  
  - Blinding of primary outcome assessor to TTM allocation  
  - Low loss to follow-up  
  - Multiple cognitive outcome measures assessed  
  - Use of CONSORT diagram  

- **Weaknesses**
  - Baseline cognitive function differences not assessed  
  - Small sample size increases probability of random baseline differences  
  - RBMT may be insensitive to mild impairment; updated version unavailable at study start  
  - FAB not validated in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest population  
  - Fewer TTM patients with poor neurological recovery followed up, potentially underestimating cognitive impairment  

## Bottom Line
In patients with rapid initiation of basic life support after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, targeted temperature management at 33°C versus 36°C does not affect cognitive function at six months.
