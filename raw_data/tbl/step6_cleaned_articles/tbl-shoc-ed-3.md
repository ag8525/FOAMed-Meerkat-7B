  The SHoC-ED 3 study investigates the impact of a standardized point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) protocol on resuscitation length, intervention rates, and clinical outcomes in emergency department patients experiencing cardiac arrest. Conducted as a retrospective analysis at a Canadian tertiary center, the study included 223 patients, with 180 undergoing POCUS assessment. Results indicated that POCUS use, particularly when cardiac activity was visualized, led to longer resuscitation efforts and higher rates of return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) and survival to hospital admission, though not to hospital discharge. Despite these findings, the study's non-randomized design and lack of quality assessment of POCUS examinations limit its conclusions, emphasizing the need for further prospective trials to validate these observations.

# Does Point-of-care Ultrasound Use Impact Resuscitation Length, Rates of Intervention, and Clinical Outcomes During Cardiac Arrest? (SHoC-ED 3)

## Clinical Point
In patients presenting to the Emergency Department (ED) in cardiac arrest, does a standardised point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) protocol, compared with usual care, affect the length of resuscitation, frequency of interventions, and clinical outcomes?

## Background
The use of POCUS has been increasing in acute medical specialties, with several protocols developed (e.g., RUSH). Despite this, studies show mixed outcomes regarding patient improvement. The SHoC-ED trial previously showed no mortality benefit in patients with undifferentiated shock treated with a standardised POCUS protocol compared to usual care. Retrospective analysis of the MIMIC-III database indicated improved 28-day mortality with transthoracic echocardiography in critically ill patients. In cardiac arrest, POCUS can diagnose aetiology and guide care but may interrupt basic life support.

## Design
- Single-centre
- Non-randomised, unblinded
- Retrospective analysis of healthcare records using structured chart review
- Scans performed by competent personnel
- Sample size of 185 required for 80% power to detect a 5% difference in survival to hospital discharge at alpha 0.05, assuming a baseline discharge rate of 5%

## Setting
- Tertiary healthcare centre in Canada
- Study period: 2010 to 2014

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 19 or over
- Cardiac arrest at presentation to the ED

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Resuscitation halted due to end-of-life decisions
- Cardiac arrest as inpatient

**Enrollment:**
- 223 patients met inclusion criteria (180 had POCUS assessment; 21 had visualised cardiac activity, 159 did not)
- Similar baseline characteristics, including rates of witnessed arrest and bystander CPR

## Intervention
- POCUS assessment during Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS)
- Further sub-divided based on visualised cardiac activity or standstill during initial POCUS examination
- Images acquired using standard POCUS techniques with curvilinear or phased array probes by competent sonographers

## Control
- No POCUS during ACLS
- Management common to both groups based on ACLS protocols

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Patients in the POCUS groups received longer mean duration of resuscitation and higher intervention rates compared to those without POCUS
  - Length of resuscitation:
    - POCUS cardiac activity: 27.3 mins (95% CI: 17.7–37.0 mins)
    - POCUS standstill: 11.51 mins (95% CI: 10.2–12.8 mins)
    - Control: 14.36 mins (95% CI: 9.89–18.8 mins)

### Secondary Outcome
- Patients in the POCUS group had significantly higher rates of ROSC and survival to hospital admission compared to those without POCUS
- No difference in rate of survival to hospital discharge

## Authors’ Conclusions
Patients with cardiac activity on POCUS received increased resuscitative effort and had improved clinical outcomes compared to those with negative findings or when no POCUS was performed.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - All cardiac arrests included for analysis
  - POCUS protocol consistent with international practice
- **Weaknesses**
  - Retrospective analysis (non-randomised, unblinded, single-centre)
  - Power calculation based on secondary outcome
  - Unclear if cohorts were matched to even out covariates
  - Actual delays in CPR not recorded
  - Performed by competent personnel – definition unclear
  - Quality and accuracy of POCUS examination not assessed
  - Quality of life indicators not assessed in survivors

## Bottom Line
The observation that patients with cardiac activity visualised on POCUS received longer resuscitation attempts, more interventions, and had a higher rate of ROSC is unsurprising. The decision to terminate or stop intervention is a self-fulfilling prophecy. Despite higher rates of ROSC in patients with cardiac activity on POCUS, the rate of survival to hospital discharge falls to that of patients who received no POCUS. I will continue to use POCUS during cardiac arrest management, but priority remains on good quality chest compressions and resuscitation. Further testing by prospective trial is needed as this study is hypothesis-generating, has biological plausibility, and is relevant to health economics and safety.
