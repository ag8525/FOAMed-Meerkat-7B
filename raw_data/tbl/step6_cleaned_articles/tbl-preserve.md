# Outcomes after Angiography with Sodium Bicarbonate and Acetylcysteine

## Clinical Point
In high-risk patients undergoing angiography, does the administration of bicarbonate and/or acetylcysteine, compared with placebo, prevent major adverse events and acute kidney injury?

## Background
Contrast-induced nephropathy (CIN) is defined as renal impairment occurring within 48 hours of intravascular radiographic contrast material administration, not attributable to other causes. CIN is a significant clinical issue, potentially leading to death, long-term dialysis, and increased healthcare costs. Despite widespread use, high-quality evidence supporting the benefit of intravenous sodium bicarbonate and oral acetylcysteine in preventing CIN is lacking. Previous studies, including the Acetylcysteine for Contrast-Induced Nephropathy Trial (ACT), found no benefit, and the association between modern intravenous contrast and nephropathy remains controversial.

## Design
- Multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study
- 2 x 2 factorial design
- Centralised, computer-generated permuted-block randomisation with stratification by trial site
- Modified intention-to-treat analysis

## Setting
- 53 sites across 4 countries (35 Veterans Affairs Hospitals in the USA, 13 Australian sites, 3 Malaysian sites, 2 New Zealand sites)
- Study period: February 2013 to March 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients scheduled for coronary or non-coronary angiography
- Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 15 to 44.9 ml/min/1.73 m² or 45 to 59.9 ml/min/1.73 m² with diabetes mellitus

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Emergency angiography
- Unstable baseline creatinine levels (≥25% change within 3 days before angiography)

**Enrollment:**
- 5177 patients randomised; 4993 included in primary analysis

## Intervention
- **Sodium Bicarbonate Group:** 1.26% sodium bicarbonate IV, protocol-specified dosing before, during, and after angiography
- **Acetylcysteine Group:** 1200 mg oral acetylcysteine before and after angiography, continued twice daily for 4 days

## Control
- **Sodium Chloride Group:** 0.9% sodium chloride IV
- **Placebo Group:** Matched placebo capsules for acetylcysteine

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Composite of death, dialysis, or >50% increase in creatinine at 90-104 days
  - Sodium bicarbonate vs. sodium chloride: 4.4% vs. 4.7% (OR 0.93; 95% CI 0.72–1.22; p=0.62)
  - Acetylcysteine vs. placebo: 4.6% vs. 4.5% (OR 1.02; 95% CI 0.78–1.33; p=0.88)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in contrast-associated acute kidney injury between groups

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with impaired kidney function undergoing angiography, there was no benefit from peri-procedural IV isotonic sodium bicarbonate or oral acetylcysteine regarding the risk of dialysis, death, or acute kidney injury.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - High power (90%) to detect significant differences
  - Robust randomisation and blinding processes
  - Well-matched baseline groups
  - Composite primary outcome of patient-centred measures
  - High-risk patient group, enhancing external validity
- **Weaknesses**
  - Early study termination after interim analysis
  - Predominantly male sample due to Veteran’s Hospitals
  - Debate over the risk of contrast-induced nephropathy with modern agents
  - Limited generalisability to other radiographic procedures
  - Potential undetected transient creatinine rises

## Bottom Line
In a predominantly male, high-risk population undergoing angiography, sodium bicarbonate and/or acetylcysteine did not prevent major adverse events. The incidence of contrast-associated nephropathy was low, supporting current practice of not administering these agents before contrast administration.
