# Induced Hypothermia in Patients with Septic Shock and Respiratory Failure (CASS Trial)

## Clinical Point
In septic and mechanically ventilated patients aged 50 or over, does 24 hours of induced hypothermia followed by 48 hours of normothermia improve 30-day mortality compared to routine care?

## Background
The search for effective treatments to improve sepsis outcomes has been ongoing, with therapeutic hypothermia showing promise in animal models. This study investigates whether similar benefits are observed in humans with severe sepsis or septic shock.

## Design
- Multinational, multicentre, randomised controlled open-label trial
- Randomisation via an online system with computer-generated blocks stratified by age, APACHE II score, and study site
- Allocation blinded to assessors
- Intention-to-treat analysis
- Interim safety analyses conducted

## Setting
- 10 ICUs across Denmark, the Netherlands, and the USA
- Study period: November 2011 to November 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 50 years or older
- Severe sepsis or septic shock
- Mean arterial pressure (MAP) < 70 mmHg
- Intubated and ventilated
- Likely ICU stay > 24 hours
- Enrolled within 6 hours of meeting criteria

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnant or breastfeeding
- Bleeding disorder/uncontrolled bleeding
- Recent surgery (within 24 hours)
- Detained under a psychiatry order

**Enrollment:**
- 5695 patients screened, 436 randomised
  - 220 to intervention group (203 per-protocol, 217 intention-to-treat)
  - 216 to control group (215 intention-to-treat)

## Intervention

**Induced Hypothermia Group:**
- Cooling to 32-34°C for 24 hours using external cooling systems or intravenous cooling catheter
- Rewarming to 37°C over 48 hours, maintaining 36-38°C

## Control
- Standard thermal management
- No antipyretics for the first 24 hours, routine management thereafter

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- 30-day all-cause mortality was higher in the hypothermia group but not statistically significant
  - Hypothermia: 44.2%
  - Control: 35.8%
  - Risk Ratio (RR): 1.2 (95% CI: 1.0-1.6, p=0.07)

### Secondary Outcomes
- More days with respiratory support, renal replacement therapy, and vasoactive medication in hypothermia group
- No difference in ICU length of stay or 180-day mortality
- Increased inflammatory markers and sedation requirements in hypothermia group

## Authors’ Conclusions
Induced hypothermia for 24 hours followed by 48 hours of normothermia did not improve 30-day mortality in patients aged 50 and over with septic shock requiring mechanical ventilation. It increased morbidity with more days on ventilators and renal replacement therapy.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Multinational, multicentre RCT
  - Strong randomisation and allocation concealment
  - Interim safety analyses
- **Weaknesses**
  - Trial stopped early due to futility
  - Higher mortality trend in hypothermia group
  - Limited to patients aged 50 or over
  - Protocol violations in intervention group
  - Control group mortality lower than expected

## Bottom Line
Induced hypothermia did not improve mortality or organ dysfunction in patients over 50 with severe sepsis on a ventilator. The results suggest increased mortality and morbidity, and current management should not change until further evidence is available.
