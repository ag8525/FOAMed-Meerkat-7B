  The AIRWAYS-2 trial investigated whether using a supraglottic airway (SGA) device compared to tracheal intubation as the initial advanced airway management strategy in adult patients with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest improves functional neurological outcomes. Conducted as a multicenter, cluster-randomized trial across four ambulance services in England, the study included 9,296 patients. # Effect of a Strategy of a Supraglottic Airway Device vs Tracheal Intubation During Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest on Functional Outcome (AIRWAYS-2)

## Clinical Point
In adult patients with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does a supraglottic airway device compared with tracheal intubation as the initial advanced airway management strategy improve functional neurological outcome?

## Background
- Optimal airway management during out-of-hospital cardiac arrest is uncertain due to limited high-quality research.
- Tracheal intubation is considered a definitive airway management technique, but supraglottic airway (SGA) devices offer a simpler, faster alternative requiring less training.
- Another study (PART) assessing laryngeal tube insertion vs endotracheal intubation was published in the same JAMA issue.

## Design
- Multicenter, cluster randomised trial
- Paramedics from 4 ambulance services in England
- Automatic enrollment under a waiver of consent
- Paramedics randomised 1:1 using secure internet-based system
- Paramedic discretion in choosing airway device was allowed
- 9,070 patients needed to detect a difference of 8% vs 10% with 90% power

## Setting
- 4 ambulance services in England serving 21 million people
- June 2015 to August 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Patients aged ≥18 years with non-traumatic out-of-hospital cardiac arrest treated by a participating paramedic

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Previously recruited to the trial
- Resuscitation deemed inappropriate
- Advanced airway already in place
- Enrolled in another prehospital RCT
- Mouth opening <2 cm

**Enrollment:**
- 9,296 patients (4,886 SGA group; 4,410 Tracheal Intubation group)
- 4,882 and 4,407 patients included in primary outcome analysis

## Intervention

**SGA Group:**
- Supraglottic airway device as initial advanced airway management
- Second-generation device with soft non-inflatable cuff (i-gel)
- 82% received SGA first; 15% no airway management; 2.4% intubation first

## Control

**Tracheal Intubation Group:**
- Tracheal intubation as initial advanced airway management
- Direct laryngoscopy; 2-person technique with bougie recommended
- 62% received intubation first; 22% no airway management; 14% SGA first

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Standard approach to airway management (basic to advanced techniques)
- End-tidal CO₂ monitoring for device placement confirmation
- Care per international resuscitation guidelines
- Paramedics received additional training in their allocated intervention

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No statistical difference in Modified Rankin Scale score at discharge or 30 days
  - SGA: 311/4,882 (6.4%) good outcome (mRS 0-3)
  - Intubation: 300/4,407 (6.8%) good outcome
  - Adjusted risk difference: -0.6% (95% CI: -1.6% to 0.4%)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in survival, regurgitation, aspiration, or ROSC
- Significant difference in initial ventilation success (87.4% vs 79.0%, p<0.001)
- Unintended loss of airway: 10.6% vs 5.0%, p<0.001
- Subgroup: More good outcomes in SGA group (3.9% vs 2.6%)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Among patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, a strategy of advanced airway management with a supraglottic airway device compared with tracheal intubation did not result in a favourable functional outcome at 30 days.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large-scale, pre-hospital trial addressing an important clinical Point
  - Patient-focused primary outcome measure
  - Excellent summary infographic
- **Weaknesses**
  - Included patients with and without advanced airway management
  - Greater use of advanced airway management in SGA group
  - Imbalance in patient numbers between groups
  - Crossover between groups allowed for practical and ethical reasons
  - Paramedics were volunteers, possibly not representative
  - Findings may not apply to physician-led EMS or different SGA devices

## Bottom Line
In out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, SGA and tracheal intubation strategies had comparable functional outcomes at 30 days. Familiarity and proficiency with the airway device are crucial.
