# Endovascular Therapy for Acute Stroke with a Large Ischemic Region

## Clinical Point
In patients with large cerebral infarction volumes, does the use of endovascular therapy alongside medical care improve 90-day functional outcomes compared to medical care alone?

## Background
Endovascular treatment (EVT) for acute stroke due to large vessel occlusion is effective in reducing death and disability, as demonstrated in multiple trials (e.g., MR CLEAN, DAWN, EXTEND-IA, DEFUSE III, SWIFT PRIME). The ASPECTS score, ranging from 10 to 0, characterizes the extent of middle cerebral artery (MCA) stroke, with lower scores indicating larger infarct burdens. Previous trials often excluded patients with larger infarct volumes due to bleeding risk concerns. However, a systematic review found no increased hemorrhage risk and improved outcomes with EVT in such patients.

## Design
- Open-label, parallel-group, randomised controlled trial
- Central randomisation in a 1:1 ratio
- Algorithm balanced trial group assignments based on age, time from last known well to arrival, NIHSS score, and rt-PA use
- Assessors of mRS were blinded
- Clinical assessments at baseline, 12 hours, 24–72 hours, 5–9 days, and 60–120 days post-randomisation
- Power calculation based on local registry data, assuming an adjusted OR of 2.7
- Primary analysis included all randomised patients with assessable outcome data
- Per-protocol analysis for those not excluded

## Setting
- 45 hospitals in Japan
- Study period: November 2018 to September 2021

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults >18 years old
- NIHSS score ≥6
- Pre-morbid mRS of 0 or 1
- Internal carotid or M1 segment MCA occlusion on CTA/MRA
- ASPECTS score 3–5
- Within 6 hours of last known well, or 6–24 hours with recent infarct on MRI FLAIR sequence
- EVT initiated within 60 minutes post-randomisation

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Clinically significant mass effect with midline shift
- Acute intracranial hemorrhage
- Pregnancy or suspected pregnancy
- Evidence of chronic occlusion
- High hemorrhage risk (Plts <40, APTT >50, INR >3)
- Likely non-compliance or uncooperativeness

**Enrollment:**
- 235 assessed for eligibility; 203 randomised
- 100 in EVT group (6 excluded post-adjudication); 94 per protocol
- 102 in medical group (8 excluded); 94 per protocol

## Intervention

**EVT Group:**
- Endovascular therapy and medical care
- EVT method chosen by treating physicians (e.g., stent retriever, aspiration catheter)
- 98% underwent thrombectomy
- Success defined as TICI2b reperfusion

## Control

**Medical Care Group:**
- Medical care only
- rt-tPA allowed per Japanese guidelines
- Medical care followed AHA and American Stroke Association guidelines

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- mRS of 0-3 at 90 days: EVT 31% vs Medical Care 12.7%
  - Relative Risk 2.43 (95% CI 1.35–4.37), p = 0.002
  - Absolute Risk Reduction 18.3% (95% CI 7.1–18.3%)
  - Number Needed to Treat 6 (95% CI 3.4–14.1)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in mRS of 0-2 or 0-1 at 90 days
- Significant ordinal shift in mRS towards better outcomes in EVT group
- Improvement in NIHSS ≥8 points at 48 hours: EVT 31.0% vs Medical Care 8.8%

### Safety Outcomes
- Higher rates of any intracranial hemorrhage within 48 hours in EVT group
- Similar rates of symptomatic ICH
- Other safety outcomes, including death and recurrence of infarction, were similar

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients with acute stroke and large ischemic regions, endovascular therapy with medical care improved 90-day functional outcomes compared to medical care alone, despite a higher incidence of intracranial hemorrhage.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised trial addressing an important clinical Point
  - Balanced baseline characteristics
  - Intention-to-treat analysis with minimal drop-out
  - Trained and blinded assessors for primary outcome
- **Weaknesses**
  - Single-country setting may limit external validity
  - Low participant numbers in many centers
  - mRS only assesses physical ability; no other QoL metrics reported
  - High MRI use for ASPECTS scoring, which may differ from other countries' practices
  - Low rt-PA use compared to other countries

## Bottom Line
This trial supports discussing EVT use in large ischemic strokes, but decisions should be multidisciplinary, considering external generalisability, higher hemorrhage rates, and significant disability in many patients.
