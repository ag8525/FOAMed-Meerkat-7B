summary: |
  This randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial investigated the effect of adrenaline on survival in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients. Conducted in Western Australia, the study included 534 adult patients, with adrenaline administered every 3 minutes after the third unsuccessful shock or upon IV access in non-shockable rhythms. The primary outcome showed no significant improvement in survival to hospital discharge with adrenaline (4%) compared to placebo (1.9%), with an odds ratio of 2.2 (p = 0.15). However, adrenaline significantly increased the rate of pre-hospital return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) to 25.3% versus 8.4% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). Despite these findings, the study's limited sample size and the small number of survivors to discharge restrict the ability to draw definitive conclusions, highlighting the need for larger trials to assess the impact of adrenaline on long-term survival and neurological outcomes.

# Effect of Adrenaline on Survival in Out-of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: A Randomised Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Trial

## Clinical Point
In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does adrenaline, compared to placebo, improve survival to hospital discharge?

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Double-blinded
- Randomisation at the time adrenaline was required

## Setting
- Pre-hospital setting in Western Australia
- Study period: 11 August 2006 to 30 November 2009

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Resuscitation not commenced

**Enrollment:**
- 534 patients included out of 601 randomised

## Intervention
- Adrenaline 1 mg every 3 minutes
- Administered after the third unsuccessful shock or after IV access in non-shockable rhythms

## Control
- Placebo administered at the same intervals as adrenaline

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Survival to hospital discharge:**
  - Adrenaline group: 4%
  - Placebo group: 1.9%
  - Odds ratio: 2.2 (95% CI: 0.7–6.3), p = 0.15

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Pre-hospital return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC):**
  - Adrenaline group: 25.3%
  - Placebo group: 8.4%
  - p < 0.001

- **Cerebral performance category 1 or 2 (good neurological outcome) in survivors:**
  - Adrenaline group: 100%
  - Placebo group: 81.8%
  - p = 0.31

## Authors’ Conclusions
Adrenaline improves ROSC but does not improve survival to hospital discharge.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised and double-blinded design
  - Randomisation occurred at the time of adrenaline administration, ensuring relevance to the studied population
- **Weaknesses**
  - Planned recruitment was 5000 patients, but only 534 were included due to withdrawal of ambulance services following adverse press and political lobbying
  - Only 16 patients survived to hospital discharge, limiting the ability to draw definitive conclusions

## Bottom Line
Adrenaline increases ROSC following cardiac arrest but does not improve survival to hospital discharge or neurological outcomes. Larger trials are needed to determine the definitive impact of adrenaline on survival and neurological outcomes.
