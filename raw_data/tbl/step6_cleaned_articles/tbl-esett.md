  The ESETT trial investigated the efficacy of levetiracetam, valproate, and fosphenytoin as second-line treatments for benzodiazepine-refractory status epilepticus in a randomized, blinded, and adaptive design across 57 U.S. emergency departments. # Randomized Trial of Three Anticonvulsant Medications for Status Epilepticus (ESETT)

## Clinical Point
In patients with status epilepticus who have been treated with benzodiazepines, is levetiracetam, valproate, or fosphenytoin more effective in leading to seizure cessation and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes?

## Background
Benzodiazepines are the first-line treatment for seizures, but there is limited evidence guiding second-line treatments. Previous trials (ConSEPT and EcLIPSE) compared levetiracetam and phenytoin in pediatric status epilepticus, finding no superiority of levetiracetam.

## Design
- Randomised controlled trial
- Investigator-initiated
- Blinded
- Response-adaptive comparative-effectiveness design
- Age-stratified "use next" medication box for allocation concealment
- Registered on clinicaltrials.gov
- Primary outcome determined by treating ED physician
- Follow-up until hospital discharge or 30 days

## Setting
- 57 Emergency Departments in the USA
- Included academic centers and community hospitals
- 18 pediatric departments, 26 adult departments, 13 mixed sites
- Data collected from 2015 to 2017

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age ≥ 2 years
- Treated with a minimally accepted cumulative dose of benzodiazepines
- Generalized convulsive seizure lasting >5 minutes
- Persistent or recurrent seizures 5-30 minutes after last benzodiazepine dose

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Seizure precipitant: major trauma, hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, cardiac arrest, post-anoxia, pregnancy
- Intubated
- Known contraindications to trial drugs
- Patients already on anticonvulsants were included

**Enrollment:**
- 720 patients provided 90% power to detect treatment differences
- Stopped early at interim analysis due to futility criteria

## Intervention

**Levetiracetam Group:**
- 60 mg/kg (max 4500 mg)

**Valproate Group:**
- 40 mg/kg (max 3000 mg)

## Control

**Fosphenytoin Group:**
- 20 mgPE/kg (max 1500 mg)

**Common Management:**
- Drugs administered via infusion pump over 10 minutes
- Rescue therapy as clinically determined for persistent seizures after 20 minutes
- Unmasking allowed after primary outcome determination at 60 minutes

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Absence of clinically apparent seizures and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes without additional anticonvulsant medication
  - Levetiracetam: 47%
  - Fosphenytoin: 45%
  - Valproate: 46%
  - Posterior probability of being most effective: 0.41 (levetiracetam), 0.24 (fosphenytoin), 0.35 (valproate)

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in ICU admission, safety outcomes, or acute seizure recurrence

## Authors’ Conclusions
In benzodiazepine-refractory convulsive status epilepticus, levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, and valproate each led to seizure cessation and improved alertness in approximately half of the patients, with similar adverse event rates.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Large sample size
  - Weight-based dosing
  - Adaptive statistical design
  - Double-blinded
- **Weaknesses**
  - Inclusion of patients not meeting enrollment criteria
  - Unblinding occurred in 200 out of 400 enrollments
  - Early termination
  - Inclusion of both children and adults, potentially affecting treatment response

## Bottom Line
In benzodiazepine-refractory status epilepticus, levetiracetam, valproate, and fosphenytoin each led to seizure cessation and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes in nearly half of the patients.
