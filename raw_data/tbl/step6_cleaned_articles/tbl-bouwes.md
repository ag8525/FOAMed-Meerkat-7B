# Prognosis of Coma after Therapeutic Hypothermia: A Prospective Cohort Study

## Clinical Point
In patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest, can clinical examination, neuron-specific enolase (NSE), and median nerve somatosensory-evoked potentials (SEPs) predict poor outcomes?

## Design
- Prospective observational cohort study
- Blinding of treating physicians to NSE results and initial SEP results
- Non-randomised

## Setting
- 10 Dutch mixed medical-surgical intensive care units
- Study period: December 2007 to August 2009

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients who remained in coma after CPR
- Treated with therapeutic hypothermia (32°C–34°C)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pre-existing disease with life expectancy <6 months
- Severe disability before CPR
- CPR due to hypovolemic shock

**Enrollment:**
- 391 patients included
- Mean age: 64 years
- Presenting rhythm of VF/VT: 76.5%
- Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: 86.7%
- Median time to bystander BLS: 2 minutes
- Median time to ROSC: 20 minutes (IQR: 11-30)

## Tests of Interest
- **Neurological Examination:** GCS and brainstem reflexes at 48 and 72 hours post-CPR
- **Neuron-specific Enolase (NSE):** Serum samples at ICU admission, 12 hours after target temperature, and 36-48 hours post-collapse
- **Cortical N20 SEP:** Performed during hypothermia and repeated when normothermic and sedatives cleared

## Gold Standard Test
- Telephone interview at 6 months to determine Glasgow Outcome Scale

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death, vegetative state, or severe disability (Glasgow Outcome Scale 1-3) at 6 months
- 53% of the study population had a poor outcome

### Accuracy of Tests
- **Specificity of Tests:**
  - Neurological examination at 72 hours: 90-99%
  - NSE > 33 μg/L: 90-93%
  - N20 SEP during hypothermia: 98%
  - N20 SEP during normothermia: 100%
- **Optimum NSE Cutoff:**
  - Levels at 48 hours had the largest area under ROC curve: 0.82 (95% CI: 0.77-0.86)
  - NSE >81.8 µg/L had specificity of 100% and sensitivity of 18%

## Authors’ Conclusions
In patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest, absent pupillary light responses or corneal reflexes at 72 hours, or bilaterally absent cortical N20 SEP, can reliably predict a poor outcome.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Prospective, multi-centre study
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Blinding of treating physicians to NSE and initial SEP results
- **Weaknesses**
  - Clinicians not blinded to repeat SEP results, potentially leading to a self-fulfilling prophecy
  - Unclear duration before determining sedative clearance
  - Unstated blinding of primary outcome assessors
  - Disagreement in SEP interpretation without reported Kappa score
  - Post-hoc analysis included a small number of patients
  - Wide confidence intervals due to moderate sample size

## Bottom Line
In patients treated with therapeutic hypothermia following cardiac arrest, clinical examination at 72 hours, neuron-specific enolase, and somatosensory-evoked potentials can help predict poor outcomes. Due to methodological limitations and wide confidence intervals, a multimodal approach is recommended as per ERC/ESICM guidelines.
