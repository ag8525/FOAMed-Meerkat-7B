  The SHoC-ED trial investigated whether a standardized point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) protocol improves survival outcomes in emergency department patients with undifferentiated hypotension compared to usual care. Conducted across six centers in North America and South Africa, this multicenter randomized controlled trial included 273 patients and utilized a POCUS protocol combining elements of ACES and RUSH. Despite the rigorous design and standardized protocol, the study found no significant difference in 30-day or hospital discharge survival between the POCUS and control groups. Secondary outcomes, including fluid administration, hospital admission rates, and ICU metrics, also showed no significant differences. The trial's early termination due to slow recruitment and futility, along with the inherent limitations of POCUS as a diagnostic tool, suggest that while POCUS remains valuable for diagnosis and management, its impact on mortality in this context is inconclusive.

# Does Point-of-Care Ultrasonography Improve Clinical Outcomes in Emergency Department Patients With Undifferentiated Hypotension? An International Randomized Controlled Trial From the SHoC-ED Investigators

## Clinical Point
In selected non-trauma patients presenting to the Emergency Department (ED) with undifferentiated hypotension, does a standardised point-of-care ultrasound protocol, compared with usual care, improve 30-day or hospital survival?

## Background
Patients with undifferentiated shock present a diagnostic challenge in the ED, with increased mortality. Point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) is used to diagnose various pathologies in the ED. Single-centre studies and case series have shown improved diagnostic accuracy and mortality with POCUS, directing management changes in up to 50% of patients. However, no multicentre prospective randomised control trials of POCUS in shock in the ED have been conducted.

## Design
- Multicentre randomised control trial
- Non-blinded
- All centres had accredited EM physicians and active POCUS programmes
- Scans performed by trained physicians or supervised residents
- Competencies agreed upon before study initiation
- Randomised convenience-sampling blocks by site, using computer-generated randomisation into 100 sealed envelopes (50 POCUS, 50 control)
- Allocation concealment ensured by sealed envelopes
- Sample size of 400 required for 80% power to detect a 10% difference in primary outcome at alpha = 0.05, assuming baseline mortality of 30%

## Setting
- 6 Centres: 3 in North America (tertiary centres), 3 in South Africa (1 tertiary, 1 large regional, 1 large district hospital)
- Study period: September 2012 to December 2016

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Age 19 or over
- Sustained systolic BP <100 mmHg OR Shock index (systolic BP/HR) >1.0 (with systolic BP <120)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy
- Necessity of CPR before screening/enrolment
- Significant trauma in last 24 hours
- 12-lead ECG diagnostic of myocardial infarction
- Clear mechanism for shock identifiable by clinician
- Previously known diagnosis from another hospital
- Vagal episode
- Non-pathological low blood pressure

**Enrollment:**
- 347 patients assessed, 273 enrolled (138 POCUS, 135 Control)
- Terminated at interim analysis due to slow recruitment and futility
- 3 patients lost to follow-up
- Similar baseline characteristics, slight male preponderance in POCUS group (73% vs 65%)
- Significant differences in final diagnoses between North American and South African cohorts

## Intervention
- POCUS protocol combining elements of ACES and RUSH protocols
- Performed within 60 minutes of patient visit, after normal clinical assessment
- Documented on standardised form
- Views: Cardiac (PLAX, PSAX, Apical, Subcostal), Lung (base scans), Abdominal (RUQ, LUQ, pelvic, aorta, IVC)

## Control
- Normal physician clinical assessment
- Management common to both groups
- Secondary clinical assessment 60 minutes after primary assessment

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- No significant difference in 30-day or hospital discharge survival
  - POCUS: 104/136 (76.5%, 95% CI 68.4%-83.3%)
  - Control: 102/134 (76.1%, 95% CI 68.0%-83.1%)
  - Difference = 0.35%, 95% CI = -10.2 to 11.1%

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant differences in:
  - Median fluid volumes administered
  - Hospital admission rates
  - Inotrope administration rates
  - CT scanning rates
  - ICU admission rates
  - ICU length of stay

## Authors’ Conclusions
In ED patients with undifferentiated hypotension, the addition of a POCUS protocol did not translate to additional benefit.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised with good allocation concealment
  - Registered with clinicaltrials.gov
  - Very few patients lost to follow-up
  - Standardised POCUS protocol limiting inter-observer bias
  - Pragmatic approach to an important research Point
- **Weaknesses**
  - POCUS is a diagnostic test; expecting a mortality benefit may be a cognitive fallacy
  - Convenience sampling slowed recruitment
  - Exclusion of patients with high suspicion of certain diagnoses biased in favour of control
  - Early termination reduced statistical power
  - Incomplete POCUS studies
  - No suggestion that POCUS findings altered management

## Bottom Line
In this trial of selected ED patients with undifferentiated shock, the use of a POCUS protocol did not affect 30-day or in-hospital mortality. I will continue to use targeted POCUS to diagnose and manage patients with shock, awaiting further trials on its efficacy and effectiveness.
