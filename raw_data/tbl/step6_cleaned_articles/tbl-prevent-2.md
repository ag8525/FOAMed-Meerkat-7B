summary: |
  The PreVent trial investigated the impact of bag-mask ventilation (BMV) on oxygen saturation levels during tracheal intubation in critically ill adults in the ICU. Conducted across seven US ICUs, this randomized trial compared BMV with no ventilation between induction and laryngoscopy. The study found that BMV significantly improved the lowest oxygen saturation levels and reduced the incidence of severe hypoxaemia (oxygen saturation <80%) compared to no ventilation. Despite these benefits, the trial was not powered to assess the risk of aspiration, and its findings may not apply to emergent intubations or high-risk patients. The study underscores BMV as a potentially effective strategy to mitigate hypoxia during intubation, though further research is needed to evaluate aspiration risks.

# Bag-Mask Ventilation during Tracheal Intubation of Critically Ill Adults (PreVent Trial)

## Clinical Point
In adult ICU patients undergoing tracheal intubation, does bag-mask ventilation (BMV) between induction and laryngoscopy reduce the risk of hypoxaemia?

## Background
- Intubation in ICU has high morbidity and mortality.
- BMV may prevent hypoxia but can increase aspiration risk.
- DAS guidelines suggest gentle mask ventilation prolongs time to desaturation.

## Design
- Multi-centre, parallel-group, pragmatic, non-blinded randomised trial.
- Randomisation: 1:1 ratio with permuted variable block size.
- 90% power calculation based on a 5% difference in lowest oxygen saturation.
- 401 patients enrolled: 199 BMV, 202 no ventilation.

## Setting
- Seven ICUs in the USA.
- Study period: March 2017 to May 2018.

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult ICU patients >18 years requiring tracheal intubation.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Pregnancy, incarceration, need for immediate intubation, high risk of hypoxaemia or aspiration, inability to tolerate hypercapnoea.

## Intervention
- BMV with 100% oxygen at 10 breaths/min using an oropharyngeal airway.
- Structured education on BMV best practices.

## Control
- No ventilation between induction and laryngoscopy.
- Pre-oxygenation and apnoeic oxygenation allowed in both groups.

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Lowest oxygen saturation during induction to 2 minutes post-intubation:
  - BMV: 96% (IQR 87-99)
  - No ventilation: 93% (IQR 81-99)
  - Mean difference: 4.7 percentage points (95% CI: 2.5–6.8), p = 0.01

### Secondary Outcomes
- Severe hypoxaemia (O₂ saturation <80%):
  - BMV: 10.9%
  - No ventilation: 22.8%
  - Relative risk: 0.48 (95% CI: 0.30–0.77)

## Authors’ Conclusions
BMV during the interval between induction and laryngoscopy results in higher oxygen saturations and lower rates of severe hypoxaemia compared to no ventilation.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Relevant study on ICU intubation practices.
  - Multi-centre RCT with appropriate power calculation.
  - Standardised BMV technique with training.
- **Weaknesses**
  - Did not address patient-centred outcomes.
  - Unblinded study with potential allocation concealment issues.
  - Differences in baseline variables.
  - Not applicable to emergent intubations or high-risk patients.
  - Underpowered to assess aspiration risk.

## Bottom Line
BMV appears to be a reasonable intervention to prevent hypoxia during intubation, though the study is underpowered to assess aspiration risk.
