# LEAP2: Oral Lefamulin versus Moxifloxacin for Community-Acquired Bacterial Pneumonia

## Clinical Point
Is 5-day oral lefamulin non-inferior to 7-day oral moxifloxacin in the management of community-acquired bacterial pneumonia (CABP)?

## Background
- CABP is a leading cause of hospitalization and death in the United States.
- Current first-line treatments include macrolides, β-lactams, and fluoroquinolones.
- Lefamulin is the first pleuromutilin antibiotic approved for intravenous and oral use in humans, inhibiting protein synthesis via interactions with ribosomal RNA.
- LEAP1 demonstrated non-inferiority of lefamulin to moxifloxacin in moderate to severe CABP when initiated intravenously.

## Design
- Phase 3, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel-group randomized clinical trial.
- Patients were randomized 1:1 with a block size of 6 and stratified by PORT risk class, geographic region, and prior receipt of short-acting antibiotic therapy for CABP.
- Intention-to-treat (ITT) design included all randomized patients.
- Additional population analyses: Modified ITT, Clinically evaluable, and Microbiological ITT populations.
- Sample size: 738 patients randomized 1:1, providing 90% power to establish non-inferiority for early clinical response.

## Setting
- 99 sites in 19 countries across Europe, North America, South America, Asia, and Africa.
- Study period: August 2016 to January 2018.

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with PORT risk class II, III, or IV radiographically documented pneumonia.
- Acute illness (≤7 days) with 3 or more CABP symptoms (dyspnea, new or increased cough, purulent sputum production, and chest pain).

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- More than 1 dose of a short-acting oral or intravenous antibacterial for CABP within 72 hours before randomization.
- Hospitalization for 2 days or longer within 90 days.
- Confirmed or suspected methicillin-resistant S. aureus.
- At risk of major cardiac events or dysfunction.
- Significant hepatic disease.

**Enrollment:**
- 738 randomized patients; 685 completed treatment.

## Intervention

**Lefamulin Group:**
- Lefamulin 600 mg orally every 12 hours for 5 days.
- Plus oral moxifloxacin placebo every 24 hours for 7 days.

## Control

**Moxifloxacin Group:**
- Moxifloxacin 400 mg orally every 24 hours for 7 days.
- Plus oral lefamulin placebo every 12 hours for 5 days.

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- **Early clinical response at 96 hours (US FDA definition):**
  - Lefamulin: 90.8%
  - Moxifloxacin: 90.8%
  - Absolute Difference: 0.1% (1-sided 97.5% CI: -4.4% to ∞)

### Co-primary Outcomes
- **Investigator-assessed clinical response at test of cure (5-10 days post-treatment, EMA definition):**
  - Lefamulin: 87.5%
  - Moxifloxacin: 89.1%
  - Difference: -1.6% (1-sided 97.5% CI: -6.3% to ∞)

### Secondary Outcomes
- **Investigator-assessed clinical response for clinically evaluable population:**
  - Lefamulin: 89.7%
  - Moxifloxacin: 93.6%
  - Difference: -3.9% (1-sided 97.5% CI: -8.2% to ∞)

**Adverse Outcomes:**
- Treatment-emergent adverse events: Lefamulin 32.6%, Moxifloxacin 25.0%.
- Common adverse events for Lefamulin: Diarrhea (12.2%), Nausea (5.2%), Vomiting (3.3%).
- Serious adverse events leading to death: 3 in Lefamulin group, 3 in Moxifloxacin group (none related to study drug).

## Authors’ Conclusions
This study demonstrated the non-inferiority of oral lefamulin to oral moxifloxacin for the treatment of CABP.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Appropriate design to Point the clinical Point.
  - Non-inferiority methodology allows efficient study and appropriate conclusion.
  - Randomization method and prior-concealment prevent recruitment bias.
  - Stratification and block randomization reduce uneven allocation by chance.
  - Study sponsors were blinded to treatment allocation unless unblinding was necessary for medical management.
  - CABP endpoints defined by regulatory bodies in the US and Europe.
  - Sponsor did not have the right to veto or suppress publication.

- **Weaknesses**
  - Potential bias as study sponsors were responsible for design, conduct, data management, and manuscript preparation.
  - Some secondary endpoints not reported.
  - Broad exclusion criteria may limit generalizability.
  - Limited MIC data due to non-culture identification of many pathogens.
  - Patients with suspected MRSA were excluded due to poor coverage with moxifloxacin.

## Bottom Line
Despite potential sources of bias, this is a generally well-designed study. Oral lefamulin appears efficacious for CABP treatment, albeit with a higher rate of adverse reactions. Cost may be a barrier, and antimicrobial stewardship may dictate reserving lefamulin to avoid resistance development.
