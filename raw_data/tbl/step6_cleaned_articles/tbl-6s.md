# Hydroxyethyl Starch 130/0.42 versus Ringer’s Acetate in Severe Sepsis (6S Trial)

## Clinical Point
In critically ill adults with severe sepsis, does 6% hydroxyethyl starch (HES) compared to Ringer’s acetate reduce the incidence of death or end-stage kidney failure?

## Background
- Septic shock involves fluid deficiency and vasoplegia.
- Intravenous fluid therapy is crucial, with guidelines recommending up to 30 ml/kg as boluses.
- Colloids and crystalloids are used for volume restoration.
- High molecular weight and substitution ratio starches are linked to acute kidney injury.
- This trial investigates a starch-based colloid with lower molecular weight and substitution ratio.

## Design
- Investigator-initiated, blinded, stratified, parallel-group clinical trial.
- Computer-generated allocation sequence.
- Concealed treatment assignments.
- Randomisation stratified by shock presence, haematological cancer, and hospital type.
- 800 patients needed for 80% power to detect a 10% difference in primary outcome at 0.05 significance level.

## Setting
- 26 ICUs (13 university, 13 non-university) in Denmark, Finland, Norway, and Iceland.
- Data collected from December 2009 to November 2011.

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adults needing fluid resuscitation in ICU.
- Severe sepsis within the previous 24 hours (sepsis plus ≥1 organ failure).
- Organ failure defined as SOFA score >2.

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- Medical reasons (e.g., burns, intracranial bleeding, renal replacement therapy).
- >1000 ml synthetic colloid received in the previous 24 hours.
- Enrolled in another ICU study.

**Enrollment:**
- 1211 assessed, 804 randomised (400 in each group).

## Intervention

**Starch Group:**
- 6% HES 130/0.42 in Ringer’s acetate (Tetraspan 6%) for volume expansion.
- Prepared by non-trial staff, hidden by opaque bag.

## Control

**Ringer’s Acetate Group:**
- Ringer’s acetate for volume expansion.
- Prepared by non-trial staff, hidden by opaque bag.

## Management Common to Both Groups
- Routine management maintained, only resuscitation fluid altered.
- Fluids given for up to 90 days.
- Maximum daily dose: 33 ml/kg of ideal body weight.
- Further fluid (if needed) was open-label Ringer’s acetate.
- Trial fluid stopped if bleeding or renal replacement therapy occurred.

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Composite of death or dialysis dependence at 90 days:
  - HES Group: 51%
  - Ringer’s Acetate Group: 43%
  - Relative risk (RR): 1.17 (95% CI 1.01–1.36; p=0.03)
  - Absolute risk increase (ARI): 7.5% (95% CI 0.6%–14.4%)
  - Number needed to harm (NNH): 14
  - Fragility Index: 2

### Secondary Outcomes
- Renal replacement therapy: 22% HES vs 16% Ringer’s acetate (RR: 1.35; p=0.04)
- Severe bleeding: 10% HES vs 6% Ringer’s acetate (RR: 1.52; p=0.09)
- Death at 28 days: 39% HES vs 36% Ringer’s acetate (RR: 1.08; p=0.43)

## Authors’ Conclusions
Patients with severe sepsis receiving hydroxyethyl starch had a higher risk of death within 90 days and were more likely to require renal replacement therapy compared to those receiving Ringer’s acetate.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Low risk of ascertainment bias due to blinding.
  - Effective randomisation and allocation concealment.
  - Pragmatic approach with few exclusion criteria, enhancing generalisability.
- **Weaknesses**
  - Some patients received fluid volumes exceeding protocol.
  - Routine management not standardised across sites.
  - No haemodynamic monitoring used.
  - Composite outcome may reduce power to conclude association with each component independently.
  - Low Fragility Index suggests potential statistical fluke.

## Bottom Line
The trial suggests hydroxyethyl starch increases the risk of death and renal replacement therapy in severe sepsis, aligning with other similar trials. Caution is advised in using starch-based fluids in this population.
