# Early Restrictive or Liberal Fluid Management for Sepsis-Induced Hypotension (CLOVERS Trial)

## Clinical Point
In patients with sepsis-induced hypotension, does a restrictive fluid strategy (with early vasopressor usage) compared to a liberal fluid strategy result in lower mortality before discharge by day 90?

## Background
- The 2021 SSC Guidelines recommend large volumes of fluid (30 ml/kg) during initial resuscitation of septic shock, based on low-quality evidence.
- Practice varies widely; an Australian study showed a median of 4.2L of fluid given to septic patients within the first 24 hours.
- The CLASSIC trial showed no difference in 90-day mortality between restrictive fluid strategy and standard care, but randomisation occurred post-initial fluid resuscitation.

## Design
- Multi-centre, randomised, unblinded superiority trial
- Patients randomly assigned 1:1 to restrictive or liberal strategies
- Randomisation via central web-based system, stratified by trial site
- Protocol amended in October 2019
- Study stopped at the second interim analysis due to futility, with no harm concerns from DSMB
- Informed consent required from patients or legal representatives
- Protocol adherence monitored for first 300 patients and in 10% (random sample) during remainder of trial

## Setting
- 60 US centres
- Study period: March 2018 to January 2022

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- Adult patients with suspected/confirmed infection
- Sepsis-induced hypotension (SBP < 100 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg after > 1000 ml IV fluid)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- >4 hours since meeting inclusion criteria for sepsis-induced hypotension
- >24 hours since hospital presentation
- >3L IVT (including pre-hospital)
- Inability to obtain informed consent
- Pregnancy
- Fluid overload
- Blood pressure at known or reported baseline level
- Hypotension suspected to be from non-sepsis cause
- Severe volume depletion from non-sepsis causes

**Enrollment:**
- 12,276 met inclusion criteria → 4,868 met eligibility criteria → 1,563 randomised
  - 782 to restrictive group
  - 781 to liberal group

## Intervention

**Restrictive Group:**
- If SBP < 100 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg after 1–3L crystalloid:
  - Cease all bolus and maintenance fluids
  - Up to 2L fluid boluses allowed at clinician's discretion
  - Titrate norepinephrine ± second vasopressor aiming MAP > 65 mmHg
  - Fluids limited to KVO, medications, and nutrition once MAP in target
  - Rescue fluids recommended for severe or refractory hypotension, high lactate, high HR, or extreme hypovolaemia

## Control

**Liberal Group:**
- If SBP < 100 mmHg or MAP < 65 mmHg after 1–3L crystalloid:
  - Halt all maintenance
  - Prescribe 2L at randomisation (to be completed within 180 mins)
  - Further 500 ml bolus if MAP < 65, high lactate, low UOP, high HR, or vasopressor requirement
  - Rescue vasopressors allowed for severe hypotension, high lactate, or fluid overload

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Death before discharge home by day 90: 14.0% in restrictive group vs 14.9% in liberal group
  - Estimated difference: -0.9% (95% CI: -4.4 to 2.6), p = 0.61

### Efficacy and Safety Outcomes
- No significant difference in:
  - Days free from organ support therapy by day 28
  - Days free from ventilator use at 28 days
  - Days out of ICU from day 1 to 28
  - New intubation with mechanical ventilation by day 28
  - ARDS onset between day 1 and day 7
- Serious Adverse Events: 3 vs 0 counts of extravasation of vasopressors in restrictive group (all managed conservatively)

### Subgroup Analysis
- No subgroup favoured liberal or restrictive fluid use

### Post-hoc Analysis
- Higher rates of ICU admission in restrictive group

## Authors’ Conclusions
A restrictive fluid strategy (with earlier vasopressor use) did not result in significantly lower (or higher) mortality before discharge home by day 90 than a liberal fluid strategy.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Randomised, multi-centre trial with broad inclusion criteria
  - High internal validity and protocol adherence
  - Achieved separation in fluid administered in first 24 hours
  - Excellent safety outcome reporting
- **Weaknesses**
  - Unblinded
  - Single country may limit external validity
  - Early cessation
  - Large numbers eligible but not enrolled, introducing potential selection bias
  - Patients may not have been severely ill at baseline
  - No dynamic methods used to assess fluid responsiveness
  - Fluid regimes not adjusted for patient weight/size
  - Significant clinician preference allowed, introducing bias

## Bottom Line
This trial complements the CLASSIC trial regarding fluid restriction timing in critically ill septic patients. Both approaches appear safe, and a multi-modal approach to fluid and vasopressor management remains appropriate.
