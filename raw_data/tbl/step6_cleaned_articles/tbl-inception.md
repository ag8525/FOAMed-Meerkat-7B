# Early Extracorporeal CPR for Refractory Out-Of-Hospital Cardiac Arrest: The INCEPTION Trial

## Clinical Point
In patients with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) and an initial ventricular arrhythmia, does extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) improve survival with a favourable neurologic outcome compared to conventional CPR (CCPR)?

## Background
OHCA is a significant public health issue with high mortality. Refractory cardiac arrest is defined as lasting >15 minutes despite resuscitation attempts. Conventional CPR is often ineffective after 20-30 minutes. ECPR can restore perfusion and limit hypoxic brain injury in selected patients, but evidence from previous trials is limited and not generalisable.

## Design
- Multicentre, randomised, unblinded, parallel-group, investigator-initiated clinical trial

## Setting
- 10 cardiosurgical centres and 12 emergency medical services in the Netherlands
- Recruitment: May 2017 to February 2021
- Pauses during COVID-19 outbreaks in 2020 and 2021

## Population

**Inclusion Criteria:**
- OHCA patients aged 18-70 years
- Witnessed arrest
- Shockable initial rhythm (VF or VT)
- Refractory arrest (>15 minutes advanced life support)

**Exclusion Criteria:**
- ROSC with sustained recovery within 15 minutes
- Severe pulmonary disease, disseminated oncologic disease, pregnancy
- Bilateral femoral bypass surgery, contraindications for ECPR
- Advance directive prohibiting resuscitation
- Expected >60 minutes from arrest to cannulation
- Known severe neurologic disability before arrest
- Multi-injury trauma, terminal heart failure

**Enrollment:**
- 160 patients randomised
- 26 excluded post-randomisation

## Intervention

**ECPR Group:**
- Initiated by qualified physician via bifemoral approach
- No prespecified protocol; local hospital protocols used

## Control

**Conventional CPR Group:**
- Continued until ROSC or declaration of death

## Outcomes

### Primary Outcome
- Survival with favourable neurologic outcome (CPC score 1 or 2) at 30 days
  - ECPR: 14/70 (20%)
  - CCPR: 10/62 (16%)
  - OR 1.4; 95% CI, 0.5 to 3.5; P = 0.52

### Secondary Outcomes
- No significant difference in 3-month or 6-month survival with favourable neurologic outcome
- Higher ICU admission in ECPR group
- Adverse events more common in ECPR group

## Authors’ Conclusions
ECPR and CCPR had similar effects on survival with favourable neurologic outcomes at 30 days in patients with refractory OHCA due to ventricular arrhythmia.

## Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**
  - Web-based randomisation
  - Balanced baseline factors
  - Minimal loss to follow-up
  - Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Multicentre, broad metropolitan coverage
- **Weaknesses**
  - Only 66% of ECPR group received the intervention
  - Underpowered due to higher than expected control survival
  - Lack of blinding
  - Variability in intervention delivery
  - Prolonged low flow times

## Bottom Line
ECPR did not significantly improve neurologically favourable outcomes in this trial. The potential benefit of ECPR in different settings has not been disproven, and future research should focus on optimal systems for effective ECPR delivery.
