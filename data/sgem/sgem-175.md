## Summary
A randomized controlled trial by Motov et al. evaluated the analgesic efficacy of intravenous ketorolac at doses of 10mg, 15mg, and 30mg for acute pain management in the emergency department. The study found no significant difference in pain reduction among the three doses, with the 10mg dose effectively reducing pain scores from 7.7 to 5.2, similar to higher doses. Importantly, the 10mg dose did not increase adverse effects, which included dizziness, nausea, and headache, and did not necessitate additional rescue analgesia. Given the analgesic ceiling effect and the increased risk of gastrointestinal hemorrhage at higher doses, the study supports using 10mg IV ketorolac for moderate to severe pain, aligning with the authors' conclusion and the SGEM recommendation.

## Case
A 37-year-old presents to the emergency department with sudden onset right-sided flank pain, hematuria, and vomiting. He rates his pain as 8/10 and is writhing around on the stretcher. He says it feels like his previous kidney stones. His vital signs are within normal limits. You perform a quick bedside ultrasound, which reveals a normal aorta and mild right-sided hydronephrosis. You diagnose him with renal colic and he is requesting analgesia. He says morphine has made him nauseous in the past, but a shot of 30mg of ketorolac has helped. You decide to start by giving ketorolac and an anti-emetic. What dose of ketorolac do you give?

## Background
Ketorolac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used in emergency settings for moderate to severe acute pain management. It is characterized by an "analgesic ceiling," where increasing the dose beyond a certain point does not enhance pain relief but increases the risk of adverse effects. The analgesic ceiling for ketorolac is suggested to be 10 mg, which is significantly lower than the 30 mg to 60 mg doses often recommended. This is clinically significant due to ketorolac's higher risk of gastrointestinal hemorrhage compared to other NSAIDs, a risk that escalates with higher doses. Additionally, intramuscular doses of 15 mg, 30 mg, and 60 mg have been associated with increased post-operative bleeding.

## Clinical Point
Does 10mg of ketorolac intravenously (IV) provide similar analgesic efficacy for treatment of acute pain compared to doses of 15mg or 30mg?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Use 10mg IV ketorolac when treating moderate to severe pain in the emergency department.

## Case Resolution
You give the patient 10mg IV of ketorolac, his pain improves from an 8/10 to 5/10, and he would like to go home. He is given a prescription for analgesic to take home and discharge instructions.

## Clinical Application
Based on this high-quality randomized control trial and other information, I am going to begin using 10mg IV ketorolac for the management of moderate to severe pain in the emergency department.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
I can see you are in a lot of pain. We are going to give you a powerful drug called ketorolac through the intravenous line. Traditionally doctors have used 30mg of this drug but a recent high-quality study showed there is a "ceiling" to the effective dose and 10mg works just as well.
