## Summary
A recent trial, the WATERFALL study, evaluated fluid resuscitation strategies in acute pancreatitis, comparing aggressive versus moderate approaches. The study found that aggressive fluid resuscitation, defined as a 20 cc/kg bolus of lactated Ringer's followed by 3.0 cc/kg/hour, resulted in a higher incidence of fluid overload without improving clinical outcomes compared to a moderate strategy (10 cc/kg bolus followed by 1.5 cc/kg/hour). The trial, which was stopped early due to safety concerns, demonstrated a significant increase in fluid overload in the aggressive group (20.5% vs. 6.3% in the moderate group). The findings suggest that moderate fluid resuscitation should be preferred to avoid potential harm, as aggressive hydration did not reduce the progression to severe pancreatitis and was associated with increased fluid overload. This evidence supports a shift in clinical practice towards more conservative fluid management in acute pancreatitis.

## Case
A 38-year-old male presents to the emergency department (ED) with acute mid epigastric abdominal pain with nausea and vomiting. As part of the patient’s workup, he has an elevated lipase, and a CT abdomen and pelvis ultimately shows the patient to have acute pancreatitis. You remember a new trial was just published on whether to use aggressive versus nonaggressive goal-directed fluid resuscitation in the early phase of acute pancreatitis and wonder which would be better for this patient.

## Background
Acute pancreatitis is a condition characterized by inflammation of the pancreas, with standard management traditionally emphasizing hydration, analgesia, and investigation for underlying causes. The clinical significance of acute pancreatitis includes its potential severity and complications, which can be exacerbated by inappropriate treatment strategies. Recent evidence has questioned the routine use of aggressive large volume fluid resuscitation, suggesting it may increase the severity of the condition and lead to fluid overload. However, high-quality evidence demonstrating the harms of such aggressive fluid resuscitation in acute pancreatitis remains insufficient.

## Clinical Point
Does the use of a moderate fluid resuscitation strategy in acute pancreatitis decrease the rate of progression to moderate/severe pancreatitis in comparison to aggressive fluid resuscitation?

## Case Resolution
Based on this new high-quality level of evidence you decide to treat your patient with a moderate fluid strategy.

## Clinical Application
This high-quality randomized clinical trial should change clinical practice with the administration of smaller fluid boluses (10cc/kg) in patients with hypovolemia (and no bolus in those with normovolemia) with the addition of 1.5cc/kg/hr of maintenance fluids.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You have acute pancreatitis. This is inflammation of your pancreas. We would like to admit you to the hospital and treat your condition with pain medication and IV fluids. The newest evidence suggests we should give you a moderate amount of IV fluids. If we are too aggressive with IV fluids it may cause you harm.
