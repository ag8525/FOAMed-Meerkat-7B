## Summary
The document discusses the application of the Ottawa Ankle Rule (OAR) in children to determine the necessity of x-rays for ankle and midfoot injuries. The OAR, originally validated in adults, is shown to be a reliable tool for excluding fractures in children over five years old, significantly reducing unnecessary x-rays. The rule requires x-rays only if there is tenderness in specific areas or inability to bear weight. A meta-analysis involving 3,130 children demonstrated that the OAR has high sensitivity, missing only 10 fractures, most of which were clinically insignificant. The study concludes that the OAR can be safely applied in pediatric cases, optimizing x-ray use without compromising fracture detection.

## Case Scenario
An eight-year-old girl was brought into the emergency department by her mother after twisting her ankle on a trampoline. She was able to walk on it but it has become very swollen. The mother wants to know if she should get an x-ray to see if her daughter broke the ankle.

## Background
The Ottawa Ankle Rules (OAR), developed by Dr. Ian Stiell in 1992, are a widely validated clinical decision tool designed to reduce unnecessary radiography in acute ankle and midfoot injuries. The rules indicate that ankle or foot x-rays are warranted only if there is bony tenderness along the distal 6 cm of the posterior tibia or fibula (extending to the malleolar tips), at the base of the fifth metatarsal, or at the navicular bone, or if the patient is unable to bear weight for four steps immediately after injury and in the emergency department; they are not applicable to pregnant patients, those with intoxication, or head injuries. In the original studies involving 900 patients, the OAR demonstrated 100% sensitivity and 40% specificity for detecting fractures, meaning all clinically significant fractures were identified while potentially reducing radiographic imaging by more than one-third.

## Point
Can the Ottawa Ankle Rule safely exclude ankle and foot fractures in children?

## BEEM Bottom Line
Application of the OAR in children 5 and older can be safely used to guide x-ray utilization.
