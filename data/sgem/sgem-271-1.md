## Summary
The document discusses the use of a bougie as a first-line tool for intubation in patients with difficult airways, particularly in emergency settings. Traditionally considered a backup device, recent studies suggest that using a bougie can increase first-pass intubation success rates compared to the standard endotracheal tube with stylet. A retrospective study by Driver et al. showed a 95% success rate with bougie use versus 86% without, while a subsequent randomized clinical trial confirmed these findings with a 96% success rate for bougie use. Despite concerns about generalizability due to the study settings and operator experience, the evidence supports considering bougie use to improve intubation outcomes, especially in challenging airway scenarios. However, further randomized controlled trials are needed to validate these findings across different clinical environments.

## Case
You are preparing for a rapid sequence intubation in a patient suffering from respiratory distress. While doing your airway assessment you notice some difficult airway characteristics (obese patient with a small mouth opening). In the past you’ve had failed first pasts attempts on a similar patient and used a bougie as your back up device. You wonder if this time you would be more successful using the bougie for your first attempt.

## Background
The bougie is a medical device traditionally used as a backup or rescue tool during intubation, typically employed after failed attempts. However, multiple intubation attempts are associated with increased mortality and morbidity, highlighting the importance of improving first-pass intubation success rates to enhance patient outcomes.

## Clinical Point
Does using a bougie increase first pass intubation success?

## SGEM Bottom Line
The use of a bougie is associated with increased first pass success rates for intubations in the emergency department but an RCT is needed to further explore this topic.

## SGEM Bottom Line
Consider using a bougie to improve your first pass success rates in routine intubations and in those patients with difficult airway characteristics.

## Case Resolution
You decide to use the bougie on your first attempt for this patient in hope you will get the tube on your first attempt. You make sure your team is ready with the ETT. You visualize a grade 2 view and slip the coude’ tip under the epiglottis and through the cords. You feel the rumble strips as you advance to the hold up at the carina. The respiratory therapist slides the tube over the bougie, and you advance it to the proper depth. You have equal lung sounds on both sides and confirmation with wave form capnography.

**Clinical Application**: It all depends. I personally use a bougie only as a rescue device and have only missed one airway in 24 years. There is one doctor in my shop who uses it all the time. I think if you are comfortable with it and having great success then carry on with what works. If you have struggled in the past and found yourself reaching for the bougie more often than you would like as a rescue device than consider using it for the first attempt.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
Probably not a whole lot to tell your patient since they are likely preoccupied with breathing or are unresponsive.
