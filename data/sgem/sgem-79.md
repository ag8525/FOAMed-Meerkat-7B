## Summary
In the case of a 24-year-old woman presenting with symptoms indicative of bacterial meningitis, including fever, purpuric rash, and meningismus, the use of corticosteroids, specifically dexamethasone at 0.6mg/kg/day, is recommended alongside antibiotics in high-income countries. This approach, while not significantly reducing overall mortality, has been shown to decrease the risk of severe hearing loss and other neurological sequelae. The Cochrane review highlights that corticosteroids are particularly beneficial in reducing hearing loss in cases of H. influenzae meningitis in children and in high-income settings, but not in low-income countries. The administration of steroids should occur before or with the first dose of antibiotics to maximize potential benefits.

## Case Scenario
24-year-old woman presents to the emergency department with a 3-day history of progressively worse flu-like symptoms. She has a fever of 39.6C, HR 114, BP 120/80, and RR 22. You notice a purpuric rash and she has clinical meningismus. Her WBC is elevated and the lactate is borderline elevated. The lumbar puncture demonstrated turbid appearing fluid and no blood.

## Point
Do steroids prevent death, hearing loss, or other neurologic sequelae in bacterial meningitis?

## Background
Bacterial meningitis is a severe infection of the protective membranes covering the brain and spinal cord, with a fatality rate of 5-40% in children and 20-50% in adults, even with appropriate antibiotic treatment. It is clinically significant due to its association with serious neurological sequelae, including sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL), which affects up to one-third of survivors bilaterally. The etiology of SNHL in bacterial meningitis is multifactorial, involving the extension of infection along the eighth cranial nerve, cochlear pathology from serofibrinous exudate and inflammation, septic emboli and thrombotic occlusion due to vasculitis, and labyrinthitis ossificans, which is the pathological formation of new bone within the otic capsule. Historically, outcomes have been worse in lower-income countries.

## Case Resolution
This woman with suspected bacterial meningitis is given appropriate broad-spectrum IV antibiotics. Dexamethasone 0.6mg/kg/day is given at the same time. She is sent to the ICU and you hope she does well.

## Clinical Application
If practicing in a developed country, you should consider giving steroids with your antibiotics in patients with bacterial meningitis.

## What do I tell patients
We suspect you have bacterial meningitis. We are going to give you antibiotics. At the same time, we are going to give you steroids. This has been shown to prevent swelling, decrease hearing, and may help save your life.
