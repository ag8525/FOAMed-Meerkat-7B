## Summary
The document discusses the impact of rudeness on medical team performance, particularly in the context of NICU staff, and explores interventions to mitigate its effects. A study involving NICU teams exposed to rudeness during resuscitation simulations found that rudeness significantly impaired both medical and teamwork performance. Pre-exposure Cognitive Bias Modification (CBM) was effective in mitigating these negative effects, whereas post-exposure narrative interventions were not. The study highlights the potential for CBM to protect against the adverse impacts of rudeness, though its practical application remains limited without further evidence of long-term benefits. The findings suggest that while rudeness can detrimentally affect team dynamics and potentially patient care, interventions like CBM may offer a way to reduce its impact, though further research is needed to confirm these results in different clinical settings such as the ED. The document emphasizes the importance of fostering a culture of kindness and understanding within medical teams to enhance performance and patient safety.

## Case
You are working in the emergency department (ED) and have just been involved in a difficult case in the resuscitation room. During the resuscitation, a relative of the patient you have been treating named Rudy makes a derogatory/rude comment about Emergency Medicine (EM) staff.

## Background
Rudeness in medicine, whether from patients, their relatives, or within the medical team, is a pervasive issue that has traditionally been dismissed as part of the profession's culture. This behavior, often seen as a means of coping in a challenging environment, is increasingly recognized as a form of bullying and harassment that undermines staff development and collaborative culture. More critically, rudeness can impair staff performance, potentially endangering patient safety. While complete eradication of rudeness may be unrealistic, mitigating its impact through pre-emptive education or post-event debriefing is possible. Emphasizing kindness, as advocated by Dr. Brian Goldman, can transform interactions from adversarial to mutually beneficial, fostering empathy and understanding, and ultimately enhancing both personal and professional well-being.

## SGEM Bottom Line
Rudeness can have an impact upon how medical teams function and there may be a way to mitigate staff against this negative influence.

## Case Resolution
Whilst waiting for further information about the practical use of CBM, you decide to try and reduce the amount of rudeness in your department by setting a good example to others. You also have a private conversation with Rudy later about his derogatory comment about the EM staff and politely discuss how it could be perceived and negatively impact the team performance during resuscitations. In addition, you use this event as a teaching opportunity for your staff to explore the topic of rudeness, explain the impact it can have on team performance, and encourage them to try and always be kind to patients and each other.

## Clinical Application
We are unsure how to apply this information clinically. It does appear that rudeness can have an impact on team performance. What we do not know is if it has a negative impact on patient-oriented outcomes in the ED. In addition, would CBM mitigate the effect of rudeness in this clinical setting?

**What Do I Tell the Team?**
Sometimes patients and relatives can be rude to us, but we can also be very rude to each other. It is a practice of tribalism that has gone on for too long in medicine. This can have a negative influence on our team performance and could impact patient care and safety. Let’s try to be kind to each other and always remember that we are all working for the best interest of the patients.
