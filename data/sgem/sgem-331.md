## Summary
A 32-year-old female presented with a concussion following a minor motor vehicle collision, with no need for imaging due to a normal neurological exam. The document discusses the management of mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), highlighting a study comparing early light exercise to standard rest protocols. The study found no significant difference in post-concussive syndrome (PCS) rates at 30 days between patients advised to engage in light exercise and those told to rest until symptom resolution. Despite the lack of statistical significance, early light exercise may be encouraged as tolerated, as strict rest has not shown patient-oriented benefits. The study's limitations include potential biases, adherence issues, and a high loss to follow-up, which affect the strength of its conclusions. The clinical application suggests allowing light activity post-mTBI after a brief rest period, emphasizing the importance of preventing further head injuries.

## Case
A 32-year-old female presents with headache after a low-speed motor vehicle collision as a restrained driver. She was ambulatory on scene. The patient is not anticoagulated, has no midline neck pain, and no evidence of other injuries. She is generally well appearing without any deficiencies on neurological examination, given her minor mechanism, and normal examination no imaging or further testing is required. You tell her you believe she has a concussion.

## Background
Concussions, or mild traumatic brain injuries (mTBI), are frequently diagnosed in emergency departments, with most patients recovering within a week, though 15-30% experience persistent post-concussive symptoms. Traditionally, cognitive and physical rest have been advised post-mTBI, with exercise resumption recommended only after symptom resolution. However, recent studies suggest that early physical activity within a week of injury may expedite symptom reduction. Diagnostic imaging considerations include the Canadian CT Head Rule (CCHR) for adults and the PECARN protocol for pediatric patients, which guide the use of CT scans in minor head injuries. In children, a two-day rest followed by gradual activity resumption is favored over a five-day rest, as prolonged rest may exacerbate post-concussive symptoms. For patients aged 65 and older, the CCHR may help reduce unnecessary CT scans, though further research is needed. There remains limited guidance on preventing post-concussion syndrome.

## Clinical Point
Are patients presenting to the ED with mild concussion who are prescribed light exercise less likely to develop post-concussive syndrome at 30 days compared with those given standard discharge instructions?

## SGEM Bottom Line
After a couple days rest it is reasonable to suggest patients with minor traumatic brain injury start light-physical exercise if they are feeling well enough.

## Case Resolution
She is sent home with discharge instructions that allow for light physical exercise (30 minutes of daily walking) after a 48-hour period of rest.

## Clinical Application
When has strict bed rest been good for anything? Once people are feeling better it is reasonable for them to start some light activity. While this study does not provide robust evidence for light-physical activity we do not have good evidence that strict rest provides a patient-oriented benefit. Given the lack of compelling information to guide our care in either direction it reasonable to allow patients to start light activities a couple of days after their mTBI.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
It appears that you have a concussion. While you recover, it is very important to prevent another head injury, which means avoiding things like contact sports. Most people get better within a few days, but some people do have persistent symptoms. While we recommend you take it easy over the next couple of days, if you are feeling better after that point, you can begin re-introducing some very light exercise like walking. If you are getting worse or are worried, please return to the ED for reassessment.
