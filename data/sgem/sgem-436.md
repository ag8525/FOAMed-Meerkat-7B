## Summary
In this clinical case, a 70-year-old woman presented with acute ischemic stroke symptoms, including right hemiplegia and aphasia, with a proximal middle cerebral artery occlusion identified on imaging. The TIMELESS trial investigated the efficacy of Tenecteplase (TNK) administered between 4.5 to 24 hours post-stroke onset, using perfusion imaging for patient selection. The study concluded that TNK did not improve clinical outcomes compared to placebo in patients with large vessel occlusions, with no significant difference in functional independence or reperfusion rates, though recanalization was higher at 24 hours with TNK. Safety outcomes showed similar rates of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and mortality between groups. The trial's findings suggest that thrombolytics beyond 4.5 hours offer no benefit and may pose increased risks, reinforcing the preference for mechanical thrombectomy in such cases. The patient in this case underwent successful thrombectomy with full reperfusion achieved.

## Case
A 70-year-old woman was brought into the emergency department by EMS after her family reported she was having trouble talking. They noticed this earlier in the day and let her rest, but when she had trouble moving her right arm, they called 911. Initial evaluation by medics revealed right hemiplegia, a right facial droop, left gaze deviation, and aphasia. When she arrives in your ED, her family tells you she was last seen normal about 12 hours ago. A code stroke is activated, and the initial CT head shows no signs of hemorrhage or early ischemic changes. A CT angiogram shows a proximal middle cerebral artery occlusion. CT perfusion showed a 10 mL core and 189 mL penumbra. As you speak to your stroke team, the Point of thrombolysis comes up, as her core is quite small, and the stroke may still be very early in its time course.

## Background
Thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke, initially established by the NINDS trial in 1995, has evolved significantly with numerous studies assessing its benefits and risks. The standard of care has shifted towards mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion, especially after the 2015 thrombectomy trials and the 2018 extension of the thrombectomy window following the DAWN and DEFUSE-3 trials. Thrombolysis remains applicable within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Tenecteplase (TNK) has replaced alteplase (tPA) as the preferred thrombolytic agent in many stroke centers after extensive research since 2012. Perfusion imaging, which identifies salvageable ischemic tissue, aids in patient selection for reperfusion therapies but requires advanced software typically available only at comprehensive stroke centers. The DEFUSE-3 trial provides the standard criteria for intervention.

## Clinical Point
Does giving TNK between 4.5 – 24 hours, using perfusion imaging to select patients, improve outcomes after stroke?

## SGEM Bottom Line
There continues to be a lack of evidence to support the use of thrombolytics beyond 4.5 hours in acute ischemic stroke.

## Case Resolution
Thrombolysis was not offered to the patient, and she was taken urgently for mechanical thrombectomy. TICI 3 recanalization was achieved (full reperfusion) and she was admitted to the neurocritical care unit for post-procedure monitoring.

## Clinical Application
This study adds to the literature base that shows the "longest time" for thrombolytics remains <4.5 hours. While no superiority for thrombolysis beyond the 4.5-hour window with a suggestion of increased harm. Patients with LVOs should preferentially be directed toward thrombectomy without delays.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
It looks like you are having a stroke. Because it has been more than 4.5 hours, the "clot-busting drugs" have not been shown to work, and because a big artery is blocked, we need to do a procedure to take the clot out quickly. The stroke team is arranging this, and it will happen shortly.
