## Summary
The document discusses the evaluation of a child sex trafficking (CST) screening tool in a pediatric emergency department setting, focusing on patients aged 10-18 with high-risk complaints. The study highlights the tool's high sensitivity (90.9%) and negative predictive value (99.0%), making it effective for initial CST screening in high-risk populations. Key findings include the identification of CST victims, with a prevalence of 5.4% among participants, and the association of CST with factors such as substance use, multiple sexual partners, and prior STIs. The study underscores the importance of using the CST screening tool in emergency settings to identify potential victims and facilitate appropriate interventions.

## Case
A 15-year-old girl presents to the emergency department with pelvic pain. She is with a parent and after the initial introductions and history, you have her parent leave the room to ask more sensitive questions. Upon further history, you discover that she has been having pelvic pain with genital discharge and has had more than ten sexual partners in their lifetime. Eventually, you discover that she has also been drinking alcohol and endorses that she has exchanged sex for drugs in the past.

## Background
Child sex trafficking (CST) is a severe global human rights violation involving minors in sex acts in exchange for something of value, either monetary or non-monetary. CST encompasses activities such as child prostitution, survival sex, involvement in sex-oriented businesses, and the production of child sexual abuse materials. In the United States, 85% of identified sex trafficking victims are citizens or legal residents, with 55% being minors. In Canada, police-reported incidents of human trafficking have risen, with a rate of 0.94 per 100,000 people, and 95% of victims are women, 72% of whom are under 25. Risk factors for CST include a history of abuse, substance use, involvement with the juvenile justice system, running away from home, and LGBTQ status. Victims face numerous health issues, such as physical injuries, chronic pain, sexually transmitted infections, substance use disorders, and psychiatric conditions like PTSD, depression, and suicide. Despite these risks, 88% of CST victims have sought medical care during their exploitation.

## Clinical Point
What is the utility of a CST screening tool in a high-risk patient population presenting to a large inner-city pediatric emergency department?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Child Sex Trafficking is a global problem and can be discovered in the emergency department through the application of a simple screening tool.

## Case Resolution
After discovering that your patient has traded sex for drugs, you have your social worker see the patient while you continue the work up for her acute medical illness.

## Clinical Application
Use a CST screening tool in adolescents with high-risk presenting complaints in the ED.
