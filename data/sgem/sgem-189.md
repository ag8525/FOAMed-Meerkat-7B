## Summary
The document discusses the management of out-of-hospital cardiac arrests (OHCA) and evaluates the effectiveness of advanced cardiac life support (ACLS) in pre-hospital settings, particularly for patients who are candidates for extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (E-CPR). The OPALS study and subsequent research indicate that while ACLS increases the rate of return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC), it does not improve survival to hospital discharge or neurological outcomes. A cohort study by Cournoyer et al. supports these findings, showing no significant benefit of ACLS in survival to discharge for OHCA patients, including E-CPR candidates, though it does improve prehospital ROSC. The study also notes a longer delay to hospital arrival with ACLS. Consequently, the decision was made to focus resources on enhancing bystander CPR, reducing EMS response times, and expediting defibrillation rather than deploying ACLS units to all cardiac arrests.

## Case
You are the medical director of an EMS system in a large city deciding on whether to respond to all out of hospital cardiac arrests (OHCA) with ACLS capabilities, or if resources should be directed to those candidates for extracorporeal CPR.

## Background
Sudden cardiac arrest (SCA) affects approximately 500,000 individuals annually in the United States, with about half occurring outside of hospitals (OHCA), where survival rates are notably low. The American Heart Association's 2015 CPR and ECC Guidelines introduced a "Chain-of-Survival" for OHCA, comprising five critical steps: recognizing cardiac arrest and activating emergency medical services (EMS), performing high-quality bystander CPR, rapid defibrillation of shockable arrhythmias, and early advanced care involving advanced cardiovascular life support (ACLS) skills such as intubation and intravenous drug therapy. The OPALS study, a pivotal observational study, indicated that while ACLS increased the return of spontaneous circulation and survival to hospital admission, it did not improve survival to hospital discharge or neurological outcomes. Subsequent research has questioned the efficacy of ACLS drugs like epinephrine for OHCA, and there is growing interest in extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (E-CPR) for selected cases of refractory OHCA.

## Clinical Point
In patients with OHCA that are candidates for E-CPR, does ACLS in the pre-hospital setting improve rates of ROSC, survival to hospital discharge and survival with good neurological outcome?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Even in E-CPR candidate patients, there is no evidence that ACLS provides a patient-oriented benefit.

## Case Resolution
You decide to conserve resources and not to dispatch ACLS units to all cardiac arrests. You will, however, keep an eye on the literature to see if future results will change this decision.

## Clinical Application
Patients undergoing OHCA arrest do not benefit from ACLS during transport. Efforts should be focused on increasing bystander CPR, decreasing EMS response times, and decreasing time to defibrillation.
