## Summary
This document discusses the diagnostic and management challenges of pulmonary embolism (PE) in high-risk patients, emphasizing the role of D-dimer testing. It highlights the potential of age-adjusted D-dimer thresholds to safely exclude PE in patients with high clinical probability, as explored in a post hoc analysis of three European studies. The study found no missed PEs in patients with D-dimer levels below the age-adjusted threshold, suggesting a possible role for D-dimer in high-risk cases. However, due to limitations such as selection and information biases, wide confidence intervals, and study asymmetry, the certainty of using D-dimer in high-risk patients remains unclear. The document concludes that while there may be a role for D-dimer in these patients, more definitive evidence is needed before it can be routinely recommended, and current practice should still prioritize imaging like CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA) for high-risk individuals.

## Case
A 57-year-old woman presents to the emergency department (ED) with pleuritic posterior chest/back pain, shortness of breath, and left leg swelling. Her oxygen saturation is 95% on room air, blood pressure is 125/70, and her heart rate is 106 beats per minute. She has a swollen left leg that is tender but neurovascularly intact, without signs of cellulitis. She is on oral hormone replacement therapy (estrogen), and has a history of hypertension (on lisinopril). Chest x-ray shows no pneumonia or pneumothorax and bedside ultrasound reveals no B-lines or effusion.

## Background
Pulmonary embolism (PE) is a critical condition requiring careful consideration in emergency settings due to its potential lethality. The diagnostic challenge involves balancing the risk of missing a PE against the harms of unnecessary imaging. D-dimer testing, a sensitive but non-specific biomarker for venous thromboembolism (VTE), plays a pivotal role in modern PE diagnostic pathways, particularly for patients with low to moderate pretest probability, where a negative result can effectively rule out PE without imaging. Clinical prediction rules such as the Wells Score, Revised Geneva Score (RGS), and YEARS guide the use of D-dimer testing. In high-risk patients, the test's low positive predictive value (PPV) and the high prevalence of PE often lead to direct imaging with CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA). However, recent studies suggest that age-adjusted D-dimer thresholds and Bayesian approaches may refine risk stratification, potentially allowing for safe use of D-dimer testing in selected high-risk patients.

## Clinical Point
Can an age-adjusted D-dimer strategy safely exclude PE in patients with a high clinical probability of PE?

## SGEM Bottom Line
We cannot recommend using a D-dimer strategy in ruling out PE in high-risk patients.

## Case Resolution
Based on the patient’s clinical risk, you send the patient for CTPA. She has bilateral segmental pulmonary emboli. You risk stratify the patient and she meets criteria for outpatient treatment. You give her an oral anticoagulant and discuss expectant management.

## Clinical Application
At this time, I will await more definitive evidence before routinely using D-dimer in patients with high clinical probability of PE.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
I suspect you may have a blood clot in your leg. Part of this clot has likely broken off and traveled to your lungs, giving you the pain and shortness of breath. We are going to get a CT scan to confirm if you have a pulmonary embolism (blood clot in your lung).
