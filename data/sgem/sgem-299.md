## Summary
The document reviews the diagnostic accuracy of various methods for COVID-19 in the emergency department, highlighting the limitations of history, physical exams, and routine labs, with fever and smell/taste disorders being the only somewhat distinguishing symptoms. The rt-PCR test, despite being the criterion standard, has a high false-negative rate, necessitating serial testing and possibly imaging, though no pathognomonic findings exist. The review underscores the importance of adhering to STARD guidelines to avoid biases such as spectrum, incorporation, and differential verification biases, which can skew sensitivity and specificity. The implications of false negatives and positives are significant, affecting patient management and public health measures. Future research should focus on improving diagnostic accuracy and reporting standards to mitigate these issues.

## Case
You are working in the emergency department during the COVID-19 outbreak, and you see a patient with oxygen saturations of 75% on room air, a fever, and a cough. Upon review of systems, you learn that she lost her sense of taste about two days ago. Your hospital performs COVID reverse transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (rt-PCR) nasal swabs on suspected patients, so you order this test and await the results.

## Background
COVID-19, caused by the novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, emerged in early 2020 in Wuhan, China, and quickly became a global pandemic, significantly impacting healthcare systems due to its high morbidity and mortality rates. The disease presented with a wide range of symptoms, complicating case identification, and testing initially lagged behind the rapid spread. In response, the US FDA authorized the emergency development and use of rt-PCR assays, leading to the release of numerous assay kits by various companies. The diagnostic accuracy of rt-PCR for COVID-19, along with the evaluation of clinical signs, symptoms, imaging, and other laboratory tests, is critical for effective disease management.

## Clinical Point
What is the diagnostic accuracy of history, clinical examination, routine labs, rt-PCR, immunology tests, and imaging tests for the emergency department diagnosis for COVID-19?

## SGEM Bottom Line
The limitations for diagnostic testing for COVID-19 must be understood. Current PCR tests have a fairly high false negative rate, so serial testing should be performed. There may be a role for imaging in suspected patients, but there are no pathognomonic findings for COVID-19.

## Case Resolution
Your patient tests negative for the virus. Despite this, your suspicion is high, so you continue to use appropriate personal protection equipment (PPE) when entering the room.

## Clinical Application
Be aware of high false negative rates for rt-PCR testing and maintain a high level of suspicion in high-risk patients.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
Your initial COVID test came back negative. However, given the suspicion we have, we are going to continue to protect ourselves, staff and other patients, and are going to care for you as if you have the virus.
