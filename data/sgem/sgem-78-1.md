## Summary
A randomized clinical trial investigated the impact of ambient music on distress and pain in children aged 3-14 undergoing intravenous placement in a pediatric emergency department. The study compared standard care with and without music, using tracks like "The Planets Op. 32 Jupiter" and "Sunny Days." The primary outcome was patient distress measured by the Observational Scale of Behavioural Distress-Revised, with secondary outcomes including self-reported pain, heart rate, and satisfaction levels of parents and healthcare providers. Results indicated no significant change in behavioral distress, but the music group maintained stable pain scores, unlike the standard care group, which experienced increased pain (P=0.04). While heart rates and parental anxiety showed no differences, healthcare providers reported higher satisfaction and ease with music (P=0.02 and P=0.03, respectively). Despite limitations such as small sample size and potential biases, the study suggests that music is a low-cost, non-pharmacologic intervention that may benefit children during painful procedures, warranting further research.

## Point
Does adding ambient music to standard care of pediatric patients prevent distress during painful procedures?

## Bottom Line
There appear to be some benefits in playing music during painful procedures in children in the ED. This is a low-cost, non-pharmacologic intervention with no adverse effects. It’s a good idea that needs a bit more research, but if the capability is there, can be implemented easily.

## Case Resolution
The child care specialist sets up the child to listen to Sunny Days while you start the IV line.

## Clinical Application
For children in the emergency department undergoing painful procedures should be offered music as a non-pharmacologic way to address their pain.

## What do I tell Patients
We are going to play some music to help you (and us) relax during this procedure!
