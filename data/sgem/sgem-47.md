## Summary
Epiglottitis, a life-threatening airway emergency primarily caused by Haemophilus influenzae type B (Hib), has seen a significant decrease in incidence due to the Hib vaccine. The condition presents with symptoms such as fever, sore throat, and difficulty swallowing and breathing. Modern management involves aggressive antibiotic therapy and supportive care, with airway management through intubation or tracheotomy if necessary. Historical practices like bloodletting, as seen in the case of George Washington, have been replaced by evidence-based treatments, highlighting the evolution of medical understanding and the importance of vaccination in preventing severe infections.

## Case Scenario
67 former president of the USA presents with fever, lethargy, sore throat, difficulty swallowing, and breathing.

## Point
What is the best treatment for epiglottis?

## Background
Epiglottitis is the inflammation of the epiglottis, primarily caused by infection, with Haemophilus influenzae type B (Hib) historically being the most common pathogen. It is a critical medical condition due to its potential to cause life-threatening airway obstruction. The introduction of the Hib vaccine has dramatically reduced the incidence of epiglottitis, which previously affected approximately 20,000 children under five annually in the United States, resulting in about 1,000 deaths each year. By 2006, the number of reported Hib cases had decreased to just 29, highlighting the significant impact of vaccination as a public health measure.

## Case Resolution
George Washington had 5 pints of blood removed. One doctor suggested a tracheotomy but was overruled. President Washington went into shock and died the next day.
