## Summary
The document discusses the integration of Point of Care Ultrasound (PoCUS) into high-fidelity simulation (HFS) for emergency medicine training, emphasizing its diagnostic and procedural applications. The study by Olszynski et al. compared two PoCUS simulation methods: the edus2 simulator and a laptop with ultrasound videos, finding the edus2 to be superior due to its real-time handling and functional integration into critical care. The study highlights the importance of repeated competency assessments and the use of hybrid simulations to bridge task training with clinical integration. The authors advocate for PoCUS in HFS to enhance diagnostic accuracy and resuscitation skills, suggesting it as a critical step before real patient encounters. The document concludes with a recommendation to incorporate PoCUS into HFS programs to improve emergency medicine training outcomes.

## Case
An emergency medicine resident in your institution has learned some basics of ultrasound training, but feels uncomfortable performing ultrasound in a crashing patient. They come to ask you how they can learn to use their ultrasound skills in critically ill patients, other than doing it in real time.

## Background
Point of care ultrasound (PoCUS) is categorized into diagnostic and procedural applications, with simulators available for both. Diagnostic applications are particularly significant for invasive procedures like transesophageal echocardiography in critical states and transvaginal ultrasound in early pregnancy complications. Procedural applications show strong evidence of effectiveness, as demonstrated by studies like Barsuk et al., where trainees using ultrasound for central venous line placement outperformed those trained traditionally. There is a growing trend towards hybrid simulation experiences that integrate diagnostic and procedural training into broader clinical simulations, supporting competency-based training. Competency in PoCUS requires repeated assessments over time, focusing on the trainee's ability to decide when to use the ultrasound, perform scans, interpret images, and integrate findings into patient care. Resuscitative PoCUS involves understanding indications, generating and interpreting images, and integrating these into clinical decisions. High-fidelity simulation can enhance PoCUS training for medical residents.

## Clinical Point
What ultrasound simulation method is preferred by trainees and instructors in high fidelity simulation?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Consider integrating PoCUS into your high fidelity simulation program.

## Case Resolution
You suggest that PoCUS be integrated into a high fidelity simulation case, either by creating an edus2 type trainer with an ultrasound probe and laptop, or simply putting a laptop with ultrasound clips at the bedside.

## Clinical Application
PoCUS skills are useful at the bedside in real patients and this integration into high fidelity simulation is the last step prior to using them in real patient encounters.

**What Do I Tell My Learners?**
We know that use of PoCUS during simulation is beneficial for your learning and will help develop your knowledge of PoCUS indications, and your skills of image interpretation, clinical integration of ultrasound images and resuscitation choreography.
