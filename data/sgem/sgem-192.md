## Summary
In patients with suspected or confirmed acute myocardial infarction (AMI) who are not hypoxic, routine administration of supplemental oxygen does not provide a patient-oriented benefit, as evidenced by a study involving 6,629 patients which showed no significant difference in one-year all-cause mortality between those receiving supplemental oxygen and those breathing ambient air. The study, which included patients with oxygen saturation greater than 89% and excluded those with hypoxia or ongoing oxygen therapy, found no significant differences in secondary outcomes such as 30-day mortality or rehospitalization rates. Despite the potential for selection bias and the study being underpowered for its primary outcome, the findings align with previous systematic reviews and the AVOID trial, suggesting that supplemental oxygen may not be beneficial and could potentially cause harm in non-hypoxemic AMI patients. Consequently, the routine use of supplemental oxygen in these patients is not recommended, and clinical protocols may need to be adjusted accordingly.

## Case
A 68-year-old man with a history of hypertension and dyslipidemia develops chest pain while grocery shopping. Emergency medical services are called, arrive quickly and find a man with 7/10 chest pain. His vitals are: heart rate of 72 beats per minute, blood pressure of 150/90, respiratory rate of 14 breaths per minute and oxygen saturation of 93%. The ECG shows an inferior ST-Elevated Myocardial Infarction (STEMI). They give him 180mg of ASA to chew and package him for transportation to the hospital. While preparing to depart, they wonder if they should provide him with supplemental oxygen?

## Background
Oxygen therapy, while commonly used in medical settings, can have both beneficial and harmful effects, particularly in the context of acute myocardial infarction (AMI). Studies have indicated that oxygen can cause vasoconstriction, increase blood pressure, and reduce coronary artery blood flow. A systematic review highlighted limited evidence from randomized controlled trials, with one study showing increased mortality in patients receiving supplemental oxygen and another showing no significant reduction in ventricular tachycardia. The AVOID trial further suggested that high-flow oxygen in non-hypoxemic patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction may increase myocardial injury and infarct size. A 2016 Cochrane review found no evidence supporting the routine use of inhaled oxygen in AMI patients and suggested potential harm, emphasizing the need for a well-designed RCT to guide clinical guidelines. The American Heart Association's 2015 guidelines reflect this uncertainty, recommending that supplemental oxygen may be withheld in normoxic patients with suspected or confirmed acute coronary syndrome.

## Clinical Point
Does routine administration of supplemental oxygen in patients with suspected or confirmed acute myocardial infarction who are not hypoxic provide a patient-oriented benefit?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Routine administration of supplemental oxygen in patients with suspected or confirmed acute myocardial infarction who are not hypoxic does not appear to provide a patient-oriented benefit.

## Case Resolution
This patient is not hypoxic with an oxygen saturation of 93%. The paramedics load him up and head to the hospital notifying them that an inferior STEMI is on the way.

## Clinical Application
This study agrees with the Cochrane review of five RCTs failing to demonstrate benefit of supplemental oxygen in these patients. As such, I will not provide oxygen to these patients routinely. I also plan to talk with my EMS director about changing their protocol to only administer oxygen to patients with suspected or confirmed acute myocardial infarctions who are hypoxic.

**What do I tell my patient?**
It looks like you are having a heart attack. We have given you ASA and will take you quickly and safely to a hospital that can treat your heart attack. Your vital signs are good right now.  We will monitor them on the way to the hospital. Specifically, your oxygen level is fine and we do not need to give you any extra oxygen.
