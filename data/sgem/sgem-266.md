## Summary
The document discusses the diagnostic accuracy of the Canadian CT Head Rule (CCHR) in predicting clinically important brain injuries (CIBI) in patients aged 65 and older with mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI). The study suggests that adjusting the CCHR age criterion to 75 years could safely reduce unnecessary CT scans, as the rule demonstrated a sensitivity of 100% and a specificity of 13.9% for this age group. However, the study's retrospective nature and methodological limitations, such as potential biases and wide confidence intervals, necessitate further prospective validation before clinical implementation. The current recommendation remains to perform CT imaging in this demographic due to the high prevalence of CIBI, despite the potential for reduced CT usage.

## Case
It’s a busy night in the emergency department, your next patient is a well appearing 70-year-old man, presenting after a mechanical fall from standing with loss of consciousness. He is neurologically intact with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of 15 one hour after the fall. There are no other external signs of trauma on your exam. He is not on anticoagulation and there is no history of seizures.

## Background
Head trauma is a prevalent issue in emergency departments, with 2.5 million visits in the US in 2014, particularly affecting the elderly. It can lead to a range of brain injuries from mild to severe. While severe cases clearly necessitate CT imaging, mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) presents a diagnostic challenge due to minimal or absent clinical evidence of injury. To optimize resource use and outcomes, Clinical Decision Rules (CDRs) like the Canadian CT Head Rule (CCHR) and New Orleans Criteria (NOC) have been developed to guide imaging decisions. The CCHR, applicable to patients with minor head injuries characterized by blunt trauma and a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of ≥13, is the most extensively researched CDR, demonstrating high sensitivity and specificity. It excludes non-traumatic cases, GCS <13, patients under 16, those on anticoagulants, or with open skull fractures. The CCHR includes age ≥65 as a high-risk factor for clinically important brain injury (CIBI), defined as acute brain findings on CT necessitating hospital admission and follow-up. With the aging population and increasing emergency visits for traumatic brain injury, further refinement of decision aids is crucial for effective risk stratification in this high-risk group.

## Clinical Point
What is the diagnostic accuracy of the CCHR in patients 65 years of age or older in predicting clinically important brain injuries?

## SGEM Bottom Line
This paper opens the door for further research to try to narrow the criteria in the Canadian Head CT Rule to further reduce unnecessary head CT imaging in the emergency department. However, further, high quality prospective studies are required prior to clinical application.

## Case Resolution
Given the current evidence, and prevalence of CIBI in the patient population 65 years and older, you obtain head CT imaging. The CT scan is negative for acute pathology, both you and the patient are relieved. You provide the patient with return precautions and discharge him home.

## Clinical Application
The original Canadian Head CT Rule that was published back in 2001 has successfully reduced the number of head CT imaging obtained in the emergency department for mTBI. One major limitation to the decision instrument is using 65 years of age or older as a high-risk criterion. While this new study attempting to increase the age cutoff to 75 years is interesting and hypothesis generating it is not yet ready for clinical application at this time.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
There is some new evidence suggesting that we do not need to CT all patients older than 65 who have minor traumatic brain injury. However, best practice evidence at this time, still indicates that CT imaging should be performed in this patient population given the increased prevalence of CIBI.
