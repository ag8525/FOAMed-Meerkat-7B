## Summary
Emergency contraception (EC) is a critical intervention following unprotected intercourse to prevent pregnancy, with several options available including hormonal regimens and the copper intrauterine device (IUD). The Cochrane review highlights that intermediate-dose mifepristone is superior to levonorgestrel (LNG) and the Yuzpe regimen, with the copper IUD being the most effective method, also offering ongoing contraception. In the case scenario, a 21-year-old woman was provided with levonorgestrel (Plan B) after a condom failure, reflecting its availability and effectiveness in Canada and the USA. Despite the availability of more effective options like mifepristone and ulipristal acetate (UPA) in the US, they are not accessible in Canada, where LNG remains the preferred choice due to its efficacy and reduced side effects compared to the Yuzpe method.

## Case Scenario
21-year-old presents to the emergency department at 8am very distraught. She reports the condom broke last night during intercourse and requests the morning after pill.

## Background
Emergency contraception (EC) is defined as the use of a device or drug after unprotected intercourse to prevent pregnancy, a practice that became effective in the 1960s with hormonal regimens. It serves as a critical backup method to primary pregnancy prevention, given the annual occurrence of over 40 million abortions worldwide. The Yuzpe method, a combination of estrogen and progestogen, gained popularity in the 1970s, followed by other hormonal treatments such as progestogen-only (levonorgestrel), anti-gonadotropin (danazol), and anti-progestins (mifepristone and ulipristal acetate). Additionally, a copper intrauterine device (IUD) offers a non-hormonal option that can be inserted up to five days postcoital and can remain in place for long-term contraception.

## Point
What is the best intervention for emergency contraception?

## BEEM Bottom Line
In Canada and USA if Plan A fails then Plan B (LNG) as a single dose of 1.5mg levonorgestrel.

The anti-progestins (Ru486 and UPA) are not currently available in Canada, but are apparently available in the US. The Yuzpe method is available in Canada but must be prescribed by a physician, is less effective than LNG, and therefore, should no longer be routinely used.

Since the introduction of LNG in Canada, the ED visits solely for Emergency Contraception has declined dramatically, but knowledge of the agents and their effectiveness is still important for Emergency Physicians.

## Case Scenario Conclusion
The young woman was provided with information on her options including EC. The effectiveness and common side effects of EC were discussed. Shared decision making took place and you provide her with levonorgestrel (Plan B).
