## Summary
In the context of emergency department (ED) management of severe pain, the study by Brichko et al. evaluates the effectiveness of inhaled methoxyflurane compared to standard analgesic care. The randomized controlled trial included adult patients with severe pain, excluding those with specific medical conditions or vital sign abnormalities. The primary outcome was the proportion of patients achieving a 50% reduction in pain score at 30 minutes, which showed no significant difference between methoxyflurane and standard care. However, methoxyflurane was associated with a significant reduction in pain scores at various time points and had a longer median time to rescue analgesia. No serious adverse effects were noted, and the study supports the use of methoxyflurane for rapid analgesia at triage, providing immediate pain relief while awaiting further treatment.

## Case
A 46-year-old female presents to the emergency department (ED) with sudden onset, severe right flank pain. She is pacing around at triage in tears and says she has a history of kidney stones. She is asking for something to help with her pain, but the department is very busy, and it will be some time before she can get into a treatment space.

## Background
Oligoanalgesia, the inadequate management of pain due to underuse of analgesics, is a significant issue in emergency departments (EDs), impacting patient care quality and outcomes. Factors contributing to oligoanalgesia include ED overcrowding, language barriers, and demographic variables such as age, gender, and ethnicity. Delays in analgesia administration can lead to extended ED stays and decreased patient satisfaction, with studies showing median wait times of 40-70 minutes in Australia and a mean of 116 minutes in the USA. To address these delays, strategies such as advanced protocols, oral analgesics at triage, and novel analgesics have been implemented. Methoxyflurane (Penthrox), an inhaled non-opioid analgesic, has gained attention for its rapid, short-term pain relief capabilities. Widely used in Australia since 1975 for pre-hospital analgesia, methoxyflurane is now used globally at sub-anesthetic doses, offering a favorable safety profile without reports of addiction or abuse. While most studies focus on its use for traumatic pain, recent research aims to evaluate its effectiveness for both traumatic and non-traumatic pain.

## Clinical Point

## SGEM Bottom Line
Consider using methoxyflurane for rapid analgesia at triage in the ED.

## Case Resolution
You give your patient a methoxyflurane inhaler and they obtain some pain relief immediately. They still have quite severe pain and are moved to a treatment space for further analgesic once it is available.

## Clinical Application
Methoxyflurane is a rapid, short-acting analgesic that can be provided in a timely fashion at ED triage.
