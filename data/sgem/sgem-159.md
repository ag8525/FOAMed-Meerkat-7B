## Summary
The document discusses the impact of computerized provider order entry (CPOE) on emergency department (ED) throughput, focusing on metrics such as wait time (WT), length of stay (LOS), and the proportion of patients leaving without being seen (LWBS). The study, conducted in two Canadian quaternary hospitals, found that CPOE implementation increased median WT by 5 minutes, LOS by 10 minutes, and LWBS by 0.9%, with a notable 63-minute increase in LOS for admitted patients. Despite statistically significant changes, the clinical significance remains uncertain, and the study raises concerns about CPOE's potential negative effects on patient safety and physician satisfaction. The authors suggest that the initial implementation phase may have contributed to these outcomes, and further research is needed to assess long-term impacts on patient-oriented outcomes and physician burnout.

## Background
Emergency department crowding is a significant issue in Canada, and the implementation of computerized provider order entry (CPOE) in tertiary care emergency departments (EDs) necessitates an evaluation of its impact on ED metrics. CPOE has been shown to have no effect on mortality and may enhance pain management and adherence to medication protocols for conditions like stroke and renal colic. However, its influence on throughput is mixed, with potential negative effects such as reduced physician productivity, increased length of stay for admitted patients, and longer times to order labs and imaging. CPOE can correct some errors but also introduce new ones, leading to physician frustration. A study in a community hospital ED found that over 40% of time was spent on data entry, with less than 30% on direct patient care, and nearly 4,000 mouse clicks in a 10-hour shift. There is a lack of direct studies on CPOE's impact on ED wait times, a critical factor in addressing crowding.

## Point
What impact will CPOE have on emergency department patient throughput?
