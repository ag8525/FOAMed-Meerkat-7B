## Summary
The document discusses the potential of large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Claude in automating critical appraisal tasks in evidence-based medicine, specifically using tools such as PRISMA, AMSTAR, and PRECIS-2. The study by Woelfle et al. evaluated the performance of five LLMs against human raters in assessing systematic reviews and RCTs, finding that while LLMs alone underperformed compared to humans, a collaborative human-AI approach improved accuracy significantly for PRISMA and AMSTAR tasks. However, LLMs struggled with the more complex PRECIS-2 tasks. The study highlights issues such as bias in training data, limited contextual understanding, and lack of transparency in LLM decision-making. Despite these challenges, the integration of LLMs with human expertise shows promise in reducing workload without compromising accuracy, particularly for simpler appraisal tasks. The document concludes that while LLMs are not yet ready to replace human raters, they can augment human decision-making in medical research.

A resident has been assigned to report on the PRISMA quality checklist for the SRMA they will discuss. She has been playing around with ChatGPT 3.5 from OpenAI and wonders if it could do this task quickly and, more importantly, accurately.

## Background
Large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4 and Claude are increasingly being explored for their potential to automate critical appraisal in evidence-based medicine, a process that evaluates the quality, validity, and applicability of studies using structured tools such as PRISMA, AMSTAR, and PRECIS-2. PRISMA is a set of guidelines aimed at improving the reporting of systematic reviews and meta-analyses, focusing on transparency and completeness. AMSTAR assesses the methodological quality of systematic reviews of healthcare interventions, covering areas like search strategies and risk of bias. PRECIS-2 evaluates how pragmatic or explanatory a randomized controlled trial is, helping align trial design with research goals. Traditionally manual and expertise-intensive, these tasks could be enhanced by LLMs, which can interpret and analyze complex data, potentially increasing the efficiency of appraisals. While research into the accuracy of LLMs in this context is nascent, their ability to perform critical appraisals with human-like accuracy and reliability could significantly impact medical research, raising questions about their role in augmenting human decision-making.

## Clinical Point
Can large language models accurately assess critical appraisal tools when evaluating systematic reviews and randomized controlled trials?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Large language models alone are not reliable enough to replace human raters for complex evidence appraisals. However, the human-AI collaboration strategy shows promise, especially for simpler tasks like PRISMA and AMSTAR, by reducing the workload for human raters without sacrificing accuracy.

## Case Resolution

## Clinical Application
