## Summary
Haloperidol is a commonly used antipsychotic for managing psychosis-induced aggression or agitation, particularly in emergency settings. The Cochrane review analyzed 32 studies and found that while haloperidol is effective, it is associated with significant side effects such as dystonia and akathisia, which can be mitigated by adjunctive medications like promethazine. The review suggests that while haloperidol can be life-saving in extreme emergencies, its sole use without additional drugs to counteract adverse effects may be unethical. The addition of benzodiazepines does not significantly reduce side effects and may increase sedation risks. Despite the availability of newer atypical antipsychotics, haloperidol remains a viable option due to its efficacy, availability, and cost-effectiveness, though it should ideally be used in combination with other medications to minimize side effects.

## Case Scenario
23-year-old man with schizophrenia presents with police after being picked up for a violent outburst at a coffee shop. He is clearly agitated and you need to chemically restrain him.

## Point
Is haloperidol the safest and most effective method of tranquilization for patients with psychosis-induced aggression or agitation?

**Serious Reactions** (from Epocrates) and delivered by Dr. Michael del Castillo-Hegyi (Chief Resident AUMS):

- Neurologic (extrapyramidal, tardive dyskinesia, akathisia, dystonia, and seizure)
- Hyperpyrexia or heat stroke
- Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome
- Pneumonia
- Hypotension or hypertension
- Cardiac
- Sudden Death
- Hyponatremia
- Hepatic impairment
- Hematologic (leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis)
- Ocular (cataracts and retinopathy)

## Case Resolution
You sedate this 23-year-old known schizophrenic with Haloperidol 5mg IM. He is settled and sleeping in 2 hours. Screening blood work is sent off and he waits for psychiatry service to assess.
