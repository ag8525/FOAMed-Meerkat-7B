## Summary
The document discusses the management of pediatric community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), focusing on the efficacy of a five-day versus a ten-day course of high-dose amoxicillin in children aged six months to ten years. The study, a non-inferiority trial, found that a five-day course was not non-inferior to a ten-day course, with clinical cure rates of approximately 90% in both groups. Despite the statistical outcome, the clinical significance of the findings is debated, as the absolute risk difference was minimal. The trial's external validity is limited, being conducted in two Canadian pediatric emergency departments. The authors suggest that clinical guidelines consider recommending a five-day course in line with antimicrobial stewardship, though this may lead to a slight increase in non-cure rates. The document emphasizes the importance of clinical stability and follow-up in managing pediatric CAP.

## Case
Six-year-old Morten comes into your emergency department (ED) with what looks like pneumonia. He has been febrile with a temp of 39 degrees Celsius, he is mildly tachypneic but shows no real signs of respiratory distress and you can hear some crackles in the right mid-zone. His chest X-ray (CXR) confirms your findings, but he is well enough to be treated as an outpatient.

## Background
Pediatric community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is a common condition characterized by lung infection in children, often caused by viral pathogens, particularly in preschool-aged children. Clinically, it is significant due to its prevalence and the potential for morbidity, though most cases in young children do not require antibiotics. High-quality evidence suggests that chest X-rays are unnecessary for outpatient management of CAP. For school-aged children, antibiotics are recommended, with treatment duration traditionally set at 10 days, though recent studies indicate that a 5-day course of amoxicillin may be equally effective for mild cases. The Infectious Diseases Society of America (IDSA) guidelines recommend that antibiotic duration should be based on clinical stability, with a minimum of 5 days of treatment. These guidelines are supported by moderate-quality evidence and are consistent with recommendations for adult CAP management under specific conditions.

## Clinical Point
Is a five day course of antibiotic therapy non-inferior to a ten day course to achieve clinical cure for paediatric community-acquired pneumonia?

## SGEM Bottom Line
A five-day course of antibiotics was not non-inferior to the traditional ten-day course of antibiotics for children with CAP treated as outpatients.

## Case Resolution
We would give a five day course of antibiotics with a good safety net in place. We would ensure the parents arranged follow-up with their primary care physician and knew when it was important to come back to the emergency department.

**Clinical Application**: If you are giving out antibiotics to children with suspected CAP then it is reasonable to consider a five-day course rather than a ten-day course of amoxicillin. However, it could result in more children not achieving a clinical cure. This should be discussed with parents/care-givers.

**What Do I Tell My Patient**? I would tell the parents/care-givers that your child has pneumonia. It is safe to treat him with antibiotics at home. We have some evidence that five days is almost as good as ten days of treatment. Would you like the short course or long course of antibiotics?
