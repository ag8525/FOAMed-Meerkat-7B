## Summary
The document discusses the application of the Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria (PERC) in a clinical setting, specifically within a French population, as evaluated by the PROPER trial. The study focused on low-risk patients presenting with symptoms suggestive of pulmonary embolism (PE) and compared a PERC-based strategy to usual care. The trial demonstrated that using the PERC rule did not result in a higher rate of thromboembolic events over three months, supporting its safety and efficacy in ruling out PE without the need for further imaging like CTPA in very low-risk patients. The study highlighted the importance of risk stratification and the potential to reduce unnecessary imaging, with the PERC rule proving non-inferior to traditional methods. The case resolution involved discharging a patient with undiagnosed chest pain after ruling out high-risk factors using the PERC rule, emphasizing the tool's utility in clinical decision-making.

## Case
A 47-year-old woman presents to the emergency department with a 24-hour history of chest pain and shortness of breath. She has a past medical history of hypothyroidism and gastroesophageal reflux disease. She is on levo-thyroxine and a proton pump inhibitor. Vital signs are normal (HR 72, BP 130/80, RR 12, O2 Sat 97% and 37.1C) and physical examination does not indicate the cause of her complaint. ECG, chest x-ray and routine lab work is also normal, including a troponin. You risk stratify her by gestalts and Well’s criteria as not high risk and wondering if you can PERC Rule her out and not order a d-dimer.

## Background
The Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria (PERC) Rule is an eight-item clinical decision tool used to assess the likelihood of pulmonary embolism (PE) in patients. It includes criteria such as age 50 or older, heart rate of 100 beats per minute or higher, oxygen saturation on room air below 95%, unilateral leg swelling, hemoptysis, recent surgery or trauma, surgery or trauma within the past four weeks requiring general anesthesia, prior history of PE or deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and hormone use, including oral contraceptives, hormone replacement, or estrogenic hormones in both male and female patients. If any of these criteria are positive, a d-dimer test is indicated to further evaluate the risk of PE.

## Clinical Point
Does PERC Rule work in France?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Risk stratify your patients and use the PERC Rule for those that are not high-risk.

## Case Resolution
The patient is Well’s criterial non-high risk and PERC Rule negative. You explain this to the patient and discharge her home with a diagnosis of chest pain not yet diagnosed.

## Clinical Application
This study validates the PERC rule can be used to decrease the number of patients getting a CTPA without increasing the PE miss rate.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
We looked into why you are having some chest pain and shortness of breath. The tests show us you have not had a heart attack and have a less than 1% chance of having a blood clot in your lungs. You can go home and try some acetaminophen or ibuprofen for the pain. Please follow-up with your family physician next week. If your pain gets worse, you develop new symptoms (fever, bloody cough, rash, etc) or are worried, we would be happy to see you again in the emergency department.
