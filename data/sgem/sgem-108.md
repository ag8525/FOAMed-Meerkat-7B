## Summary
The document discusses the case of a 58-year-old woman with symptoms indicative of Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV), characterized by dizziness exacerbated by head movements, nausea, and vomiting. BPPV is caused by canalithiasis, where debris in the inner ear's semicircular canals disrupts normal fluid movement, leading to vertigo. The Dix-Hallpike maneuver is used for diagnosis, eliciting nystagmus and vertigo symptoms. The Epley Maneuver, a canalith repositioning technique, is highlighted as an effective treatment for posterior canal BPPV, with evidence from 11 RCTs showing significant symptom resolution and conversion of a positive Dix-Hallpike test to negative. Despite a high recurrence rate of 36%, the Epley Maneuver is deemed safe and effective, with outcomes comparable to other maneuvers but superior to Brandt-Daroff exercises. The patient in the case experienced near-complete symptom resolution following the Epley Maneuver, underscoring its clinical utility in emergency settings.

## Case
58-year-old woman with diabetes and hypertension presents with two days of feeling *“dizzy”*. Her symptoms worsen when she turns her head. She has also noticed that is much worse when bending over and looking up while turning her head. She has extreme nausea and has had several episodes of vomiting. She has had some improvement in her symptoms with lying flat and closing her eyes. She is very worried about something serious and asks what can be done to help with her symptoms.

## Background
Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a transient condition characterized by vertigo, often triggered by rapid head movements, due to canalithiasis, where debris in the inner ear's semi-circular canals causes endolymph fluid to continue moving after head motion ceases. BPPV primarily affects individuals aged 50-70, with an incidence of 11 to 67 per 100,000 annually. Although symptoms may resolve spontaneously within weeks, they can be severe enough to necessitate medical intervention. Diagnosis is often confirmed using the Dix-Hallpike maneuver, which provokes vertigo and nystagmus, characterized by torsional and up-beating eye movements. The Epley Maneuver, a canalith repositioning technique involving a sequence of head positions, is a standard treatment to alleviate symptoms by relocating the debris within the posterior semi-circular canal.

## Clinical Point
Is the Epley Manoeuvre effective in the treatment of posterior canal BPPV?

## SGEM Bottom Line
The Epley Manoeuvre is a safe and effective procedure that can reduce/alleviate the symptoms of BPPV in ED patients.

## Case Resolution
The patient was treated with the Epley Manoeuvre and had near complete resolution of her symptoms and was discharged home with positional restrictions and ENT follow-up as needed.

## Clinical Application
Treatment with the Epley Manoeuvre is an effective method of treatment for cases of BPPV.
