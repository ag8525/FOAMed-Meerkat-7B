## Summary
The document discusses the challenges in diagnosing spinal epidural abscess (SEA) in emergency departments, highlighting the frequent diagnostic delays and high misdiagnosis rates due to non-specific presentations. A study evaluated the impact of implementing a Clinical Management Tool (CMT) aimed at improving SEA diagnosis. Despite increased imaging and laboratory testing rates, the CMT did not significantly reduce the time to diagnosis or the proportion of SEA cases with prior related visits. The study suggests that while the CMT altered diagnostic processes, it did not improve patient outcomes, indicating the need for careful consideration before policy changes. The recommendation is to assess local practices before implementing such tools.

## Case
You are in your group meeting and have heard about a case at a nearby emergency department (ED) where the diagnosis of a spinal epidural abscess was delayed, and a substantial settlement has been made out of court. Your group director is concerned with avoiding the same thing happening in your department and wants to know if you should implement an evidence-based clinical management tool (CMT) to reduce delays in diagnosis.

## Background
Spinal epidural abscess (SEA) is a condition characterized by the accumulation of pus in the epidural space of the spine, often leading to diagnostic challenges and delays. It is clinically significant due to its high misdiagnosis rate, with studies indicating that 90% of patients are initially misdiagnosed, and diagnostic errors occur in 55% of cases, with a median delay of 12 days. The classic triad of symptoms—fever, back pain, and neurologic deficit—is present in only 10-15% of cases, contributing to the difficulty in diagnosis. SEA is the condition with the highest misdiagnosis rate in emergency departments, leading to significant long-term sequelae and medico-legal costs. Despite these challenges, there is a caution against over-investigation to avoid parallels with the over-diagnosis of conditions like pulmonary embolism.

## Clinical Point
Does implementation of a Clinical Management Tool improve time to diagnosis and change testing rates for spinal epidural abscess?

## SGEM Bottom Line
In this study, implementation of a Clinical Management Tool changed diagnostic processes but not patient-oriented outcomes.

## Case Resolution
You suggest to your group director that the evidence in this field is mixed and before you change policy you should look at your local data.

## Clinical Application
There is no national recommendation for the assessment of potential SEA in the UK so I will be taking this information back to my home institution to look at how we manage it locally.
