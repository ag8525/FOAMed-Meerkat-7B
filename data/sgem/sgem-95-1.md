## Summary
The document discusses the management of fever in children, emphasizing that fever itself is not harmful to generally healthy children and may be beneficial. The American Academy of Pediatrics advises that the primary goal of antipyretic therapy should be to enhance the child's comfort rather than to normalize body temperature. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Purssell and While examined whether antipyretics prolong febrile illness in children, concluding that there is no evidence to suggest that antipyretics delay fever resolution. However, the study had limitations, including a lack of generalizability due to the inclusion of studies on malaria and varicella, and methodological issues such as lack of blinding in some studies. The recommendation remains to use antipyretics for comfort rather than focusing on reducing fever, and parents should be informed that the use of these medications is to improve the child's comfort during illness.

## Case
2-year-old presents with flu-like symptoms and a fever.

## Background
Fever in children is a common concern among parents, often leading to unnecessary emergency department visits due to misconceptions about its dangers. According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, fever itself is not harmful to a generally healthy child and may even be beneficial. The primary objective of antipyretic treatment is not to reduce the fever but to enhance the child's comfort and overall well-being.

## Point
Will treating the fever make her sicker, longer?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Antipyretics should be used to improve comfort during an illness.

## Clinical Application
In children with fever due to illness, the current recommendation is that antipyretics are used to improve comfort and less attention should be paid to actual temperature.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
We are going to use these drugs to make you (your child) more comfortable.
