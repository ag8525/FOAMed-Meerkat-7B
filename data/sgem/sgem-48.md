## Summary
The document discusses the evaluation of clinical decision rules (CDRs) for ruling out subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) in patients presenting with acute headaches. The study, a prospective multi-center cohort, assessed three CDRs, including the Ottawa SAH Rule, which demonstrated high sensitivity (100%) but low specificity (15.3%) for identifying SAH. The Ottawa SAH Rule, incorporating factors like age over 40, neck pain, and thunderclap headache, could potentially reduce the SAH miss rate from 5% to nearly 0%, though it may increase investigation rates. Despite its promise, the rule requires further validation before routine clinical application. The case resolution involved a negative CT scan and a shared decision to forgo a lumbar puncture, with the patient advised to return if symptoms worsen.

## Case Scenario
A 34-year-old woman presents with acute onset of headache peaking in 30 minutes with no recent trauma, focal deficits, and a normal neurologic examination.

## Point
Can a clinical decision rule be used to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)?

## Background
Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) is a critical condition that accounts for 1-3% of headache-related emergency department visits, with a misdiagnosis rate of approximately 5% during initial assessments. This misdiagnosis is partly due to the fact that 50% of SAH cases present without neurological deficits. The standard diagnostic approach traditionally involves a non-contrast CT scan followed by a lumbar puncture (LP), although the necessity of LPs has been debated, with some experts suggesting they may not always be required, as indicated by a number needed to treat (NNT) of 700.

## BEEM Bottom Line
Ottawa SAH Tool is not ready for prime time to rule out low-risk patients from investigations.

## Case Resolution
You are clinically concerned and get a non-contrast CT head which is negative. You discuss the risks and benefits of an LP with the patient. A shared decision is made with the patient not to do an LP. She is discharged home with appropriate analgesia. She is to return to the emergency department if she develops focal neurologic symptoms, pain increases, LOC, seizure, or is otherwise worried.
