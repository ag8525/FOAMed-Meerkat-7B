## Summary
In the case of a 66-year-old male with sudden onset back/flank pain and a history of hypertension and smoking, there is a high suspicion of an acute abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA). Emergency department (ED) bedside ultrasound, performed by trained emergency physicians, is highly reliable for diagnosing AAA, with a sensitivity and specificity of 0.99. Despite potential biases and variability in operator training, the diagnostic performance remains excellent, making it a critical tool in symptomatic patients. In this case, the ultrasound revealed a 6cm AAA, prompting immediate vascular surgery consultation. The study underscores the importance of ED ultrasound in rapidly identifying AAA, especially in high-risk populations, and suggests further imaging if uncertainty persists.

## Case
66-year-old man develops sudden onset back/flank pain. He has a history of hypertension and smokes cigarettes. You are concerned about a potential acute abdominal aneurysm. A CT abdomen has been ordered and is pending.

## Background
Triple A, or abdominal aortic aneurysm, is a condition with a prevalence of 1.3-15% among adults in emergency departments, increasing with age and particularly affecting males over 65 with a history of smoking and hypertension. In symptomatic patients in emergency and critical care settings, prevalence can reach 23%. The condition is critical due to its high mortality rate of approximately 90% upon rupture. Screening ultrasounds are recommended for high-risk individuals, as no combination of history or physical examination can reliably exclude an acute abdominal aneurysm.

## SGEM Bottom Line
Emergency department US, when applied by “trained” emergency physicians, is an excellent accurate diagnostic modality to detect triple A’s in symptomatic adult patients. When in doubt, go on to more definitive imaging.

## Case Resolution
You do a bedside ultrasound of the 66-year-old man with a history of hypertension and smoking and discover a 6cm acute abdominal aneurysm and contact vascular surgery.

## Clinical Application
Adult emergency department patients with any presentation consistent with potential triple A should be scanned.

## What Do I Tell My Patient
Emergency department ultrasound is a safe, noninvasive, painless, and accurate way to check to see if you have a serious life-threatening condition called triple A (acute abdominal aneurysm).
