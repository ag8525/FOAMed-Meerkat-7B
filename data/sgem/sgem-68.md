## Summary
The document discusses the normal ranges for heart rate and respiratory rate in children, referencing a systematic review by Fleming et al. that compiles data from 69 observational studies involving over 150,000 data points. This review provides evidence-based centile charts for children from birth to 18 years, intended to update clinical and resuscitation guidelines, and suggests these charts should replace existing norms from PALS and APLS. In a case scenario, an 18-month-old girl with viral gastroenteritis presents with a heart rate of 165 bpm, which is assessed against these updated charts. Management includes oral ondansetron and rehydration therapy, highlighting the importance of accurate vital sign assessment in pediatric emergency care. The document emphasizes the utility of these charts for clinicians and nurses in triage to determine the acuity of pediatric patients.

## Case Scenario
18-month-old girl presents to the emergency department with viral gastroenteritis. She has vomiting, diarrhea, and fever. Her heart rate is 165 beats per minute.

## Clinical Point
What are the normal ranges for heart rate and respiratory rate in children?

## Background
Pediatric vital signs can be assessed using several methods, including Clinical Gestalt, Pediatric Advanced Life Support (PALS), Advanced Pediatric Life Support (APLS), various formulae, and applications such as PediStat. These approaches are essential for evaluating the health status of pediatric patients and guiding clinical decision-making.

## SGEM Bottom Line
This is a well-conducted systematic review of the pediatric normal values for heart rate and respiratory rate. The provided graphs should replace existing values from other sources.

## Clinical Application
I quote and reference this paper all the time! Triage vitals at my hospital are measured against this graph. It is easy to have a PDF of these charts on your smartphone to use as a reference.

## What Do I Tell the Parents
Your daughter has abnormal vital signs. I am going to treat her symptoms and see if we can make her better.

## Case Resolution
18-month-old girl presents to the emergency department with viral gastroenteritis. She has vomiting, diarrhea, and fever. Her heart rate is 165 beats per minute. I provide her with oral ondansetron (8-15kg=2mg, 15-30kg=4mg, and >30kg=8mg) and oral rehydration therapy.
