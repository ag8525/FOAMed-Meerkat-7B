## Summary
The document discusses the management of a first episode of complete primary spontaneous pneumothorax, focusing on a randomized non-inferiority trial comparing simple aspiration to chest tube drainage. The study found that while simple aspiration was statistically non-inferior to chest tube drainage for lung re-expansion, it had a higher failure rate but was better tolerated with fewer adverse events. The trial highlighted the importance of considering both statistical and clinical non-inferiority, emphasizing that statistical findings should not solely guide clinical decisions. The study also explored the nuances of non-inferiority trial analysis, including the challenges of determining appropriate non-inferiority margins and the implications of different analytical approaches. The authors concluded that while simple aspiration is less invasive and better tolerated, it may not be as effective as chest tube drainage, and the choice of treatment should consider patient preferences and clinical context.

## Case
A 25-year-old female medical student presents with right chest pain and dyspnea. Chest x-ray (CXR) shows a “complete pneumothorax.”

## Background
A spontaneous pneumothorax is characterized by the presence of air in the pleural space between the lung and chest wall without an obvious precipitating factor, and it can occur with or without underlying lung disease. Clinically, it is significant due to its potential complications, which occur in 9–26% of cases. The standard first-line treatment for a primary spontaneous pneumothorax is chest tube drainage, although simple aspiration is a less invasive alternative. The optimal management strategy for a first episode remains uncertain. Post chest tube placement, a chest X-ray (CXR) is typically obtained, though it may not alter management unless the patient is deteriorating or the tube is not functioning. The correct placement of the chest tube in the pleural space is crucial, and in cases of traumatic hemothorax in hemodynamically stable patients, small percutaneous catheters are as effective as large open chest tubes. For tension pneumothorax, the anatomical location for needle decompression is debated, with no evidence supporting the superiority of the second intercostal space-midclavicular line over the fourth/fifth intercostal space-anterior axial line. Conservative management of a first large spontaneous pneumothorax may be reasonable with close follow-up.

## Clinical Point
Is simple aspiration non-inferior to chest tube drainage for first-line lung expansion in patients with complete primary spontaneous pneumothorax?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Needle aspiration to treat first-time spontaneous pneumothorax was statistically non-inferior to inserting a chest tube, better tolerated but did fail more often.

## Case Resolution
You discuss the options of aspiration, tube or observation. The patient prefers to be observed in the emergency department. The patient remains stable, and a CXR two hours later shows no change in the pneumothorax. She is discharged home with good social support, clear instructions on when to return to the emergency department and a follow-up appointment with her friendly neighborhood Thoracic surgeon the next day.

## Clinical Application
This is not what we routinely do in Canada. We don’t tend to aspirate. If you are going to insert a drain, why not leave it in and hook it up to a one-way Heimlich valve and send the patient home? Who wants to be observed in the ED with a drain overnight? We generally recommended short observation and if stable discharge home with appropriate support and follow-up. Any concern they get a small tube with a valve. I don’t see any value in this form of aspiration.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
Your lung has collapsed. Traditionally we carefully insert a chest tube to re-inflate your lung. Another option, which is a chest tube, is to insert a needle in the chest to re-inflate the lung. The chest tube does work better but may have to stay in play longer and is more uncomfortable. We could also put in a small tube with a one-way valve and have you go home later today. What would you like to do?
