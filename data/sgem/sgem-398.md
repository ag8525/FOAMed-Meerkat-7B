## Summary
In this prospective cohort study, the utility of point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) performed by emergency physicians in diagnosing acute biliary disease was evaluated against formal radiology-performed ultrasound (RUS) in a tertiary care setting. The study found that POCUS was sufficient to guide surgical decision-making in 90% of cases, with the surgical plan changing only 10% of the time after RUS. The study highlights the reliability of POCUS in diagnosing acute biliary conditions, suggesting that in non-jaundiced patients, emergency physicians trained in biliary ultrasound can effectively inform surgical decisions. However, the study's findings are limited by its single-center design and the need for replication in diverse healthcare settings. The authors conclude that POCUS offers a safe and efficient diagnostic pathway, though further large-scale studies are recommended to validate these findings across different clinical environments.

## Case
A forty-year-old woman presents to the emergency department (ED) complaining of epigastric pain and nausea for 24 hours. The junior doctor has performed a physical examination and blood work. She has some right upper quadrant (RUQ) tenderness on palpation and an elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) of 42. Her white blood cell count and liver function tests are normal. The very efficient junior doctor has arranged for a formal ultrasound, but this will be done later this afternoon, after six hours of fasting. As luck would have it, the admitting surgeon is currently in the ED seeing another patient. The supervising ED physician has been to a few training courses and is keen to try out his new sono-skills. This seems like a good case to try and decide if this patient has significant biliary disease such as acute cholecystitis or an impacted gallstone. A focused Biliary US in the ED (BUSED) may be all that is required to guide the surgeon’s decision-making for this patient. Do they need surgery, an ERCP, or just analgesia and non-operative care?

## Background
Point of care ultrasound (POCUS) is increasingly utilized in emergency departments (ED) as a first-line imaging modality for diagnosing acute biliary disease. It is performed by ED clinicians and is compared in accuracy to the traditional "gold standard" of radiology-performed ultrasound. While numerous small trials have assessed the diagnostic accuracy of POCUS relative to radiology-performed ultrasound, there is limited information on how POCUS findings influence clinical decision-making in the ED setting.

## Clinical Point
When compared to point-of-care ultrasound, what is the value of formal radiology-performed ultrasound in terms of the surgical decision-making in acute biliary disease?

## SGEM Bottom Line
In non-jaundiced patients with suspected acute biliary disease, an emergency physician trained in biliary US can correctly inform surgical decision-making in most cases.

## Case Resolution
The ED physician performs a focused biliary ultrasound which shows a gallbladder full of stones, some gallbladder wall thickening, and a positive sonographic Murphy’s sign. The team can make a diagnosis of acute cholecystitis and refer to the inpatient surgical team and commence antibiotics.

## Clinical Application
ED doctors can and should be trained in the use of biliary US to help guide their diagnosis in the workup of patients with abdominal pain. They do need to consider the clinical context and receive appropriate training to make accurate and timely diagnoses. This would be like other medical conditions where we rarely use only one data point to inform care.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
Your ultrasound, in combination with the blood tests and clinical picture, all suggest that you have acute gallbladder inflammation – this is usually treated with pain medicine and antibiotics, some patients do need to undergo surgery to remove their gallbladder. We will discuss the findings with our excellent Surgical colleagues who will take good care of you and discuss the plan further.
