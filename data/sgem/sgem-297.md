## Summary
The document discusses the reanalysis of the ECASS III trial data regarding the use of alteplase (tPA) for thrombolysis in acute ischemic stroke patients treated 3-4.5 hours after symptom onset. The reanalysis, adjusting for baseline imbalances, found no significant benefits and confirmed increased harm, particularly symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, challenging the original trial's conclusions of improved outcomes. The document highlights the importance of considering baseline imbalances and the fragility of the original trial's findings, emphasizing the need for replication studies. It also critiques the "time is brain" paradigm, suggesting that factors like collateral circulation and precise imaging are more critical in determining treatment efficacy. The document concludes with a call for clinicians and policymakers to reconsider the use of tPA in this time frame, given the lack of confirmed benefit and potential for harm.

## Case
A 65-year-old man arrives from home to the emergency department by EMS with right-sided weakness beginning three hours prior. Advance neuroimaging demonstrates he does not qualify for endovascular clot retrieval. He has an NIHSS score of 11 and no contra-indications for systemic thrombolysis.

## Background
Thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke, particularly the use of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), has been a contentious topic since the publication of the NINDS trial in 1995, which influenced guidelines to recommend thrombolysis within three hours of symptom onset despite subsequent trials showing mixed results and increased bleeding risks. The NINDS trial's findings have been questioned due to potential baseline imbalances and methodological concerns, leading to reanalyses that highlight the importance of replication studies. The ECASS III trial reported a 7% absolute benefit in functional outcomes at 90 days but also noted increased risks of intracranial hemorrhage. The American College of Emergency Physicians (ACEP) provides level B recommendations for offering tPA within three hours and possibly up to 4.5 hours post-onset, emphasizing the need for systems to safely administer the drug and the importance of shared decision-making due to the risk of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage. Despite the ongoing debate, thrombolysis remains a critical consideration in acute ischemic stroke management, with clinical decisions requiring careful evaluation of potential benefits and harms.

## Clinical Point
Is thrombolysis for acute ischaemic stroke in the 3-4.5 hour time frame post symptom onset, safe and effective?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Reanalysis of the original ECASS III data does not support the potential benefit of tPA given between 3-4.5 hours after onset of stroke symptoms and confirms the known potential harm.

## Case Resolution
You provide the man with the latest information on thrombolysis for stroke. He decides not to move forward with systemic tPA administration.

## Clinical Application
This furthers our skepticism about the thrombolytic literature for acute ischemic stroke especially in this later time frame. It will modify how we present the information to patients. On a broader scale, you decide locally to discuss this with your EM group and advocate for policy makers to re-consider their recommendations.

**What Do I Tell My Patient?**
You are having a stroke. It is being caused by a clot in the brain. There is a drug that has been used to try and dissolve clots. Older data showed that 7% of people may do better by 3 months. The drug does cause a potential 10% increase in bleeding in the brain. Two percent more of people getting this drug will have symptoms of the brain bleeding. It is not a life-saving drug and the same number of people die from this type of stroke (~8%). The existing guidelines say this medication may be offered to patients like you. However, a new look at the original data has raised some concerns. When re-analyzing the evidence, it showed the increase in harm was the same (bleeding in the brain). However, the potential benefits were unfortunately not confirmed. That means we are not confident this drug will have any benefit. We are confident that puts you at greater risk for bleeding.
