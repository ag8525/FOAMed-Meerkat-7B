## Summary
In the case of a 58-year-old woman diagnosed with superficial thrombophlebitis, the recommended treatment is fondaparinux, a synthetic factor Xa inhibitor, administered subcutaneously once daily for six weeks. This approach is supported by a Cochrane review, which found that fondaparinux significantly reduces the risk of progression to deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE) without increasing bleeding risk, compared to placebo. The review highlighted the efficacy of fondaparinux in reducing the composite endpoint of death, symptomatic PE/DVT, and extension or recurrence of superficial thrombophlebitis by 85%. While other treatments like low molecular weight heparin (LMWH) and NSAIDs were considered, they presented methodological issues and potential adverse effects, such as gastric complications. Therefore, fondaparinux is recommended for its effectiveness and safety profile, with caution advised in patients with renal dysfunction due to its renal excretion.

## Case Scenario
A 58-year-old woman arrives at the ED with a painful leg. You conduct an appropriate history and physical examination. She is Well’s criteria low, so you order a d-dimer. The d-dimer comes back elevated, so you request an ultrasound. This imaging test returns with "no evidence of deep vein thrombosis". You make the diagnosis of superficial thrombophlebitis.

## Point
What should you do to treat this woman’s superficial thrombophlebitis (NSAIDs, coumadin, LMWH, surgery, nothing)?

## Background
Superficial thrombophlebitis is a common condition characterized by the presence of a thrombus and inflammation in the superficial veins, typically of the leg. Clinically, it manifests as local pain, redness, itching, and hardening of the surrounding tissue. While generally less severe, there is a concern that superficial thrombophlebitis may progress to deep vein thrombosis, a more serious condition.

## BEEM Bottom Line
Fondaparinux SC OD for 6 weeks should be considered for treating thrombophlebitis of the leg.

## Case Resolution
You treat this woman with Fondaparinux to relieve her symptoms and prevent extension to DVT/PE.
