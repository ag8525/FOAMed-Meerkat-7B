## Summary
The document discusses the management of rib fracture pain in older adults, focusing on the use of serratus anterior plane blocks (SAPB) as an adjunct to standard care. Rib fractures, particularly in those over 65, pose significant risks due to pain-related complications like pneumonia. The SABRE randomized clinical trial evaluated SAPB's efficacy, showing it significantly improved pain control and reduced opioid use compared to standard care alone. The trial, however, faced limitations such as potential selection bias, lack of blinding, and a small sample size. Despite these, SAPB is highlighted as a promising analgesic technique, warranting further research to confirm its benefits and safety in broader patient populations.

## Case
A 67-year-old male presents to your hospital emergency department (ED) after a fall from his bicycle. He has three left-sided rib fractures on imaging without underlying complications. He has severe pain with breathing and asks you what the best way to manage his pain may be.

## Background
Rib fractures are a prevalent injury, especially in individuals over 65, due to decreased bone density and increased fragility associated with aging. These fractures are clinically significant as they cause severe pain, reduced mobility, impaired respiratory function, and increased morbidity and mortality, particularly due to complications like pneumonia. Standard management includes pain control, with recent advancements highlighting the efficacy of ultrasound-guided erector spinae plane blocks (ESPB) and serratus anterior plane blocks (SAPB). ESPB provides effective analgesia with reduced opioid requirements, while SAPB, performed under ultrasound guidance, targets thoracic intercostal nerves to offer substantial pain relief and improve respiratory function, thereby reducing the risk of pulmonary complications in elderly patients with rib fractures.

## Clinical Point
Does the addition of a serratus anterior plane block to a protocolized bundle of care in the emergency department improve pain control?

## SGEM Bottom Line
Serratus anterior plane blocks may reduce pain in ED patients with rib fractures however larger studies including consecutive patients with adequate masking that report adverse events are required.

## Case Resolution
You advise your patient that his pain may be difficult to control and that multiple modes of analgesia are likely to be effective. You discuss the potential benefits and harms of a serratus anterior plan block which he elects to undergo in combination with other analgesia. His pain significantly improves, and he is admitted to the ward to continue his care.

## Clinical Application
Ultrasound-guided serratus anterior plane blocks are a promising analgesic modality in a patient population with significant pain. We look forward to more research being published in this area.

**What Do I Tell the Patient?**
You have three broken ribs. These are very painful fractures. We will use some medications to help control your pain. There is an injection we can use in combination with other treatments that show promise. It is called a serratus anterior plane block. There is evidence it could decrease your pain but there are potential harms to any medical procedure. Would you like to discuss this further?
