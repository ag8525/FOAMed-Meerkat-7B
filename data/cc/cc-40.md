## Summary

Injury prevention and control are critical components of trauma management, recognizing trauma as a predictable disease rather than a random event. The document outlines three key aspects of injury control: prevention, acute care, and rehabilitation. Prevention involves understanding epidemiology and biomechanics, and implementing education, research, and public policy. Acute care requires a structured trauma system with referral hospitals, EMS, and care guidelines, followed by outcome research. Rehabilitation includes physical and occupational therapy, as well as mental health support. The Injury Triangle, developed by Dr. William Haddon, and strategies for preventing energy transfer are also discussed, emphasizing the role of healthcare practitioners in injury prevention.

## Overview

### Rosens in Perspective

- Trauma is a DISEASE
- This is a cognitive shift emphasizing that trauma isn’t the outcome of some misfortune or random event, but a predictable outcome of a specific chain of events.
- Injury from trauma is the leading cause of death for most age categories in North America and costs the system over 117 billion dollars alone in the US annually.
