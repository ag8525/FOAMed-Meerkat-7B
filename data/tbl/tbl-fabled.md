## Summary

The FABLED trial investigates the sensitivity of blood cultures taken after the initiation of antimicrobial therapy in patients with severe sepsis. The study, conducted across seven urban emergency departments in Canada and the USA, found that blood cultures obtained 30-120 minutes post-antimicrobial therapy had a significantly reduced sensitivity (56.3% per protocol) compared to those taken before treatment. The rate of positive blood cultures was notably higher when drawn pre-antibiotics (30.3% vs. 19.7% post-antibiotics). The findings underscore the importance of obtaining blood cultures before starting antimicrobial therapy to ensure accurate diagnosis and effective treatment tailoring, despite the potential for treatment delays. The study highlights the critical balance between timely antibiotic administration and diagnostic accuracy in managing septic shock.

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe sepsis, what is the sensitivity of blood cultures taken after the initiation of antimicrobial therapy?

## Background

Early empiric antibiotic therapy is crucial in managing septic shock, with delays in administration linked to increased mortality. Inappropriate antibiotic selection also raises mortality risk. The Surviving Sepsis Campaign recommends obtaining blood cultures before starting antimicrobial therapy, but concerns exist about potential delays in treatment.

## Bottom Line

Positive blood cultures are more likely if drawn before antibiotic therapy, aiding in tailoring and de-escalating treatment. Efforts should be made to obtain cultures pre-antibiotics without delaying treatment.
