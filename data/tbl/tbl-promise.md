## Summary

The ProMISe trial investigated whether early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) compared to standard care reduces 90-day mortality in adult patients with septic shock. Conducted across 56 hospitals in England, this randomized controlled trial included patients with suspected infection and refractory hypotension or hypoperfusion. The EGDT group received a protocolized treatment involving central venous catheterization and specific hemodynamic targets, while the control group received usual care without ScvO₂ monitoring. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in 90-day mortality between the EGDT (29.5%) and usual care (29.2%) groups. Secondary outcomes, including SOFA scores and ICU stay length, also showed minimal differences. The study concluded that EGDT did not improve outcomes and was not cost-effective, aligning with findings from the ARISE and ProCESS trials, thus challenging the benefits of EGDT as initially suggested by the Rivers study.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with septic shock, does early goal-directed therapy (EGDT) compared with standard therapy reduce mortality at 90 days?

## Bottom Line

ProMISe does not demonstrate any difference in 90-day survival between usual care and strict EGDT in hospitals in England. Importantly, this study also demonstrates that EGDT is not cost-effective. This is a landmark paper and completes the international triumvirate of sepsis studies. ProMISe, ARISE, and ProCESS have shown no benefit (or harm) with EGDT over ‘usual care’, unlike the Rivers study.
