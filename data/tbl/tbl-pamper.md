## Summary

The PAMPer trial investigated the impact of prehospital plasma resuscitation on 30-day mortality in trauma patients at risk for hemorrhagic shock, compared to standard care without plasma. Conducted as a multicenter, cluster-randomized trial across 27 air medical services, the study found that early administration of plasma significantly reduced 30-day mortality (23% vs. 33%) with a number needed to treat of 10. The trial included severely injured patients with blunt or penetrating trauma, excluding those with specific conditions such as isolated falls or severe burns. While the intervention showed a mortality benefit, secondary outcomes like 24-hour mortality and multiorgan failure rates showed no significant differences. The study highlighted the safety and potential life-saving benefits of prehospital plasma, though it noted limitations such as lack of blinding and generalizability concerns. Further research is recommended to validate these findings across different trauma systems.

## Clinical Point

In severely injured patients at risk for hemorrhagic shock, does prehospital plasma resuscitation, compared with standard-care resuscitation (excluding plasma), reduce 30-day mortality?

## Background

Traditionally, bleeding trauma patients are managed with prehospital crystalloids and component transfusion (RBCs, plasma, platelets) initiated upon hospital arrival. Recent evidence suggests early administration of blood components resembling whole blood may prevent trauma coagulopathy and improve outcomes. The PROPPR trial (2015) showed no significant mortality difference with different plasma, platelets, and RBC ratios, but reduced death from exsanguination. The COMBAT trial (2018) found no mortality difference with prehospital plasma versus saline, but had rapid transport times and was single-center.

## Bottom Line

Early prehospital plasma initiation significantly reduces mortality in high-risk bleeding patients without increasing harm. Further studies are needed to confirm benefits and applicability in different systems.
