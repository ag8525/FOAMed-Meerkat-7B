## Summary

The document discusses a multicenter, randomized trial comparing the ramped position to the sniffing position during endotracheal intubation in critically ill adults. The study aimed to determine if the ramped position reduces oxygen desaturation compared to the sniffing position. Results showed no significant difference in the lowest oxygen saturation between the two positions. However, the ramped position was associated with a worse view of the larynx, increased difficulty of intubation, and a higher likelihood of requiring multiple attempts. The study suggests that while the ramped position does not improve oxygenation, it may complicate the intubation process, indicating that patient positioning should be individualized based on clinician experience and patient-specific factors.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill patients undergoing emergency intubation, does the ramped position compared to the sniffing position reduce the incidence of oxygen desaturation?

## Background

Hypoxaemia occurs in nearly 50% of critical care intubations. Patient positioning may improve variables such as functional residual capacity and laryngeal anatomy. Observational data suggest the ramped position (torso and head elevated) may be beneficial compared to the sniffing position (patient supine, neck flexed, and head extended).

## Bottom Line

Among adult ICU patients, the ramped position did not improve the lowest recorded saturations but did increase the difficulty of intubation and the likelihood of repeated attempts. Each intubation should be assessed on a case-by-case basis, considering previous experience and comfort level when choosing patient position.
