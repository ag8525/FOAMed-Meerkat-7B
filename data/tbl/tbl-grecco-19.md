## Summary

The GRECCO-19 trial investigated the effects of colchicine on clinical and laboratory outcomes in hospitalized COVID-19 patients. This open-label, randomized controlled trial conducted across 16 Greek hospitals found that colchicine administration did not significantly affect peak high sensitivity cardiac Troponin or time to CRP elevation but did reduce clinical deterioration compared to standard care, with an odds ratio of 0.11. Secondary outcomes showed improved 10-day event-free survival in the colchicine group, though no significant differences were observed in all-cause mortality or need for mechanical ventilation. Diarrhea was a more common adverse event in the colchicine group. Despite these findings, the trial's small sample size and early termination limit its impact, suggesting that further research is necessary before colchicine can be recommended as a standard treatment for COVID-19.

## Clinical Point

In patients hospitalized with symptomatic COVID-19, does the administration of colchicine improve clinical and laboratory outcomes?

## Background

COVID-19 has prompted numerous trials to explore potential treatments. Colchicine, an anti-inflammatory drug used in conditions like gout and pericarditis, was hypothesized to benefit COVID-19 patients due to its effects on inflammatory pathways.

## Bottom Line

This trial suggests colchicine may reduce clinical deterioration in COVID-19 patients, but the evidence is not robust enough to change practice. Further research is needed before considering colchicine as a standard treatment.
