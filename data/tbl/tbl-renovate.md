## Summary

The RENOVATE trial compared high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) to noninvasive ventilation (NIV) in adult patients with acute respiratory failure (ARF) to assess their effectiveness in reducing endotracheal intubation or death within 7 days. Conducted across 33 Brazilian hospitals, this prospective, randomized controlled trial found that HFNO was non-inferior to NIV in preventing these outcomes in nonimmunocompromised, COPD, ACPO, and COVID-19 patient groups. While both interventions showed similar primary outcomes, HFNO was noted for superior patient comfort. Despite the trial's strengths, such as its large sample size and adaptive design, limitations included unblinded participants and clinicians, and high intubation rates in COPD patients. The findings suggest HFNO as a viable first-line treatment option in specific ARF scenarios due to its ease of use and patient tolerability.

## Clinical Point

In adult patients with acute respiratory failure, does high-flow nasal oxygen (HFNO) compared to noninvasive ventilation (NIV) reduce the rate of endotracheal intubation or death at 7 days?

## Background

- HFNO and NIV are used for acute respiratory failure (ARF) management.
- HFNO offers better patient comfort and ease of use compared to NIV.
- Previous trials (FLORALI, HERNANDEZ et al) showed mixed results regarding intubation rates and patient comfort.

## Bottom Line

HFNO is non-inferior to NIV for preventing intubation and mortality at 7 days in certain ARF subgroups. HFNO's simpler setup and improved tolerability make it a compelling first-line option in selected scenarios.
