## Summary

This randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial investigated the effect of adrenaline on survival in out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients. Conducted in Western Australia, the study included 534 adult patients, with adrenaline administered every 3 minutes after the third unsuccessful shock or upon IV access in non-shockable rhythms. The primary outcome showed no significant improvement in survival to hospital discharge with adrenaline (4%) compared to placebo (1.9%), with an odds ratio of 2.2 (p = 0.15). However, adrenaline significantly increased the rate of pre-hospital return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) to 25.3% versus 8.4% in the placebo group (p < 0.001). Despite these findings, the study's limited sample size and the small number of survivors to discharge restrict the ability to draw definitive conclusions, highlighting the need for larger trials to assess the impact of adrenaline on long-term survival and neurological outcomes.

## Clinical Point

In patients with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does adrenaline, compared to placebo, improve survival to hospital discharge?

## Bottom Line

Adrenaline increases ROSC following cardiac arrest but does not improve survival to hospital discharge or neurological outcomes. Larger trials are needed to determine the definitive impact of adrenaline on survival and neurological outcomes.
