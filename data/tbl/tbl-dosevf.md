## Summary

The DOSEVF trial investigated the efficacy of Double Sequential External Defibrillation (DSED) and Vector-change (VC) Defibrillation compared to standard defibrillation in patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. Conducted as a randomized controlled trial across six paramedic services in Ontario, Canada, the study found that DSED significantly improved survival to hospital discharge (30.4% vs. 13.3%) and termination of VF compared to standard defibrillation. VC defibrillation also showed improved survival rates (21.7% vs. 13.3%) but did not significantly enhance return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) or neurological outcomes. Despite these promising results, the trial's early termination and limited outcome events suggest that the effect size may be overestimated, warranting further research to confirm these findings.

## Clinical Point

In patients with refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF), does Double Sequential External Defibrillation (DSED) or Vector-change (VC) Defibrillation compared to standard defibrillation improve survival to hospital discharge?

## Background

Refractory VF occurs in up to half of patients following an out-of-hospital VF arrest. Anti-arrhythmics have shown limited efficacy in improving survival. Case series suggest potential benefits of DSED or VC defibrillation in treating refractory VF by targeting different parts of the ventricle.

## Bottom Line

DSED and VC defibrillation showed higher survival to hospital discharge in refractory VF. However, due to early trial termination and low event numbers, further research is needed. DSED or VC defibrillation may be reasonable options for refractory VF, but outcome benefits remain unclear.
