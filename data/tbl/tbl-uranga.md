## Summary

In a multicentre randomised controlled trial conducted in four Spanish teaching hospitals, the efficacy of a five-day antibiotic regimen for hospitalised patients with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) was compared to standard practice. The study included patients over 18 years with CAP, excluding those with conditions like HIV or recent antibiotic use. The trial found no significant difference in clinical success rates at day 10 and day 30 between the five-day intervention group and the control group, which followed usual care. The intervention group had a significantly shorter median antibiotic duration (5 days vs. 10 days) and lower hospital readmission rates by day 30. The study supports the safety of a shorter antibiotic course in stable CAP patients, aligning with IDSA/ATS guidelines, though results may not be generalizable to regions with different antibiotic prescribing practices.

## Clinical Point

In hospitalised patients with community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), is five days of antibiotic therapy non-inferior to standard practice regarding clinical improvement?

## Bottom Line

Stopping antibiotics at day five is a safe strategy for stable hospitalised patients with mild to moderate CAP in Northern Spain. Results may not generalise to the UK due to different prescribing practices. Further studies could enhance antibiotic stewardship.
