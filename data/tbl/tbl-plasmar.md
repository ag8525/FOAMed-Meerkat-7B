## Summary

The PlasmAr trial investigated the efficacy of convalescent plasma in hospitalized adults with severe Covid-19 pneumonia, comparing it to a placebo in a double-blind, multicenter study conducted in Argentina. The trial enrolled 334 patients, with 228 receiving convalescent plasma and 105 receiving placebo. The primary outcome, clinical status at 30 days, showed no significant difference between the groups, with an odds ratio of 0.83. Secondary outcomes, including 30-day mortality and need for invasive ventilatory support, also showed no significant differences. Notably, a subgroup analysis suggested a potential benefit of placebo in patients under 65 years. The study concluded that convalescent plasma did not provide a clinical benefit in severe Covid-19 pneumonia, highlighting the need for further research on its efficacy in milder cases or with earlier administration.

## Clinical Point

In hospitalized adult patients with severe Covid-19 pneumonia, does convalescent plasma compared with placebo improve patients’ clinical status at 30 days?

## Background

Convalescent plasma has been used for various infections, including SARS, MERS, and influenza. Its efficacy in Covid-19 has been frequently claimed but lacked conclusive data from randomized trials.

## Bottom Line

PlasmAr data show no benefit of convalescent plasma in severe Covid-19 pneumonia for reducing mortality or improving clinical outcomes at 30 days. Efficacy in milder cases or with earlier administration remains unclear.
