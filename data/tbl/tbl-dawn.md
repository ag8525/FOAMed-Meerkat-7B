## Summary

The DAWN trial investigated the efficacy of thrombectomy performed 6 to 24 hours after acute ischemic stroke in patients with a mismatch between clinical deficit and infarct volume. This randomized clinical trial demonstrated that thrombectomy, using the Trevo device, significantly improved functional outcomes at 90 days compared to standard medical care alone, with a notable increase in functional independence (mRS 0-2) from 13% to 49%. The study highlighted the importance of advanced imaging to identify patients who could benefit from delayed intervention. Despite its strengths, such as effective randomization and blinded outcome assessment, the trial's early termination and industry sponsorship were noted as limitations. The findings suggest that thrombectomy is beneficial in selected patients, although the availability of necessary imaging and resources may limit its broader application.

## Clinical Point

In patients 6-24 hours post-acute stroke with a mismatch between clinical deficit and infarct, does thrombectomy plus standard medical care improve functional outcomes at 90 days compared to standard medical care alone?

## Background

Previous trials showed benefit of thrombectomy within 6 hours of stroke onset. Limited data exists on thrombectomy after 6 hours. Patients with ischaemic brain tissue not yet infarcted may benefit from reperfusion, identified by a clinical deficit disproportionate to infarct volume on imaging.

## Bottom Line

Thrombectomy with the Trevo device improves functional outcomes at 90 days in patients with stroke last seen normal 6-24 hours prior. The study's advanced imaging techniques and comprehensive stroke care may not be available to smaller centres. Further studies are needed to validate these findings across broader stroke populations.
