## Summary

The document discusses a trial comparing conservative versus interventional treatment for primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP) in patients aged 14-50 years with moderate to large pneumothorax. The study, conducted across 39 hospitals in Australasia, was a prospective, randomized control, non-inferiority trial with 316 participants. The primary outcome was complete radiological resolution at 8 weeks, with conservative management proving non-inferior to interventional treatment, showing a 94.4% resolution rate compared to 98.5% in the intervention group. Conservative management resulted in fewer hospital days, less surgery, and fewer adverse events, suggesting it as a viable option for PSP, challenging the necessity of immediate drainage in all cases. The study highlights the importance of discussing treatment options with patients, considering non-intervention as a reasonable approach.

## Clinical Point

In patients with primary moderate-large spontaneous pneumothorax, does conservative management compared to interventional treatment result in acceptable rates of lung re-expansion at 8 weeks?

## Background

- Annual hospitalization rate for spontaneous pneumothorax: ~140 per million (Halifax, JAMA 2018)
- Interventional drainage is common but carries risks (pain, infection, organ injury)
- Mean hospital stay for primary spontaneous pneumothorax: 4 days (Thelle, ERS 2017)
- Lack of robust trial data on conservative management; historical support from a 1966 cohort study

## Bottom Line

Conservative management is a viable option for moderate-large primary spontaneous pneumothorax, reducing hospital days and adverse events without chest tube insertion. Discuss treatment options with patients, considering non-intervention as reasonable.
