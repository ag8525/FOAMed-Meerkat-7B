## Summary

The 3Mg Trial investigated the efficacy of intravenous (IV) and nebulised magnesium sulphate (MgSO₄) in adults with severe acute asthma, comparing these interventions to standard therapy. Conducted as a double-blinded, placebo-controlled, multi-centre trial across 34 UK emergency departments, the study included 1084 patients but was underpowered due to early termination. The primary outcomes showed no significant reduction in hospital admissions or improvement in breathlessness with either form of MgSO₄ compared to placebo. Secondary outcomes, including mortality and need for respiratory support, also showed no significant differences. The trial concluded that nebulised MgSO₄ is ineffective in this context, and while IV MgSO₄ may have a limited role, its clinical benefit is minimal. The study's strengths include its robust design and adherence to established guidelines, but its early termination and protocol changes are noted weaknesses.

## Clinical Point

Does intravenous or nebulised MgSO₄ improve symptoms of breathlessness and reduce the need for hospital admission in adults with severe acute asthma?

## Bottom Line

This is the largest trial to date on MgSO₄ in severe adult asthma. The results do not support nebulised magnesium use, and while IV MgSO₄ might still be considered in life-threatening asthma, its expected clinical impact is low.
