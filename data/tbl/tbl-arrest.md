## Summary

The ARREST trial investigates the efficacy of early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation in adults with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) and refractory ventricular fibrillation (VF) compared to standard ACLS. Conducted as a phase 2, single-centre, open-label RCT, the study found that ECMO significantly improved survival to hospital discharge (43% vs. 7% in the ACLS group) and demonstrated better cumulative survival and functional outcomes at six months. Despite its strengths, such as rapid ECMO initiation and robust safety reporting, the trial's limitations include its single-centre design, small sample size, and lack of cost analysis. The findings suggest that ECMO may be a superior strategy for refractory VF in OHCA, warranting further multicentre trials to confirm these results and assess broader applicability.

## Clinical Point

In adults suffering out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) with an initial rhythm of VF or pulseless VT, does early ECMO-facilitated resuscitation improve survival to hospital discharge compared to standard ACLS resuscitation?

## Background

- OHCA has a poor survival rate.
- PARAMEDIC 2 trial showed a 30-day survival rate of 2.8% for all-cause OHCA.
- Half of OHCA patients with VF have refractory VF, with worse prognosis.
- Evidence for ECMO in OHCA is from observational studies; no prior randomised trials.
- ELSO registry (2016) showed 29% survival to discharge for refractory OHCA.
- Other studies show survival rates from 5.5% to 45% for all-cause OHCA.

## Bottom Line

This trial shows early ECMO initiation results in 43% survival to discharge for refractory shockable OHCA. Further multicentre trials are needed to validate these findings and assess generalisability.
