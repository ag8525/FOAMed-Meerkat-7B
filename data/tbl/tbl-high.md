## Summary

The document discusses a study evaluating the use of high-sensitivity troponin assays in patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in the emergency department. The study, a stepped-wedge, cluster-randomised controlled trial conducted in Scotland, compared high-sensitivity troponin assays to standard assays to determine their impact on myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death within one year. Despite reclassifying 17% of patients with myocardial injury, the high-sensitivity assay did not significantly reduce the incidence of myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death compared to standard assays. The study highlights that while high-sensitivity assays lower the diagnostic threshold, they do not improve patient-centered outcomes within a year, suggesting the need for longer-term studies in diverse settings to evaluate potential benefits.

## Clinical Point

In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS), does the use of a high-sensitivity troponin assay reduce subsequent myocardial infarction or cardiovascular death at 12 months compared to a standard troponin assay?

## Background

Despite the use of biomarkers for myocardial injury, some patients with significant ACS are discharged from the ED. High-sensitivity troponin assays may identify more patients for treatment, potentially preventing future coronary events. However, increased sensitivity might reduce specificity, leading to more testing without patient-centered benefits.

## Bottom Line

High-sensitivity troponin assays lower the diagnostic threshold for myocardial infarction but do not improve patient-centered outcomes at one year. Longer-term data in broader settings are needed to assess downstream benefits.
