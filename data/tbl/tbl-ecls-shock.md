## Summary

The document discusses the use of venoarterial extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (VA ECMO) in patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) was conducted to determine if early, unselected application of VA ECMO reduces 30-day all-cause mortality in these patients. The study found no significant difference in mortality between the intervention and control groups, with a relative risk of 0.98. However, the intervention group experienced higher rates of complications, such as limb ischemia and bleeding. The trial's findings suggest that early unselective use of VA ECMO does not improve survival and may increase the risk of complications, indicating that VA ECMO should be reserved for patients who are refractory to medical therapy and have potential for myocardial recovery or require destination therapy.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock undergoing revascularisation, does early unselected application of extracorporeal life support (ECLS) reduce 30-day all-cause mortality?

## Background

- VA ECMO is an advanced technology providing temporary cardiac and respiratory support.
- Observational studies suggest VA ECMO reduces mortality in cardiogenic shock.
- VA ECMO is associated with high complication risks and may increase left ventricular afterload.
- A recent RCT showed no benefit with early ECMO application in cardiogenic shock.

## Bottom Line

This trial suggests early unselected VA ECMO in acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock does not reduce mortality and may increase complications. VA ECMO should be considered for patients refractory to medical therapy with potential for myocardial recovery or destination therapy.
