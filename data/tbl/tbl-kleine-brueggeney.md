## Summary

This multicentre randomized controlled trial evaluated the performance of six videolaryngoscopes in 720 patients with simulated difficult airways using a cervical collar. The study found that the McGrath and C-MAC D-blade videolaryngoscopes had the highest first-attempt intubation success rates of 98% and 95%, respectively, while the A.P. Advance had the lowest at 37%. The trial highlighted the importance of blade design in achieving successful intubation, with the McGrath and C-MAC also showing the lowest rates of tissue trauma. Despite the lack of a standard Macintosh laryngoscope as a control and the exclusion of patients with predicted difficult airways, the study provides valuable insights into the handling and performance variations among different videolaryngoscopes.

## Clinical Point

In patients undergoing elective surgery with a simulated difficult airway (using a cervical collar), does the choice of videolaryngoscope affect the first-attempt intubation success rate?

## Bottom Line

The primary hypothesis of a first-attempt intubation success rate >90% was accepted only for the McGrath. The A.P. Advance performed below other comparators. Despite limitations in internal validity due to subjectivity and unblinded operators, the trial provides a useful overview of performance and handling variations among videolaryngoscopes.
