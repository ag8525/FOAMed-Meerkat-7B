## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the effectiveness of lung ultrasound (LUS) integrated with clinical assessment in diagnosing acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) in the emergency department, compared to the standard approach using chest radiograph (CXR) and NT-proBNP levels. Conducted in two Italian academic centers, the study included adults presenting with acute dyspnea, excluding those on positive pressure ventilation or with trauma-related dyspnea. The trial found that LUS significantly improved diagnostic accuracy for ADHF, with higher sensitivity and specificity compared to CXR/NT-proBNP. The study highlights the potential of LUS to enhance diagnostic precision in emergency settings, although its generalizability may be limited by the high ICU admission rate and the predominance of a single recruitment center. The findings suggest that LUS could be a valuable tool in the clinical evaluation of acute dyspnea, supporting its use over traditional methods.

## Clinical Point

In patients presenting to the Emergency Department with acute dyspnoea, does the addition of lung ultrasound improve diagnostic accuracy for acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) compared to the standard approach?

## Background

Point-of-care ultrasound, including lung ultrasound, is increasingly used in assessing critically unwell patients. Previous studies have focused on the accuracy of lung ultrasound compared to traditional imaging modalities like chest radiographs. However, no randomized controlled trial has compared lung ultrasound's use in diagnosing acute heart failure against the standard clinical approach.

## Bottom Line

Adding LUS to clinical evaluation improves diagnostic accuracy for ADHF, whereas adding CXR and NT-proBNP does not. NT-proBNP is not routinely used in my practice, and this study supports that. I will continue using ultrasound for lung and heart evaluation in acute dyspnoea cases.
