## Summary

The After Eighty Study investigates the efficacy of an early invasive strategy versus a conservative approach in patients aged 80 years or older with non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) or unstable angina. Conducted as a randomized controlled trial across 16 hospitals in Norway, the study found that an invasive strategy significantly reduced the composite outcome of myocardial infarction, urgent revascularization, stroke, and death compared to conservative management, with a hazard ratio of 0.53 and a number needed to treat of 5. The invasive approach involved early coronary angiography and potential percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), while the conservative strategy relied on optimal medical treatment with angiography reserved for specific complications. The study highlights the benefit of invasive management in reducing myocardial infarction and urgent revascularization, though no significant advantage was observed in patients over 90 years. The trial's strengths include its randomized design and intention-to-treat analysis, while limitations involve the exclusion of many patients due to logistical issues and the use of a composite outcome measure.

## Clinical Point

In patients aged 80 years or older with unstable angina or non-ST elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), does an early invasive strategy compared with a conservative strategy reduce the composite outcome of myocardial infarction, need for urgent revascularisation, stroke, and death?

## Bottom Line

In patients over 80 years with NSTEMI/unstable angina, an invasive approach reduces the composite outcome of death, myocardial infarction, stroke, or need for urgent revascularisation, mainly due to reduced myocardial infarction and urgent revascularisation. In patients over 90 years, no benefit was found, but caution is needed due to low numbers.
