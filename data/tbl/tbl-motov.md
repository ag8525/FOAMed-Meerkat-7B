## Summary

The Motov Trial investigated the efficacy of intravenous subdissociative-dose ketamine compared to morphine for analgesia in patients with acute moderate to severe pain in the emergency department. This randomized controlled trial found no significant difference in pain score reduction at 30 minutes between ketamine and morphine, although ketamine was associated with more minor adverse effects such as dizziness and disorientation shortly after administration. Both medications were prepared and administered in a double-blinded manner, and the study concluded that subdissociative ketamine is as effective and safe as morphine for acute pain management. However, ketamine led to a higher need for rescue analgesia at 120 minutes, indicating potential limitations in its duration of effect. Further research is needed to consider ketamine as a standard alternative to morphine.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute, moderate to severe pain in the emergency department (ED), does ketamine compared with morphine reduce pain scores by an equivalent amount?

## Bottom Line

In patients with acute moderate to severe pain, subdissociative ketamine achieved a similar reduction in pain scores compared with morphine. It resulted in an increased number of minor side effects post-injection, which resolved by 30 minutes. Further evidence is required to change from the current standard of morphine.
