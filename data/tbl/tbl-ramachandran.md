## Summary

This randomized controlled trial investigated the efficacy of nasal oxygen administration in prolonging oxygen saturation levels during simulated difficult laryngoscopy in obese patients. Conducted at a single university hospital, the study included 30 patients with a BMI of 30-35 undergoing elective surgery with general anesthesia. The intervention group received 5 L/min of nasal oxygen from pre-oxygenation until post-intubation, while the control group did not. Results demonstrated that nasal oxygen significantly extended the duration of SpO₂ ≥ 95% and maintained higher oxygen saturation levels at 6 minutes compared to the control group. Despite the study's limitations, such as its small sample size and lack of blinding, the findings suggest that nasal oxygen should be considered standard practice in managing difficult airways in critically ill patients to prevent hypoxia.

## Clinical Point

In obese patients with simulated difficult laryngoscopy, does the use of nasal oxygen prolong the duration that oxygen saturations remain ≥95%?

## Bottom Line

The addition of nasal oxygen from pre-oxygenation until post-intubation significantly prolonged the duration that oxygen saturations remained ≥ 95% in simulated difficult airways. Despite some methodological limitations, particularly the lack of blinding, the significant benefits and minimal side effects of nasal oxygen suggest it should become standard practice for critically ill patients. If hypoxia occurs during RSI without nasal O₂, the rationale for its omission should be questioned.
