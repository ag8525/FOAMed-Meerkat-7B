## Summary

This retrospective cohort study investigates the impact of delayed antibiotic administration on mortality in septic shock patients across 14 ICUs. The study found that each hour of delay in administering effective antimicrobial therapy after the onset of septic shock is associated with a 7.6% decrease in survival, emphasizing the critical importance of early intervention. The primary outcome showed a stark contrast in survival rates, with 82.7% survival if antibiotics were administered within 30 minutes, compared to 42% if delayed up to 6 hours. Despite the study's non-randomized design and potential biases, it underscores the necessity of prompt antimicrobial therapy as a key component of the Surviving Sepsis Campaign, highlighting a significant gap in timely treatment, as only half of the patients received antibiotics within 6 hours.

## Clinical Point

Does a delay in antibiotic administration in patients with septic shock result in increased mortality?

## Bottom Line

This landmark study highlights the critical importance of early and appropriate antibiotic therapy in septic shock. Despite methodological limitations, the findings underscore the necessity of timely antimicrobial administration as part of the Surviving Sepsis Campaign.
