## Summary

The POKER study, a randomized, double-blinded clinical trial conducted in three Australian Emergency Departments, compared the safety and efficacy of ketofol (a combination of ketamine and propofol) versus propofol alone for procedural sedation in adult patients. The primary outcome was the occurrence of adverse respiratory events, which showed no significant difference between the ketofol (7%) and propofol (9%) groups. Secondary outcomes revealed that hypotension was more common with propofol, while emergence delirium was more frequent with ketofol. Despite these differences, both sedation methods were deemed equally safe regarding respiratory complications, and patient satisfaction was high with both agents. The study supports the use of either agent based on clinician familiarity, emphasizing the importance of adequate analgesia and adherence to sedation protocols.

## Clinical Point

In adult Emergency Department patients requiring intravenous sedation for noxious procedures, does a combination of propofol and ketamine (ketofol), compared with single-agent propofol, cause fewer adverse respiratory events?

## Bottom Line

This study suggests that ketofol and propofol are equally safe regarding respiratory complications for procedural sedation in the ED, provided patients receive adequate analgesia and are sedated using an appropriate protocol. Clinicians should continue to use the agent they are most familiar with.
