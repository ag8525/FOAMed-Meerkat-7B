## Summary

The CURASMUR trial investigated whether rocuronium is non-inferior to suxamethonium for successful first-pass intubation in out-of-hospital emergency settings. This nationwide, single-blinded, randomized non-inferiority study involved 1248 patients and found that rocuronium did not meet the non-inferiority margin compared to suxamethonium, with a first-pass success rate of 74.6% versus 79.4%, respectively. Despite the lower side-effect profile of rocuronium, the study's design did not demonstrate its superiority, and limitations such as unblinded physician reporting and lack of video laryngoscope use were noted. Rocuronium remains a viable option for emergency intubations, but the debate over its efficacy compared to suxamethonium continues.

## Clinical Point

In patients requiring emergency out-of-hospital endotracheal intubation, is rocuronium non-inferior to suxamethonium in providing a successful first-pass attempt?

## Background

- Suxamethonium and rocuronium are commonly used paralytic agents for emergency intubation.
- Suxamethonium has a higher side effect profile (e.g., hyperkalaemia, malignant hyperthermia).
- Rocuronium at 1.2 mg/kg is thought to provide comparable intubating conditions to 1 mg/kg of suxamethonium.
- Previous Cochrane review suggested rocuronium was slightly less effective than suxamethonium for intubating conditions.
- This trial is the first RCT comparing rocuronium and suxamethonium in prehospital emergency care.

## Bottom Line

Rocuronium at 1.2 mg/kg remains a reasonable choice for emergency intubations, but study limitations prevent firm conclusions on superiority. The debate on intubating conditions versus rare adverse reactions continues.
