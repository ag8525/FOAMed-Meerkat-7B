## Summary

The AVOID trial investigated the impact of supplemental oxygen therapy in normoxic patients with ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). The study found no significant difference in mean peak troponin levels between the oxygen and no oxygen groups, but a significant increase in mean peak creatinine kinase (CK) levels and recurrent myocardial infarction at discharge in the oxygen group. The trial suggests that routine oxygen therapy may not be beneficial and could potentially increase myocardial injury. The study's limitations include its focus on surrogate endpoints and lack of generalizability to all acute myocardial infarctions. The findings highlight the need for further research, such as the DETO2X-AMI trial, to assess clinically meaningful outcomes.

## Clinical Point

In patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) without hypoxia, does receiving supplemental oxygen compared with no supplemental oxygen reduce early myocardial injury?

## Bottom Line

This trial fails to show any benefit in giving oxygen to normoxic patients with STEMI undergoing PCI and may cause harm. Results should be interpreted cautiously as endpoints are surrogate outcomes only. Await results of DETO2X-AMI for more clinically meaningful outcomes.
