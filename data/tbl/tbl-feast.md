## Summary

The FEAST trial investigated the impact of fluid boluses on mortality in African children with severe febrile illnesses and impaired perfusion. Conducted across six centers, the randomized controlled trial compared outcomes between children receiving boluses of normal saline, 5% albumin, or no bolus. The study found that fluid boluses significantly increased 48-hour mortality compared to no bolus, with no difference between saline and albumin. Secondary outcomes showed increased mortality at four weeks in bolus groups and a higher incidence of pulmonary edema with albumin. The trial highlighted the potential harm of fluid boluses in resource-limited settings, particularly where malaria and severe anemia are prevalent, and critical care resources are scarce.

## Clinical Point

In paediatric patients with severe febrile illnesses in a resource-limited setting, do fluid boluses with albumin versus saline versus no fluid boluses affect mortality?

## Bottom Line

In an African setting, febrile children with impaired perfusion had increased mortality if treated with a fluid bolus compared to no fluid bolus. The study population had high malaria and severe anaemia incidence, managed with a low transfusion threshold and without critical care facilities, differing from other settings.
