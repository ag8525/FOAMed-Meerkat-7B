## Summary

This systematic review examines the risk of extravasation and local tissue injury from vasopressor administration via peripheral and central venous catheters. The review included 85 studies, highlighting that 64% of extravasation events led to local tissue injury, predominantly associated with peripheral catheters. The most common injuries were skin and tissue necrosis, with 98% of injuries linked to peripheral lines. The review suggests that short-term use of peripheral IV lines for vasopressors, especially when placed proximally, is generally safe in emergencies, but should be replaced by central venous access as soon as possible. The study underscores the need for further research to establish clearer guidelines, given the limitations in calculating precise complication rates due to narrative review constraints.

## Clinical Point

In patients administered vasopressor medication intravenously, what are the published reports on local tissue injury and extravasation?

## Bottom Line

This narrative review provides no firm conclusion. From the distribution of events described, it is reasonable to conclude that using proximal venous catheters for not more than 6 hours in emergencies, while a central venous catheter is inserted, is acceptable. This work should form a base for subsequent trials if the Point remains clinically relevant.
