## Summary

This prospective, randomized study compared intravenous levetiracetam to phenytoin for controlling status epilepticus (SE) and cluster seizures (CS) in patients over 15 years old. Conducted at a single university hospital in Oman, the trial found no significant difference in seizure control between the two drugs, with levetiracetam achieving control in 82% of SE cases and 81.6% of CS cases, compared to 73.3% and 80% for phenytoin, respectively. Secondary outcomes showed a better functional outcome at discharge for CS patients treated with levetiracetam. Despite these findings, the study's methodological limitations, including non-blinding, randomization issues, and being underpowered, suggest that these results should not alter current clinical practice, which typically favors phenytoin following benzodiazepines.

## Clinical Point

In patients with status epilepticus (SE) and cluster seizures (CS), does intravenous levetiracetam compared to intravenous phenytoin improve the rate of seizure control?

## Background

Status epilepticus is a medical emergency with high morbidity and mortality. Current guidelines recommend benzodiazepines followed by phenytoin. Levetiracetam, a newer agent, has shown promise in small studies but lacks extensive evaluation.

## Bottom Line

This trial found no significant difference in seizure control rates between levetiracetam and phenytoin. However, due to significant methodological concerns and underpowering, the results should not change clinical practice.
