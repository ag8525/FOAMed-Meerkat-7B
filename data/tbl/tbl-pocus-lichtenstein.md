## Summary

The document discusses the use of lung ultrasound, specifically the BLUE protocol, in diagnosing acute respiratory failure in ICU patients. Conducted as an observational diagnostic cohort study in two French ICUs, the study involved 301 patients, with 260 included in the analysis. The ultrasound, performed within 20 minutes of ICU admission, utilized a 5-MHz microconvex probe to assess various lung artifacts and conditions. The study found that the BLUE protocol provided an accurate diagnosis in 90.5% of cases, although the study's design had limitations such as post-hoc exclusions and lack of a validation group. Despite these limitations, the study suggests that bedside ultrasound can be a valuable diagnostic tool, though further validation is needed to confirm its efficacy.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute respiratory failure, can bedside ultrasound, compared to standard diagnostic methods, provide an accurate diagnosis?

## Bottom Line

In patients with acute respiratory failure, bedside ultrasound appears to help determine the diagnosis. Validation studies are required to confirm the utility of the BLUE protocol. Due to biases in the study design, the performance of the BLUE protocol is likely to be less accurate than reported.
