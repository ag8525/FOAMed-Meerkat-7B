## Summary

The INSTINCT trial investigated the efficacy of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) in patients with necrotising soft tissue infection (NSTI) compared to placebo, focusing on patient-reported physical outcomes. Conducted as a randomised, blinded, placebo-controlled trial in Denmark, it included 100 adult ICU patients with confirmed NSTI. The primary outcome, the mean physical component summary score at 180 days, showed no statistically significant difference between the IVIG and placebo groups. Secondary outcomes, including mortality, resolution of shock, and serious adverse events, also showed no significant differences. Despite the study's strengths, such as its randomised design and early intervention, limitations include the single-centre setting and lower IVIG dosing compared to UK practice. The trial concluded that IVIG did not improve physical functioning outcomes in NSTI patients, though it provides a basis for future research in specific infection subgroups.

## Clinical Point

In patients with necrotising soft tissue infection (NSTI), does the use of intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG) compared to placebo improve patient-reported physical outcomes?

## Background

- IVIG consists of pooled plasma from 2000-10000 donors and has anti-infective and immunomodulatory properties.
- NSTIs are characterised by fulminant tissue destruction, systemic signs of toxicity, and high mortality.
- Prior to INSTINCT, the effects of IVIG vs placebo were assessed in only one small RCT, which was stopped early due to slow recruitment.

## Bottom Line

This study does not demonstrate a statistically significant difference in patient-centred outcomes when IVIG is used with surgery, antimicrobials, and hyperbaric oxygen. Despite weaknesses, it provides valuable data for future NSTI studies, particularly in identifying and treating subgroups with streptococcal and staphylococcal infections.
