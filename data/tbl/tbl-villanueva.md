## Summary

In patients with acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding, a restrictive transfusion strategy (transfusion threshold of 7 g/dL and target hemoglobin of 7-9 g/dL) significantly reduces all-cause mortality at 45 days compared to a liberal strategy (threshold of 9 g/dL and target hemoglobin of 9-11 g/dL), with mortality rates of 5% versus 9%, respectively. The study, conducted in a single Spanish hospital, also found that the restrictive strategy led to fewer transfusions and complications, including transfusion reactions and cardiac events. Early endoscopy within 6 hours of presentation is crucial for the success of this approach, emphasizing the importance of 24-hour emergency endoscopy services. The findings suggest that a restrictive transfusion strategy should be standard care for upper GI bleeding when rapid endoscopy is available.

## Clinical Point

In patients presenting with an upper gastrointestinal (GI) bleed, does a restrictive transfusion strategy compared to a liberal transfusion strategy affect mortality?

## Bottom Line

A restrictive transfusion strategy is effective for upper GI bleeding when early endoscopy is available, highlighting the need for 24-hour emergency endoscopy services.
