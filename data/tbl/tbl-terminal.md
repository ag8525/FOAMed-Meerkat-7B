## Summary

Terminal QRS distortion is a critical ECG finding that differentiates benign early repolarization (BER) from left anterior descending artery occlusion (LADO) in patients presenting with chest pain. This retrospective study found that terminal QRS distortion is absent in 100% of patients with BER, making its presence highly specific for acute myocardial infarction. The study analyzed ECGs from patients with non-ischemic chest pain and confirmed early repolarization, noting that the absence of both an S wave and a J wave in leads V2 and V3 is indicative of myocardial infarction rather than BER. Despite its strengths, such as the use of two reviewers and clear criteria, the study's retrospective nature and demographic limitations suggest caution in generalizing the findings.

## Clinical Point

In patients with benign early repolarization (BER), is there an absence of terminal QRS distortion on the ECG?

## Background

Differentiating ECG findings of benign early repolarization from left anterior descending artery occlusion (LADO) can be challenging in patients presenting with chest pain. Terminal QRS distortion is often present in LADO cases. This study investigates its presence in BER patients.

## Bottom Line

In patients with benign early repolarization, terminal QRS distortion is absent. The presence of both an S wave and a J wave in V2/V3 is typical in BER. Absence of both suggests a diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction should be considered.
