## Summary

The IABP-SHOCK II trial investigated the efficacy of intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation (IABP) in patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock. Conducted across 37 centers in Germany, this randomized controlled trial included 600 patients and compared IABP with standard medical therapy. The primary outcome, 30-day all-cause mortality, showed no significant difference between the IABP group (39.7%) and the control group (41.3%), with a relative risk of 0.96. Secondary outcomes, including rates of re-infarction, peripheral ischemic complications, severe bleeding, and sepsis, also showed no significant differences. The study concluded that IABP did not reduce mortality or improve long-term outcomes in this patient population, although it did not significantly increase complications either.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute myocardial infarction (MI) and cardiogenic shock, does intra-aortic balloon counterpulsation (IABP) compared to medical therapy alone improve mortality?

## Bottom Line

In patients with cardiogenic shock following acute MI, IABP did not improve mortality or long-term secondary outcomes. The use of IABP did not significantly increase complications.
