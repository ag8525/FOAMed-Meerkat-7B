## Summary

The document discusses a retrospective observational study examining the impact of timely completion of a 3-hour sepsis care bundle on in-hospital mortality. Conducted across 185 hospitals in New York, the study found that each hour of delay in completing the bundle increased the odds of mortality by 4%. The bundle includes early administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics, blood cultures, and lactate measurement, with rapid antibiotic administration being particularly crucial. The study highlights the importance of prompt sepsis management, although it acknowledges limitations such as potential confounders and the observational nature of the data. The findings support the practice of delivering sepsis interventions as early as possible to improve patient outcomes.

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe sepsis or septic shock, does a faster time to completion of a 3-hour bundle of care, compared with a slower time, affect in-hospital mortality?

## Background

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition with high mortality. Sepsis "bundles" combining high-impact interventions have been popularized since the 2000s, notably the Early Goal Directed Therapy (EGDT) approach, part of the Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC). These bundles typically include early antibiotics, source control, and rapid hemodynamic resuscitation. New York City mandated their sepsis bundle in 2013.

## Bottom Line

This study demonstrates an association between a delay in administering a 3-hour bundle of care and increased mortality in a large cohort of patients in New York. This does not change my current practice of delivering high-impact interventions in sepsis as early as possible.
