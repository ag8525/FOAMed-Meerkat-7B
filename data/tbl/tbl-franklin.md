## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the efficacy of high-flow oxygen therapy versus standard oxygen therapy in infants with bronchiolitis and hypoxaemia. The study found that high-flow therapy significantly reduced treatment failure rates, with a risk difference of -11 percentage points, indicating fewer escalations of care compared to standard therapy. However, there were no significant differences in secondary outcomes such as ICU admissions, duration of hospital stay, or intubation rates. The trial was conducted across 17 hospitals in Australia and New Zealand, involving 1,638 infants. Despite its strengths, including a large sample size and pragmatic design, the study's unblinded nature and potential for selection and detection biases were noted as limitations. High-flow therapy is suggested as a viable option for managing bronchiolitis in infants, particularly in ward settings with adequate support.

## Clinical Point

In infants (<12 months) with bronchiolitis and hypoxaemia (saturations <92%) in the emergency department or general paediatric ward, does early treatment with high-flow therapy versus standard oxygen therapy result in fewer infants having treatment failure that leads to escalation of care?

## Background

Bronchiolitis is the leading cause of non-elective hospital admissions in infants, with a significant increase in intensive care unit admissions. There are no disease-modifying therapies for bronchiolitis, and the effectiveness of high-flow therapy in reducing intensive care admissions or intubation has been primarily reported in observational studies.

## Bottom Line

High-flow therapy reduces treatment failure in infants with bronchiolitis but does not significantly impact ICU admissions or other secondary outcomes. It may be suitable for ward use with appropriate education and support.
