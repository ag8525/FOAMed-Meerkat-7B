## Summary

The CRYOSTAT-2 trial investigated whether early empiric administration of high-dose cryoprecipitate (6g fibrinogen) within 90 minutes of randomization improves survival in trauma patients with critical bleeding requiring a major haemorrhage protocol. Conducted across 26 major trauma centers in the UK and USA, this phase 3 RCT included 1604 patients, with 799 receiving cryoprecipitate and 805 receiving standard care. The study found no significant difference in 28-day all-cause mortality between the cryoprecipitate group (25.3%) and the standard care group (26.1%), with an odds ratio of 0.96. Secondary outcomes, including mortality at various time points and transfusion requirements, also showed no significant differences. Despite strong internal validity, the trial's unblinded design and lack of tailoring to individual fibrinogen levels were noted as limitations. The findings do not support the routine use of empiric cryoprecipitate therapy in this setting, suggesting further research into optimal timing is needed.

## Clinical Point

In trauma patients with critical bleeding requiring activation of a major haemorrhage protocol, does the empiric administration of 3 pools of cryoprecipitate (6g fibrinogen) within 90 minutes of randomisation improve survival compared to standard care?

## Background

- Trauma is a leading cause of death globally, with haemorrhage being a primary mechanism.
- Fibrinogen stabilises clot formation, but levels drop rapidly post-trauma.
- Previous studies, like the PAMPer trial, showed benefits of early plasma administration.
- The CRYOSTAT pilot indicated feasibility and potential benefits of early cryoprecipitate administration.

## Bottom Line

The trial does not support empiric cryoprecipitate therapy in trauma patients with major haemorrhage. Further exploration of the timing of administration is warranted.
