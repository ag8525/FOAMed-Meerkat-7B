## Summary

The CRASH-1 trial investigated the effect of intravenous corticosteroids on mortality in adults with clinically significant head injuries. This large, randomized, placebo-controlled trial included 10,008 patients across 239 hospitals in 49 countries. The study found that corticosteroids increased the risk of death within 14 days, with a mortality rate of 21% in the corticosteroid group compared to 18% in the placebo group, resulting in a relative risk of 1.18. At 6 months, mortality and severe disability rates were also higher in the corticosteroid group. The trial concluded that corticosteroids should not be routinely used for head injuries due to the increased risk of death, highlighting the importance of avoiding this treatment unless specific indications justify its use.

## Clinical Point

In adults with head injury, do early corticosteroids compared to placebo reduce death and disability?

## Bottom Line

Corticosteroids should not be given to patients with head injuries unless other specific indications exist that outweigh the increased risk of death demonstrated by this trial.
