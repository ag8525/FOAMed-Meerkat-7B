## Summary

The REVERT trial investigated the efficacy of a modified Valsalva manoeuvre, incorporating a leg lift, in converting supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) to sinus rhythm in emergency department settings. Conducted across 10 UK hospitals, this randomized controlled trial included 428 patients with regular, narrow complex tachycardia. The modified technique significantly improved conversion rates to sinus rhythm (43% vs. 17% with standard Valsalva), with an odds ratio of 3.7 and a number needed to treat of 4. The study concluded that this simple, cost-free modification should be considered as a first-line treatment for stable SVT patients, offering a practical and effective alternative to the standard approach.

## Clinical Point

Does a modified Valsalva technique, incorporating a leg lift procedure, improve success in converting supraventricular tachycardia to sinus rhythm in patients presenting to the Emergency Department?

## Bottom Line

The modified Valsalva technique is a simple, effective, cost-free, and well-tolerated postural modification to the standard Valsalva technique. This study supports its use over the standard approach as an early treatment for haemodynamically stable patients with supraventricular tachycardia.
