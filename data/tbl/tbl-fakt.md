## Summary

The FAKT study investigated the impact of adding fentanyl to ketamine and rocuronium during rapid sequence intubation (RSI) in emergency departments, focusing on maintaining systolic blood pressure (SBP) within 100-150 mmHg. This double-blind, placebo-controlled trial found no significant difference in the primary outcome of SBP stability between the fentanyl and placebo groups. However, the fentanyl group experienced a higher incidence of hypotension and a lower incidence of hypertension compared to placebo. The study suggests that while fentanyl may mitigate hypertension, it could increase hypotension risk, indicating the need for careful consideration of haemodynamic targets when using fentanyl as a co-induction agent. The trial's strengths include its robust design and high data completion rate, though its findings are limited by regional focus and potential selection bias.

## Clinical Point

In patients requiring rapid sequence intubation (RSI) in the emergency department, does the addition of fentanyl to ketamine and rocuronium reduce the frequency of patients having a systolic blood pressure (SBP) outside of a target range of 100-150 mmHg?

## Background

- The INTUBE study showed high rates (42%) of cardiovascular instability in patients undergoing tracheal intubation in ICU, ED, and wards.
- Ketamine is commonly used for RSI due to its preferable haemodynamic profile.
- Fentanyl may prevent the pressor response to laryngoscopy and episodes of hypertension, but its effect on hypotension risk is unclear.

## Bottom Line

Fentanyl, when given in a fixed ratio with ketamine, may increase the risk of peri-intubation hypotension. Clinicians should individually select doses of induction agents based on patient characteristics and co-induction agents used.
