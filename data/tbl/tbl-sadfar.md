## Summary

This meta-analysis evaluates the efficacy of chlorhexidine-impregnated dressings in preventing catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI) compared to conventional site care. The analysis included nine prospective randomized controlled trials, primarily involving adult ICU and haematology/oncology populations. Results indicate a significant reduction in CRBSI and catheter colonization rates with chlorhexidine dressings, particularly in adult ICU and haematological malignancy patients, though not in pediatric populations or for long-term catheters. The study highlights the potential of chlorhexidine dressings to reduce infection rates, but notes limitations such as significant heterogeneity among studies, lack of double-blinding, and limited applicability to settings with low baseline CRBSI rates. Contact dermatitis was the most common adverse event reported.

## Clinical Point

Does chlorhexidine-impregnated dressing compared to conventional site care prevent catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSI) and catheter colonisation?

## Bottom Line

This meta-analysis suggests chlorhexidine-impregnated dressings may reduce CRBSI, but the findings are limited by significant heterogeneity. For adult ICU patients, the results are less applicable due to variability in baseline CRBSI rates and skin preparation methods.
