## Summary

The PEG-eD study evaluated the safety of using an adjusted d-dimer threshold to exclude pulmonary embolism (PE) in low and moderate-risk patients in the emergency department. By employing a d-dimer cut-off of <1000 ng/mL for low clinical pre-test probability (C-PTP) and <500 ng/mL for moderate C-PTP, the study found that PE could be safely ruled out without imaging in these groups, significantly reducing the need for chest imaging from 51.9% to 34.3%. The study demonstrated that none of the patients with negative d-dimer results developed venous thromboembolism (VTE) at 90-day follow-up, supporting the use of this strategy to minimize unnecessary radiation exposure and contrast-related risks. Despite some limitations, such as a lower than expected PE rate and potential selection bias, the findings suggest that adjusting d-dimer thresholds based on clinical probability is a viable approach to streamline PE diagnosis in the ED.

## Clinical Point

In low and moderate-risk patients presenting to the Emergency Department with symptoms suggestive of pulmonary embolism (PE), can a higher d-dimer threshold be used to safely exclude PE without the need for imaging?

## Background

- Diagnostic testing for PE in the ED typically involves clinical assessment, biomarker levels, and imaging.
- A d-dimer cut-off of <500 ng/mL is commonly used to exclude PE in low-risk patients.
- Moderate-risk patients often undergo imaging rather than relying on d-dimer.
- Imaging, such as CTPA, exposes patients to radiation and potential contrast allergies, and can increase ED length of stay.
- Previous studies (ADJUST-PE, YEARS) suggested adjusted d-dimer thresholds based on age or pre-test probability could safely exclude PE.
- The PEG-eD study aimed to evaluate if a d-dimer cut-off twice the usual threshold could safely exclude PE, including in moderate-risk patients.

## Bottom Line

Using a d-dimer cut-off adjusted for clinical probability appears safe to reduce diagnostic imaging in low-risk PE patients. This approach should be adopted by EDs for suspected PE work-up.
