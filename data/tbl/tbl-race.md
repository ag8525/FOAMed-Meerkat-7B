## Summary

The RACE trial investigated the efficacy of levocarnitine as an adjunctive treatment for septic shock, comparing it to a placebo in reducing organ failure at 48 hours. Conducted in 16 urban medical centers in the U.S., this prospective, randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled trial utilized Bayesian response-adaptive randomization. Despite the theoretical benefits of levocarnitine in enhancing mitochondrial fatty acid metabolism, the study found no significant reduction in organ failure with low, medium, or high doses of levocarnitine compared to placebo. The primary outcome, change in SOFA scores, did not meet the threshold for superiority, and secondary outcomes showed no significant differences in ICU length of stay or care withdrawal. The trial underscores the challenges in finding effective pharmacologic interventions for sepsis and suggests that the doses used may not have been sufficient to detect small treatment effects.

## Clinical Point

In patients with septic shock, does levocarnitine, compared with placebo, reduce organ failure at 48 hours?

## Background

Despite extensive research, specific pharmacologic therapies targeting sepsis pathophysiology remain elusive. Levocarnitine may mitigate metabolic effects of sepsis by enhancing fatty acid entry into mitochondria and reducing toxic effects.

## Bottom Line

Levocarnitine at low, medium, or high doses did not significantly reduce organ failure in septic shock patients compared to placebo. This trial highlights the potential of metabolic modulation in sepsis but shows no benefit with the doses used.
