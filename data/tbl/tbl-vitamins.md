## Summary

The VITAMINS trial investigated whether a combination of intravenous vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine could more rapidly resolve septic shock compared to hydrocortisone alone. Conducted across 10 ICUs in Australia, New Zealand, and Brazil, the study included 211 patients with septic shock. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in the median time alive and free of vasopressors between the intervention and control groups. Secondary outcomes revealed a significant change only in the SOFA score at day 3. The trial concluded that the HAT protocol did not improve outcomes in septic shock, highlighting the need for further research on the timing and potential benefits of these interventions. The study's open-label design and lack of separate assessment for vitamin C and thiamine effects were noted as limitations.

## Clinical Point

Does treatment with vitamin C, hydrocortisone, and thiamine lead to a more rapid resolution of septic shock compared with hydrocortisone alone?

## Background

Sepsis is a life-threatening condition characterized by organ dysfunction due to a dysregulated host response to infection. High-dose intravenous vitamin C has been explored as an adjunctive therapy in sepsis for its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. Previous studies have suggested potential benefits of vitamin C and thiamine in sepsis management.

## Bottom Line

The VITAMINS trial does not support the use of the HAT protocol (hydrocortisone, ascorbic acid, and thiamine) for treating septic shock. Further research is needed to explore the timing and potential benefits of these interventions.
