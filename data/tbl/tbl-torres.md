## Summary

The document discusses a study on the use of corticosteroids in patients with severe community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and a high inflammatory response. Conducted as a multi-centre, double-blind, randomized-controlled trial across three Spanish hospitals, the study aimed to determine if methylprednisolone could reduce treatment failure compared to placebo. The primary outcome showed a reduction in treatment failure in the corticosteroid group, particularly in late treatment failures marked by increased pulmonary infiltrates. However, no significant differences were observed in secondary outcomes such as time to stability, length of stay, or mortality. Despite the study's strengths, including its robust design and adequate power calculation, limitations such as a small sample size, long trial duration, and an unusual composite primary outcome were noted. The authors concluded that while corticosteroids may reduce treatment failure in severe CAP with high inflammatory response, further research is necessary to confirm these findings and inform clinical practice changes.

## Clinical Point

In patients with severe community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) and high inflammatory response, does the use of corticosteroids compared to placebo improve outcomes?

## Bottom Line

This study suggests corticosteroids may reduce treatment failure in severe CAP with high inflammatory response, but small sample size and composite outcomes limit generalisability. Further research is needed before changing clinical practice.
