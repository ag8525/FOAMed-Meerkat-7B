## Summary

The ANNEXA-I trial investigated the efficacy of Andexanet in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) associated with recent factor Xa inhibitor use, compared to standard care. Conducted as an international, multi-center, unblinded RCT, the study included 530 patients and found that Andexanet significantly improved hemostatic efficacy, with 67% achieving this outcome versus 53% in the usual care group. Despite these findings, the trial noted an increased incidence of thrombotic events with Andexanet, and no significant improvement in long-term functional outcomes was observed. The study highlights the potential of Andexanet to control hematoma expansion but advises caution due to the associated thrombotic risks.

## Clinical Point

In patients who have recently taken factor Xa inhibitors and have an intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), does the administration of Andexanet result in better hemostatic efficacy compared to standard care?

## Background

- Factor Xa inhibitors (e.g., apixaban, rivaroxaban) are widely used but can lead to ICH.
- Andexanet is a novel antidote hypothesized to rapidly reverse FXa inhibitors' effects, potentially reducing hematoma expansion and improving outcomes.
- Conventionally, prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) is used for reversal, as specific antidotes like Idarucizumab are unavailable for FXa inhibitors.

## Bottom Line

This trial shows a reduction in ICH hematoma expansion with Andexanet but no evidence of long-term patient-important benefits. Increased thrombotic events suggest caution in its use.
