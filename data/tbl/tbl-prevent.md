## Summary

The PReVENT trial investigated whether a low tidal volume ventilation strategy (6 mL/kg) compared to an intermediate tidal volume strategy (10 mL/kg) would increase ventilator-free days in ICU patients without ARDS. Conducted across six ICUs in the Netherlands, the study included 961 adult patients who required invasive ventilation and were not expected to be extubated within 24 hours. The trial found no significant difference in ventilator-free days at day 28 between the two strategies, with both groups averaging 21 days. Secondary outcomes, including ICU/hospital stay and mortality rates, also showed no differences. Despite the trial's robust design, limitations such as inadequate separation between strategies and potential selection bias were noted. The findings suggest that current practices of targeting lower tidal volumes in non-ARDS patients may continue until further evidence emerges.

## Clinical Point

In mechanically ventilated patients without acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), does a low tidal volume ventilation strategy (6 mL/kg) compared to an intermediate tidal volume strategy (10 mL/kg) reduce the number of ventilator-free and alive days at day 28?

## Background

- Current guidelines recommend low tidal volumes (<6-8 mL/kg) for ARDS patients.
- Uncertainty exists regarding benefits for non-ARDS patients.
- Non-ARDS patients are a larger group and have been excluded from ARDS trials.
- Previous studies suggest lower tidal volumes may reduce ventilation time and ICU stay but could increase patient-ventilator asynchrony and sedation needs.

## Bottom Line

No clear difference was found between lower and higher tidal volume strategies. Clinicians are likely to continue targeting lower tidal volumes (6-8 mL/kg) in non-ARDS patients until further evidence suggests otherwise.
