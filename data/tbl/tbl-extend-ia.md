## Summary

The EXTEND-IA TNK trial evaluated the efficacy of tenecteplase compared to alteplase in patients with anterior circulation strokes undergoing endovascular thrombectomy within 4.5 hours of stroke onset. This randomized controlled trial demonstrated that tenecteplase was non-inferior to alteplase in achieving reperfusion, with a higher rate of >50% reperfusion or absence of thrombus at initial angiography (22% vs. 10%, p=0.002 for non-inferiority). Tenecteplase also showed better functional outcomes at 90 days, with a median modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 2 compared to 3 for alteplase (p=0.04). There were no significant differences in early neurological improvement, recovery to independent function, cerebral hemorrhage, or mortality between the groups. The study supports tenecteplase as a viable alternative to alteplase, with potential advantages in pharmacodynamics, though further research is needed to confirm superiority.

## Clinical Point

In patients with anterior circulation strokes undergoing endovascular thrombectomy, is IV thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of stroke onset with tenecteplase non-inferior to alteplase at establishing reperfusion?

## Background

- Management of acute stroke has evolved, incorporating endovascular thrombectomy with alteplase for large-vessel occlusions.
- Tenecteplase, a variant of alteplase, offers superior fibrin specificity, longer half-life, and rapid bolus administration.
- Previous studies suggested tenecteplase may improve cerebral reperfusion and clinical outcomes.

## Bottom Line

Tenecteplase was non-inferior to alteplase for achieving >50% reperfusion in acute anterior circulatory strokes. Its pharmacodynamic properties favor its use over alteplase, though conclusions on superiority await further studies.
