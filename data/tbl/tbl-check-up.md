## Summary

The document discusses a multicenter randomized trial evaluating the effectiveness of a pre-intubation checklist in preventing oxygen desaturation and systolic hypotension in critically ill adults undergoing endotracheal intubation. Despite the checklist's potential to improve non-technical skills and preparation, the study found no significant difference in the primary outcomes of lowest median oxygen saturation and systolic blood pressure between the checklist and usual care groups. Secondary outcomes, including the number of intubation attempts and life-threatening complications, also showed no significant differences. The study's strengths include its prospective, multicenter design and focus on trainees, while weaknesses involve the complexity of the factorial design and incomplete checklist adherence. The authors conclude that while the checklist did not reduce complications, its use should not be discouraged as there may be other unmeasured benefits.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill patients undergoing intubation, does the use of a pre-intubation checklist compared to no checklist prevent oxygen desaturation or systolic hypotension?

## Background

Complications occur in up to one-third of critically ill patients undergoing airway intubation, with hypoxaemia and hypotension being the most common events. Operator inexperience and inadequate preparation are associated with increased incidence of complications. A checklist is a cost-effective intervention that can improve non-technical “human” factors during times of stress and high-intensity mental activity.

## Bottom Line

This study suggests that the use of a pre-intubation checklist does not reduce peri-procedural complications and may delay intubation. The use of checklists should not be discouraged based on this trial alone, as there may be other unmeasured benefits.
