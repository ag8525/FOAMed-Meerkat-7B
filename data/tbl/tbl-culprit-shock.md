## Summary

The CULPRIT-SHOCK trial evaluated the outcomes of immediate multi-vessel PCI versus culprit lesion-only PCI in patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock and multi-vessel disease. The study found that culprit-lesion-only PCI significantly reduced the 30-day composite outcome of all-cause mortality or renal failure requiring replacement therapy compared to multi-vessel PCI. However, at one year, there was no significant difference in mortality, renal replacement therapy, or recurrent myocardial infarction between the groups, although the culprit-lesion-only group experienced higher rates of rehospitalization and repeat revascularization. These findings suggest that while culprit-lesion-only PCI may offer short-term benefits, it may necessitate further interventions, aligning with recent guideline changes that recommend against routine non-culprit lesion intervention in this patient population.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute myocardial infarction complicated by cardiogenic shock and multi-vessel disease, does immediate multi-vessel PCI compared to culprit lesion-only PCI (with staged revascularisation) improve outcomes?

## Background

- Early revascularization reduces mortality in acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock.
- Most patients with cardiogenic shock have multi-vessel coronary artery disease, associated with higher mortality.
- ESC and AHA/ACC 2014 guidelines recommend PCI of all high-grade lesions in cardiogenic shock, but the CULPRIT-SHOCK trial challenged this by showing lower risk of death or severe renal failure with culprit-lesion-only PCI.

## Bottom Line

In acute myocardial infarction with cardiogenic shock, a targeted PCI strategy may be more beneficial, though further interventions may be needed. Supports recent ESC guideline changes downgrading non-culprit lesion intervention.
