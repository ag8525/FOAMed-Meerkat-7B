## Summary

The 6S trial compared the effects of 6% hydroxyethyl starch (HES) 130/0.42 with Ringer’s acetate in critically ill adults with severe sepsis, focusing on the incidence of death or end-stage kidney failure. Conducted across 26 ICUs in Scandinavia, the trial found that patients receiving HES had a higher risk of death or dialysis dependence at 90 days compared to those receiving Ringer’s acetate, with a relative risk of 1.17 and an absolute risk increase of 7.5%. Secondary outcomes showed increased renal replacement therapy and severe bleeding in the HES group. The study's strengths include effective blinding and randomization, while weaknesses involve protocol deviations and lack of standardized management. The findings suggest caution in using starch-based fluids for sepsis due to increased mortality and renal complications.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill adults with severe sepsis, does 6% hydroxyethyl starch (HES) compared to Ringer’s acetate reduce the incidence of death or end-stage kidney failure?

## Background

- Septic shock involves fluid deficiency and vasoplegia.
- Intravenous fluid therapy is crucial, with guidelines recommending up to 30 ml/kg as boluses.
- Colloids and crystalloids are used for volume restoration.
- High molecular weight and substitution ratio starches are linked to acute kidney injury.
- This trial investigates a starch-based colloid with lower molecular weight and substitution ratio.

## Bottom Line

The trial suggests hydroxyethyl starch increases the risk of death and renal replacement therapy in severe sepsis, aligning with other similar trials. Caution is advised in using starch-based fluids in this population.
