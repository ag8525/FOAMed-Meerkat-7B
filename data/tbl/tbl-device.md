## Summary

In a pragmatic, multicenter, unblinded randomized trial conducted across 17 sites in the USA, the use of videolaryngoscopy (VL) was compared to direct laryngoscopy (DL) for tracheal intubation in critically ill adults. The study found that VL significantly improved the first-pass success rate of intubation (85.1% for VL vs. 70.8% for DL, with an absolute risk difference of 14.3%, p < 0.001). Despite the higher success rate with VL, the incidence of severe complications within two minutes post-intubation was similar between the two groups. The trial's strengths include its robust design and large sample size, while limitations involve the open-label nature and variability in blade choice. The findings suggest that VL may enhance intubation success, particularly for less experienced intubators, although it may not drastically change practices for those already proficient with VL.

## Clinical Point

In critically ill adults undergoing tracheal intubation, does the use of a videolaryngoscope (VL) compared to a direct laryngoscope (DL) improve the first-pass success rate?

## Bottom Line

Videolaryngoscopy improves first-pass success in critically ill patients undergoing tracheal intubation by relatively inexperienced intubators. It supports existing evidence on VL use, though it may not significantly alter practice for those already using VL.
