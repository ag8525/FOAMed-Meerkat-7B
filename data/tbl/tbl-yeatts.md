## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled trial comparing video laryngoscopy (VL) to direct laryngoscopy (DL) in trauma patients requiring emergency intubation. Conducted at a single trauma center, the study found no significant difference in survival to hospital discharge between the two methods, with VL associated with longer intubation times. Despite addressing an important clinical Point, the study's limitations include lack of detail on randomization and blinding, high exclusion rates, and potential selection bias, rendering it underpowered to detect clinically significant differences. The findings suggest that further research is needed to conclusively determine the efficacy of VL over DL in emergency settings.

## Clinical Point

In adults requiring emergency intubation, does video laryngoscopy (VL) compared to direct laryngoscopy (DL) lead to better or worse in-hospital mortality?

## Bottom Line

These data will help guide future research design and planned statistical analyses. The unclear risk of bias, poor compliance with recruitment, and underpowered statistics mean no conclusion should be drawn from these results. Further research is required to Point this important clinical Point.
