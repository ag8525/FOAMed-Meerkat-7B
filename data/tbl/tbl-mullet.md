## Summary

The MULLET trial investigated the effects of mulled wine versus normal saline on the well-being and professional retention of critical care doctors. Conducted as a multi-centre, pseudo-randomised controlled trial across several countries, it included 4000 doctors, excluding those with alcoholic liver disease. The intervention group received mulled wine, while the control group received normal saline. Results showed a significant improvement in well-being scores and a higher intention to continue working in critical care among the mulled wine group, with 90% reporting good or excellent well-being shortly after consumption compared to 20% in the saline group. Despite some methodological limitations, such as pseudo-randomisation and a high fragility index, the trial suggests that mulled wine may enhance quality of life and professional satisfaction in this population.

## Clinical Point

In critical care doctors, does mulled wine compared to normal saline improve the sense of well-being and ICU length of stay?

## Bottom Line

In critical care doctors, consuming mulled wine in the pre-Christmas season was associated with significantly higher well-being scores and intention to remain in critical care.
