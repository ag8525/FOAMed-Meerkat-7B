## Summary

The INCEPTION trial investigated whether extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) improves survival with favorable neurologic outcomes compared to conventional CPR (CCPR) in patients with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) and initial ventricular arrhythmia. Conducted across multiple centers in the Netherlands, the trial included patients aged 18-70 with witnessed arrests and shockable rhythms, excluding those with rapid return of spontaneous circulation or significant comorbidities. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in 30-day survival with favorable neurologic outcomes between ECPR and CCPR groups (20% vs. 16%, respectively). Despite higher ICU admissions and adverse events in the ECPR group, the trial concluded that ECPR did not significantly enhance neurologic outcomes, highlighting the need for further research on optimal ECPR delivery systems.

## Clinical Point

In patients with refractory out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) and an initial ventricular arrhythmia, does extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) improve survival with a favourable neurologic outcome compared to conventional CPR (CCPR)?

## Background

OHCA is a significant public health issue with high mortality. Refractory cardiac arrest is defined as lasting >15 minutes despite resuscitation attempts. Conventional CPR is often ineffective after 20-30 minutes. ECPR can restore perfusion and limit hypoxic brain injury in selected patients, but evidence from previous trials is limited and not generalisable.

## Bottom Line

ECPR did not significantly improve neurologically favourable outcomes in this trial. The potential benefit of ECPR in different settings has not been disproven, and future research should focus on optimal systems for effective ECPR delivery.
