## Summary

This retrospective cohort study analyzed data from NHS hospitals in England to assess whether weekend admissions are associated with increased 30-day mortality compared to weekday admissions. The study included over 14 million cases and found that patients admitted on weekends had a higher risk of 30-day mortality, with hazard ratios of 1.11 for Saturday and 1.16 for Sunday admissions compared to Wednesday. Conversely, being an in-patient over the weekend was associated with a reduced in-hospital mortality risk. Subgroup analyses indicated that the increased mortality risk was more pronounced in elective admissions and certain diagnostic categories like stroke and heart failure. Despite the large cohort and comprehensive statistical adjustments, the study's observational nature limits causal inferences, and it highlights the need for further research to explore underlying factors contributing to these findings.

## Clinical Point

Do patients admitted to English NHS hospitals on weekends have an increased 30-day mortality compared to those admitted on weekdays?

## Bottom Line

This study shows an association between weekend admissions and increased 30-day mortality, but many confounding variables limit definitive conclusions. It should be considered hypothesis-generating only.
