## Summary

The ARREST trial investigated whether expedited transfer to a cardiac arrest centre (CAC) for patients with non-ST-elevation out-of-hospital cardiac arrest improves 30-day all-cause mortality compared to transport to the nearest emergency department (ED). Conducted as a multicentre, open-label, randomised trial in London, the study included 862 patients with return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) and excluded those with non-cardiac causes, pregnancy, ST-elevation myocardial infarction, or a do-not-resuscitate order. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in 30-day mortality between the CAC and ED groups (63% in both). Secondary outcomes, including 3-month mortality and modified Rankin Scale scores, also showed no significant differences. Subgroup analysis suggested a potential benefit for patients under 57 years old in the CAC group. The study concludes that prehospital transfer to a CAC does not improve outcomes for all patients with resuscitated cardiac arrest without ST elevation, though potential benefits for younger patients warrant further investigation.

## Clinical Point

In patients with non-ST elevation out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, does expedited delivery to a cardiac arrest centre, compared with delivery to the geographically closest hospital, improve all-cause mortality at 30 days?

## Background

Regional variations in survival following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest have been reported. Concentrating services within regionalised centres may improve survival by utilising the skills and infrastructure of these centres. However, previous studies have shown that expedited coronary angiography following OHCA without ST elevation does not improve survival and may worsen outcomes. It is unknown if direct transfer to a cardiac centre in this context is beneficial.

## Bottom Line

This study does not support prehospital transfer of all patients to a cardiac arrest centre following resuscitated cardiac arrest without ST elevation.
