## Summary

This document discusses a randomized controlled trial evaluating the timing of endoscopy in patients with acute upper gastrointestinal (UGI) bleeding and a Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS) ≥ 12. The study compared outcomes of endoscopy performed within 6 hours versus 6–24 hours after gastroenterological consultation. Conducted in Hong Kong, the trial found no significant difference in 30-day mortality between the urgent and early endoscopy groups, with mortality rates of 8.9% and 6.6%, respectively. Despite more frequent endoscopic interventions in the urgent group, there were no differences in re-bleeding, hospital stay duration, or transfusion requirements. The study concludes that early endoscopy does not improve mortality outcomes, though it emphasizes that these findings should not delay endoscopy in critically ill patients.

## Clinical Point

In patients with acute upper GI bleeding and a Glasgow-Blatchford score (GBS) ≥ 12, does endoscopy performed within 6 hours of gastroenterological consultation, compared with endoscopy performed within 6–24 hours, reduce 30-day mortality?

## Background

- Endoscopy for acute UGI bleed is recommended within 24 hours by an International Consensus Group.
- The optimal timing within the first 24 hours is unclear.
- Previous studies showed no differences in mortality or re-bleeding when comparing immediate vs. delayed endoscopy.

## Bottom Line

In patients with high-risk UGI bleeding treated with pre-endoscopy PPI and not in persistent shock, endoscopy at a median of 10 hours vs. 25 hours post-presentation does not reduce 30-day mortality. The study's findings should not be used to delay endoscopy in critically unwell patients.
