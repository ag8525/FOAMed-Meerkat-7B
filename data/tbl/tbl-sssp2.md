## Summary

The SSSP2 trial investigated the impact of an early resuscitation protocol on in-hospital mortality among Zambian adults with sepsis and hypotension, revealing that aggressive fluid and vasopressor management increased mortality compared to usual care. Conducted in a resource-limited setting, the trial found a 15.1% absolute risk increase in mortality with the protocol, highlighting potential harm from fluid overload and dopamine use, particularly in patients with hypoxia and tachypnea. The study underscores the need for cautious application of sepsis protocols in developing countries, as the results are not directly applicable to developed settings due to differences in patient demographics and healthcare resources.

## Clinical Point

In Zambian adults with sepsis and hypotension, does an early resuscitation protocol improve in-hospital mortality compared with usual care?

## Background

- Sepsis protocols have reduced mortality in developed countries.
- High sepsis mortality persists in developing countries.
- Uncertainty exists about the effectiveness of early sepsis resuscitation protocols in resource-limited settings.
- Usual care may lead to under-resuscitation, suggesting a protocol promoting aggressive fluid, blood, and vasopressor use might improve outcomes.
- The FEAST study indicated increased mortality with fluid boluses in African children with sepsis.
- A Zambian trial on fluids and vasopressors in sepsis was halted due to potential harm in patients with hypoxia and tachypnoea.

## Bottom Line

Protocolised fluids and dopamine appear harmful in African patients with severe sepsis and should be abandoned in this setting. Results are not applicable to developed countries due to differences in patient characteristics and resources.
