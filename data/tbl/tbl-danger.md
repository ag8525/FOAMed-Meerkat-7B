## Summary

The study investigates the efficacy of microaxial flow pumps (MAFP) in reducing mortality in patients with STEMI-related cardiogenic shock compared to standard care. Conducted as an international, multi-center RCT, the trial enrolled 360 patients, revealing that MAFP use reduced 180-day mortality from 58.5% to 45.8% (HR 0.74), with a number needed to treat of 8. However, MAFP was associated with increased adverse events, including severe bleeding and renal replacement therapy. Despite these risks, the trial suggests a mortality benefit, prompting consideration of MAFP in clinical practice for STEMI-related cardiogenic shock, though the findings are tempered by the study's unblinded design and industry involvement.

## Clinical Point

In adults presenting with STEMI and cardiogenic shock, does the use of a microaxial flow pump (Impella CP) compared to standard care reduce death from any cause at day 180?

## Background

Cardiogenic shock is a frequent complication of STEMI, associated with significant morbidity and mortality. Microaxial flow pumps (MAFP) are percutaneously inserted devices that pump blood from the left ventricle into the ascending aorta. Previous RCTs have been small, showing no benefit and some harm (e.g., bleeding) in registry studies.

## Bottom Line

This trial shows a mortality benefit with MAFP use in STEMI-related cardiogenic shock, despite increased adverse events. It should prompt discussions on MAFP use in local healthcare settings.
