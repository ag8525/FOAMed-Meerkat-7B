## Summary

The CHEER trial investigates a protocolized approach for managing refractory cardiac arrest using mechanical CPR, hypothermia, ECMO, and early reperfusion, conducted at The Alfred Hospital in Melbourne. This prospective pilot observational study included 26 patients with out-of-hospital and in-hospital cardiac arrests, primarily due to cardiac etiology, and demonstrated a 54% survival rate with good neurological recovery. The protocol involved mechanical chest compressions, rapid induction of hypothermia, ECMO initiation, and urgent coronary intervention. Despite the high survival rates, complications such as major bleeding and vascular injuries were noted. The study highlights the feasibility and potential benefits of this approach in a specialized ECMO center, though further multi-center randomized controlled trials are needed to validate these findings.

## Clinical Point

In patients experiencing refractory cardiac arrest, does a protocolised management approach involving mechanical CPR, hypothermia, ECMO, and early reperfusion improve survival rates?

## Bottom Line

The CHEER protocol improves survival with favorable neurological outcomes following cardiac arrest compared to historical data. It is effective in an experienced ECMO center with pre-hospital service engagement. A multi-center RCT is needed to determine if these results can be replicated.
