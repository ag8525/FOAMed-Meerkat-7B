## Summary

In hypotensive patients with penetrating torso trauma, a randomized controlled trial compared immediate versus delayed fluid resuscitation strategies, revealing that delaying fluid resuscitation until operative intervention improved survival to discharge (70% vs. 62%, p = 0.04). The study, conducted in a pre-hospital setting in the USA, included 598 patients and found that the delayed group received significantly less fluid prior to surgery (386 ml vs. 2611 ml). The authors concluded that aggressive IV fluid administration should be postponed until surgical intervention, emphasizing the importance of timely surgical management over pre-operative fluid resuscitation. Despite its strengths, such as appropriate power calculation and intention-to-treat analysis, the study's limitations include non-blinded randomization and potential lack of generalizability due to its single-centre design.

## Clinical Point

In hypotensive patients with penetrating torso trauma, does a delayed fluid resuscitation strategy, compared to immediate fluid resuscitation, improve survival?

## Bottom Line

Aggressive fluid resuscitation in hypotensive patients following penetrating torso trauma should be avoided. The priority in bleeding trauma patients is timely surgical intervention.
