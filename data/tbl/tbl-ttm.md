## Summary

The TTM trial investigated whether targeted temperature management at 33°C compared to 36°C in adults who suffered out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause affects mortality or neurological outcomes. Conducted across 36 intensive care units in Europe and Australia, the randomized controlled trial included 950 unconscious patients. The primary outcome showed no significant difference in all-cause mortality between the two temperature targets, with a hazard ratio of 1.06 (95% CI: 0.89–1.28, p = 0.51). Secondary outcomes also revealed no difference in neurological status. The study concluded that targeting 33°C does not offer additional benefits over 36°C, suggesting that if cooling to 33°C causes adverse effects, maintaining 36°C is a reasonable alternative without impacting outcomes.

## Clinical Point

In adults who suffered out-of-hospital cardiac arrest of presumed cardiac cause, does induced therapeutic hypothermia targeting 36°C compared to 33°C reduce mortality or neurological deficit?

## Bottom Line

This trial has not shown any benefit of therapeutic hypothermia at 33°C over 36°C after OOH cardiac arrest. If cooling to 33°C causes unwanted effects, targeting 36°C instead is reasonable and probably will not affect outcomes.
