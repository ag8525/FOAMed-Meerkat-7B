## Summary

The PART trial investigated the impact of initial airway management with a laryngeal tube (LT) versus endotracheal intubation (ETI) on 72-hour survival in adults experiencing out-of-hospital non-traumatic cardiac arrest. Conducted across 27 EMS agencies, the study found that the LT group had a higher 72-hour survival rate (18.3%) compared to the ETI group (15.4%), with an adjusted absolute risk reduction of 2.9% and a number needed to treat of 35. Secondary outcomes also favored the LT group, including higher rates of return of spontaneous circulation, hospital survival, and favorable neurological status at discharge. Despite the trial's strengths in addressing a critical clinical Point with a robust methodology, limitations such as potential systematic biases due to the cluster-randomized design and lack of blinding were noted. Overall, the study suggests that LT may be a more effective initial airway management strategy in this context, though findings may not be generalizable to other supraglottic devices.

## Clinical Point

In adults suffering out-of-hospital non-traumatic cardiac arrest, does a strategy of airway management initially with a laryngeal tube (LT) compared to endotracheal intubation (ETI) improve survival at 72 hours?

## Bottom Line

This trial suggests that a strategy involving laryngeal tubes as the initial airway management device may be superior to endotracheal intubation in pre-hospital, non-traumatic, adult cardiac arrest settings. However, potential systematic biases may affect the observed difference.
