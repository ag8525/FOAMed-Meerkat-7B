## Summary

The DEFUSE 3 trial investigated the efficacy of thrombectomy performed 6-16 hours after ischemic stroke onset in patients with proximal internal carotid or middle cerebral artery occlusion and salvageable brain tissue, as identified by perfusion imaging. The study demonstrated that endovascular therapy, in addition to standard medical treatment, significantly improved functional outcomes at 90 days, as measured by the modified Rankin Scale, compared to medical therapy alone. The trial highlighted the importance of patient selection using perfusion imaging to identify those with favorable ischemic core to penumbra ratios. Despite its promising results, the trial's applicability to broader clinical settings may be limited due to its specific inclusion criteria and early termination.

## Clinical Point

In patients with proximal internal carotid (ICA) or middle cerebral artery (MCA) occlusion with likely salvageable ischemic brain tissue, does thrombectomy at 6-16 hours post-ischemic stroke, in addition to medical therapy, improve functional outcome at 90 days compared to medical therapy alone?

## Background

- Ischemic stroke is a leading cause of death and disability worldwide.
- Current therapies include thrombolytics up to 4.5 hours post-onset in select patients.
- Previous trials showed mixed results for late stroke care with thrombolytics.
- Recent trials (e.g., MR CLEAN) demonstrated benefits of endovascular therapy (ET) up to 6 hours post-stroke.
- DEFUSE 3 examines the 6-16 hour window using CT perfusion to select candidates.

## Bottom Line

In patients with large vessel strokes and favorable ischemic core to penumbra ratios, endovascular therapy at 6-16 hours post-stroke onset, in addition to medical therapy, improved functional neurological outcomes at 90 days compared to medical therapy alone. The results are promising but may not be broadly applicable in clinical practice.
