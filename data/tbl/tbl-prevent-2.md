## Summary

The PreVent trial investigated the impact of bag-mask ventilation (BMV) on oxygen saturation levels during tracheal intubation in critically ill adults in the ICU. Conducted across seven US ICUs, this randomized trial compared BMV with no ventilation between induction and laryngoscopy. The study found that BMV significantly improved the lowest oxygen saturation levels and reduced the incidence of severe hypoxaemia (oxygen saturation <80%) compared to no ventilation. Despite these benefits, the trial was not powered to assess the risk of aspiration, and its findings may not apply to emergent intubations or high-risk patients. The study underscores BMV as a potentially effective strategy to mitigate hypoxia during intubation, though further research is needed to evaluate aspiration risks.

## Clinical Point

In adult ICU patients undergoing tracheal intubation, does bag-mask ventilation (BMV) between induction and laryngoscopy reduce the risk of hypoxaemia?

## Background

- Intubation in ICU has high morbidity and mortality.
- BMV may prevent hypoxia but can increase aspiration risk.
- DAS guidelines suggest gentle mask ventilation prolongs time to desaturation.

## Bottom Line

BMV appears to be a reasonable intervention to prevent hypoxia during intubation, though the study is underpowered to assess aspiration risk.
