## Summary

Delayed Sequence Intubation (DSI) is a technique evaluated in a prospective observational study involving 62 patients across three hospitals, aimed at improving pre-intubation oxygen saturation in patients who do not tolerate traditional pre-oxygenation methods. The study utilized ketamine to facilitate pre-oxygenation, resulting in a significant increase in SpO₂ from 88.9% to 98.8% prior to muscle relaxation. The intervention involved positioning patients head-up, using a non-rebreather mask and nasal cannula, and switching to CPAP if SpO₂ remained below 95%. No major complications were reported, and the study concluded that DSI is a safe and effective method for improving oxygenation in challenging intubation scenarios, though further randomized controlled trials are recommended to validate these findings.

## Clinical Point

In patients requiring emergency intubation who do not tolerate pre-oxygenation, does the use of 'delayed sequence intubation' (DSI) improve pre-intubation oxygen saturations (SpO₂)?

## Bottom Line

In this prospective observational study, DSI significantly improved oxygen saturations in patients requiring emergency intubation who did not tolerate traditional pre-oxygenation methods. A randomised controlled trial is warranted to compare DSI with other techniques. Meanwhile, DSI can be considered in selected cases based on this level 4 evidence.
