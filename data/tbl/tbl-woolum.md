## Summary

In patients with septic shock, the administration of intravenous thiamine within 24 hours of ICU admission was associated with more rapid lactate clearance and a significant reduction in 28-day mortality, particularly benefiting female patients. This retrospective, matched cohort study utilized data from a single academic center and included 1049 patients, with 123 receiving thiamine. Despite the study's strengths, such as its large sample size and matching to control confounding variables, limitations include its retrospective nature, potential residual confounding, and variable thiamine dosing. While the findings suggest potential benefits of thiamine in septic shock, they are considered hypothesis-generating due to the study's design limitations.

## Clinical Point

In patients with septic shock, does the administration of thiamine lead to more rapid lactate clearance and improved clinical outcomes?

## Background

Septic shock is characterized by a hypermetabolic state similar to thiamine deficiency. Thiamine (Vitamin B1) deficiency is common in critically ill patients. Previous pilot RCTs have shown lower lactate levels and improved mortality in thiamine-deficient septic shock patients.

## Bottom Line

This study suggests intravenous thiamine may improve outcomes in septic shock, but due to inherent biases, it should be considered hypothesis-generating. Given low risk, thiamine may be considered in critically ill sepsis patients.
