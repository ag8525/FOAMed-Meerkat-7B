## Summary

The document discusses a randomized controlled non-inferiority trial comparing the supraclavicular and infraclavicular approaches for ultrasound-guided right subclavian venous catheterization in elective neurosurgery patients. The study found that the supraclavicular approach, using a long-axis in-plane ultrasound technique, resulted in significantly fewer catheter-related complications, particularly catheter misplacement, compared to the infraclavicular approach, which used a short-axis out-of-plane technique. Both approaches had similar success rates and times for catheterization, but the supraclavicular method demonstrated a lower complication rate, with a mean difference of -10.4% in catheterization-related complications. The trial was conducted at Seoul National University Hospital and included 401 patients, highlighting the supraclavicular approach as a superior method for reducing complications in this clinical setting.

## Clinical Point

In patients undergoing elective neurosurgery, is a supraclavicular in-plane ultrasound-guided approach non-inferior to an infraclavicular out-of-plane ultrasound-guided approach for catheterisation-related complications?

## Background

- Central venous lines are commonly placed in the internal jugular, femoral, and subclavian veins.
- Subclavian lines have a lower infection risk but a higher pneumothorax risk.
- Previous RCTs suggest the supraclavicular approach is quicker with similar success and fewer complications than the infraclavicular approach.

## Bottom Line

In elective neurosurgery patients, the supraclavicular approach with in-plane ultrasound is non-inferior to the infraclavicular approach with out-of-plane ultrasound, with significantly fewer catheter-related complications, mainly due to reduced misplacement.
