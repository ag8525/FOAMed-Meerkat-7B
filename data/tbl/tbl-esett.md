## Summary

The ESETT trial investigated the efficacy of levetiracetam, valproate, and fosphenytoin as second-line treatments for benzodiazepine-refractory status epilepticus in a randomized, blinded, and adaptive design across 57 U.S. emergency departments. The study included patients aged 2 years and older who continued to experience seizures after benzodiazepine administration. The primary outcome was the cessation of seizures and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes without additional anticonvulsant medication. Results showed similar efficacy among the three drugs, with seizure cessation rates of 47% for levetiracetam, 45% for fosphenytoin, and 46% for valproate, and no significant differences in secondary outcomes such as ICU admission or safety. The trial concluded that all three medications are similarly effective, with the study's strengths including its large sample size and adaptive design, though it faced limitations such as early termination and unblinding issues.

## Clinical Point

In patients with status epilepticus who have been treated with benzodiazepines, is levetiracetam, valproate, or fosphenytoin more effective in leading to seizure cessation and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes?

## Background

Benzodiazepines are the first-line treatment for seizures, but there is limited evidence guiding second-line treatments. Previous trials (ConSEPT and EcLIPSE) compared levetiracetam and phenytoin in pediatric status epilepticus, finding no superiority of levetiracetam.

## Bottom Line

In benzodiazepine-refractory status epilepticus, levetiracetam, valproate, and fosphenytoin each led to seizure cessation and improved responsiveness at 60 minutes in nearly half of the patients.
